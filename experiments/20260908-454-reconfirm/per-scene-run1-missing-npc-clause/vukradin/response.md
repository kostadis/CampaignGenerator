Harbin Wester's office smells of ink and the particular sweat of a man who has just been told that things are about to move faster than he would like. I have laid it out for him in full — the dragon, the orcs, the treasure sitting in the town's care like a stone in a boot, and the meeting of the Privy Council that must happen now, today, while the town is still grateful and the gold is still warm. He looks at me the way a man looks at a bill.

"So soon?" he says. "So quickly? Right now?"

I lean on the honeyed register first, because persuasion is my instrument and a room is only a room until you have found its key. He does not budge. So I try the other thing, the low thing, the thing Brewbarry does without trying — I put some steel in my shoulders and let my voice drop, and I tell him the council will meet.

It comes out thin. I can hear it come out thin.

Harbin actually turns his back to me. "Well," he says over his shoulder, "your intimidation lands as flatly as your sweet tongue would have landed well."

Right. The hubris is rising — I can feel it climbing my neck like a fever — and I am aware, in some far room of my mind, that a mayor refusing a meeting is not in itself corruption, only cowardice, which is corruption's quieter cousin. Fine. Fine!

"I'll gather the people myself," I tell him, and I go.

---

Outside, in the road, I put it to my friends. "We could put on a rock concert and get them all out there," I say, "get them all happy and bouncing around before I make my big speech."

Brewbarry nods once. "I can make my voice boom to announce that there is a gathering."

"Decent performance outside. Not as good as inside — but they're drunker now, so they're easier to persuade." I look up the road toward the green. "I guess I would probably perform on the green, right?"

So that is where we go. Brewbarry's announcement rolls over the roofs of Phandalin like weather. I tune, I plant my feet in the grass, and I play the set I would play for a full tavern, only louder and with more sky.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: The townsfolk gather on the green, including Harbin, Petunia, Big Al Kalazorn, Qelline Alderleaf, Linene Graywind, Ser Kaelen, Toblen and his bar team, Elsa, Barthen, and the woodworker.]

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: The crowd's mood — dancing, relaxed, easy.]

This is the room I wanted. This is the room I could not have gotten out of Harbin with a crowbar. When the last chord settles into the grass, I step forward and open my arms.

"Ladies and gentlemen, we are adventurers! We've killed the dragon! We drove out the orcs! We saved Phandalin! Yay!"

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Brin and Giles are in the crowd, engaged, and once again sunburnt.]

"Kissed by Lathander," Soma says beside me, flat as a hearthstone, and I do not have time to laugh because the crowd is mine and a crowd does not stay yours.

"What I wanna say is… that your government, led by Wester over here —" I find him in the crowd and I point, so there is no ambiguity about which Wester — "believes that the way adventurers should work is that they should go do heroic things, and then claim all the treasure for themselves."

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: The crowd falls quiet and stares.]

The silence is not against me. I know the difference. This is the silence of a room waiting to be told what note comes next.

"That treasure came from you. That is your treasure. It was stolen from you." I let it sit. "I want to give it back to you."

A hand goes up near the front. Linene Graywind, who has never in her life let a number pass unexamined. "Well, strictly speaking, it may not have been stolen from me — but it may have been stolen from somebody else a long time ago. And so by you bringing it into the market…" She stops, and starts again. "Hold on. By you bringing it into the market, I get to — it recirculates." She is working it out in front of the whole town, and to her credit she does not pretend otherwise. "Right? And so when you spend that money, you're… you're making it mine. Because when it's over there in the hands of the monsters —"

"Hang on," I tell her, warmly, because she is on my side whether she knows it or not. "All your questions will be answered."

Near her a woman I have seen at the council's elbow — Jenna Roscoe, of the Lord's Alliance, if I have her right — says, with the tired authority of someone who has read the book, "This has been very well covered in the dialectics of the financials."

I do not know what that means and I do not need to.

"If we can find the rightful owner of that treasure," I say, "then it needs to be returned. But we have found, as adventurers, that many times we cannot find the rightful owners of this treasure." Cryovain's hoard has no ledger. The orcs kept no receipts. I have looked. Lathander knows I have looked. "And so I swear to you, now, here: a new, universal, basic treasure."

"UBT," Soma murmurs, trying the shape of it in her mouth.

Here is the thing I know about myself: on a bad day, this speech dies in the grass. It is not the words. The words are good. It is whether the room has decided to like me before I open my mouth, and today, after the concert, with Brewbarry's voice still ringing in their ears, the room has decided. I feel it land.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: General cheering from the crowd; Jenna Roscoe looks appalled.]

And now the man himself. "I'd like to hear," I say, turning, "what Harbin Wester thinks about this plan. Is he in favor of it, or does he need to be replaced?"

Harbin comes forward with the smile of a man stepping onto ice. "I am so glad! This was a… this was… I'm —" He stops. "My good friend, and member of the Privy Council —" and here he stalls completely, mouth open, hunting for it — "Vukradin!"

He could not remember my name. He is about to stand in front of Phandalin and take my policy, and he could not remember my name. Sounds about right.

"That is an extraordinary idea," Harbin goes on, gathering speed the way cowards do once they have found the safe direction, "and that is why we have declared him extraordinary — we have declared the income that he produces as part of his adventuring career: Vukradin's UBT. The Privy Council had heard this proposal, and we were waiting for a few more days before we would make a formal announcement — but this is as good a way to make an announcement as possible. All hail to Vukradin and the…" He falters again. "What is the name of your adventuring group?"

Scandalous. The council heard this proposal? The council heard it ten minutes ago, from me, in a room where the council told me no. I file it. I file it next to the six gold pieces by post and the name of every man who has ever lied to a crowd in my hearing. But the crowd is cheering, and the crowd is what the gold is for, and I will not spend their moment on my grievance.

"Did we ever make one?" Soma asks.

"Slayers of Cryovain," I offer.

"We'll have to have a slight sidebar," I concede, when nobody bites. "I don't know, what name do you guys want?"

"No," Soma says, "it would be the band's name, right? At this point it's a —"

"Well, not yet." I look at her. "Soma's not in the band, so…"

"No, no, she's just a tour guide," Soma agrees, of herself, with no offense at all. "I'm your manager at this point."

"Valphine and Vukradin's death metal…" I try, and it is not right either, and Valphine gives me nothing to work with.

Harbin gives up before we do. "Yeah — well, all hail the adventuring group!"

Good enough. I turn back to him while he is still holding the pose. "Okay, so — Wester, we're gonna be writing into the town charter, then, that all adventurers will distribute their treasure as universal basic treasure to the citizens of Phandalin. Unless they can find the rightful owner, of course."

"I think that's a wonderful idea!" says Harbin, who has never held a thought long enough to think it. "Huzzah, huzzah, huzzah!"

"So it's written in," I say. "Alright."

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Jenna Roscoe looks even more appalled.]

"Is she outvoted?" I ask.

"Democracy sucks," Soma observes, to the air.

"And I would say, as the first contribution to the UBT fund," I go on, because momentum is a real thing and you do not stop to admire it, "we have this melted hoard of dragon gold that we can't do anything else with."

Brewbarry sets it down in the grass. It is enormous — Cryovain's coins run together into one bright, ugly lump, more anvil than treasure, and the crowd leans in and then leans back.

"Nobody told us that," I say.

There is one man on this green with an Order behind him and a sense of duty in front of him. "Kaelen," I call, "can you take care of this?"

"Yeah," Brewbarry says, "be useful for once."

"This seems like your kind of thing," I tell the knight, ignoring my barbarian. "Get this money to the people. They need it."

Ser Kaelen holds up a hand. "Sidebar," he says. "Sidebar…"

"Do it, old man!"

"Sidebar. Does the Order of the Gauntlet get a share, or is this only strictly for the citizens of Phandalin? And what do we mean by citizens of Phandalin?"

Valphine, who has been silent through the whole rally in the way that means she is counting something, says only that I should put it to the crowd.

"Excellent, excellent idea, Valphine!" And I do.

Brewbarry answers first. "I would say property owners within the bounds of Phandalin."

"Landed gentry," Soma says.

"What about renters?"

"Wait, do they actually have property?" Soma asks. "I thought they were all serfs or whatever."

They do. I have signed enough of their receipts to know.

"As long as you live within the bounds of Phandalin, then you're a Phandalin citizen," Brewbarry amends. "How is that? Is that open enough?" And then, as an honest afterthought, "I need to get myself a house in Phandalin, by the way."

"Well, what if somebody just comes down from Neverwinter for the day, right?" I say. "And immigrates to Phandalin, then suddenly they are entitled to the UBT?"

"That's why we need to build a wall," Soma says.

"You have to live within Phandalin's boundary for at least fifty-plus percent of the year," Brewbarry decides.

"Primary residence, I mean…"

"That's right, that's right."

"…and attest under oath with the Zone of Truth spell in effect," I add, because a policy without a save is just a wish.

"And you have to pay your taxes in Phandalin, too, right?" Brewbarry tries, and then reverses himself before anyone can answer: "No, no. You can just determine if they pay taxes in Phandalin, then they are Phandalin citizens."

Linene has waited for exactly this gap. She steps forward with a paper already in her hand — a real one, foxed at the edges, Candlekeep's stamp on it. "I think this is a fabulous idea. I've been working on such a proposal for years. The Sage's Universal Theorem for Income Distribution." She holds it so I can see it and the crowd cannot. "If I could suggest, Vukradin."

"Yes, of course."

"Perhaps a subcommittee of the Privy Council, led by myself — obviously with your supervision — could provide a draft set of rules in time for next week, maybe two weeks from now, for how the treasure would be distributed."

Two weeks. "So the people need the money sooner than that," I say, and I say it to the crowd, not to her. "They're poor! They're suffering! They need UBT!"

The children at the front have found the word and will not let it go. Do we get UBT? Do we get it too?

"We never have to work a day in our lives?" Soma asks them, in a tone I would not call encouraging.

Well — as long as there are adventurers, someone says. As long as adventurers come.

"Yeah," Soma allows. "It's true."

"There's some sort of magical intelligence stealing their jobs," Valphine says, dry as chalk.

"Of course, Vukradin is absolutely a member of the Privy Council. But — the Tortle! And the… the Barbarian! Their exact ties to the community!"

"You're talking to the muscle of this party."

"Five percent of the blood money," I say slowly, "now becomes organic fair trade?"

"Well," Soma says, "it's been laundered through the UBT."

"Effectively, yes."
