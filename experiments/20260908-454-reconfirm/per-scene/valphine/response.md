Brewbarry has already decided how this conversation ends before it begins. I can see it in his shoulders. He leans over the little banker's desk the way a cliff leans over a road.

"You're not gonna help me. I'm gonna help you, old man. Don't get things confused in that little head of yours." He lets that sit. "Now, please tell me, is there some business to be done here, or should I go elsewhere?"

Aurelan, to his small credit, does not flinch so much as recalculate. In Menzoberranzan a functionary in his position would already be composing the report that shifts the blame; here he only folds his hands and asks the obvious question.

"Well, what are you making?"

"What do you mean, what am I baking?"

"Making."

"Am I supposed to tell you what I'm making? I've got this letter here." Brewbarry produces Linene's letter and puts it down as though it were an ultimatum.

The banker reads it. "Yes, well. What the letter says is that Linene is saying that she wants us to lend her money so that she can start her business, but she doesn't describe the nature of the business."

Brewbarry draws breath, and what comes out is not a business plan but a creed. "I'm gonna show the world how cruel this world is, and I'm giving this softness to all the good people of the Forgotten Realms by supplying them with bathrobes."

It is sincere. That is the difficulty with Brewbarry: he means everything, and the banker has no shelf on which to file a man who means everything.

"That's very interesting," Aurelan says, in the tone of one who has heard more interesting things.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Aurelan takes out a small piece of paper and a quill and begins scribbling notes as he questions Brewbarry.]

"Do you know how much this will cost to make?"

"I have absolutely no idea, which is why I have a wealth management consultant. I suggest that you talk to her."

"Very well. I will make a note of talking to her. And how do you intend to ship your goods?"

"What do I look like? Some merchant? The logistics guy? No." He straightens to his full height, which in this room is an argument in itself. "I hack and slash."

Aurelan writes that down too. I watch the quill. A man who records "I hack and slash" as a shipping method is either very stupid or very careful, and his eyes are not stupid. He is building a file. He has the letter, the consultant, the intended shipper, and he reaches the inference before Brewbarry offers it.

"Oh, I see you intend to use Lionshield Coster as your shipping agent and business arrangement." He pauses, choosing the next words with the delicacy of someone stepping around a sleeping animal. "Mr. Brewbarry, I wish to apologize. I want you to know that I have no objection to Lionshield Coster's business, and I've done wonderful business with them, but their rates have gone ridiculously up, and I'm afraid that, based on my calculations—"

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Aurelan produces a sheet of calculations that none of the party can read.]

"Well, I will have to double-check the numbers, but unfortunately, I have to be honest with you. With the margins we expect, even with Margaster's very generous margins and prices, and the cost of moving goods in and out of Phandalin, plus—" He is warming to it now, the way a priest warms to the part of the liturgy he actually understands. "I don't think… I feel that the Counting House—"

"Sorry, let me, let me, let me cut you off." Brewbarry raises one hand. "Yadda yadda yadda yadda, you get my blood pumping. Just tell me you're gonna get it done, and that's it. Otherwise, I'm gonna get upset. You don't want to see me upset."

It is a crude instrument, but it is an instrument, and I note that the banker's quill has stopped. Crude does not mean ineffective. The Coster's rates are the wall he keeps returning to, and his arithmetic, whatever it says, is arithmetic about the wrong quantity. He is pricing bathrobes as though they were sold one at a time to strangers.

"We're not tallying in two additional factors," I say.

Aurelan looks up, and for the first time looks at me rather than at Brewbarry's chest.

"The temple markup." I let him absorb that a temple is involved. "And the church of Lathander's bulk demand."

He blinks. He looks at the sigil, at my face, and the arithmetic in his head visibly reroutes. "Oh," he says. "You are one of the blessed of Lathander."

Then the second thing arrives, a beat behind the first, as it always does with surface dwellers. "And you're a drow."

Surface dwellers are so easily surprised by the obvious. I do not answer him; the sun answers for me every morning. It is Brewbarry who supplies the verdict, flat as a dropped stone.

"You're very perceptive, Aurelan."

The banker recovers by retreating into the thing he was always going to say. "But Mr. Brewbarry, I wish to inform you that I do have a solution to your problem. Since your business is in production, what I would suggest you do is, instead of using Lionshield Coster for distribution of goods, you reach out to House Margaster. They're the ones who have managed to reduce the pricing of goods quite significantly, and I believe with their margins and their arrangements, you would be able to run the business most efficiently. And if you could secure a letter of agreement with them, we would be more than delighted to sign on the dotted line."

And there it is: the referral. In my House, a steward who steered a petitioner toward a rival's door would have been asked, very quietly, what the rival paid him. Brewbarry asks it less quietly.

"Do I look like a fool?" A pause. "Do I look like a fool, Aurelan?"

The halberd comes out. He does not brandish it. He simply holds it, and taps the haft into his other palm, slowly, a metronome for the banker's thinking.

"Exactly. And you tell me to go and do some business with somebody else. You think I'm an idiot? Did you have, like, some sweet deal with this other guy? I didn't give two shit about it." Tap. "Now, I told you, I have a wealth management consultant, she has a supply company, she's gonna borrow money from you, you're gonna lend it to her, and you're gonna get this done."

Vukradin steps in at precisely the moment the pressure needs a second voice, and I admire the timing even as I distrust its warmth. He makes his tone reasonable, collegial, the tone of a man who is on the banker's side against the unfortunate facts.

"Surely, surely there's an interest rate that you would find acceptable, given the increased risk of a lower margin distributor. We can come to some sort of arrangement there, right?"

He gives Aurelan a door with a number on it. The banker's eyes go to it. Between the blade tapping and the door opening, it is a well-constructed room, and I doubt either of them planned it together.

Brewbarry does not let the door stay open long. "Yeah. I also find it a bit strange that she had this technique to get her prices down for supplying things, and then suddenly she tells me that she's got some supply chain issue that I should talk to you about. You tell me you don't know about it, and then you point me to this other guy who has found a way to magically get his costs down."

"No, no, no, no, no, no, no, no, Mr. Brewbarry, and Mr. Vukradin, I think your concerns are legitimate, but I— I would be remiss, I would be remiss as— look, if what you wish to do is to borrow—"

"How remiss you're gonna be if you continue down this path, my friend."

"You see, so— hold on, Mr. Brewbarry. I felt, as your banker, it was my duty to offer you a better path. But if you wish to do business with the Lionshield Coster—"

"Are you my wealth management consultant?"

Aurelan opens his mouth.

"Are you? No, you're the money man. So what you're gonna do is give us money so that we can do business."

The banker looks at the halberd, at Vukradin's open and reasonable face, at me. He has run out of paths. "Very well, Mr… well, Mr. Brewbarry."

"That's what I want to hear." Brewbarry lowers the halberd and, terribly, smiles. "You want to be my friend, Aurelan."

"We'll be able to kickstart your business with…" Aurelan corrects himself under the weight of the letter. "Linene will be able to kickstart your bathrobe business."

Brewbarry takes the papers without reading them. "So now, let's go back to the topic of supply chain issues. Are you sure you don't know anything about it? Because she told me you would."

"Unfortunately, all I know is that there was some kind of system that Lionshield Coster was using that stopped working some time ago for reasons that are obscure and apologetic, but I do not… you'd have to go consult House Margaster if you wanted to learn more. I personally just know that as a result of this sudden change in their shipping methods, the prices of goods shipped by Lionshield Coster have gone up considerably, and their business—"

A system that stopped working, reasons obscure, and the beneficiary of the stoppage is the house he keeps recommending. He does not hear himself. Whether that is innocence or discipline, I cannot yet tell; both wear the same face.

"This is how you do business, you know?" Brewbarry says, and he is not asking. "You send people left and right? No, no. So you're gonna go to House Margaster, you're gonna find out what's going on, and I'm gonna come back tomorrow, and you're gonna tell me how I can fix it. You get it, Aurelan." He taps the loan papers. "Money that I'm gonna give you."

"Anyway, it doesn't hurt to get multiple angles, so you're gonna go to this house, and you're gonna find out—"

"Yes, you are. I was about to say yes. I was about to say yes."

"You're gonna come back, and you're gonna give me what I need."

"Absolutely, mister."

"Good job."

The banker draws himself up into the last dignity available to him, which is the dignity of the institution. "Well, Mr. Brewbarry, I intend to find out what I can by tomorrow. You can rely on the Counting House."

Brewbarry turns from the desk, satisfied, and offers the room his philosophy. "I'm a barbarian, I don't know much about business, but when some wheel—" he searches for the rest of it, "—you turn, you oil it. That's what we're doing."

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: A wealthy client of the Counting House approaches from the crowd, dressed in a sincere silk imitation of Uthgardt barbarian hides.]

"What a genuine costume!"

The man reaches for Brewbarry's sleeve. Brewbarry looks down at the hand as if it were an insect. "Are you touching my clothes? Be careful."

"Wow, you are really into the bit. I am very impressed."

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: The man attempts to imitate Brewbarry's stance and bearing, awkwardly, for an uncomfortable moment.]

Brewbarry simply stops seeing him. It is the cleanest cruelty I have observed all day, and it costs him nothing. The silk barbarian, having no one to perform for, remains in his stance a moment longer than he should.

Vukradin, meanwhile, has one loose thread left, and he cannot leave a room with a loose thread in it. "I didn't find out what they count."

Aurelan stares at him. "We, we, we— the Counting House is a bank."

"Oh, you could have just said you're a bank. Alright."

"Yeah," says the banker, folding his hands on the loan he did not want to make. "We count money."
