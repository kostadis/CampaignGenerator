# Chapter 48 The Knucklebone of Truth

Date: 2026-03-09

## Scenes
### 048.01 Stabilizing the Earth Elemental

The session opened with the Ember Vanguard reaching **seventh level** in the middle of what the DM called "a bit of a war zone." There was no long rest; the new level was the reward for defeating the Pudding King. Gyrgum gained **Divine Strike**: "Once on each of your turns, when you hit a creature with an attack roll using a weapon, you can do an extra one die necrotic or radiant damage." Daz's spellbook grew, and he began learning new spells up to fourth level, among them color spray, comprehend languages, find familiar and grease.

In the aftermath of the Pudding King's defeat, the Ember Vanguard found themselves far from finished. The Earth Elemental that had held the throne room's passage open was beginning to crack under the strain, its elemental energy leaking through fissures in its ancient stone body. Thorin tried to set the cracks with raw strength and rolled a 2. Gyrgum then called upon his divine gifts, casting Mass Healing Word (2d4+4, rolled 8) to mend the elemental's wounds. With a long, relieved stretch, like Atlas finally setting down the world, the great being sighed and dropped a small glowing geode at the party's feet as a token of its favor. It then dissolved back into the earth. The hole stayed open and stable now that the Pudding King was no longer manipulating it. The party sensed that Entémoch, Prince of Good Elementals, looked on them with favor.

As the dust settled, the party noticed the fungi scattered throughout the chamber were growing faster than they should have been. It was a quiet and unsettling reminder that although Juiblex had been driven out, Zuggtmoy's influence still crept through the dark like roots through stone. It was a fight for another day: "Not every problem needs to be solved by the Ember Vanguard."

Before moving on, Thorin's player asked what an ooze like Glabbagool could actually do. Glabbagool is now a gray ooze who can move stealthily and fit in the bag of holding at any size. The party briefly imagined a 20th-level Glabbagool as a T-1000-style liquid-metal killing machine "with that voice."

#### Following the defeat of the Pudding King, the party assists a straining Earth Elemental in holding up the throne room's structure.
- The party levels up to seventh level while still in the war zone, without a long rest. Gyrgum gains Divine Strike (an extra die of necrotic or radiant damage once per turn on a weapon hit), and Daz adds a batch of new spells to his spellbook, up to fourth level.
- The party finds an Earth Elemental struggling to keep the throne room from collapsing, leaking elemental energy through cracks in its form.

- Gyrgum casts a powerful healing spell that restores the elemental's structural integrity. It is Mass Healing Word at 2d4+4, rolled as 8 for everyone.

### 048.02 The Harmonic Center of the Mirror Hall

The party pressed on to the Hall of Melting Mirrors, where Chipgrin and his wererats had been maintaining a chanting vigil to suppress the lingering psychic static left behind by the mirrors. Chipgrin turned to Zalthir and Daz, hoping one of them could locate the harmonic center of the room and silence the noise for good. The results:
- Daz rolled Arcana for a 14.
- Zalthir meditated and listened with Insight, which came out "not great."
- Gyrgum declared himself "well in tune with the spheres" and rolled a 9.

It was Thorin, the party's most reliably stabby and punchy member, who spent a luck point to re-roll his Arcana check, then paused, looked around, and simply pointed. Chipgrin allowed that it must be "the old dwarf in you." He said he should have asked the dwarf first, then caught Daz's eye and hastily clarified that he meant no offense to drow or surface dwellers. Daz, exhausted, declined to take offense.

#### The party encounters Chipgrin and the wererats in the Hall of Melting Mirrors and attempts to neutralize the psychic noise.

- Chipgrin asks the magic users of the party to find the harmonic center of the room to stop the lingering psychic static: "Well, Zalthir or Daz, maybe one of you can help find a harmonic center of this room to neutralize the psychic noise."
- Daz and Zalthir attempt to use their arcane knowledge and insight to locate the center but are unsuccessful. Daz rolls Arcana for a 14, and Zalthir meditates on the sound with Insight, rolling "not great."
- Gyrgum tries to tune into the spheres to find the source of the noise but also fails, concentrating "very hard" and rolling a 9.
- Chipgrin, exasperated, asks for "the damn psychic center" because it is "driving everybody nuts now."
- Thorin is told it's "more of the brains problem" and that he's "more about the stabby, stabby, and the slashy, slashy, and the punchy-punchy." He rolls Arcana (+2), then spends a luck point to re-roll.
- Thorin, using a bit of luck and unexpected intuition, successfully points out the exact location of the psychic center.
- Chipgrin is impressed by the dwarf's success, noting that he should have asked the dwarf first. He then checks with Daz: "we're good, right? We're chill." Daz declines to take offense.

### 048.03 Repairs and Reinforcements at the Support Beams

From there, the Ember Vanguard made their way to the support beams, where Gnomish miners were locked in a heated debate over construction materials and methods. They argued with the passionate conviction of people who had very strong opinions about zurkhwood versus steel, and their temporary bridges were over-engineered enough to carry giants. When Gyrgum asked why they didn't just use a little magic, the miners launched into a long tirade: magic users lose the ability to build with their own two hands, and patch everything with "a mend here and a mend there" until nobody understands how the contraption was built.

Several miners had burns, and Gyrgum treated them with Lesser Restoration. Thorin threw his considerable strength into winching a critical support beam into place. His 11 was pushed to a 19 with his own Tactical Mind. The deep gnomes praised dwarves as "a hardy race that you can rely on for underground construction," and Thorin humbly allowed that "everybody has their own strengths and weaknesses." Diggermattock was so moved that he personally threaded a needle and sewed a Diggermattock Miners patch onto Thorin's gear, declaring him an honorary member of the crew. Zalthir already had one sewn on his hoodie from before. Gyrgum, not to be outdone, used Mending to attach the patch with such precision that everyone present was briefly more impressed by the needlework than by the feat of engineering.

Chipgrin also shared a piece of wererat wisdom: a tip about a shortcut out of the tunnels that bypasses a dangerous pass through faerzress, whose exposure drives people mad. The DM ruled it will work once, whenever the party chooses to invoke it.

### 048.04 The Ooze Breach at Traders' Grotto

The relative calm shattered when the party emerged into the farming area beyond the tunnels. A panicked gnome farmer shouted, "Oh, you're alive. That's wonderful news." He then told them the oozes were still attacking and that his farmers had been handed weapons. Daz suspected an opportunity for loot, and Gyrgum agreed that's "half of D&D." At the Traders' Grotto, they found a large Gnomish army standing before a breached wall. Beyond it was a writhing, churning "Ooza Palooza" of oozes, jellies, and black puddings, no longer bound by the Pudding King's will and very unhappy about it. Thorin thought it was "maybe too much ooze."

A ghost insisted, in the GM's deliberately anachronistic joke, on reminiscing about how "back in my day at IBM, we solved this problem using buffer caches." Meanwhile, Chief Diggermattock and Chipgrin were already arguing about how to divide the newly opened territory, seemingly unbothered by the fact that said territory was currently full of ooze. Daz pointed out that "the whole like legalese of like who's gonna own ooze town" wasn't in their interest. Thorin then attempted to redirect their attention to the more immediate problem. His Persuasion attempt used his last luck point and a d8 boost and still came out a 6, and the argument rolled on like a gelatinous cube down a corridor.

### 048.05 Jimjar's Bet and the Bone Die

It was Jimjar who cut through the chaos, as he so often did, with a casual wager. He approached Gyrgum and offered a bet of ten platinum pieces, claiming that Glabbagool, the party's intelligent ooze companion, had more sense than the Pudding King ever had. He asked Daz whether he'd brought a sponge; Daz said it was in the bag of holding. Before anyone could fully process what was happening, Jimjar tossed a small bone die onto Glabbagool. A strange blink rippled through the air, and then something extraordinary happened. Glabbagool briefly became a bridge between every ooze in the cavern, a single vast mind connecting jellies, puddings, and cubes alike. He told them, in his gentle and earnest way, that the world was fine, that everyone could get along, and that there was really no need to kill anyone. The oozes, apparently persuaded, went mindless and peaceful, slithering away into cracks and stonework until the threat simply dissolved.

When the party pressed Jimjar on the nature of the die, he explained that he had won it off a blind Duergar in Gracklstugh in a back-alley game of Three-Dragon Ante. The Duergar claimed it had been carved from the knucklebone of a saint who never told a lie, a die with a memory for the truth. Jimjar shrugged and said he had figured that in a place where the blink had turned the world's math into a bad joke, a little uncertainty could go a long way. The die popped out of Glabbagool a moment later, spent and inert; Glabbagool complained it "doesn't seem to be responding to the acid." Jimjar offered it to the party as a keepsake, and Gyrgum tucked it into his pouch.

Gyrgum paid the ten platinum pieces without complaint. Daz insisted it come out of party funds, and the party quietly agreed it had been the best investment they had ever made. Jimjar was frank about his calculus: either he won 10 platinum or they were all dead, and a bigger bet would have made Gyrgum suspicious. When Zalthir asked whether he had any other math-altering tricks, Jimjar said no, that was the one. Glabbagool confirmed he no longer had any link to the oozes: "It's like I had, we were all part of one big mind and then we weren't." Across the grotto, the oozes went back to doing oozy things, and the party judged the area would be clear within days to a week.

### 048.06 The Council of Blingdenstone

With the ooze crisis resolved, the Ember Vanguard was ushered into a council meeting where the various factions of Blingdenstone were already at each other's throats over the future of the city. Miners, wererats, ghosts, and spiritual leaders were all present, and in the gnomish way, the beer was brought to the meeting. Dorbo stood on his chair to warn that "miracles don't pay the guards." He said there were no elementals left to dig, the ghosts were claiming the North Tunnels as a graveyard, and the rats were already marking territory in the pipes. Daz cut through the bickering, and Gyrgum followed with a passionate appeal for unity, urging the assembled leaders to set aside their egos and build an alliance strong enough to outlast the demons.

The party worked the room with careful insight:
- **Wages.** Daz rolled a 19 on Insight and spotted that the Diggermattocks didn't want to pay the working class a hero's wage. The party brokered a wage agreement between the miners guild and the working class, whose details were deferred to be discussed "underground."
- **The upper boroughs.** Insight rolls of 17, 25, 13 and 15 surfaced Diggermattock's hatred of paying for military protection. The party proposed that the Goldwhisker wererats take control of the upper boroughs as a self-funded defense force.
- **The sanctum.** They helped broker a compromise on sustainable mining practices that satisfied both the Miners Guild and the ghosts who considered the sanctum sacred ground.

One by one, the arguments quieted, hands were shaken, and a fragile but genuine peace began to take shape. Dorbo grew nervous that the gnomes might prefer Daz as a leader, so he hastily announced that the party would be sent off to the Overbright the next morning. There they were to tell the world that Blingdenstone is "open for business," and, as Dorbo stressed, not for tourism.

### 048.07 Departure and the True Nature of Jimjar

After a long rest, the city sent them off with ceremony the next morning. An officer of Dasco Pickshine's army led an honor guard to the last set of tunnels. Working-class gnomes lined the tunnels chanting their new rallying cry, "Gnome labor, not summoned labor." The Burrow Warden ghosts dipped their spectral banners in salute as Jadger watched. Chief Chipgrin pressed a braided rat's foot into Daz's hand; he called it the Voucher of the Varmint, a token recognized by wererats and outcasts from Blingdenstone all the way to Menzoberranzan.

As they waited to set out, the party overheard gnomes who had visited Gracklstugh recounting wild tales of the 'Ember Vanguard', an ultra-powerful, evil drow strike force. The gnomes had no idea they were talking about the party, who had never formally introduced themselves. The legends had grown absurd:
- Daz was depicted as a 20th-level magic user casting meteor swarm; a derro in the Whorlstone Tunnels had apparently mistaken his faerie fire for it.
- Gyrgum used Earthquake to shatter mountains.
- Thorin smashed rocks with his biceps.
- Zalthir was portrayed as a Red Dragonborn.
- The Vanguard had slain an ancient red dragon of titanic power rather than facing the fat and tired Themberchaud.

It was the Red Dragonborn detail, along with the claim that the Vanguard left Gracklstugh on the back of the dragon Themberchaud, who deposited them on the shores of the Darklake, that convinced everyone this humble group couldn't possibly be the legendary Ember Vanguard. The party listened to these stories with a mixture of amusement and mild alarm. Daz reasoned that letting the legends spread might make it easier to negotiate their way out of fights later.

It was Jimjar who delivered the final, ominous note before the party departed. He pointed toward a pale white puffball mushroom growing at impossible speed from a crack in the wall, its scent cloyingly sweet, like a feast left to rot in a hothouse. He remarked that Juiblex had been a loud and messy neighbor, but that this sister, Zuggtmoy, preferred to move in while you were sleeping. Jimjar then turned and walked back toward Blingdenstone, and Gyrgum fed the mushroom to Glabbagool ("That was good calories").

Zalthir had long been suspicious of the little gambler. Thorin sized him up with a Battle Master's eye (Know Your Enemy) and discovered a profile that matched no ordinary gnome and bore a striking resemblance to something far more divine:
- immune to poison and psychic damage;
- could not be charmed, frightened, or poisoned;
- shrugged off radiant energy and mundane weapons alike.

The DM reminded the table that since the Sundering, deities are not supposed to walk Faerûn, and that the only thing that happened was the blink bringing beings from the Abyss, so what would a deity that wasn't a demon be doing there? Zalthir noted the profile was "suspiciously similar to the stat block for angels." Jimjar noticed the scrutiny, offered a knowing wink, and vanished entirely from existence, leaving only the faint metallic clink of platinum coins echoing in the tunnel behind him and the distinct sense that this was not the last they would see of him. The DM suggested sages on the surface might shed light on who he is. Next session: the exit from the Underdark, and the sun.
