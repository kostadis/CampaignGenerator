## Events
- The party arrived in Daggerford during their journey south toward Candlekeep, where they found the town unusually quiet — the townsfolk moving carefully without explanation — and stopped for the night at the River Shining Tavern [ch 056 / 056.01].
- In Daggerford's village square, the party examined a bronze cast of a severed forearm and hand on a stone pillar, a monument to Travis Houlin, a paladin of Bahamut who died there three years prior fighting the Cult of the Dragon, which had been actively recruiting in the town [ch 056 / 056.01].
- The monument bore a Dwarven inscription reading "The move is yours" — mirroring the phrase beneath the Stroud statue in Waterdeep's Castle Ward — and a smaller Common inscription noting Milo Goodbarrel, an Order of the Gauntlet member whose work was published by Rishaal, witnessed and recorded it [ch 056 / 056.01].
- A local village priest arrived selling small pewter reproductions of Travis's hand cast to raise donations for the Order of the Gauntlet, declaring himself a "pure Bahamutian" who did not believe in the heresy of the Stroudites; he conceded the Dwarven Stroudite inscription could remain on the monument since the casting had been a gift, funded by the Metalworkers Guild — possibly with Stroud-school sponsorship [ch 056 / 056.01].
- Gyrgum donated a gold piece and received a pewter Hand of Travis medallion [ch 056 / 056.01].
- That evening at the River Shining Tavern, a weaver named Maerith of the Ford approached the party about her seven-year-old daughter Elin, who had been silent for six days, drawing the same shape on every flat surface, and speaking only in her sleep, saying "it is only the shape that holds" [ch 056 / 056.02].
- Gyrgum and Daz examined Elin but rolled poorly (a 10 and a 7) — both failed to reach a definitive diagnosis [ch 056 / 056.02].
- Dawnbringer, Thorin's sentient sun-sword, performed a lesser restoration on Elin with a pompous "Let me bring the light to you" — cast through Thorin as her attuned wielder, not from any of Thorin's own resources [ch 056 / 056.02].
- After the restoration Elin spoke briefly — "The wells are not wells anymore" — before falling silent again; the party concluded this was no ordinary disease but something twisting her mind from within, causing auditory hallucinations, with Gyrgum noting it was interior rather than exterior — "not something that can be slapped, or grappled" [ch 056 / 056.02].
- The party connected Elin's condition to the madness previously seen in the Underdark bleeding into the surface world, and Gyrgum's medicine check (23) confirmed a tenuous connection between Elin's condition and the growing pressure in Daz's own head [ch 056 / 056.02].
- The party traveled south to Beregost, the last meaningful stop before Candlekeep, and took shelter at the Burning Wizard Inn [ch 056 / 056.03].
- The gnome innkeeper Festrum recounted the inn's namesake — a wizard burned alive during a dragon cult uprising decades past — pointing to a permanent soot mark on the south wall and reciting his grandfather's words: "The wall remembers" [ch 056 / 056.03].
- A traveling bard performed, with her closing number a four-verse drinking song about a Kenku thief who learned to steal no more, the whole inn singing the chorus of "Black Hood"; Gyrgum noted the song's reference to stealing "what was needed" mapped well onto Whistler's behavior in taking only what was needed from Tiamat's horde [ch 056 / 056.03].
- After Gyrgum requested a song about Stroud — Thorin pressing a silver piece into the bard's hand on his behalf — she asked whether Gyrgum was Bahamutian or Stroudite, then sang a love song about the famous chess game, which Gyrgum observed wound "the usual claptrap about love and emotions and flowers" into a drinking song [ch 056 / 056.03].
- During the performance Daz noticed a young human scholar reciting a chant whose breathing and cadence were "almost right but wrong by a hair" — the same syncopated wrongness he had observed in Eldred, who went insane earlier in the adventure, and in the Field Ward Preacher; Daz quietly recorded the chant, planning to bring it to the scholars of Candlekeep [ch 056 / 056.03].
- That evening Thorin sat near the sea — his first real exposure to it after months underground and in the mountains — reflecting with Dawnbringer, who lit up everything around them because she does not like the dark; Thorin concluded "Life takes you in weird directions and weird places. You just gotta go with it" [ch 056 / 056.03].
- Zalthir climbed to the inn's roof to meditate and found himself — perhaps not entirely disappointingly — at peace with nothing interrupting him [ch 056 / 056.03].
- The party arrived at Candlekeep the following morning, traveling the Way of the Lion along the cliffs above the Sea of Swords, with the keep visible from miles out [ch 056 / 056.04].
- As they approached Candlekeep, Daz's pressure headache grew sharper, intensifying with each step toward the keep [ch 056 / 056.04].
- The party was received by Bookwyrm, the First Reader, a dragonborn sage entirely unbothered by the jokes about her name; she assigned the party to mentors — Daz to Sister Yvenne, Zalthir to Brother Khell-Vire, Thorin to Master Archivist Philemon, and Gyrgum to Vareth [ch 056 / 056.04].
- The matter of the shrunken House T'sarran spy in the party's bag of holding was addressed — she had been discovered when the party had to open the bag at the gate to show Glabbagool [ch 056 / 056.04].
- The Gatewarden Kalan Strongbranch was summoned and explained Candlekeep's position on prisoners: "The bell tower has cells. Comfortable, localized anti-magic. They are very rarely used. Anyone we find guilty of a major offense is exiled rather than imprisoned. Which is to say, we hold prisoners. We do not keep them. If you give her to us, what you are giving us is a delay. We will not deliver to House T'sarran, and we will not execute her. Eventually, we will release her at our gate with a warning" — leaving the decision entirely to the party [ch 056 / 056.04].
- The miniature drow spy was furious, demanding to be restored to her normal size and formally imprisoned by Candlekeep's guards; when Thorin asked Glabbagool's opinion, Glabbagool explained he had been doing prison-watching for days, was "kind of bored," but that the spy provided entertainment — "She keeps trying to kill me! And then I slap her around, and then she tries to run away, but there's nowhere to run away to, so I just sort of expand, and then she's trapped, and then she starts screaming at me, and then I try to explain to her how the way of the shadow monk is to be quiet, and that she should learn meditation, and then she screams at me about the meditation, and then we talk about things. We've become great friends!" [ch 056 / 056.04].
- Glabbagool asked Zalthir whether watching the prisoner was pre-monk training; Zalthir confirmed it absolutely was, and Glabbagool took the task with utmost seriousness, fixing all six pseudo-eyes on the spy [ch 056 / 056.04].
- The party decided to keep the spy under Glabbagool's watch rather than hand her over to Candlekeep [ch 056 / 056.04].
- Zalthir was taken to the Echoing Hall within the Atheneum, where he met Brother Khell-Vire, a 200-year-old half-elf who had left the Garden of Shadow eighty years ago after refusing an order, wearing a faded shadow monk under-robe beneath the avowed gray of Candlekeep [ch 056 / 056.05].
- On Khell-Vire's desk lay a cryptic note reading "Zalthir, the Underdark, until the Bone King sings," the meaning of which neither of them could explain [ch 056 / 056.05].
- Khell-Vire challenged Zalthir with the cryptic question "What were you sent toward?" and rephrased it when Zalthir's patience showed its limits, but Zalthir found no meaning in it; Khell-Vire sent him to the Trial of the Broken Mirror, stating: "I will not be there. This is your work, not mine" [ch 056 / 056.05].
- In the Sea Warden's Tower sparring rooms, a blind half-elf monk — Master Kenshi — tested Zalthir's shadow stepping, criticizing his form — "You skip the third breath. You don't enter your ki in the hara. You let it bleed into your fingertips like a poison. It is technically wrong in every scroll" — but noting Zalthir arrived "three seconds faster than the grandmaster" [ch 056 / 056.05].
- Zalthir failed every formal demonstration — acrobatics (3), athletics (1), and stealth (11 total) — causing the monk to nearly dismiss him and walk away in disappointment, at which point Zalthir landed a powerful hit on the departing monk from behind (rolling 30 to hit), prompting "Perhaps not all is lost" [ch 056 / 056.05].
- The monk set a final challenge — shadow stepping to intercept and smash a thrown brick mid-air; Zalthir succeeded (rolling 29 to hit), shattering the brick completely, and the monk declared the combination of shadow step and strike "redeems your lack of skills in all other possible areas" [ch 056 / 056.05].
- Zalthir demonstrated his shadow tentacles and eldritch tattoo, which the monk found less impressive, but agreed that combining those elements in an interesting way was the next frontier [ch 056 / 056.05].
- A sage of xenobiology asked whether "a mindless digestive system" could achieve enlightenment; Zalthir defended Glabbagool's intelligence and progress with conviction, noting he was by no means mindless, and the sage provided a vial of Resonant Slime [ch 056 / 056.05].
- Master Kenshi ultimately acknowledged Zalthir's unconventional mastery and taught him one of three specialized shadow techniques; Zalthir chose option C [ch 056 / 056.05].
- Thorin was brought to Master Archivist Philemon, a clinical specialist in sentient artifacts who initially assessed Dawnbringer as "a remarkable specimen — late Netherese-era recovery, sun-worship origins, sustained darkness exposure of approximately 3,000 to 4,000 years" [ch 056 / 056.06].
- Thorin corrected Philemon immediately — "What do you mean, specimen? This is my friend Dawnbringer" — and Philemon adjusted, noting with some surprise that the bond between Thorin and the blade was stronger than he had feared [ch 056 / 056.06].
- When Philemon asked Dawnbringer whether the corpse she was found with was her wielder, the sword faltered and went silent [ch 056 / 056.06].
- Philemon corrected himself mid-sentence — "Your sword does not need polishing. She can't be polished, actually" — and reframed: "Your sword needs to remember what daylight feels like without needing to fight, and then when she's ready and you are willing, she needs to grieve. The first work is light. The second work is mourning. We will start with the first," signing Thorin and Dawnbringer up for a two-phase therapeutic process [ch 056 / 056.06].
- Daz was taken to the Hall of Divination, where spectral mage hands drifted through the air fetching tomes, and met Sister Yvenne, a meticulous and authoritative keeper of the public record [ch 056 / 056.07].
- Yvenne explained that information in Candlekeep was exchanged reciprocally, and Daz told her everything: the amnesia, the jail, the Underdark, the mercenaries trying to protect him and the ones trying to kill him, and the headache growing worse since he arrived — leaving Yvenne visibly surprised by the totality of his disclosure [ch 056 / 056.07].
- Yvenne explained drow noble house politics — matron mothers, the rule of intrigue, that betrayal was upward-rewarded if it strengthened Lolth's web, and the rule that if even one member of a targeted house survived, the attacking house was forfeit and destroyed — illustrated by a story of a house that left "some blithering child idiot alive," after which the drow community tore the attacking house to the ground, leaving a black puddle; Yvenne did not know what happened to the surviving child [ch 056 / 056.07].
- Yvenne read Daz a list of houses extinct within living memory — the second seat, the fourth seat marked "struck," the eleventh seat, and the twenty-third seat; Daz's natural-20 investigation check noticed the word "struck" was strange and out of place, implying political, paid censorship rather than elimination [ch 056 / 056.07].
- Yvenne admitted there was one house she could not speak of in the room: "There is one house I cannot speak of in this room, not because I will not, because the public record is sealed, and I am today the public record. If you want what is behind the seals, you will have to want it through other doors" [ch 056 / 056.07].
- Yvenne noted the fourth seat held ritual archive privileges in Menzoberranzan, making a struck-fourth seat "a very political, expensive erasure" [ch 056 / 056.07].
- In a 200-year-old heraldry book — Geography and Cities, Menzoberranzan, Pillars of Woe — Daz discovered an entire appendix had been mechanically removed with a razor at least fifty years ago; a surviving footnote referenced the name "Daz'issin," which Yvenne explained was "a softening name. Mothers use it for boys they wanted to live," and the houses that had used it "most of them, did not last" [ch 056 / 056.07].
- Yvenne stated "It is possible your name is related, but it is unlikely. The houses that had those names have all gone," and Daz pushed back quietly: "But what if they weren't successfully all gone?" — sensing through Yvenne's careful silences and precise body language that he was close to something she could not say aloud [ch 056 / 056.07].
