## Events
- Brother Vareth led Gyrgum through the West Inner Ward to the Immortal Chambers, Candlekeep's theology library of rectangular buildings with green roofs and a tile floor bearing one deity symbol per tile; Gyrgum followed Vareth's footsteps to avoid stepping on specific deity tiles [ch 057 / 057.01; ch 057 / locations].
- Pizwog, an ancient tortle librarian, shuffled past at approximately one mile per hour and pressed his own tract, *On the Necessary Absence of the Gods Who Are Present*, into Gyrgum's hands; Vareth looked irritated but respectful during the roughly five-minute handoff, and Gyrgum tucked it into his satchel promising to read it [ch 057 / 057.01; ch 057 / items].
- In Vareth's side office, Vareth laid out the three dominant schools of thought on the Question of the Age — the Stroudite school (mortal choice is sovereign), the Bahamutian school (divine providence prevails), and the Draconic school (ancient agency rules) [ch 057 / 057.01].
- Vareth reported the controversy had become serious: libraries, taverns, and chapter houses were arguing, courts of two cities had asked for written opinions, Stroudite pamphlets crossed the Sea of Swords, and three duels had broken out at Candlekeep itself — in a library where nobody had dueled in two hundred years — prompting Sister Yvenne to threaten confiscating everyone's bookplates [ch 057 / 057.01].
- Gyrgum proposed a fourth school of thought — fixed points where choice becomes constrained while remaining free between them — and Vareth, electrified, labeled it the "strong-weak hypothesis," calling Gyrgum a "weak Stroudite and strong Bahamutian around fixed points, and a weak Bahamutian outside of fixed points" [ch 057 / 057.01; ch 057 / moment].
- Vareth pressed a collection of Stroudite notes and papers into Gyrgum's hands and rushed off to begin his own research; Gyrgum promised to return a marked-up copy when done [ch 057 / 057.01; ch 057 / items].
- That evening the party dined at the Refectory, where the eight Great Readers sat at the high table; the party noticed one reader visibly drunk, another pale and unwell (Sylvira Savikas), and two locked in hushed conversation, and saw a hooded member of the Avowed hurry past with something hidden beneath their robes [ch 057 / 057.02; ch 057 / npcs].
- From the high gallery the party heard the recited prophecies of Alaundo — recited continuously, with each removed when fulfilled — and one line stood out: "On the longest night of winter's deepest cold, the keeper falls, the heart is sold" [ch 057 / 057.02; ch 057 / moment].
- Before dawn, Fembris Lancer, the First Reader's adjutant, banged on the party's door at the House of Rest and the Hearth, shouting that the Keeper of Tomes was dead and the First Reader wanted them through the Emerald Door before the Avowed awoke [ch 057 / 057.03].
- Glabbagool tucked himself into the bag of holding with eyeballs peering out as the party set off into snow-blanketed Candlekeep; Fembris led them past the Astronomicon, the Whispering Dome, and the Drakonoikos, the latter built from the scales and bones of the ancient red dragon Fyrentennimar and containing a dragon breath simulation chamber that had claimed one Avowed life and now required the Keeper's permission to enter [ch 057 / 057.04; ch 057 / locations].
- In the Chapter House the party found the Council of Twelve in uproar: A'lai Aivenmore shouted for a vote to restore leadership, a dowdy halfling reader urged determining whether Janussi had named a successor, a slender woman cried to find the murderer, a red-cheeked wizard watched shrewdly, Daral Yashenti demanded to know why Sylvira Savikas was absent, and a bronze dragonborn academic called for the Gatewarden's immediate dismissal [ch 057 / 057.05].
- Gyrgum derailed the crisis by introducing himself as founder of the "Gyrgumite School of thought," sparking academic curiosity among the council, until Bookwyrm, shaken and weary, restored order with "FOCUS, PEOPLE." [ch 057 / 057.05; ch 057 / moment].
- Daral dismissed the party contemptuously as sell-swords unknown from Alaundo; the party defended their credentials by recounting Underdark survival and the discovery of Dawnbringer, who spoke up herself to argue the party were the greatest discoverers for finding her two-thousand-year-imprisoned tomb [ch 057 / 057.05; ch 057 / items].
- Bookwyrm appointed the party as independent investigators citing impartiality given suspicious relationships within the council, and required them to sign confidentiality papers; Thorin's remuneration phrasing drew appreciative laughter, then stunned the council into silence [ch 057 / 057.05].
- After negotiation, Bookwyrm agreed that upon successful resolution each party member would receive a copy of any book in the library, made at the House of the Binder; Thorin's demand for carte blanche to go anywhere and read anything made the council deeply uncomfortable [ch 057 / 057.05; ch 057 / locations].
- Bookwyrm publicly stripped Gatewarden Kalan Strongbranch of the investigation in front of the full council, citing his troubled relationship with the victim; Kalan looked visibly pissed, recovered himself, bowed curtly, and stormed off [ch 057 / 057.05; ch 057 / moment].
- Bookwyrm explained Janussi had asked for Kalan's resignation the morning before the murder, concerned Kalan was losing mental faculties and becoming negligent; she declined to elaborate to avoid prejudicing the investigation [ch 057 / 057.05; ch 057 / 057.06].
- Bookwyrm escorted the party up the spiraling staircase past a shrine of Oghma and a large apartment with an ajar door; a gray cat lying asleep in the middle of the stairs opened her yellow eyes without otherwise moving, and even Bookwyrm stepped carefully around her [ch 057 / 057.06; ch 057 / locations].
- Bookwyrm admitted nobody had conclusively proven whether the cat was some power dwelling in Candlekeep or not, so it was left to sit where it wished; Fembris dismissed the Avowed's reverence as superstition until Zalthir quietly asked "Is it?" [ch 057 / 057.06; ch 057 / moment].
- Outside the chamber, the 23-year-old watcher Tadric provided a timeline: the body was discovered at 7:30 a.m. by A'lai Aivenmore and Miss Hollypocket; the Gatewarden arrived with Tadric around 8 a.m.; Kazryn Nyantani appeared weeping shortly after and viewed the body only from the doorway; Fheminor Scrivenbark arrived at 8:30 wanting to inspect but was turned away; Bookwyrm arrived at 9 from the baths; the Gatewarden returned at 11 for his own inspection, looking more determined on the way out than on the way in [ch 057 / 057.06; ch 057 / npcs].
- Tadric admitted he had not watched closely enough to know whether the Gatewarden touched anything inside, and defended Kalan as a great, honest man, troubled by his removal; when asked if he had done a good job, Tadric reflected: "I don't think I did a good job." [ch 057 / 057.06; ch 057 / npcs].
- Bookwyrm instructed the party to report back when done and remove nothing from the room, then departed; Fembris was officially assigned as adjutant and hastily qualified his offer of help as anything legal upon noticing the party's composition [ch 057 / 057.06].
- The party entered Janussi's chambers — a spacious circular room with a dozen lancet windows, hazy with lingering tobacco smoke; Janussi, a human male of around seventy, lay near the foot of the mahogany desk, white robes bloodied, rib cage caved in, and a gaping hollow where his heart had been [ch 057 / 057.07; ch 057 / npcs].
- The room was in disorder: a toppled chair, a shattered marble statue and pedestal, candles burned entirely to their stubs with a waxy mess below, and a spilled ink pot; near a safe on the far side, blue gemstones and empty pouches lay scattered as though the thief had found them worthless [ch 057 / 057.07; ch 057 / items].
- Fembris took one look at the body, sprinted to the nearest window, flung it open, and vomited outside the tower; a gust of wind flowed in but nothing disturbed the evidence, and he retreated outside [ch 057 / 057.07; ch 057 / moment].
- The party examined the desk: the spellbook The Discoveries lay open at the center with notes in Celestial on papyrus musing on unfamiliar spells; a quill with midnight blue ink lay on the floor, a damp linen ink carrier sat in a wicker basket, and atop the back pile of notes were annotations in the same blue ink on The Golden Ass, beneath which lay older notes in dry purple ink, suggesting Janussi had switched inks within the last day or so [ch 057 / 057.07; ch 057 / items].
- Zalthir's Medicine roll of 17 confirmed the rib cage was smashed by what appeared to be an axe or similar weapon in several precise blows close together, and only the heart was missing — no other organs — with blood surprisingly localized, suggesting Janussi may already have been dead before his chest was opened [ch 057 / 057.07].
- Gyrgum theorized the heart was forcibly removed after death to prevent *Raise Dead*, which requires vital organs; the party considered whether someone had already cast *Speak with Dead*, which would block further attempts for ten days, with the GM answering only "perhaps" [ch 057 / 057.07; ch 057 / spells].
- Thorin asked whether waiting ten days would allow another *Speak with Dead* attempt and whether any ice was available to preserve the body, as the corpse would deteriorate considerably by then; the session ended with the party mid-investigation [ch 057 / 057.07; ch 057 / spells].
