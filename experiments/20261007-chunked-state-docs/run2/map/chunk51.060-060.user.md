### 060.01 The Academic Distraction

The academic community of Candlekeep erupted into a frenzy of intellectual excitement when Brother Vareth unveiled a startling new expansion of the Gyrgumite triangle, transforming it into a quadrangle with a fourth theoretical option. Despite the shadow of a recent murder hanging over the keep, every scholar and Avowed member abandoned their duties and flooded toward the Oval Theatre for an emergency debate. As the GM observed, nothing demonstrated Candlekeep’s academic priorities like blowing off an active murder investigation to focus on new research and publication. The chaos proved to be an unexpected gift for the party: their ever-present minder, Fembris Lancer, sheepishly requested permission to attend the disputation. Bookwyrm, Teles, Fheminor, and half the Avowed were already expected to be there, making attendance a political statement as much as an intellectual exercise. With Fembris’s departure, the keep’s corridors were suddenly, gloriously empty.

Daral, the paranoid and complicated scholar who had gifted the poisoned book to Janussi, clung to Thorin and Daz like a man drowning in a sea of his own guilt and fear. He had concluded that the two adventurers were the only people in Candlekeep who did not actively want him dead, although Thorin’s clinical interest in confirming the poison’s effects briefly made him question even that. Daral had already endured an extraordinary reversal of fortune: he had expected to be fired, learned that the man who intended to fire him was dead, celebrated the safety of his book collection, and then discovered that the poisoned gift pointed directly back at him. He also feared the party might tell Zalthir about disparaging comments he had made about Dragonborn while drunk.

Daral was eager for the party simply to cure him immediately, but they realized that once healed he would probably disappear, taking useful testimony with him. Thorin therefore persuaded him to accompany the investigation and provide a “warm intro” to Leuwin.

Meanwhile, Gyrgum quietly slipped the key once worn around Kalan Strongbranch’s neck into Daz’s keeping, unwilling to retain it while surrounded by a room full of distracted scholars. Daz cast *Identify* as a ritual and confirmed that this particular key was not linked to an instant-summoning spell. The same sapphire had to be used both to establish and trigger the spell, meaning possession of the gem could be nearly as useful as possession of the linked object. The spell could not take an object from a living possessor, but a dead person counted as possessing nothing.

### 060.03 Investigation Strategy and the Timeline of the Book

The party reconstructed the timeline of Daral’s gift. The book came from his private collection, and he believed he had wrapped it himself, although he had been drinking. At approximately 11 a.m. on Deadwinter’s Eve, he placed the clearly labeled gift beneath the evergreen tree in the Southern Dining Hall before rushing to a meeting with the Keeper. The package remained there until after dinner, when Kazryn Nyantani distributed the gifts. Janussi received the book while it was still wrapped. There was no magical seal on the wrapping because, as Daral protested, “It’s a gift. Nobody—this is Candlekeep.” He reluctantly conceded that magically sealing gifts might become a useful institutional best practice if he survived the night.

The party also examined why the killer had used a book when Janussi had eaten and drunk freely at the feast. Poisoning shared dishes would have been less precise and risked killing Sprig Summerfoot, the volunteer poison taster, or other diners. Using Daral’s recognizable gift created both a targeted delivery system and a compelling frame. The poison itself remained inert and visually unremarkable until activated by contact with a living humanoid, at which point its interaction with bodily fluids produced the telltale black discoloration. Any unused residue found elsewhere would therefore not necessarily appear black.

### 060.09 Searching the Readers’ Chambers

Alkrist’s lavish quarters told a darker story. Expensive furniture, fine materials, and fashionable artwork surrounded his spellbook and several tightly sealed glass jars. Each jar contained a dead rat whose skin was blistered and pustulated, suggesting experiments with disease or poison. On his bedside table lay an elegant book bound in black leather with stars and gold filigree: *1001 Tashalian Nights*. Zalthir carefully lifted it and found the bookmark resting on a tale about a military commander poisoned after accepting a gift of surrender—a magic horn whose mouthpiece had been coated with Midnight Tears. ### Oak Tree Apothecary

A wooden treehouse built into the boughs of a huge evergreen oak west of the Great Library, between the Sea Warden’s Tower and the House of Mechanus. Sparsely but tastefully decorated rooms featuring fine glassware, ceramics, a large mirror, and a portrait of A’lai. A cage sized for five messenger doves contained only two birds, with feeding and waste patterns showing that more had recently occupied it.

### Alkrist's Chambers

Lavishly decorated quarters filled with expensive furniture, fine materials, fashionable artwork, and Alkrist’s spellbook. The room contained sealed jars of dead rats with blistered, pustulated skin; a locked safe; and a bookmarked copy of *1001 Tashalian Nights* open to a tale about poisoning a commander with Midnight Tears applied to a gifted horn.

### Bookwyrm’s Chambers

Spacious, comfortable rooms occupying half of the top floor of the Readers’ Tower. They are neatly arranged without being extravagant. Zalthir and Glabbagool searched them but found neither significant evidence nor any detectable trace of poison.

## NPCs

### Daral

A paranoid, dramatic, and complicated scholar who is terrified after realizing that Janussi died from a poisoned book taken from Daral’s private collection. He had expected Janussi to dismiss him, briefly celebrated when the murder saved his position and library, then realized the gift made him the obvious suspect and perhaps the killer’s next target. He clings to Thorin and Daz as the only people he trusts, though their willingness to consider letting the poison progress unsettles him. He directed the party to Leuwin, identified A’lai as the person most likely to frame him, and remembered the anti-divination properties of Milil’s chalice.

### Brother Vareth

An elderly scholar whose proposed fourth answer to the Gyrgumite triangle transformed it into a quadrangle and caused an intellectual sensation throughout Candlekeep. The resulting emergency debate drew most of the Avowed to the Oval Theatre despite the active murder investigation.

### Kalan Strongbranch

The former wearer of the key Gyrgum handed to Daz for safekeeping. His decision to give the key away rather than keep it himself may have reflected fear that someone possessing the stolen sapphire could magically retrieve a linked object once its possessor died.

### Janussi

The murder victim at the center of the investigation. He presided over a tense Deadwinter supper, praised Alkrist’s military-history proposal, urged solidarity among the Great Readers, received Daral’s book from beneath the evergreen tree, and left shortly afterward. His heart was removed from his body and hidden with a blood-stained cleaver inside Milil’s lead-lined chalice.

### Fembris Lancer

An acolyte adjutant serving as the party’s minder. He is sincerely fascinated by Gyrgum and Vareth’s academic dispute and asks permission to attend the Oval Theatre event despite his duty to supervise the party. His departure grants them an invaluable unobserved hour.

### Leuwin

An elderly, absent-minded satyr druid who operates the Oak Tree Apothecary. He spends his days distilling tinctures, crushing seeds, and macerating herbs. He provided Fheminor with horehound and licorice, then accompanied A’lai to the greenhouse for fennel, unknowingly leaving his dangerous-toxins shelf accessible to a Dragonborn intruder.

### Nibbles

Leuwin’s friendly squirrel assistant, capable of fetching tools and witnessing crimes overlooked by humanoids. Nibbles trades information strictly for nuts and identified Fheminor, A’lai, and a Dragonborn visitor. The squirrel saw the Dragonborn make a mess and break something while Leuwin was occupied with A’lai.

### A'lai Aivenmore

A Great Reader and Daral’s political rival. Daral believes A’lai wants to become Keeper of Tomes and regards Daral’s advocacy of poetry and fiction as an obstacle. On the day of the murder, A’lai visited the apothecary for fennel and drew Leuwin into the greenhouse while a Dragonborn entered the pharmacy. His dove cage, sized for five messenger doves, held only two, suggesting that three had recently been sent with messages.

### Fheminor Scrivenbark

A Great Reader who visited Leuwin around 2:30 p.m. for cold medicine and received tincture of horehound and licorice. Nibbles described her as the “unfriendly girl gnome.” She had already departed before A’lai and the Dragonborn’s suspicious activity.

### Alkrist

A Dragonborn Great Reader who was conspicuously cheerful during the otherwise tense feast. He proposed *Tales of War by the Generals That Waged Them*, a compilation of firsthand military strategy, and received Janussi’s approval despite Teles’s mockery. Orrin saw him burn a scrap of paper when junior Avowed approached. His chambers contain diseased or poisoned rats, a locked safe, and a book bookmarked to a story closely matching Janussi’s murder.

### Sylvira Savikas

A tiefling Great Reader who stormed out of the Deadwinter supper after Daral’s muttered remark. A figure bearing her appearance was later seen in the kitchens at 1 a.m., searching by lantern light and refusing to speak. Because a tiefling does not need a lantern in darkness and the figure avoided using Sylvira’s voice, the party suspects magical impersonation.

### Kazryn Nyantani

The chief cleric who suggested distributing the Deadwinter gifts and personally took each package from beneath the evergreen tree to its named recipient. She therefore handled Janussi’s gift after its string had been cut and retied.

### Irony

A junior Avowed member and waitress with a compulsive need to tell the truth. She described the feast, its dishes, the guests’ moods, the gift exchange, and the suspicious late-night figure resembling Sylvira. She dislikes predictable jokes about her name and has been resisting unwanted advances from Teles Ahvoste.

### Sprig Summerfoot

A junior Avowed who enthusiastically volunteered to taste every dish served at the Deadwinter supper, despite the chefs insisting it was unnecessary. She revealed Teles’s unwanted pursuit of Irony and recommended Orrin as a witness who notices more than most people realize.

### Orrin Glass

A junior Avowed waiter who is completely deaf in one ear but exceptionally observant. He saw Alkrist burn a small scrap of paper when junior staff approached and noticed that the string around Janussi’s wrapped gift had been cut and retied.

### Teles Ahvoste

A Great Reader who mocked Alkrist’s military-history proposal by comparing it to Fheminor Scrivenbark’s unlikely program. He has also been making unwanted romantic advances toward Irony.

### Glabbagool

An intelligent gelatinous ooze accompanying the party. Glabbagool assisted Zalthir in searching the Readers’ chambers, attempted to manifest a nose to detect poison, and proudly announced that he had learned silence. He could not enter Alkrist’s safe because it lacked a sufficiently large opening.

## Items

### Kalan Strongbranch’s Key

A key previously worn around Kalan Strongbranch’s neck and handed to Daz by Gyrgum for safekeeping. Daz cast *Identify* on it and confirmed that it was not linked to an instant-summoning spell.

### Poisoned Book

A volume from Daral’s private collection, wrapped and placed beneath the evergreen tree at approximately 11 a.m. on Deadwinter’s Eve. Janussi received it during the post-dinner gift exchange. Orrin observed that the string had been cut and retied, establishing that someone tampered with the package before Janussi opened it. The book is believed to have delivered Midnight Tears while simultaneously framing Daral.

### Stolen Sapphire

A gemstone missing from a safe and theorized to be the material focus for an instant-summoning spell. The same sapphire must be used to establish and trigger the link. Crushing or otherwise activating it could summon the associated item—possibly Janussi’s missing key—provided that item was not being possessed by a living creature.

### Lead-lined Chalice of Milil

A brass chalice held by Milil’s statue in the Southern Dining Hall. It had previously been removed and used for midsummer wine. Its lead lining blocks divination such as *Locate Object*, making it an ideal hiding place for Janussi’s heart and the bloody cleaver.

### Severed Heart

A gruesome discovery hidden inside Milil’s chalice, presumed to belong to Janussi. Its removal appears intended to impede resurrection or magical questioning. Recovering it may allow the party to reunite the remains and attempt further magic.

### Blood-stained Meat Cleaver

A bloodied weapon found beside the severed heart inside the lead-lined chalice. It was likely used to remove the heart and then hidden where divination could not locate it.

### Broken Toxin Vial

A shattered container found below the dangerous-toxins shelf in the Oak Tree Apothecary. The missing substance was a component capable of becoming extremely dangerous when mixed with other materials. Nibbles witnessed a Dragonborn break something and disturb the room while A’lai occupied Leuwin elsewhere.

### Peppermint Covered Rag

A peppermint-scented cloth placed over the broken glass and spill at the apothecary in a hurried, unsuccessful attempt to conceal the theft or mask its odor.

### Tincture of horehound

A medicinal tincture provided by Leuwin to Fheminor Scrivenbark at approximately 2:30 p.m. on the day of the murder. She was instructed to put it in her tea and was also given licorice to chew.

### Messenger Dove Cage

A cage in A’lai’s chambers sized for five white messenger doves. Only two birds remained, while other physical signs showed that the cage had recently housed more. The three missing doves may have carried secret communications.

### 1001 Tashalian Nights

An attractive book bound in black leather and decorated with stars and gold filigree, found on Alkrist’s bedside table. Its bookmark marked the story of a military commander poisoned after accepting a gift of surrender: a magic horn whose mouthpiece had been coated with Midnight Tears.

## Spells

### Identify

Cast by Daz as a ritual while touching Kalan Strongbranch’s key. The spell can reveal an object’s magical properties, required attunement, charges, ongoing magical effects, and the name of any spell that created it. It confirmed that the key was not linked to the suspected instant-summoning magic.

### Speak with Animals

Used by Daz to communicate with Nibbles at the Oak Tree Apothecary. Combined with a generous supply of nuts, it produced eyewitness testimony implicating A’lai and an unidentified Dragonborn in the theft of a dangerous toxin or precursor.

### Lesser Restoration

Discussed as the easiest available countermeasure to Midnight Tears for someone with access to a cleric. Daral repeatedly hints that he would appreciate being cured immediately, while the party delays in order to retain his cooperation and investigate the poison.

### Plane Shift

Discussed as a way to evade Midnight Tears by leaving the plane before the poison’s local-midnight trigger. It is particularly relevant to creatures such as mind flayers or githyanki with innate planar travel, though much less practical than *Lesser Restoration* for most victims.

### Locate Object

Discussed both in relation to the missing key and the lead-lined chalice. Lead blocks the spell, explaining why Milil’s chalice could conceal Janussi’s heart and the cleaver from magical detection.

### Speak with Dead

Proposed as a future method of questioning Janussi after recovering his severed heart. The party intends to reunite the heart with the body and determine whether the remains can provide testimony about the killer.

### Polymorph

Suspected as one possible means by which an intruder impersonated Sylvira Savikas. The figure’s refusal to speak suggests that the disguise reproduced Sylvira’s appearance but not her voice, while the unnecessary lantern betrayed someone without tiefling darkvision.
