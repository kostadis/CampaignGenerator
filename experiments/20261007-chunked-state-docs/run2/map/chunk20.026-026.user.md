### 026.01 Arrival at the West Cleft Gate

Before play, the party finished leveling to 5th. Gyrgum gained access to third-level spells. The DM noted that "second-level spells suck, and third-level spells are awesome." Daz now has Fireball. Thorin gained a second attack and Tactical Shift, which lets him move without provoking opportunity attacks. The DM also shared an in-world wanted poster for the Ember Vanguard: "Wanted dead, as long as they can be raised from the dead or alive." The Ember Vanguard is the name the party had given itself, and the city's rumors had run with it. In those rumors Daz had become an old, wizened drow, and the brass dragonborn Zalthir had become a *red* dragonborn. Among the derro, the DM explained, "red dragon equals evil dragon."

The party arrived at the West Cleft Exit, a suffocating corridor of rough-hewn stone carved from the very bedrock of Gracklstugh. Flickering torchlight cast long, jittering shadows across walls slick with damp and mineral dust. The low clink of armor and the heavy tread of Duergar patrols filled the air with a suppressive weight of suspicion. In the DM's words, "This is no mere checkpoint. It is a gauntlet where power, politics, and peril converge."

The checkpoint had been locked down. A first guard identified each party member's species one by one and concluded that they "must not be the Ember Vanguard." He then passed them to a scarred Duergar captain named Gorglak. The captain wore a heavy iron chain with a crude clenched-fist pendant, which Zalthir's history reading identified as a mark of loyalty to the Deepking. Gorglak compared the group against the wildly exaggerated rumors of the Ember Vanguard, a fearsome band of shadowy assassins that the city's gossip had inflated far beyond the party's actual appearance. Daz, ever perceptive, quickly saw through the captain's posturing and recognized the familiar look of a corrupt official angling for a bribe.

The party played their hand carefully. They feigned ignorance of the Ember Vanguard and presented their Stone Guard passes, choosing them over their Keeper of the Flame passes. Their Stone Guard task had been to find proof that the derro were engaged in demonic possession.

Gorglak was unmoved. He declared that "passes mean little here" and demanded they prove they were not the Ember Vanguard or "confess and surrender." He also ordered them to lay down weapons and magic items for inspection and to speak the Stone Guard's secret salute. Gyrgum's years studying the rituals of Bahamut helped him recall the details, which gave the party advantage. He then performed the faction's secret salute with enough conviction to give Gorglak pause.

Zalthir, scanning the dim corridor with sharp eyes, spotted five invisible Duergar soldiers positioned throughout the hallway, ready to strike if negotiations collapsed. Daz prepared a contingency plan in case the bluff failed. He would carry the egg and other incriminating items, cast Misty Step past the guards, and claim divine favor, with Gyrgum offering to add "a magical booming voice or tremors in the ground."

### 026.06 Business with the Keepers of the Flame

When they knocked on Gartokkar's door, the man answered with barely concealed surprise. He had clearly not expected them to survive after several days' absence. He had heard the rumors of powerful wizards and shadowy assassins prowling the city and had quietly assumed the party had been caught in the crossfire. He remembered Daz only as a third-level drow wizard, so the rumored wizards clearly weren't them.

Daz, recalling the non-disclosure terms, played coy about which egg Gartokkar meant. Gartokkar called him a fool and demanded someone "say something coherent." Gyrgum answered that the egg had been obtained "and it is glorious."

The party handed over the corrupted red dragon egg, noting plainly that it had been recovered from a dark ritual and bore strange markings, with no promises made about its condition. Gartokkar accepted it without ceremony. He explained that the egg's purpose was to provide a means of replacing the dragon Themberchaud before the beast grew wise to the fact that he was not truly in control of Gracklstugh. In a red dragon, that realization would end in catastrophic violence. The DM described Themberchaud as "a fat, blimped-out, overly pedantic red dragon." The DM also reminded the party that they had separately agreed to act as Themberchaud's agents. That was safe, he said, as long as the dragon doesn't notice them.

Gartokkar paid the party their remaining fee, including the previously negotiated confidentiality sum. The original deal was 2,000 gp with 600 gp paid up front, plus a 1,000 gp confidentiality rider, for a total of 3,000 gp. This payment was 2,400 gp. He reminded them in no uncertain terms that their arrangement was to remain between them alone. After the split, the party's spreadsheet showed 1,042 gp plus one electrum each.

As he paid them, Gartokkar repeated the rumor that the Deepking's agents were everywhere. They were a mercenary band of four so fierce that even the Stone Guard feared to face them, shadowy assassins with magic and blades. He asked how the party had survived, and Thorin replied that they simply appeared "small and insignificant." Gartokkar warned that the derro were on edge after the assassination of their leaders. The Stone Guard were doubling their patrols, fearing something was about to erupt.

The DM then laid out what the party had pieced together about the derro. Mind flayer experiments had left them unstable, and the Blink pushed them into outright madness. Their High Council had long been co-opted leaders through whom the Deepking kept the derro in check. After the Blink, the council resolved to rise against the king and turned to rituals of Demogorgon. Such demonic bargains grant power at the cost of all restraint.

### 026.07 Ambush in the Shadows

The party attempted to slip away toward the Darklake District, moving quietly through the narrow passages of the city. They moved under the darkness, which gave them advantage on stealth. Their concealment, however, was not enough: Zalthir rolled a 24, but the group as a whole missed the DC 14 check.

A Derro assassin stepped from the shadows and confronted them directly. He declared that the Derro Council had sent its Gray Ghosts to exact revenge for the deaths of their High Council members. He added that the derro knew exactly who the party was, even if the rest of the city did not. Before the words had fully left his mouth, invisible figures closed in from all sides.

Zalthir moved with lethal speed, cutting down the visible leader and a second hidden attacker before they could react. Gyrgum called upon the power of his faith, casting the newly learned Spirit Guardians. A swarm of spectral, angelic guardians erupted around him, tearing through two more assassins who strayed too close, for 17 and 15 radiant damage. Thorin finished the last of them with a decisive two-attack sequence, and silence returned to the corridor. All five Derro lay dead.

The party searched the fallen Derro. Daz whistled while they searched. They found a handful of coins and a fuzzy lucky foot of indeterminate origin, which Gyrgum claimed. Most significantly, they found a letter bearing the seal of the Council of Savants. The letter ordered the Gray Ghosts to strike down the Duergar who had brought the Ember Vanguard into the city, laying bare the web of political conspiracy festering beneath Gracklstugh's streets. As the DM put it, "Your announcement of who you are had consequences."

The party stood amid the aftermath, the weight of the letter in their hands and the knowledge that every faction in the city was now moving against shadows, some of which were their own. With enemies multiplying and the city teetering on the edge of open conflict, the only sensible course was to disappear into the dark before anyone else came looking. Whether to leave the bodies in the street, hide them, or stage them was left open for next session.
