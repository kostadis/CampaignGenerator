### 064.01 The Silence of Candlekeep

The party paused beside the newly healed Tadric to take a short rest before pursuing A'lai Aivenmore. When Tadric surrendered the genuine High Tower key, he admitted, “I held it all night and I didn't know what it was. Gyrgum relented, noting that he now possessed resurrection magic if the worst happened.

Then something else changed: the endless chant of fulfilled and unfulfilled prophecies that had filled Candlekeep without interruption fell suddenly and completely silent. Gyrgum joked that he had long since stopped consciously hearing it—“Once it stops, I notice that it stopped”—but Tadric froze, stunned by something that simply never happened. Tadric turned to the party and urgently reminded them, “Sylvira, someone needs to tell Sylvira that her son is dead.” An awkward silence followed. Gyrgum suggested that the messenger should be someone who knew Sylvira, and Daz argued that such a person would have “the best understanding of how to be empathetic to her.” The party ultimately classified it as a later problem for the Ember Grapple and pressed toward the High Tower with Tadric accompanying them.

### 064.02 The Path to the High Tower

Gyrgum cast a protective blessing over his companions as they moved. Along the way they discovered a dead Watcher on an indoor walkway, killed by a crossbow bolt. Daz's exceptionally sharp investigative eye immediately recognized that the bolt's fletching was foreign to the area and inconsistent with the ammunition carried by Candlekeep's Watchers—proof that outside attackers had already passed along the same route. Examining the body, Gyrgum estimated that the Watcher had been killed within the last half hour. The direction of the shot showed that the killers had climbed the stairs behind the party's present position, murdered the guard, and continued toward the tower.

### 064.03 Infiltration of the High Tower Lobby

They arrived at the High Tower lobby to find two Zhentarim raiders looting books from shelves according to a written manifest. Their swords were bloodied, and one carried the bow that had killed the Watcher. From the entrance, the party could not see the entire room. On a landing roughly fifteen feet above them, near the sealed inner door that required both High Tower keys, stood A'lai Aivenmore himself. He was waiting for Moziqodo to arrive and did not yet realize that the demonspawn was dead or that the party possessed the other key.

The party first planned to take out the raiders and then concentrate on the powerful wizard, but the thugs were not lined up for a single spell from the doorway, so Gyrgum switched targets and launched a guiding bolt in an attempt to catch A'lai Aivenmore by surprise, but the shot failed to penetrate his magical defenses. Zalthir and Glabbagool surged into the room and delivered punishing blows to the wizard. Daz attempted to neutralize A'lai with Maximilian's earthen grasp, conjuring a hand of compacted soil. A'lai countered the first casting, but Daz immediately tried the same spell again, declaring, “I'm persistent.” This time A'lai chose to risk the saving throw rather than expend another counterspell, failed spectacularly, took fifteen points of bludgeoning damage, and was restrained. He escaped by teleporting across the chamber with Misty Step, but doing so consumed more of his magic and prevented him from unleashing one of his more devastating spells that turn.

## Locations

### Candlekeep

The great library fortress where the party is investigating a conspiracy involving stolen keys, murdered scholars, and ancient protective wards. Its prophecies are read aloud in a continuous chant, with each fulfilled prophecy removed while the remaining words continue without interruption. The chant falls completely silent during this session; Tadric is stunned, because this never happens.

### Walkway to the High Tower

An indoor passage reached by stairs and leading toward the High Tower. The party discovered a recently slain Watcher here, killed by a crossbow bolt with foreign fletching. The bolt's direction showed that the attackers had climbed the stairs, murdered the guard, and continued along the party's route. Gyrgum estimated that the killing occurred no more than half an hour earlier.

### High Tower Lobby

The entrance to the High Tower of Candlekeep, containing bookshelves, stairs, and a landing approximately fifteen feet above the main floor. The entrance provides only a partial view of the room. It served as the site of the session's climactic confrontation, with Zhentarim raiders looting selected books from a written manifest while A'lai Aivenmore waited above near a sealed inner door requiring both High Tower keys. The battle ended with one raider dead, another covered in acid, Zalthir unconscious, and A'lai still active.

## NPCs

### Tadric

Kalan's lieutenant, freshly healed as the session opens. He had carried the genuine High Tower key all night without knowing what it was. Once he learns its importance, he refuses simply to surrender it and retire: “Now that I know what it is, I'm not giving it to you and going to bed.”

Shaken but emboldened by surviving Moziqodo's attack, Tadric insists on accompanying the party and protests, “I faced Moziqodo.” He appears seized by a desperate desire to be heroic. The sudden silence of Candlekeep's prophetic chant leaves him stunned, and it is Tadric who reminds the party, “Sylvira, someone needs to tell Sylvira that her son is dead.” He also guides the group toward the High Tower.

### Moziqodo

Sylvira's demonspawn son, killed before this session opens. His corpse remains with the party and Tadric at the beginning of the session. His death creates an uncomfortable obligation to inform Sylvira, which the party postpones. A'lai Aivenmore remains unaware of Moziqodo's death and is waiting for him to arrive at the High Tower with the second key.

### A'lai Aivenmore

A powerful drow wizard and long-time scholar of Candlekeep who orchestrated the immediate conspiracy to protect his academic position. Janussi had given him ten days before taking away his chair, the only home or institutional identity A'lai believed he possessed after thirty-one years at Candlekeep.

A'lai admits that he did not initially intend to become either a murderer or an exile, but discovered he would do “a great deal” to retain his chair. His crimes reach much further back than the current crisis: for eleven years he has removed books selected by lists delivered on Manshoon's behalf.

He is self-preserving, desperate, and tactically formidable. He uses Counterspell, Misty Step, Mage Armor, and repeated Arcane Bursts, but consistently prioritizes escape and survival over fighting to the death. During the confrontation, Daz repeatedly forces him to spend magic defensively and ultimately steals the sapphire from his hand.

A'lai possesses a strange mixture of vanity and fatalistic candor. He pauses during combat to compliment the party's thoroughness and seems pleased when Gyrgum recognizes the academic grievance beneath the bloodshed. His attempt to justify Manshoon's theft as the version where nobody needed to die collapses under the weight of the bodies around him, and he revises it to the version where “fewer people had to die.”

### Manshoon

A legendary and feared wizard associated with the Zhentarim, the Moonsea, and Zhentil Keep. A'lai identifies him as the true authority behind eleven years of directed book thefts from Candlekeep. Manshoon sends lists naming the required volumes, and a courier with a metal right hand concealed beneath a glove delivers them.

A'lai warns that ignorance of Manshoon is deliberate and professionally useful: “You have not heard of it. That is not an accident. It is a career.” The party's historical knowledge establishes that Manshoon has died more than once and nevertheless returned. His present objective is a single book out of Candlekeep.

### Glabbagool

An intelligent ooze and loyal companion to the party. Glabbagool accompanies Zalthir into the High Tower battle and asks whether the elevated landing makes this the right occasion to use “the flying spell.” After A'lai knocks Zalthir unconscious, Glabbagool remains on top of the fallen monk, protecting him and positioned to stabilize him if necessary.

### Zhentarim Raiders

Mercenaries working for the Zhentarim, found looting the High Tower lobby and bagging specific books from a written manifest. Their swords are bloodied, and one carries the bow used to kill the Watcher on the walkway. They are hardened enough that one resists Thorin's Menacing Attack. Both fail to evade Gyrgum's caustic brew; one dies when the acid first burns him, while the other remains standing and coated in the corrosive substance when the session ends.

## Spells

### Bless

Cast by Gyrgum on “the three of us who are here” as they approach the High Tower, granting each target an additional 1d4 on attack rolls and saving throws. The group briefly forgets to apply it to Gyrgum's opening Guiding Bolt, but the added die is insufficient to turn the miss into a hit. Bless remains active through most of the confrontation but ends when Gyrgum begins concentrating on Tasha's Caustic Brew.

### Guiding Bolt

Launched by Gyrgum at A'lai Aivenmore from the High Tower entrance in an attempt to surprise the wizard. A'lai does not initially notice the attack, but Gyrgum's attack roll is poor even after accounting for Bless, and the radiant bolt fails to penetrate the wizard's Mage Armor.

### Mage Armor

A protective magical force surrounding A'lai Aivenmore. It raises his defenses enough to turn Gyrgum's attempted surprise Guiding Bolt aside without effect.

### Maximilian's Earthen Grasp

Cast twice by Daz during the confrontation. The spell causes a medium-sized hand of compacted soil to rise from a five-foot section of ground, seize a nearby target, inflict 2d6 bludgeoning damage, and restrain it.

A'lai negates the first casting with Counterspell. Daz immediately casts it again, forcing A'lai to choose between spending another spell slot or risking a strength save. A'lai chooses the save, fails spectacularly, takes fifteen points of damage, and is restrained. He escapes by using Misty Step rather than physically breaking free.

### Counterspell

Used by A'lai Aivenmore as a reaction to negate Daz's first casting of Maximilian's Earthen Grasp. It consumes one of A'lai's spell slots and temporarily limits his other magical options. Daz deliberately repeats the spell afterward, forcing A'lai to decide whether to expend still more magic.

### Misty Step

Used by A'lai Aivenmore to teleport across the room and escape the restraining earthen hand after failing his save against Daz's second Maximilian's Earthen Grasp. Because teleportation does not use ordinary movement, the restrained condition does not prevent it. The escape costs A'lai another spell and keeps him from responding with a more devastating leveled spell during the same turn.

### Arcane Burst

A magical ranged attack used repeatedly by A'lai Aivenmore to strike multiple party members with raw arcane energy. One burst misses Daz despite his low armor class, but another deals twenty-six points of damage. Zalthir initially takes twenty-nine points without falling. Later, after Daz steals the sapphire, A'lai unleashes four more attacks against Zalthir; enough connect to knock the monk unconscious.

### Telekinesis

Cast by Daz when A'lai Aivenmore tries to smash the sapphire. Because A'lai is carrying the gem, he must succeed on a strength saving throw to retain it. He fails against Daz's spell save DC of seventeen, allowing Daz to wrench the artifact from his hand and pull it into his own possession.

Daz considers using the spell on A'lai himself but applies it only to the object, so the wizard is not restrained. The successful theft prevents A'lai from triggering the sapphire and enables Daz's concealed transfer of it to Gyrgum.

### Tasha's Caustic Brew

Unleashed by Gyrgum in a line that catches both Zhentarim thugs. Each must make a DC 17 Dexterity save, and both fail, becoming coated in acid. The spell inflicts 2d4 acid damage at the start of each affected creature's turn until that creature uses an action to scrape or wash it away.

One thug takes eight points and dies immediately. The other takes only two and ends the session still standing, covered in acid and due to suffer another 2d4 at the start of each turn unless he removes it. Concentrating on Caustic Brew causes Gyrgum to end Bless.
