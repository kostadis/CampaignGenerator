## Events
- The drow overseers of Velkynvelve divided the prisoners into labor groups before the day began, dragging them from the slave pen and past three sneering drow at the guard post. [ch 002 / 002.01]
- Zalthir was assigned to clean and operate the outpost's lift, a creaking zurkhwood and spider-silk contraption suspended high above the cavern floor; two quaggoth guards amused themselves by dropping the basket suddenly and yanking it back up. [ch 002 / 002.01]
- Thorin, Ront, and Prince Derendil were marched to the waterfall to haul small barrels of water; Thorin assured the others, “Any feats of strength you need, I'm your guy.” [ch 002 / 002.01]
- Gyrgum was put to work in the drow dining hall preparing meals for sneering warriors lounging at circular zurkhwood tables; he joked, “I'll make sure to plate it nicely.” [ch 002 / 002.01]
- Daz, along with Stool and one other drow prisoner, was assigned to clean chamber pots, which gave him an opportunity to observe the lower levels of the outpost and the underground river flowing from the pool below. [ch 002 / 002.01]
- While being marched through the outpost, the prisoners took in the confusing vertical layout of Velkynvelve—hanging chambers, webbing, rope bridges, the lower pool, the river tunnel, and ominous creatures below—and checked their temptation to attack the guards as a total-party-kill. [ch 002 / 002.01]
- Zalthir saw the lift consisted of a swinging arm, a hand-cranked horizontal spool, several pulleys, and a heavy woven basket supported by spider-silk cord, appearing to be the only visible way to descend safely from the barracks level. [ch 002 / 002.02]
- Zalthir discovered a mysterious list of people belonging to a nearby settlement among the rubbish removed from the lift basket. [ch 002 / 002.02]
- Jimjar enlivened the lift work by proposing wagers on passengers, stops, and who would emerge first, extending credit since the prisoners had no money: “As long as you're willing to trust me as the bookkeeper, I'm willing to go on credit.” [ch 002 / 002.02]
- Eldeth occasionally accepted Jimjar's bets, and he displayed an almost freakish ability to remember exactly how much had been wagered, won, and lost. [ch 002 / 002.02]
- Zalthir studied the lift as a target for sabotage: the spider-silk cord was extremely hard to cut, the hand-cranked zurkhwood winch could not be broken without tools, though its operation could be impeded or slowed; Daz suggested freezing part of the mechanism with Shape Water. [ch 002 / 002.02]
- Zalthir realized his fire breath changed the picture: the webbing burned, and the rope would give way to flame much more readily than to a blade; the same fire could potentially damage the wooden mechanism and the extensive webs surrounding the outpost. [ch 002 / 002.02]
- Eldeth found a comb, potentially valuable in the filthy conditions of the slave pen. [ch 002 / 002.02]
- Gyrgum attempted to talk to the drow but could not understand their Undercommon; Shuushar announced he would omit the “colorful adjectives” for the sake of his spiritual and mental balance, then enumerated the insults anyway. [ch 002 / 002.03]
- Gyrgum used performance to manipulate, combine, and dramatically sizzle the ingredients; the meal's presentation and preparation impressed the drow, who offered rare praise. [ch 002 / 002.02]
- Topsy advised that stealing should be done “carefully, quietly, and not make a big production of it,” pointedly looking at Gyrgum's massive body. [ch 002 / 002.02]
- Gyrgum's first attempt to steal a knife went badly: a drow caught him holding the knife up, but Gyrgum claimed, “I'm going to sharpen this knife,” and his performance and the goodwill from his meal persuaded the drow not to punish him. [ch 002 / 002.04]
- The drow warned through Shuushar, “don't do that. Or next time, you may find yourself missing various body parts.” [ch 002 / 002.04]
- On the following day, with six or seven drow paying close attention to Gyrgum's celebrated cooking, he asked Topsy and Turvy to steal the knives; the twins successfully brought two knives back to the prisoners, which Gyrgum sharpened. [ch 002 / 002.04]
- Thorin's water-hauling group was sent to Mistress Ilvara, the cruel high priestess who commanded Velkynvelve; they crossed a steep rope bridge into the upper level of the largest hanging tower, passing through a shrine to Lolth with a lifelike ten-foot-tall sculpted spider with eight polished-jet eyes. [ch 002 / 002.03]
- A rope ladder led down from the shrine into Ilvara's private quarters, where she sat in smug comfort alongside her favored lieutenant, Shoor Vandree, and ordered the prisoners to bring the water to her bathtub. [ch 002 / 002.03]
- When Prince Derendil declared that he should be treated with dignity, Ilvara drew a knife and made it clear that “if he wants to be dealt with, he can be dealt with,” prompting him to back down. [ch 002 / 002.03]
- Thorin noticed a treasure chest in Ilvara's chamber and another descending passage that led toward Shoor's Quarters, though the work detail was not permitted to explore it; he also sensed that something about the drow at Velkynvelve seemed “crazily off.” [ch 002 / 002.03]
- Thorin realized the waterfall would allow someone to slide safely into the pool below without suffering damage; the pool was twenty feet deep and drained into an underground river that travels for miles before spilling into unknown reaches of the Underdark. [ch 002 / 002.03]
- Back in the slave pen after the day's labor, the prisoners pooled their observations; Sarith rambled through insane tangents, Stool was delighted by everyone's cleaning activities, and Jimjar continued his obsessive betting. [ch 002 / 002.04]
- Buppido repeatedly invoked “the divine plan” without identifying the divinity behind it; Thorin and Gyrgum gradually concluded Buppido might believe he was a god. [ch 002 / 002.04]
- Buppido announced his first plan, “It's quite simple. All of you will distract the enemy while I escape through the waterfall”; challenged, he admitted he needed to consider the divine plan more carefully. [ch 002 / 002.04]
- Thorin flattered Buppido with the idea that the divine plan must have arranged for everyone to escape so they could protect him; Buppido eagerly accepted and declared, “since you have pledged your swords … your spells … your lives, at least, to protecting the divine plan, I will grant you insight into its mysteries later this evening.” [ch 002 / 002.04]
- On the second day of labor, Jorlan Duskryn, an unexpected visitor during the guard shift, quietly asked the nearest prisoner, “if I gave you a means to escape, would you take it?” [ch 002 / 002.05]
- The prisoners suspected a trap and asked why a drow would help them; Jorlan answered, “hate Ilvara, and I hate Shoor. And your escape, even your attempted escape, would ruin them.” [ch 002 / 002.05]
- Jorlan proposed leaving the slave-pen gate unlocked and creating a distraction during the changing of the guard; he revealed the armory was in the chamber above the guard post, inside the hanging guard tower directly in front of the slave pen, and suggested reaching the webs below and jumping into the pool. [ch 002 / 002.05]
- When asked what they should do after reaching the water, Jorlan replied, “that is not my problem.” [ch 002 / 002.05]
- The group debated destinations: Shuushar knew how to travel toward Darklake to the west, Menzoberranzan lay to the north and was emphatically ruled out, and Gracklstugh, a duergar city where Buppido's derro kin also live, offered a possible route to the south. [ch 002 / 002.05]
- The party debated whether to flee immediately or risk retrieving weapons first; Gyrgum and Daz reasoned that the highest-ranking drow would traditionally claim captured treasure, and since none of the guards appeared to be carrying the prisoners' belongings, they concluded their gear was probably in the chest Thorin had seen in Ilvara's private chambers. [ch 002 / 002.05]
- Zalthir raised the threat of the drow's large spools of spider silk that allowed rapid descent; the group planned to scout the spools and exploit Zalthir's fire breath to destroy the drow's mobility assets. [ch 002 / 002.05]
- Gyrgum summarized the sabotage idea as “light them on fire, and fling them,” and the GM summed up the goal: “Disable. Deny the enemy the weapon.” [ch 002 / 002.06]
- Several major choices remained unresolved as they prepared for the desperate flight ahead: whether to raid the armory, whether to risk entering Ilvara's quarters for their own gear, which route to take after reaching the pool, and how much of the outpost to burn during the breakout. [ch 002 / 002.06]
