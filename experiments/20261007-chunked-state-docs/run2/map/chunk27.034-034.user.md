### 034.03 Meeting Glabbagool, the Sentient Gelatinous Cube

Farther along, the party discovered the remains of a drow, a dark metal mace, and scattered coins apparently hovering above the floor. The objects were suspended inside a gelatinous cube with two visible eyes. Rather than attacking, the cube greeted them: “Well, hello, hello, hello.” He introduced himself as Glabbagool and explained that until recently he had been “an average garden variety gelatinous cube,” moving around, cleaning floors, and decomposing whatever adventurers left behind. Now, inexplicably, he could think and see.

Glabbagool was fascinated by the implications of sentience. He did not understand whether everyone experienced awareness in the same way and readily admitted that he knew little about the world. He also had no idea which route led upward, having spent much of his brief sentient life standing in place and deciding where to go. Thorin invited him to join the expedition, promising companionship, adventure, and a reliable supply of food. Glabbagool accepted, though he politely asked, “You won’t be upset if you all die and I eat you, right?” The party agreed that once they were dead they would probably be past caring, provided Glabbagool allowed natural causes to do the killing.

As a welcoming gift, Gyrgum tossed Glabbagool a lucky foot that he had been carrying in his backpack. The cube absorbed it and declared it tasty. Glabbagool also offered a standing service to the party: “If you ever decide you want to get rid of something, just drop it on the floor and I’ll be happy to eat it.”

### 034.08 Escape from the Flooding Complex

With no obvious mundane exit remaining, the group debated potions, spells, ice barriers, and air bubbles. Thorin mentioned his potion of spider climb, but the chamber’s ten-foot ceiling offered little advantage. Daz proposed a more promising use of Shape Water: redirect the water inside five-foot cubes to create pockets containing no water. By maintaining two effects at once, Daz could create a moving, ten-foot-long refuge. The party would crowd into one pocket, create another ahead of it, and advance through the flooding passage in stages.

The magical bubbles would give the party time to survive the initial flood while Thorin prepared to use his considerable strength to break through the stone. Glabbagool could not fit inside the cramped air pockets, but this did not concern him. “Oh, that’s totally fine. Glabbagool caused more of the ceiling to collapse, doubling the volume of water entering the complex. The breach nevertheless revealed the diverted underground river that was feeding the flood. Protected by Daz’s progression of air pockets, the party endured the rising water and then swam upward for approximately thirty feet. They broke the surface in a large cavern and resumed their journey.

Their successful escape also brought them to level 6.

## NPCs

### Jimjar

A deep gnome guide who recently made a navigational error, which he frames first as an unavoidable consequence of shifting Underdark passages and later as an unexpected shortcut to Blingdenstone. He is confident in his abilities and almost compulsively prone to making bets, particularly with Thorin.

Jimjar treats even imminent drowning as an opportunity for wordplay, describing the rising flood as an especially appropriate fulfillment of the wager over “tears.” Despite his bravado, the appearance of the first gray ooze makes him admit, “I really wished I had lost this bet, Thorin.” After meeting Glabbagool, he asks whether the party can declare a mulligan because the detour produced such a charming friend.

Once the party escapes, Jimjar insists, “See, it did not end in tears,” and recasts the entire disaster as proof of his skill: “I did not get us lost. That’s the important thing is that I found us a shortcut, right?” He accepts the promise of ten slightly slimy silver pieces as settlement of the wager.

### Glabbagool

A Gelatinous Cube that unexpectedly gained sentience, sight, and the ability to speak. Before the transformation, he was “an average garden variety gelatinous cube,” mindlessly cleaning floors and decomposing debris. He is curious about the world and confused by concepts that other sentient beings take for granted, including differences in intelligence and awareness.

Glabbagool measures 10 feet by 10 feet by 10 feet. His size lets him completely seal a narrow corridor against hostile oozes, but it also prevents his companions from passing him. After accidentally blocking the party during a fight, he sensibly concludes, “Perhaps I should be in the back.”

He is unfailingly polite about his appetite. Before joining, he asks whether the party will mind if he eats them after they die and later agrees to delay digestion long enough for resurrection attempts. He also offers to consume anything the party drops on the floor. Gyrgum gives him a lucky foot, which he absorbs and declares tasty.

Glabbagool proves both brave and useful. He scouts the fountain chamber, activates the hidden gray oozes, cleans up their remains, and discovers treasure in the fountain. Unfortunately, he absorbs that treasure, temporarily becoming “a cube of holding.” He does not breathe and therefore faces no danger from the flood. His cheerful literalism and eagerness for adventure make him so charming that even Jimjar considers abandoning a wager in his honor.

### Eldeth

A dwarf who provides crucial insight into the geological phenomena causing the flooding. After Thorin’s initial examination identifies the cracks as significant, Eldeth recognizes the signs of an underground river breaking through the stone.

She has heard stories of trapped people breaking open similar formations and finding passages large enough to swim through to safety. However, she soberly acknowledges the survivor bias behind those tales: “I didn’t hear about all the people who did that and drowned anymore.” Her assessment helps the party understand both the potential escape route and the mortal risk of widening the breach.

## Locations

The party’s intended destination, a deep gnome city within the Underdark. A 10-foot-high passage with walls that glisten with dark, dripping water. Its pools rise at an alarming rate after a tremor cracks an underground reservoir or river into the complex.
