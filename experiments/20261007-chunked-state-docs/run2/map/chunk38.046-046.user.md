# Chapter 46 Zalthir's Daring Grapple

Date: 2026-02-02

## Scenes
### 046.01 Chief Dorbo's Battle Plan

Chief Dorbo Diggermattock stood before the assembled forces and gave an impassioned speech: "Today is the day. Today we will triumph. Today is victory." He pointed to Daz as the one who would lead them to victory. Then he pulled out a map of the whole area. He had apparently owned it for a long time and never shown it to the party.

The chief laid out a battle plan. The Galeb Duhr's area had been cleared, so the party could break through the wall there and enter the Pudding King's court from the south. Meanwhile, Dorbo's forces and the clans would attack from another direction to draw off the pudding court. He offered three groups of allies:
- an elemental imbued with Entémoch's power
- wererats led by Chipgrin
- the Pickshine Miners under their enthusiastic overseer, Dasco Pickshine

The GM described Dasco as the mines' "VP of sales," who had decided to be heroic because he saw a chance to "increase gross margins." The GM also explained the mechanics out of character. The party could fight, weasel, or talk their way through each encounter between the hall and the court, or spend an ally to get past it. They would reach the final battle either with allies and weakened, without allies and fresh, or somewhere in between.

The party questioned whether troops really needed to go into the ooze-infested purple area (room 28). Zalthir pointed out that the old plan was to snatch the Pudding King. Once he was gone, the slimes should revert to ordinary wandering oozes. Senni Diggermattock raised two concerns:
- If the oozes weren't distracted, they could pour south and swamp the party with "an almost infinite supply."
- If the party failed, the town might only find out when a mass of oozes burst through.

Dorbo was profit-motivated and cared about the mines outside town. Senni cared about the people and the community they had built. She also worried that her reforms would collapse if the party died. Reactions to the idea of a big battle were mixed:
- Some higher-ranking officers were disappointed.
- The ghosts had hoped for a big historic battle.
- The more gung-ho militia were bummed.
- Many others were quietly relieved.

When Zalthir joked about sending the ghosts because "they're not going to die," a ghost replied: "There will be no picking on the unalived." The party settled on a minimal distraction, a probing feint, instead of a full assault. Daz told the chief: "we have a plan that requires us to be discreet and careful."

### 046.02 Dasco Pickshine's Branding Exercise

Dasco Pickshine took every chance to promote his brand. He promised "branded tea." It turned out he doesn't mine tea; he buys it and packages it, because "we believe in making sure that we create a holistic brand around our mining operation." Gyrgum summed it up: "It's a branding exercise. It's not a digging exercise." Zalthir asked for stickers. Dasco didn't know what a sticker was, but he produced a patch. He took out needle and thread, and Zalthir helped him sew it on while bending down to the three-foot-tall gnome. Everything Dasco wore carried the Pickshine logo: Pickshine pants, Pickshine shoes, nothing but swag.

### 046.04 The Black Pudding Pit

A wererat scout slipped through the rubble and returned with bad news. There was enough space to get through, which meant the oozes could come through the other way too. Ahead, a 20-foot stretch of passage had been hollowed into a pit that looked like still dark water but rippled. It was "a massive, semi-sentient basin of black pudding waiting like a stomach for anything to fall in," under a slick ceiling dripping acid. Daz noted it was like Glabbagool's evil cousin. The pit ran wall to wall, so there was no way around.

Dasco warned that it was "a dissolved trap if I ever saw one." He then offered to bridge it with mithral braces and salt neutralizers. The catch was that his miners would have to stay behind to mine the anchors and keep the acid off the supports. Daz accepted. When Dasco asked to call him "general," Daz answered, "You can call me Daz." Dasco cried, "boys, let's show them what the Pickshine miners can do." The gnomes built the span in about twenty minutes, singing hi-ho songs under their anti-acid canopies.

### 046.06 The House of Horrors

Once the bridge was done, Gyrgum told the wererat scout to keep scouting, then caught himself: Daz was meant to give the orders. Chipgrin insisted on a clear chain of command, and Daz named Gyrgum and Zalthir (and Thorin) as lieutenants who could also give orders.

The wererat scout went further and didn't come back. After about ten minutes, while Daz was sending his bat ahead, Chipgrin went after his own people without waiting for permission. The scout was frozen in terror. The tunnel's polished, translucent salt walls had softened into sheets of gelatinous slime that worked like dark, weeping mirrors, and wet, rhythmic clicking echoed from the ceiling. Chipgrin reported: "There's some big brain slime up there singing a song that turns a man's mind into puddle."

He offered a trade. His pack would flood the rafters and howl at a frequency to drown out the "psychic filth." That would draw every ooze in the vents onto the wererats and leave the party with "no eyes in the dark and no one to watch your backs." The party took the offer. Chipgrin then bet ten gold that Daz's reflection was better looking than the real thing, daring him to look at his psychic reflection. Daz refused: "I'm not playing parlor games." Chipgrin replied that this "explains why this man should be the general."

Daz replied, "I like just being Daz," and Glabbagool asked, "What's a general?"

### 046.09 The Throne Room Assault

The Pudding King, standing and furious, ignored the party. He monologued to his pets, Princess Ebonmire (the black pudding) and Prince Livid (the gray one), and promised that Blingdenstone would become "Blingdenpuddle." Gyrgum's answer: "Do not gloat. We still plot." The signal went out and the distraction force attacked. The King cheered as he described members of the Blingdenstone army being consumed by oozes, and coached his troops: "Ooze them slowly."

The GM checked the rules for the throw. The Earth Elemental could throw Zalthir up to 30 feet; landing called for a DC 15 Dexterity save or 1d6 bludgeoning per 10 feet. Thorin declared there would be no dwarf tossing. Zalthir activated his tentacles, and the Earth Elemental broke through and hurled him into the throne room. Zalthir's first attack roll was very bad. His next attack hit, and he grappled the King with his tentacles from reach.

The Pudding King turned into ooze form. Zalthir found online that oozes can be grappled rules-as-written, and the GM agreed. The GM ruled that the cost was the ooze automatically hitting its grappler. Zalthir dragged the King 15 feet south, away from his allies, and the GM awarded him inspiration for the tactic. The session ended there. Gyrgum had rolled a 21 for initiative but had a connection problem and never got into the order; he will act before the Pudding King next time.
