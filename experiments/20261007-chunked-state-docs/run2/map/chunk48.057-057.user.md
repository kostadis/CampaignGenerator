### 057.01 The Immortal Chambers and the Question of the Age

Brother Vareth — whose narrower specialty in the puzzle of why a half-orc would convert to Bahamut had drawn him to Gyrgum in the first place — led Gyrgum through the West Inner Ward of Candlekeep to the Immortal Chambers, Candlekeep's theology and religion library: a series of rectangular buildings with green roofs and a striking tiled floor where each tile bore the symbol of a different deity. Along the way, Pizwog — an ancient tortle librarian — shuffled past at approximately one mile per hour and pressed into Gyrgum's hands his own tract, *On the Necessary Absence of the Gods Who Are Present*, a dense syncretic analysis of religions across the Sword Coast. Gyrgum tucked it into his satchel, promising to read it in due time. Once settled in Vareth's side office, the old scholar laid out the three great schools of thought consuming the intellectual world: the Stroudite school, which held that mortal choice was sovereign above all; the Bahamutian school, which argued that divine providence arranged all things; and the Draconic school, which claimed that ancient agency set the frame within which mortals merely operated.

Vareth made plain how serious these arguments had become. Libraries were arguing. Taverns were arguing. Chapter houses were arguing. The courts of two cities had asked for written opinions. The Stroudite pamphlets had crossed the Sea of Swords. Three duels had broken out at Candlekeep itself over which polemic shelf received the larger window — three, in a library where nobody had dueled in two hundred years. Sister Yvenne had threatened to confiscate everyone's bookplates. What was being argued beneath the chess games and the partisan colors was, Vareth insisted, the question of what kind of universe mortals actually lived in. At its center was Stroud, who knew no chess yet found a move in the four-hundred-year-old game that Ortho had abandoned mid-move; Protanther's acceptance of that impossible outcome made the controversy matter.

Gyrgum listened carefully and then proposed something none of the existing schools had quite assembled — a theory of fixed points: moments in time where choice became constrained by the shape of the universe itself, while the vast space between those points remained wide open and free. The effect on Vareth was disproportionate. He erupted: "A fourth school of thought?" He spoke of philosophical quadrangles, of circles whose three points described a centre that nobody had yet named. He speculated that a god wishing to control the outcome of mortal lives would not need to compel a single hand — she would only need to shape the rules of the evidence, the form of polemic, the architecture of a chapter house, the quiet. And Stroud, on the other hand, said: the universe can be silent and indifferent, or it can be silent and conspiratorial. Gyrgum agreed: indifferent outside the fixed framework, and conspiratorial as one approached it. Building on what Vareth promptly labeled the "strong-weak hypothesis" — Gyrgum a weak Stroudite and strong Bahamutian around fixed points, a weak Bahamutian outside them — the old scholar could barely contain himself as he pressed a collection of Stroudite notes and papers into Gyrgum's hands and rushed off to begin his own research. Gyrgum promised to return a marked-up copy of the materials when he was done.

### 057.07 Investigating the Keeper's Chambers

The party entered the Keeper's chambers to find a spacious circular room hazy with the lingering smell of tobacco smoke, its dozen lancet windows filling the space with pale morning light. Janussi — a human male of around seventy years — lay near the foot of an elegant mahogany desk, his white robes soaked in blood, his rib cage caved in, and a gaping hollow where his heart had been. The room was in disorder, suggesting possible violence: a chair was toppled near the desk, a marble statue and its pedestal lay shattered on the floor, and the candles of the candelabra had burned entirely to their stubs and left a waxy mess below — something Tadric confirmed Miss Hollypocket would never have permitted, suggesting the murder had occurred many hours before the body was found. Near a safe on the far side of the room, blue gemstones and empty pouches were scattered across the stone floor, as though the thief had found them worthless compared to whatever they had actually come for. Fembris took one look at the body, sprinted to the nearest window, flung it open, and vomited outside the tower; fortunately, nothing disturbed the evidence.

The party examined the desk carefully, finding a spellbook called The Discoveries lying open at its center, with notes written in Celestial on a sheaf of papyrus beside it — musings on unfamiliar spells within the book and how they might be cast. A quill lay on the floor nearby, its nib wet with midnight blue ink, and a damp linen ink carrier sat in a wicker basket close by — the vessel for the spilled pot. Atop a large pile of notes at the back of the desk, more annotations in the same midnight blue ink had been made on a book called The Golden Ass, while beneath those lay older notes on The Discoveries written in a now-dry purple ink — suggesting Janussi had switched inks recently, perhaps within the last day or so. The tobacco smoke still lingered despite the candles having been long burned out, which the party flagged as a detail worth investigating. The blood around the wound was surprisingly localized — far less than one would expect from such a violent injury, raising the grim possibility that Janussi had already been dead before his chest was opened. Zalthir's examination confirmed that the rib cage had been smashed by what appeared to be an axe or similar weapon, using several precise blows all very close to one another, and that only the heart was missing — no other organs had been taken. Gyrgum immediately theorized that the heart had been forcibly removed after death to prevent *Raise Dead*, which requires vital organs to be present. The party began to wonder whether someone had already attempted *Speak with Dead* before their arrival, potentially blocking further attempts for ten days — and whether the Gatewarden's determined expression as he left the scene meant he had found something, or done something, that the party had yet to uncover.

## NPCs
### Brother Vareth
An elderly scholar who has kept his corner of the Theology Library at Candlekeep for forty-one years. His narrow specialty is the puzzle of why a half-orc would convert to Bahamut, given that orcs and dragons are traditional enemies who occupy similar ecological niches and that a sufficient mass of orcs can mathematically bring a dragon down. Stroud's impossible chess move three years ago — despite knowing no chess — dragged this niche question into the centre of the Question of the Age after Protanther accepted the outcome. Vareth laid out all three existing schools of thought in a prepared speech to Gyrgum, but was electrified by Gyrgum's proposal of a fourth: fixed points around which choices become constrained. He immediately labeled Gyrgum a "weak Stroudite and strong Bahamutian around fixed points," declared the concept a philosophical quadrangle and the "strong-weak hypothesis," and rushed off to begin his own research — barely pausing to hand Gyrgum the Stroudite materials and hear Gyrgum's promise to return them marked up.

### Pizwog
An ancient tortle librarian at Candlekeep — the resident librarian of the Immortal Chambers — extraordinarily slow-moving, who shuffles through the theology library at approximately one mile per hour and distributes what appear to be religious tracts but turn out to be his own academic papers. He handed Gyrgum one of his own papers — a syncretic analysis of different religions across the Sword Coast — during the approximately five-minute encounter. Vareth looked irritated by the interruption but maintained respectful patience.

### Sister Yvenne
A member of the library staff at Candlekeep, mentioned by Vareth as having threatened to confiscate everyone's bookplates due to the heated duels breaking out over library shelf space in the wake of the Question of the Age controversy. She has not yet acted on the threat, but Vareth expects it.

### Fembris Lancer
An enthusiastic, boyish, somewhat out-of-shape young half-elf acolyte, adjutant to the First Reader, who brings the party the news of the Keeper's death and serves as their guide and adjutant throughout the investigation. He led the morning tour of the Candlekeep grounds with great energy, pointing out the Astronomicon, the Whispering Dome, and the Drakonoikos. He arrived at the Chapter House with aching legs. When assigned as the party's adjutant and offering to help with "anything," he hastily qualified his offer upon noticing the composition of the party.

### Glabbagool
A sentient gray ooze, formerly a gelatinous cube, who accompanies the party. He hitched a ride in the bag of holding with eyeballs peering out as the party rushed toward the council before dawn, apparently excited to be on the cusp of something new.

### Bookwyrm
A green dragonborn and the First Reader of Candlekeep. She proposed delegating the investigation to the party to ensure impartiality, agreed to the copy-of-a-book reward after negotiation, publicly stripped Kalan Strongbranch of the investigation, personally escorted the party to the crime scene, and stepped carefully around the mysterious gray cat on the stairs. A drow Great Reader who appeared to the party as a tall, handsome human man with smooth dark skin and a braided beard. A sour, thin-lipped human sage Great Reader with greasy, slick-backed gray hair. The Great Reader who curates the Infernal Fortress (Candlekeep's library of the Abyss and the Nine Hells), absent from the council meeting due to a lingering illness. Janussi's ancient gnome housekeeper, one of the first two people to discover the murder alongside A'lai Aivenmore, at around 7:30 a.m. Tadric confirmed that she would never have permitted the Keeper to let his candles burn down to nothing — which the party used to deduce that the murder had taken place many hours before the body was found.

### Kazryn Nyantani
A Great Reader who arrived at the crime scene distraught and weeping shortly after 8 a.m., wanting to see the body of Janussi. She was permitted to view it only from the doorway. Her strong emotional reaction to the death and her early arrival make her a person of interest.

### Fheminor Scrivenbark
A scholar who arrived at the murder chamber at 8:30 a.m. appearing shocked, wanting to inspect the chamber, but was turned away as the order to admit no one had already been set.
