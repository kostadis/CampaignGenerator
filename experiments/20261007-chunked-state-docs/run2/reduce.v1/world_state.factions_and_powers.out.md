## Factions and Powers

### The Drow and Related Houses
- **Ilvara Mizzrym** — cruel commander of Velkynvelve and priestess of Lolth, presiding from quarters beneath a lavish spider shrine; Thorin recognizes the ten-foot-tall spider idol as a symbol of Lolth [ch 002 / npcs; ch 002 / 002.03].
- **Drow of Velkynvelve** — the drow use nonlethal force to subdue prisoners because they remain valuable assets; the guards in the nearby barracks are bored conscripts accustomed to routine, and the exterior-post guards watch outward rather than toward the prison [ch 003 / 003.01; ch 003 / 003.02]. The drow and their quaggoth servants hold Velkynvelve and pursue escaped prisoners [ch 004 / 004.01; ch 004 / locations]. Drow pursuers were closing in on the party but Eldeth's track-masking (and the smashed-crystal disguise) slowed them to pursuit level one [ch 028 / 028.02; ch 028 / 028.03].
- **House T'sarran** — a drow house whose warriors and mage ambushed the party at the Fungal Altar under orders from their matron mother; the mage and three guards were killed by Daz's fireball and Zalthir's punch, while the elite warrior retreated with a parting threat, implying future revenge [ch 051 / 051.04; ch 051 / 051.06]. The house dispatched a force, paid to abduct Daz and bring him out alive, whose mage bore the house signet ring [ch 053 / 053.04]. Its Matron Mother has ruled for 200 years; she sent a couple of agents up to the Overbright to track the party and identify the unknown person who paid to have Daz protected, believing that benefactor would reveal themselves once Daz surfaced [ch 054 / 054.04; ch 054 / npcs]. Candlekeep will not deliver the shrunken drow spy the party holds to them [ch 056 / 056.04].
- **House Mizzrym** — the house continued sending sealed orders to Ilvara around the time of the party's escape, then grew frantic when she stopped responding [ch 053 / 053.03].
- **House Baenre** — A'lai deduced that only House Baenre in Menzoberranzan could afford to wait eleven years for nothing; "It must be Baenre. No one else can afford to wait that long for nothing," though he could not trace the payments, only the patience [ch 067 / 067.02; ch 067 / moments].
- **Drow noble houses** — Ruled by matron mothers; governed by the rule of intrigue (betrayal is upward-rewarded if it strengthens Lolth's web); a house that attacks a rival and leaves any member alive is itself forfeit and destroyed [ch 056 / 056.07].
- **Lolth** — the drow goddess whose worship Ilvara has corrupted; Asha Vandree remains loyal to her, and Daz worried that with gods slipping loose, Lolth herself might be stirring once more in Menzoberranzan [ch 049 / 049.01; ch 049 / 049.05]. Lolth's priesthood: drow male magic users have a love-hate relationship with Lolth, and her clerics distrust mages because mages can talk to gods without going through the priesthood [ch 050 / 050.01].
- **Ember Vanguard reputation** — The name the party had given itself became the city's rumor; a city-wide wanted poster read "Wanted dead, as long as they can be raised from the dead or alive," and rumors turned Daz into an old, wizened drow and Zalthir into a *red* dragonborn, which among the derro meant "red dragon equals evil dragon" [ch 026 / 026.01]. The legendary, feared agents of the Drow Empire; the citizens of Gracklstugh mistook the party riding Themberchaud for them, and in rumor Daz is an 18th-level mage able to cast Meteor Swarm [ch 027 / 027.03]. Blamed by Gracklstugh authorities for much of the recent disruption; a wanted poster reading "wanted dead or alive" (with "alive" misspelled) circulates depicting the members, though the depictions differ from the party in color, age, and ferocity [ch 040 / 040.02; ch 040 / npcs]. To avoid the embarrassment of a band of 5th-level adventurers defeating them, the Derro High Council recast the party as a powerful drow strike force called the Ember Vanguard, led by a 19th-level wizard who "casts wishes as if they were going out of style" [ch 049 / 049.03].

### Zuggtmoy, Lolth Syncretism, and the "Bride" Heresy
- **Zuggtmoy, the Demon Queen of Fungi** — confirmed to be actively corrupting and manipulating Ilvara's religious devotion as "the bride," who demands the party be hunted down and killed [ch 049 / 049.03; ch 049 / moment]. Zuggtmoy addressed the Pudding King as "my little king," calling the ooze "crude," and said she would allow his Lord to soften the shell; a psychic conference call the party was "just got placed on" [ch 044 / 044.03].
- **The "bride" heresy of Zuggtmoy** — Ilvara was seduced by this heretical cult after visiting the Neverlight Grove; it turned her followers into mindless fungal servants [ch 050 / 050.02; ch 050 / npcs]. Evidence shows the Demon Queen of Fungi's cult syncretized with Lolth worship, driving Asha and Ilvara mad and spreading through the Underdark [ch 053 / 053.03; ch 053 / 053.04].
- **Myconids of Neverlight Grove** — myconids have no gender, yet Phylo refers to the "Great Seeder" with gendered pronouns; the grove's seven circles are hunters, explorers, sowers, builders, growers, plus the new inner circle and Circle of Masters [ch 029 / 029.05; ch 029 / 029.06]. Myconid hunters do not hunt live prey but track creatures that have already died near the grove and bring the remains back for reanimation [ch 029 / 029.07]. The Myconids reanimate Grick Alphas, but Loobamub insisted "We don't reanimate shambling mounds" because they are awkward [ch 030 / 030.01; ch 030 / npcs]. The myconid colony's society is being reorganized under Sovereign Phylo, who promises a "better day" while steering it toward Zuggtmoy's plans [ch 031 / locations].
- **Infected myconids** — Several myconids from the Neverlight Grove danced frantically under Zuggtmoy's influence; Voosbur released the spores that teleported and infected them, and they departed through a spore network [ch 025 / 025.03; ch 025 / 025.08].

### Gracklstugh Factions
- **Duergar slavers** — Gray dwarf raiders who kidnap travelers and sell them as slaves in the city of Gracklstugh, capable of turning invisible and enlarging their physical size; one was captured alive for interrogation [ch 010 / npcs; ch 010 / 010.03]. Duergar followers described by Eldeth as followers of an evil god; their draconian laws and dislike of Drow make Gracklstugh a safe haven from Drow pursuit; they trade actively with subterranean and surface races [ch 011 / 011.01; ch 011 / 011.07].
- **Gracklstugh clans and factions** — The city's factions are "at each other's throats," the clan militias are straining under the extra work, and the only two factions the party can currently attract are the Stone Guard and the Keepers of the Flame; the Gray Ghosts are known only to exist [ch 013 / 013.03; ch 013 / 013.05; ch 013 / 013.06].
- **Deepking and Clan Steelshadow** — The Deepking granted all weapons contracts exclusively to his own clan, breeding deep resentment across the city; people who compete with his trade have been disappearing [ch 018 / 018.02; ch 018 / 018.04]. Proof was gathered that the Deepking is allied with assassins "trying to wipe out the opposition" [ch 025 / 025.01]. The derro High Council had long been co-opted leaders through whom the Deepking kept them in check [ch 026 / 026.06].
- **Stone Guard** — The city guard of Gracklstugh; Captain Errde Blackskull leads forces investigating mysterious deaths and believes the Empty-Scabbard myth covers government corruption [ch 014 / 014.02; ch 014 / npcs]. Captain Errde Blackskull wants proof of the Gray Ghosts' and Council of Savants' conspiracies and secretly wants evidence of the Assassin's Guild targeting her clan, which she won't openly admit exists; her deeper aim is evidence for a violent pacification mission against the Derro [ch 018 / 018.01; ch 018 / npcs]. Errde, their head, wants proof that the derro are behind the chaos in the city and to know who the assassins are; her reward is support, the return of the party's weapons, and "a pat on the back" [ch 019 / 019.01]. Leader Errde Blackskull wants proof the Council of Savants is behind the city's corruption; she would march on the king if told of the Assassin's Guild, or try to kill all the derro if told of their demonic dealings, and would likely set the drow on the party if they left without her knowledge [ch 021 / 021.02; ch 021 / npcs]. Gracklstugh's King's police force; the party holds a Stone Guard pass from their task of finding proof of derro demonic possession, and Gyrgum performed the faction's secret salute to pass the gate [ch 026 / 026.01; ch 026 / items]. Stone Guard corruption: members have recently begun taking bribes, unusual for their order, which "were always noted for their absolute integrity… They were very boring that way" [ch 018 / 018.04].
- **Keepers of the Flame** — Their power "derive in equal measure from our psionic abilities, the leadership of the Deepking, and our influence over Themberchaud"; they secretly control the dragon and are grooming a replacement from a stolen red dragon egg [ch 014 / 014.07]. Gartokkar Xundorn leads the order that tends Themberchaud; they have contracted the party to recover the egg, a Demogorgon statuette, and paperwork as evidence against the Gray Ghosts [ch 017 / 017.05]. They maintain power by managing Themberchaud, who has "started to grow up and noticed that maybe things aren't as they seem," which is why Gartokkar is frantic about the egg; without Themberchaud, the Deepking might decide the current Keepers are expendable — "A layoff" [ch 018 / 018.02]. They want only the egg and want no one, including Themberchaud, to learn it was ever lost; if the party fled, the Keepers had promised revenge [ch 019 / 019.01]. Led by Gartokkar and close to the king, they want their egg returned, want to hatch it as a weapon against the other dragon, are looking for an excuse to go genocidal on the derro, and would pursue the party "to the end of time" if betrayed [ch 021 / 021.02; ch 021 / npcs]. A rival faction to the Stone Guard, also loyal to the King, holding a pass the party chose not to use; Gartokkar is a high-ranking member [ch 026 / 026.01; ch 026 / items].
- **Themberchaud** — The red dragon declared the party his personal agents, giving each a golden badge and ordering them to report back to him first; he is unaware the Keepers plan to replace him, so "Themberchaud thinks that we work for him. What he doesn't realize is he works for the duergar." [ch 014 / 014.06; ch 014 / moment]. The red dragon hungers for information and would likely burn the city to cinders if he learned of the egg; he is "too fat and lazy" to realise the party isn't coming back for years [ch 021 / 021.02; ch 021 / npcs]. Proof was gathered that the city's red dragon is "Themberchaud the 28th" [ch 025 / 025.01].
- **Gray Ghosts** — A thieving organization that stole the red dragon egg intended to hatch Themberchaud's replacement; the Keepers have been waging a quiet war against them, and they employ the derro messenger Droki [ch 014 / 014.07; ch 014 / npcs]. Vengeful Derro assassins acting under the orders of the Council of Savants to eliminate the party; five agents (four invisible) were sent out "to try to cause chaos," and the party infers they were not supposed to be noticed [ch 026 / 026.07; ch 026 / npcs].
- **Clan Ironhead** — Barred from taking weapon orders directly, only surplus work from Clan Steelshadow; openly opposed to the Deepking; would become the premier clan if he were unseated [ch 018 / 018.03; ch 018 / 018.07]. A weaponsmithing clan that would happily deal with the gnome kingdoms if the Deepking were removed; Zalthir hoped to leave them in a stronger position [ch 021 / 021.02]. The home district of Laduguer's Furrow, which had offered support in exchange for a dead Deepking [ch 026 / 026.03].
- **Clan Xardelvar** — Fears the Deepking will take over their work as too similar to weaponsmithing; chafing at new rules and regulations [ch 018 / 018.03].
- **Clan Thrazgad** — Another clan of armorsmiths who complain their stores have been raided recently [ch 018 / 018.05]. A Duergar steelsmithing clan that "builds stuff" in the city; its name is on the dragon baby rattle [ch 021 / 021.03; ch 021 / npcs]. A duergar armorsmith clan whose belching forges sit in Gracklstugh's industrial district [ch 026 / 026.03; ch 026 / locations].
- **Clan Xundom** — Will back the Deepking if there's any contest for power [ch 018 / 018.05].
- **Assassin's Guild / Empty-Scabbard Killers** — An assassin's guild with Werz on their list; Werz claimed he thought it was "a children's story until, well, they tried to kill me," but Daz read he knows too much about working with assassins [ch 018 / 018.04]. A group of duergar assassins wielding psionic weapons; they share a common enemy with the party in the derro who have allied with Demogorgon [ch 019 / 019.01; ch 019 / 019.02; ch 019 / npcs]. Rumored to have returned to Gracklstugh; the merchant noted the guild at least maintained the ethic of not assassinating anyone unless someone paid for it [ch 040 / 040.02; ch 040 / npcs].

### Derro and Demogorgon Cult
- **Council of Savants** — The derro governing body in Gracklstugh made up of more powerful derro with special abilities; according to Buppido, it has always promised to lead the derro to freedom and always failed [ch 015 / npcs; ch 015 / 015.04]. A psionic group the flumph has been "feeding off of quite energetically," led by Zubriska, meeting near the secret passage toward the obelisk [ch 017 / 017.04]. The chamber held notes "teeming with evidence of corruption and foul deeds connected to the Council of Savants," linking them to nefarious activities and demonic entanglements [ch 021 / 021.01]. The ruling council of the derro, implicated in a demonic conspiracy that Errde Blackskull of the Stone Guard wants to expose with physical evidence [ch 022 / npcs; ch 022 / 022.06]. The party killed two of its members and holds heretical texts damning enough to "bring down the entire Council for all time" [ch 023 / 023.01; ch 024 / 024.04]. The derro council whose letter ordered the Gray Ghosts to strike down the Duergar who brought the Ember Vanguard into the city [ch 026 / 026.07; ch 026 / items].
- **Derro High Council** — The derro the party killed in the caverns were members of the derro High Council; the city blamed the Ember Vanguard for their assassination, and the derro were on edge [ch 026 / 026.03; ch 026 / 026.06]. Derro history: mind flayer experiments left the derro unstable, the Blink pushed them into madness, their High Council had long been co-opted leaders through whom the Deepking kept them in check, and after the Blink the council resolved to rise against the king through Demogorgon rituals; demonic bargains "grant power at the cost of all restraint" [ch 026 / 026.06].
- **Cult of Demogorgon (derro)** — Derro who have allied with Demogorgon, plaguing the caverns; the party agreed to eliminate them as part of their deal with Eldgrim [ch 019 / 019.01; ch 019 / 019.02]. Mad, fanatical Underdark dwellers obsessed with the egg, believing whoever possesses it holds absolute authority; the GM read lore that derro were once a slave race to the Mind Flayers, are predisposed to madness, and cooperate only "out of necessity and when riled up by a charismatic leader" [ch 020 / npcs; ch 020 / 020.01]. The cult has a zombie pit tended by low-ranking cultists, a savant named Ulnara running macabre experiments on the undead, a cult leader Narrak running experiments with trillimac cap books in the brimstone cavern, and a higher authority figure named Plinki who Skiit said promised him a better job and preached that followers of Demogorgon would make the derro "great again" [ch 022 / 022.02; ch 022 / 022.06; ch 022 / npcs]. A group of Derro cultists led by Narrak, a member of the Council of Savants, performed rituals to graft second heads onto stone giant statues in the name of Demogorgon, aiming to destroy Gracklstugh [ch 023 / 023.01; ch 024 / 024.03]. The Cult of Demogorgon and assassins keep fighting each other in the previously-Mind-Flayer-marked area [ch 018 / 018.01].

### Blingdenstone Factions
- **Clan Diggermattock** — Took part of Blingdenstone from the Drow, making intelligence about Drow activity essential to the settlement's defenses [ch 036 / locations].
- **Blingdenstone leadership** — The city is politically split between Chief Dorbo's mining coalition (dominant) and Senni's civilian community, with the Goldwhisker Clan, rumored to be wererats, excluded from its former territory [ch 037 / 037.01]. The leadership is trying to claim credit for the party's victory over the gelatinous cubes while keeping the party's "revolutionary tactics" away from the crowd, exposing political tensions between Dorbo's wealth focus and Senni's advocacy for the people [ch 038 / 038.01]. Chief Dorbo, Senni Diggermattock, and Chipgrin; Senni seized control of the meeting from Dorbo and formed "a coalition of the willing" against the Pudding King [ch 045 / 045.02; ch 045 / npcs]. Chief Dorbo Diggermattock is profit-motivated and more concerned with the mines outside town than the civilian areas, while Senni Diggermattock is people-focused, worried about the community and her reforms; Dorbo is revealed to have owned a map of the whole area all along that he never showed the party [ch 046 / 046.01].
- **Diggermattock clan** — Already split between workers and the mining corporations; Chief Dorbo is unhappy about the elemental-labor ruling because "this affects gross margins," while Senni (representing the miners) is "really pumped" [ch 045 / 045.02]. The Diggermattock Miners Guild reached a wage agreement with the working class detailed to be discussed "underground," and agreed to sustainable mining practices respecting the ghosts' sacred ground [ch 048 / 048.06].
- **Clan Goldwhisker** — Wererat gnomes in contested territory north of the Traders' Grotto, dismissed as squatters by Dorbo but advocated for by Senni; their lycanthropic resilience to non-silver weapons could make them invaluable against oozes [ch 038 / 038.06; ch 038 / locations]. A community of wererat gnomes led by Chief Chipgrin; they hold court on a moss-covered rise, refused to be placed under Dorbo's command or expended as cannon fodder, and agreed to join the fight against the Pudding King [ch 039 / 039.04; ch 039 / 039.05]. Goldwhisker wererats took control of the upper boroughs of Blingdenstone as a self-funded defense force, removing Diggermattock's need to pay for military protection; Senni supported the arrangement [ch 048 / 048.06]. Wererats of Blingdenstone: debate continues over whether they should be considered gnomes at all and whether they should be welcomed or opposed [ch 040 / 040.02].
- **Burrow Wardens** — Jadger is secretly training young gnomes to re-establish the Wardens, including one he believes will soon become their leader and who may offer insights on the ooze problem [ch 037 / 037.04]. Burrow Warden ghosts dipped their spectral banners in salute as the party departed, watched by Jadger [ch 048 / 048.07].
- **Juiblex's influence** — The party theorized a demonic influence (likely Juiblex, demon lord of oozes) empowered the Pudding King and amplified greed and aggression throughout Blingdenstone's population [ch 039 / 039.06; ch 039 / npcs].
- **Stoneheart Enclave** — Reported to be "closing in on a solution" to Ogrémoch's Bane, which the party found amusing given the solution was evidently to send them to deal with it [ch 040 / 040.02]. Mentioned during the council negotiations as one of the presence factions [ch 048 / 048.06].
- **Blingdenstone's assembled forces** — The forces include an elemental imbued with Entémoch's power, wererats led by Chipgrin, Pickshine Miners led by Dasco Pickshine, and Blingdenstone ghosts, with reactions to the battle plan mixed: higher-ranking officers disappointed, ghosts wanting a big historic battle, gung-ho militia bummed, many others quietly relieved [ch 046 / 046.01; ch 046 / 046.02].
- **Ghosts of Blingdenstone** — Claim the North Tunnels as a graveyard, according to Dorbo; their clergy and spirits agreed to a sustainable mining compromise over the throne room [ch 048 / 048.06].
- **Gnomish religious leaders** — Went on a 20-minute rant agreeing that enslaving summoned creatures violated the Gnomish gods [ch 045 / 045.02].
- **Employees of Blingdenstone** — Working gnomes celebrated the party as "the toasts of the town" after their elemental-labor stand [ch 045 / 045.02].

### Candlekeep Factions
- **The Avowed** — The scholars, sages, and monk-professors who staff Candlekeep; the party donated five books to earn time with them, and the First Reader directs which scholar each party member consults [ch 054 / locations; ch 054 / 054.06]. New research and publication take precedence over ordinary emergencies; an active murder investigation was blown off for an emergency disputation [ch 060 / 060.01]. Kalan predicts that at the naming ceremony, suspicion will prevent both Bookwyrm and A'lai from becoming the new head, with Teles Ahvoste or Fheminor likely to prevail; a newly elected leader was expected to offer Bookwyrm a choice between submitting to a Zone of Truth and retaining her position [ch 062 / 062.02]. Every Avowed was once expected to memorize the High Tower of Exaltation's height, a practice fallen out of fashion [ch 068 / 068.03]. The scholars were astonished that dwarves solved the academic puzzle, fueling scholarly debate that may be studied for years; their reverence for Alaundo's monument kept the ink pot mechanism hidden for generations [ch 067 / 067.03; ch 067 / 067.04; ch 067 / moments].
- **Council of Twelve** — Candlekeep's ruling council, a dodecagonal table with places for the Keeper of Tomes, the First Reader, the eight Great Readers, the Gate Warden, and the Lorekeeper, was thrown into uproar by the Keeper's murder, with members split over voting a new leader, determining a successor, finding the murderer, and dismissing the Gatewarden [ch 057 / 057.05; ch 057 / locations].
- **The three schools of the Question of the Age** — The Stroudite school holds mortal choice sovereign, the Bahamutian school holds divine providence prevails, and the Draconic school holds ancient agency sets the frame; the controversy has spread to arguing libraries, taverns, and chapter houses, drawn written opinions from two cities' courts, moved Stroudite pamphlets across the Sea of Swords, and caused three duels at Candlekeep itself [ch 057 / 057.01].
- **Bahamutian / Stroudite split** — The Daggerford priest identifies as a "pure Bahamutian" who does not believe in "the heresy of the Stroudites," though he tolerates the Dwarven Stroudite inscription on the monument: "After all, wasn't Travis trying to save us all, regardless of what actually happened in that game of chess?" [ch 056 / 056.01]. A traveling polemicist in Mirabar preaches the "human agency hypothesis," rejecting both the Bahamutian hypothesis and the idea that Protanther let events unfold; a bronze Stroud bust stands at the Smiths Guildhall, and a tavern called The Knight Beneath the Knight bears his name [ch 055 / 055.03]. Real-world fiction enthusiasts: fans of Milo Goodbarrel's accounts who buy three copies of everything and debate the travel physics and print variations of his books [ch 055 / 055.06].
- **Order of the Gauntlet** — The Daggerford priest sells Travis's-hand medallions to raise donations for the order; Milo Goodbarrel, an Order of the Gauntlet member whose work was published by Rishaal, witnessed and recorded the monument [ch 056 / 056.01]. Met
