# Chapter 53 Out of the dark and into the darkness

Date: 2026-04-22

## Scenes

### 053.01 The Mercenary Contract

With the chaos of battle finally settled, the party turned their attention to the two Drow mercenaries who had fought alongside them — Nym and Kaelira Duskryn — as the pair made a quiet but deliberate move toward the exit. Daz thanked them and immediately began asking questions. The mercenaries explained that they had been hired in Menzoberranzan by an unknown benefactor and knew nothing about their employer beyond the source of the money. Their contract required them to ensure that other Drow factions did not get Daz killed, specifically protecting him from Ilvara of House Mizzrym and agents of House T'sarran.

Daz's knowledge of Drow politics made the scale of the arrangement clear: whoever hired Nym and Kaelira had paid a substantial fee to counter a hit squad from a rival noble house. The protection contract was therefore part of the vicious struggles among Menzoberranzan's houses, but its protection extended to Daz alone, not the rest of the party. Thorin asked what it would take to retain the mercenaries, but they struggled not to laugh before explaining that their ongoing services came at a price the party could not afford. Their reaction revealed that they were established professionals among the sort of people wealthy enough to hire Drow assassins.

The party challenged the mercenaries' confidence that the danger had passed, pointing out that Asha Vandree still lived and that a demonic power was trying to kill them. Nym and Kaelira dismissed Asha as a low-level cult initiate and explained that evil demon gods were well outside the terms of their contract. They regarded Asha as beneath their concern, flatly stating that if Daz could not handle someone of her caliber, whoever had paid for his protection deserved to lose the money. Nevertheless, Daz persuaded them that allowing him to die within minutes of their departure might reflect poorly when they attempted to collect their fee. They grudgingly agreed to escort him the remaining few miles to the exit.

#### The party interacts with two Drow mercenaries who were hired to protect Daz from specific threats.

- Nym and Kaelira Duskryn attempted to leave quietly once the fighting ended, having concluded that their contract was fulfilled.

- Daz stopped them to offer his thanks and ask who had hired them. They knew only that the money had been supplied in Menzoberranzan.

- The mercenaries described themselves without apology as Drow professionals motivated by money. When Daz called them truthful mercenaries, they responded, “What do you mean, very truthful mercenaries? We're drows.”

- Daz's exceptional recollection of Drow affairs allowed him to recognize that the unknown patron had spent heavily to counter a rival noble house's operations.

- The contract required Nym and Kaelira to prevent other Drow factions from killing Daz. It did not extend the same protection to the rest of the party.

- Their two designated threats were Ilvara of House Mizzrym and agents of House T'sarran. They had hoped the party would be wise enough to avoid Ilvara and believed they could have handled whatever force House T'sarran sent.

- The mercenaries congratulated the party for defeating House T'sarran's force without their direct assistance: “Bravo, bravo, bravo.”

- Thorin asked what it would cost to retain them. They tried not to laugh before explaining that the party could not afford their fee, revealing that they were highly regarded among the circles that hired such specialists.

- When questioned about Asha Vandree, they dismissed her as a low-level cult initiate whom Daz should be capable of killing within seconds.

- Zalthir reminded them that an evil demonic power was also trying to kill the group. They replied that protection from evil demon gods was not part of their contract.

- The party learned that Nym and Kaelira genuinely did not know their patron's identity.

### 053.02 The Fate of Asha Vandree

Asha Vandree, meanwhile, had emerged from the chaos utterly convinced that she had been divinely touched by Lolth. She had watched her entire crew descend into madness and ruin, yet she alone had kept the faith and survived. Riding high on this delusion, she began issuing commands to the party, treating non-Drow members and Drow males with the customary contempt of a Lolthian priestess. She marched forward and declared, “Excellent! Now that I am in charge!”

The party decided to put a period after that declaration. Gyrgum attempted to strike Asha with a guiding bolt, but the spell missed as she triumphantly cried, “I am blessed by Lolth!” Thorin closed the distance, seized her in a grapple, injured her with the strike, and clamped a hand over her mouth. Nym watched with visible exasperation and declared that one did not simply grapple a cleric of Lolth; one used a blade and cut off her head. Thorin left that decision to his companions. Zalthir judged Asha to be an evil cleric whose death would benefit the Underdark and unleashed a relentless flurry of strikes and tentacle attacks that killed her outright. Kaelira applauded, congratulated him, and observed to Nym that Daz was not completely defenseless after all.

#### The party confronts Asha Vandree, a Drow cleric who has become delusional with power, leading to a swift and lethal confrontation.

- Asha recovered from her ecstatic experience of worship and felt triumphant about being the sole survivor of her mission.

- She interpreted the destruction of her companions and her own survival as proof that she had kept the faith and been personally blessed by Lolth.

- Asha remained unaware that Daz's apparent sign from Lolth had been a deception. She believed she had participated in a divine revelation rather than a short con.

- As a Lolthian priestess, she expected obedience from non-Drow and Drow males alike. She treated Gyrgum with immediate condescension and marched forward declaring, “Excellent! Now that I am in charge!”

- Gyrgum, thoroughly unimpressed, suggested putting a period after the claim that Asha was in charge.

- The party had previously discussed allowing Asha to help them, then killing her and taking her magical belongings. Daz confirmed that this remained the plan.

- Gyrgum opened the confrontation with guiding bolt. The spell missed, prompting Asha to cry, “I am blessed by Lolth!”

- Thorin advanced, grappled Asha, inflicted five points of damage, and put a hand over her mouth to prevent her from speaking.

- Nym returned long enough to criticize this approach, explaining that clerics of Lolth should be decapitated rather than merely restrained. He told Daz that his concern for his safety had apparently been justified.

- Thorin explained that he did not like killing people unnecessarily and had restrained Asha so that the others could decide her fate.

- Zalthir concluded that Asha was an evil cleric whose removal would benefit the Underdark and committed to the party's original plan.

- Zalthir struck repeatedly with unarmed attacks, manifested tentacles, and an additional focused attack, dealing enough damage to kill Asha outright.

- Kaelira clapped and declared, “Well done, well done, well done!” She then told Nym, “See, he wasn't completely defenseless.”

- The mercenaries remained baffled by Thorin's reluctance to kill an obvious enemy, commenting that it was amazing the party had survived so long.

- Thorin confirmed that if he attacked Daz, the mercenaries would defend their charge, then reassured Daz that he was only asking.

### 053.03 Looting the Fungal Altar

With their enemies defeated, the party turned to the grim but satisfying work of looting the Fungal Altar. Among the fallen guards and the payroll chest Ilvara had been hauling since the breakout from Velkynvelve, they recovered 480 gold pieces and 1,200 silver pieces — the wages of soldiers who would never collect them. A chest of confiscated prisoner belongings yielded something far more personal for Thorin: his tooth, which Ilvara had apparently carried throughout her long pursuit of the party. Also tucked inside were a half-water-damaged journal in Dwarvish belonging to a presumed dead prisoner and a stoppered clay flask of Overbright brandy that had somehow survived completely intact. The party also recovered the jailer's ring of keys, an object that would have been invaluable at the beginning of their imprisonment and now served as a reminder of how far they had come.

Asha's body gave up a holy symbol of Lolth, scale mail with spider-fretted trim, obsidian-and-bone prayer beads, coins, and a sealed wax pouch bearing her personal seal. Daz recognized the sealing technique as one Lolth's clerics used when they wanted to know whether private writings had been opened. Inside was a long, tedious theological argument explaining why Zuggtmoy's claims upon Lolth were heresy and exploring the attempted fusion of the two beings. The writings dated from after Asha's descent into madness. Daz also claimed the Drow cloak of elvenkind found among the spoils, a garment that granted its wearer an uncanny gift for going unseen but would disintegrate in sunlight.

Ilvara's own belongings told a darker story. Her prayer book to Lolth was filled with Zuggtmoy-related marginalia written in another hand, preserving a timeline in which ordinary devotion gradually gave way to corruption. Two recent sealed orders from House Mizzrym confirmed that Ilvara had still been receiving and obeying instructions from her house when Zuggtmoy overtook her. As Ilvara stopped responding, the house's messages became increasingly frantic: where was she, what had she been doing, and why had she ignored the previous orders? Daz and Gyrgum inferred that House Mizzrym's inability to locate its missing agent had become known, prompting House T'sarran to investigate and eventually dispatch its own force.

A partially legible letter from Neverlight Grove had been written by Ilvara's agent before that unfortunate soul became fungus food. It referenced “the bride” and a meeting that never occurred. Ilvara also possessed a corrupted five-tailed scourge whose lashes were living serpent heads. Attacking with it could aerosolize poisonous spores around its wielder, but the party learned that it could be purified. After some theatrical objections from Dawnbringer, the cleansing was accomplished during the party's rest.

### 053.04 Looting the Fallen and the Heart Fungus

On the body of the Drow mage, the party found a signet ring bearing the seal of House T'sarran. Daz recognized the ring as both proof of the house's involvement and a tool with which convincing orders could be forged in its name. The same mage carried an elaborate but nonmagical obsidian arcane focus tipped with a captive spider preserved in amber. Partially burned orders contained a surviving instruction concerning “the mage Daz” and confirming that “the extraction” was to be alive and unharmed. The damaged portions could not be restored through ordinary mending, although the party considered whether sufficiently powerful divination might recover more.

Gyrgum investigated the heart of the corrupted fungal mass that had dominated the chamber. At its center he found a dense, fist-sized, pearl-like node radiating residual necromantic energy, worth approximately 300 gold pieces to the right buyer. The party also recovered three intact spore sacks that an alchemist might transform into something useful once they reached a city.

Zalthir then turned his fire breath upon the remnant of Zuggtmoy's corruption atop Ilvara's elevated stone platform. After a faltering first effort prompted jokes about performance anxiety, he burned it to the ground. Beneath the smoldering remains lay a leather-wrapped bundle containing two unused incense cones, a small silver bowl, and a folded cloth stitched with the sigils of Lolth and Zuggtmoy, one nested inside the other. These were ceremonial objects and physical evidence of the syncretism that had driven Asha and Ilvara mad — proof that something profoundly unnatural was spreading through the Underdark.

When Nym and Kaelira examined the burned extraction orders, they confirmed that House T'sarran's team had apparently been paid to abduct Daz and bring him out alive. The mercenaries found the situation baffling. Daz belonged to no known noble house, had a mysterious background, and had seemingly appeared from nowhere, yet someone had paid an extraordinary amount for his protection while another faction sought to extract him. Kaelira compared his speculation about hidden importance to every orphan imagining a secret noble heritage and asked whether he had “daddy issues” or “mommy issues,” though even the mercenaries admitted the expense was unusual. Their final assessment was simple: they had not been paid to think that hard.

### 053.05 Reflections and the Road to the Surface

The evidence led the party to reconsider the beginning of their ordeal. They had all been together when captured, raising the possibility that Daz had been the true target and everyone else had been swept up because they were traveling with the wrong Drow. Daz remained deeply unsettled and deeply curious. He proposed that the party help him investigate why powerful factions wanted him protected or extracted, offering to split any eventual proceeds. He also returned to his familiar solution for obscure conspiracies: auditing their books, a suggestion the party had heard often enough to greet with groans.

After months of trudging through the darkness of the Underdark, Nym and Kaelira guided the party through the remaining miles to the exit. The companions finally crawled into the blinding, glorious light of the surface world — the Overbright. Daz prudently packed away his sunlight-fragile cloak before emerging. Everyone received a long rest, and the corrupted serpent-headed scourge was purified.

Glabbagool, the sentient grey ooze formerly shaped like a gelatinous cube, peered out from a bag of holding with enormous googly eyes. He had not even known that the Overbright existed and was delighted by the entirely new world around him. Dawnbringer, meanwhile, protested the indignity of being treated like a ritual implement: she was a high-quality sunblade that killed things, not “some kind of magical wizard thingy” to be waved over corrupted objects. A reminder of the elaborate Vaultmaster Locking Scabbard purchased for her eventually persuaded the blade to “do this solid” for the party.

#### The party discusses the mysterious focus on Daz and the strange circumstances of their imprisonment as they prepare to leave the Underdark.

- Zalthir revisited the circumstances of the party's original capture, asking whether they had been apprehended together or separately.

- The group established that they had been traveling together when captured. This raised the possibility that Daz was the true target and his companions had merely suffered for befriending “the wrong Drow.”

- Daz had no explanation for the attention. He wondered whether it was simply because he was the party's only Drow, but the evidence offered no clear answer.

- The mercenaries suggested the entire affair could be a game between noble houses, though Gyrgum observed that it was an unusually expensive game.

- Daz dismissed unsupported speculation and returned to the practical suggestion that auditing the factions' books might reveal the truth.

- The GM noted that Daz canonically possessed and had read Milo Goodbarrel's book. By this point the party had heard his auditing suggestion roughly twenty times and responded with groans.

- Daz proposed that his companions help investigate the factions interested in him and split whatever proceeds emerged from the mystery.

- Thorin agreed to remain involved largely because he wanted to learn “what the fuck's going on.”

- The party retained other unresolved mysteries, including the true nature of Jimjar.

- Dawnbringer objected to being used to purify the serpent-headed scourge, insisting, “I am a high-quality sunblade. I kill things.”

- Thorin reminded Dawnbringer of the Vaultmaster Locking Scabbard. The blade relented enough to say, “I suppose I could do you this one. I could do this solid for you.”

- The long rest completed the scourge's purification before the party entered the surface world.

### 053.06 Emergence into the Overbright

The party agreed to remain together during several months of downtime. Daz wanted to investigate why so many powerful forces had focused upon him. Thorin was curious enough to follow the mystery and intended to acquire javelins, improve Dawnbringer's offensive potential, and perhaps locate a “sword whisperer” who could help the sentient weapon process the trauma of being trapped in darkness. Gyrgum offered to assist Daz's research and hoped to study Bahamut at Candlekeep, even if the fortress had no dedicated shrine.

Their destination became Candlekeep, the great fortress-library south of Waterdeep, where experts in obscure subjects could be found. There they intended to research House T'sarran, learn who or what Jimjar truly was, investigate Daz's hidden history, and consult Candlekeep's literature concerning Bahamut and traumatized intelligent weapons. They also discussed using their accumulated wealth to improve their adventuring equipment, despite Thorin's joking proposal that they create a private-equity firm and adventure on the side.

#### After months of traveling through the Underdark, the party finally reaches the surface and plans their next moves during a period of downtime.

- With Nym and Kaelira guiding them through the final miles, the party escaped the Underdark after several months of travel.

- Daz removed and stored the Drow cloak of elvenkind before exposing it to sunlight, preventing its disintegration.

- The group reached the surface fully rested and in possession of the relevant recovered evidence and treasures.

- Glabbagool remained with the party. He had not known the Overbright existed and watched the new world from inside a bag of holding, his two large googly eyes peering outward.

- The party was granted several months of downtime to rest, recover, conduct research, and acquire equipment.

- Daz wanted the party to remain together so they could investigate why powerful factions were trying either to protect or extract him.

- The party identified three major research questions: who Jimjar really was, what House T'sarran was, and why anyone would invest so heavily in an apparently unimportant Drow.

- Candlekeep was selected as the obvious destination because it was the Sword Coast's greatest concentration of scholars and specialists in obscure subjects.

- Candlekeep lay south of Waterdeep, the surface city with which the party was most familiar.

- Thorin acquired javelins during the downtime and expressed interest in making Dawnbringer more deadly.

- Dawnbringer approved of the idea of increased offensive power but objected when Thorin suggested also making her less afraid of darkness: “I'm not afraid of the dark, I just don't like the dark.”

- Thorin proposed finding a therapist specializing in traumatized intelligent weapons. The imagined Candlekeep specialist was dubbed “the Sword Whisperer.”

- Dawnbringer maintained that nothing was wrong with her, though she allowed that Thorin could waste his own money on consultation if he wished.

- Gyrgum offered to help Daz with his research and planned to study Candlekeep's literature about Bahamut. Although there was no dedicated Bahamut shrine, the library contained extensive stories and religious material, including pamphlets.

- The party agreed that its considerable shared wealth should be invested in useful adventuring equipment.

- Thorin jokingly proposed consolidating local merchants and turning the group into a private-equity firm that adventured on the side.

- The group discussed the rarity of people capable of becoming adventurers, the extraordinary mortality rate among them, and the magical ceiling that prevented advancement beyond twentieth level.

- Powerful adventurers were framed as strategic assets, while quest givers functioned as talent-acquisition specialists attempting to identify promising adventurers before they died.

- This explained why low-level monsters tried so hard to kill novices: if left alive, those novices might return weeks later with fireballs.

## NPCs

### Nym Duskryn

A professional Drow mercenary and fighter hired in Menzoberranzan to protect Daz from Ilvara of House Mizzrym and House T'sarran. Nym is blunt, arrogant, and highly conscious of professional standards. He criticized Kaelira for not preparing dispel magic and was appalled when Thorin merely grappled a cleric of Lolth. He regards Asha Vandree as too trivial a threat to fall within the contract and believes needing protection from her would insult both his abilities and his patron's judgment. Although his assignment was complete, he agreed to escort Daz the remaining few miles to the surface.

### Kaelira Duskryn

A Drow wizard and mercenary partnered with Nym. She responds to criticism with “critics, there's always a critic” and meets violence with sardonic professional approval. After Zalthir killed Asha, she applauded and observed that Daz was not entirely defenseless. Kaelira helped interpret the extraction orders, emphasized the vast cost of hiring her and Nym, and mocked Daz's suggestion that his mysterious past made him secretly important. Her ultimate position was that she had not been paid to think hard about the reasons behind the contract.

### Asha Vandree

A Drow cleric of Lolth who survived the recent chaos and became convinced that she had been divinely chosen. She mistook Daz's deception for a genuine sign from Lolth and interpreted the destruction of everyone around her as confirmation that she alone had kept the faith. Her immediate attempt to command the party ended when Gyrgum attacked, Thorin grappled and gagged her, and Zalthir killed her with a barrage of strikes. Her belongings included Drow armor, Lolthian holy symbols, obsidian-and-bone prayer beads, coins, and a sealed theological text written after her corruption.

### Ilvara

The defeated leader of the House Mizzrym force that pursued the party, whose belongings were examined during this session. She had continued receiving instructions from her house while Zuggtmoy's influence took hold, then disappeared and stopped responding. Her prayer book preserves a visible timeline of that corruption through Zuggtmoy marginalia. She had been carrying Velkynvelve's payroll, confiscated prisoner belongings, House Mizzrym's increasingly frantic orders, a letter from Neverlight Grove, and a corrupted five-tailed scourge with living serpent heads. The fused ceremonial objects on her platform provide physical evidence that she had combined the worship of Lolth and Zuggtmoy.

### Dawnbringer

A sentient sunblade carried by Thorin. Dawnbringer considers herself a high-quality weapon designed to kill things and objects loudly to being treated as a ritual implement. She reluctantly assisted in purifying Ilvara's corrupted scourge after being reminded of her prestigious Vaultmaster Locking Scabbard. She is enthusiastic about becoming more deadly, insists she merely dislikes rather than fears darkness, and rejects the suggestion that she needs therapy despite Thorin's interest in finding a specialist at Candlekeep.

### Glabbagool

A sentient grey ooze formerly shaped like a gelatinous cube who continues to travel with the party. Glabbagool did not know the Overbright existed before the party reached the surface. He now observes the unfamiliar world from a bag of holding, peering out through two enormous googly eyes and appearing delighted by the discovery.

## Locations

### Menzoberranzan

The principal city of the Drow and the location where the contract protecting Daz was funded. The unknown patron paid Nym and Kaelira there, placing the arrangement within the noble-house struggles of the city. The mercenaries did not know the patron's identity, while Daz could not recall useful details about House T'sarran despite his familiarity with Menzoberranzan.

### The Fungal Altar

The site of the party's battle with Ilvara's corrupted forces and the final confrontation with Asha Vandree. Its spoils included Velkynvelve's payroll, confiscated prisoner belongings, Ilvara's papers, the heart fungus, and the Zuggtmoy spore remnant on Ilvara's elevated stone platform. The chamber's corrupted circles could still cause blindness, while the large central fungal manifestation had collapsed into dust.

### The Overbright

A Drow and Underdark term for the surface world. The party finally emerged into its sunlight after months underground. Sunlight would destroy certain Drow-crafted objects, including Daz's cloak of elvenkind, so those items had to be packed away before the ascent. It was an entirely unknown world to Glabbagool.

### Candlekeep

A great fortress-library on the Sword Coast, south of Waterdeep, filled with scholars and experts in obscure fields. The party selected it as the best place to research House T'sarran, Jimjar, and Daz's mysterious background. Gyrgum also intends to consult its extensive writings about Bahamut, while Thorin hopes it may house someone capable of helping a traumatized intelligent weapon.
