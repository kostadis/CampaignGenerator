# Chapter 54 Candlekeep Library and the Littlest Drow in Faerun

## Scenes

### 054.01 Farewell to Eldeth

The party emerged from the Underdark into the warm embrace of the surface world, and for a long moment, they simply breathed in the open air and let the sunlight wash over them. Glabbagool, the peculiar sentient grey ooze (formerly a gelatinous cube) who had become an unlikely companion, gazed up at the sky with his googly eyes, visibly moved by the experience. Zalthir watched him quietly, contemplating the strange and winding path that had brought such an unlikely group together, reflecting on how he had — in some sense — offered to teach Glabbagool the ways of a monk. The GM noted that Glabbagool was very much looking forward to the day he would do the "run, run, slap, slap, slap" of a monk's combat style, though nobody had the heart to tell him yet that he moves extremely slowly and taking a single step would be a good start. Dawnbringer, the party's sentient sword, was practically radiant with joy, declaring it the best decision she had ever made to travel with them.

The celebration was bittersweet, however, as Eldeth prepared to part ways with the group. The dwarven warrior spoke with deep sincerity, telling Thorin that they were like brothers who had fought, bled, and faced abyssal horrors together. She explained that she needed to go to Mithral Hall first, to warn the dwarves and explain matters to the king, and only afterwards speak to the lords of Gauntlgrym and Mithral Hall about the dangers lurking in the Underdark, and asked Thorin to wait before following her. Thorin was clear that he took her at her word — "I'm taking this seriously. I don't think you're just being socially nice here. I think you really do want me to" — and Eldeth responded by swearing on her honor that once she had explained the situation to the lords, Thorin would be embraced by her clan. Dawnbringer threw a small hissy fit at being described as "a really cool sword who's kind of annoying sometimes," which Thorin acknowledged with characteristic diplomacy. Spiderbait, the small and anxious goblin of the group, initially declared his excitement about exploring the surface world freely, confident that goblins were totally integrated into surface society and that nobody would attack him just for being a goblin. The party gently disabused him of this notion, with Gyrgum noting that the state of goblin equality and inclusion was "rough at the moment." Spiderbait quickly revised his plans and asked to travel with the party to Candlekeep, where he hoped to find quiet, innocuous work.

### 054.02 Shadows at Dusk

The journey west was peaceful at first, a welcome contrast to the relentless pursuit they had endured in the depths below. Dawnbringer reveled in every moment of sunlight and starlight, cheerfully narrating her delight. But on the third day, as dusk settled over the forest road, something shifted in the air. Most of the party felt it instinctively — the prickling certainty that they were not alone. Gyrgum, rolling a 9 on his Insight check, confidently concluded the presence was a deer. Thorin, Daz, and Zalthir, each rolling a 20, 16, and 16 respectively, correctly identified that someone had been following them for some time — and that the tracker was within 20 to 40 feet, coming from the northern area.

#### While traveling toward Candlekeep, the party realizes they are being stalked by an unknown tracker.

- The party travels west for several days toward Candlekeep.
- Dawnbringer is "totally thrilled" — there is sunlight, there are stars, and there is no such thing as perfect darkness. She tells the party she made the best decision hanging out with them.
- On the third day at dusk, the GM calls for Insight and Investigation checks.
- Gyrgum rolls a 9 and confidently concludes the presence is a deer; the GM confirms: "You conclude that what is following you is a deer." Gyrgum accepts this and prepares to nap.
- Thorin rolls a 20, Zalthir rolls a 16, and Daz rolls a 16. The three of them quickly realize someone has been following them for some time and is now very close — within 20 to 40 feet, coming from the northern area.
- Thorin, frustrated that his Tremor Sense requires stone and the party is on a dirt road through a forest, launches into a brief rant: "I picked it because we were gonna be fucking underground all the time, and I figured there would always be some stone, and then the one time I need it... we're not underground, and we're not surrounded by fucking stone." Dawnbringer comments that Thorin has anger issues.

- The party debates options: fireball the trees, use magic, set an ambush. Daz proposes getting intelligence rather than burning everything down.

### 054.03 A Shadow in the Woods

Daz moved first, vanishing from the road in a curl of smoke and reappearing high in the branches of a large tree to scout. He spotted a drow figure concealed in the brush below, but the spy's perception was sharp enough to notice him in return — a tense mutual awareness. Daz signaled the party with a prearranged bird call, impersonating a cardinal (a bird the party noted has no business being in these particular trees), and Zalthir acted without hesitation, teleporting directly onto the spy and delivering a swift, precise series of blows — grappling her, striking her a second time for a combined 25 damage, and forcing a failed DC 13 Constitution save that left her stunned. The rest of the party closed in quickly, with Daz climbing down from the tree to tie the spy up while delivering the dry observation that "it's very rude to follow people without just coming up and talking to them, so we'll talk to you now." Thorin loomed over the incapacitated figure, demanding to know who she worked for — though since the spy was still stunned, she simply stared at him blankly.

### 054.04 Interrogation of the Drow Spy

Once the spy recovered her senses, she was defiant, offering only her piwafwi as a trophy rather than her name, identifying herself as a member of House T'sarran, and declaring that her matron would know one of hers had fallen out here, and that was enough. Thorin suggested an alternative fate worse than death: letting her go and telling her matron how pathetic she had been, which Gyrgum correctly noted was "almost the same as killing her." Then Gyrgum, reading her as a low-ranking agent stuck with a miserable above-ground assignment who was putting on a brave face, took a more disarming approach and casually offered her a job. The spy was visibly confused at the prospect of surviving the encounter at all. Then, without a word of explicit threat, he simply handed her the prayer beads of Bahamut and told her to put them on. The religious implications — a faith whose rituals she did not know; used to a demonic cult of her own, she did not know what to expect — sent a visible wave of panic through her, and the defiance in her eyes flickered. Dawnbringer's blinding light held close to her face added to the pressure.

Under this combined intimidation, the spy began to talk. She revealed that her orders had been to track the party to their surface destination and observe who approached them — she had not been sent to engage at all. More importantly, Daz observed through a 27 Insight check that the spy was specifically and carefully trying not to make it obvious that she was only watching him, not the whole party. The spy's Matron Mother had not sent her after Daz himself — she had sent her after whoever had paid to have Daz protected. The mercenaries who had helped the party earlier had not known who employed them. House T'sarran believed this unknown and powerful benefactor would reveal themselves now that Daz had surfaced, and the spy's mission was to observe and report. Daz realized with some discomfort that he was being used as bait: "So basically, you're treating me as bait... you're watching and observing so that when something happens to me, you can go report back what happened to me." The spy confirmed this without apology: "We are looking for the one who paid to have you protected."

The spy proposed a mutual information-sharing arrangement, but Thorin was skeptical: "The minute she has the information she needs, she's off, and we get jack shit." The party briefly entertained the plan of temporarily killing Daz to draw out his protector — Gyrgum noted he had a Revivify spell, making it "just a death light" — which made Daz visibly uncomfortable. The spy ultimately suggested being escorted to Candlekeep and handed to the guards, implying she already knew how to escape. The party debated this, with Thorin noting she seemed "so confident she can escape" that bringing her into the stronghold was unwise.

### 054.05 The Pocket Spy and the Road to Candlekeep

After considerable debate, the party arrived at a creative and deeply undignified solution. They produced magical shrinking mushrooms from their supplies, reduced the spy to a tiny, squeaking, furious fraction of her former size, and nicknamed her Polly Pocket. She was placed inside the bag of holding — with Thorin proposing they poke a couple of holes in it so she could breathe "like a hamster" — with Glabbagool assigned as her personal guard. Gyrgum and Thorin conspicuously mentioned to each other, within the spy's earshot, that the shrunken prayer beads were in fact more potent at their reduced size. The GM noted the indignity was beginning to get to her, with the spy starting to wonder whether there were other fates she might prefer. Thorin offered one small mercy: at least the bag of holding provided shade from the sun. Glabbagool, for his part, took his new responsibility seriously, fixing one eye permanently on the indignant prisoner as the party continued west.

The great fortress-library of Candlekeep rose before them at last. The party learned that entry required the donation of a unique book not already held in its vast collection, and that donating more books would grant them more time with the Avowed — the scholars, sages, and monk-professors who staffed the library. They offered all five of their rare texts: the Velkynvelve Prisoner Journal, Princess Ebonmire's spellbook, Ilvara's prayer book with Zuggtmoy marginalia, Asha Vandree's theological argument, and Plinki's Journal. Glabbagool, deeply confused about why anyone would not simply donate every book to be able to ask questions until the end of time, was nevertheless granted his own question to put to the scholars, as the party felt he deserved to feel like a full member of the group.

### 054.06 Entry into Candlekeep

The First Reader received the Prisoner Journal with barely contained excitement, calling it a masterpiece and noting it as an excellent addition to her existing collection of drow prison journals — an apparently robust genre. Thorin suggested the party could simply be interviewed as primary sources, sparing her the reading. The party also presented their shrunken drow captive as a living exhibit, which prompted the tiny spy to declare "This is highly irregular, and House T'sarran will get its vengeance!" — to which Daz replied dryly, "Tiny, tiny little vengeance." Glabbagool was assigned to keep her under control, and demonstrated his authority by suddenly manifesting a large number of eyes, making the situation deeply unsettling for the prisoner. The First Reader efficiently directed the party to their relevant scholars: Zalthir to learn about Jimjar, Thorin and Dawnbringer to Philemon (a specialist in the psychological care of sentient magical artifacts, with a noted track record of success), and noted that the avowed scholar Khell-Vire had specifically expressed interest in Gyrgum.

Before the party could fully settle in, a frantic elderly half-elf scholar came rushing in, practically quivering, and declared to the First Reader that he could not believe he had not been told about the arrival of a cleric of Bahamut. This was Vareth, whose previously obscure research into orc conversions to the worship of Bahamut had become the hottest field in academia following the legendary events of Stroud and Protanther. He grabbed Gyrgum's hand and practically dragged him toward his office, describing Gyrgum as "a real living orc cleric of Bahamut" and a "living data point," and revealing that he had managed to secure a record of the legendary chess game known as Stroud's Gambit. Gyrgum confirmed he was familiar with the game, which only intensified Vareth's excitement: "This is all novel news! I can't wait to take advantage of it!" The party reflected on their shared familiarity with Milo Goodbarrel's multi-volume Account of the War of the Dragons, which Daz has cited to the point of exhaustion throughout their travels — and Daz mused privately that he has some interest in eventually writing his own account, one designed to obscure certain truths so that the real story can remain larger than the published one.

## Locations

### The Overbright

The surface world above the Underdark, characterized by sunlight, stars, and open air. The party revels in their freedom here after months underground. Dawnbringer is particularly ecstatic. The surface is noted to be deeply uncomfortable for drow like the spy from House T'sarran, who found the above-ground posting miserable.

### Gauntlgrym and Mithral Hall

The ancestral dwarven strongholds where Eldeth intends to return to warn her people of the abyssal threats lurking in the Underdark. Eldeth specifically wants to speak to the lords there before Thorin arrives, so that his unusual background (dwarf raised by giants, traveling with a drow) does not derail the briefing.

### Forest Road

A dirt road through a forested area west of the Underdark exit, where the party attempted to set up camp at dusk and discovered they were being followed. The road runs through terrain the tracker used effectively for cover. Notably, it is not stone — rendering Thorin's Tremor Sense useless, much to his frustration.

### Candlekeep

A legendary, impregnable fortress-library on the Sword Coast. Entry is granted only in exchange for a unique book not already in its vast collection; donating more books earns more time with the Avowed — the scholars, sages, and monk-professors who staff it. The First Reader heads the institution. The GM describes it as "basically an impregnable fortress" and analogizes it to "the Alexandrian Library" in terms of its scope. The party notes that once inside, it will be practically impossible for House T'sarran to send agents after them.

A valuable magical tome, donated by the party to Candlekeep as one of their entry offerings. Potentially useful for researching Daz's magical lineage.

A religious text featuring marginalia related to Zuggtmoy, donated by the party to Candlekeep. The GM noted this book may be particularly relevant to questions about Jimjar.

A journal recovered from the Whorlstone Caverns, written by a figure involved with Demogorgon, detailing experiments in creating demonic monsters. Daz privately muses about writing a counter-account designed to obscure certain truths and keep the real story larger than what is publicly known.

## NPCs

### Eldeth

A dwarven warrior who fought alongside the party throughout the Underdark. She departs for Mithral Hall and Gauntlgrym to warn her clan and their lords of the dangers below. She speaks sincerely and at length to Thorin: "We are like brothers. We have fought together, we have bled together, we have faced the abyssal creatures together." She swears: "After I have spoken and explained to the lords of Gauntlgrym and Mithral Hall the dangers of the Underdark, I am certain they will want to speak to you, and I will make it a statement of purpose that you shall be embraced by our clan." She also gives Zalthir a knowing look about the political wisdom of preparing the lords before Thorin's unusual background becomes the main topic of conversation.

### Glabbagool

A sentient grey ooze, formerly a gelatinous cube, with googly eyes — which, he explains, are affectations he adopted because he discovered "if you have these cute eyes, people take you more friendly." He is overjoyed to be on the surface and visibly moved by seeing the sun for the first time. He is fascinated and confused by the concept of a library and cannot understand why one would not simply donate every book and ask questions forever. He is granted his own question to put to the Candlekeep scholars. He serves as the party's guard over their shrunken drow captive, fixing one eye on her permanently, and demonstrates his authority when needed by manifesting a large number of eyes at once, which the GM describes as becoming "very creepy very quickly."

### Spiderbait

A small goblin who has never been on the surface before and is genuinely excited about it — until the party informs him that goblin-surface relations are not as harmonious as he had assumed. He asks to travel with the party to Candlekeep in hopes of finding safe, innocuous work.

### Drow Spy

An agent of House T'sarran sent to the surface to track Daz and identify his mysterious protector. She is stealthy, defiant, and refuses to give her name, offering her piwafwi as a trophy of her failure. She identified herself as a member of House T'sarran. Under sustained pressure — Zalthir's Stunning Strike and grapple, Thorin's intimidation attempts, Gyrgum's disarming friendliness, the prayer beads, and Dawnbringer's light — she eventually reveals her mission: to observe and report on whoever paid for Daz's protection. She proposes a mutual information-sharing deal and then suggests being handed to Candlekeep's guards. She is ultimately subdued, reduced to tiny size with a shrinking mushroom (becoming "very squeaky and indignant"), nicknamed Polly Pocket, stored in a bag of holding, and assigned to Glabbagool's custody. The indignity is noted to be getting to her.

### Matron Mother of House T'sarran

The powerful ruler of House T'sarran, said to have held power for 200 years. She is orchestrating the surveillance of the party from afar, specifically seeking to identify the unknown entity that paid for Daz's protection. When Daz claims not to have heard of her, the spy finds this baffling: "You have not heard? Are you not drow?"

### The First Reader

The head of the Avowed at Candlekeep. She is an enthusiastic collector of rare texts, particularly those concerning drow imprisonment, and receives the Velkynvelve Prisoner Journal with barely contained excitement, calling it "a masterpiece of interesting value" that will "go great with my other editions in drow prison journals." She efficiently directs the party to the appropriate scholars for their questions.

### Vareth

An elderly, frantic, and officious half-elf scholar and member of the Avowed. He specializes in orc conversions to the worship of Bahamut — a field that was previously so obscure and ignored that colleagues had been ignoring him for years. Since the legendary events of Stroud and Protanther, his research has exploded: he is now receiving funding, conference invitations, and serious academic attention. He rushes in furious that the First Reader did not immediately summon him upon a cleric of Bahamut's arrival, grabs Gyrgum's hand, and practically drags him toward his office, describing him as "a real living orc cleric of Bahamut" and "a living data point." He has secured a record of the legendary chess game, and when Gyrgum identifies it by name as "Stroud's Gambit," Vareth declares: "This is all novel news! I can't wait to take advantage of it! The field of Bahamut orc conversions will benefit tremendously from your interview."

### Philemon

An Avowed monk at Candlekeep who specializes in "psychological preservation and historical cataloging of ancient sentient artifacts" and has a "tremendous track record of success in working through the interpersonal conflict issues of the sentient artifact." He is assigned to work with Thorin and Dawnbringer. Thorin is enthusiastic: "I'm all up for couples counseling with Dawnbringer."

### Khell-Vire

A scholar at Candlekeep who expressed a specific interest in studying Gyrgum, prompting the First Reader to note that it was "very excellent" that the party had arrived, as Khell-Vire wishes to learn about Gyrgum specifically — in parallel with Zalthir's own questions about Jimjar.

### Sister Yvenne

A scholar at Candlekeep designated to speak with the party regarding their deeper questions, mentioned by the First Reader when directing the party to their relevant contacts.
