# Chapter 69 One Manshoon Dead, at least one or two more to go.

Date: 2026-09-08

## Scenes
### 069.01 Battle at the Magma Chamber

Combat resumes beside the basalt tower above the magma. Manshoon is already thirteen points down from Daz's Phantasmal Killer and protected by Mirror Image. Daz stands at the beginning of the standing anti-magic band on the bridge, and Edvaldo remains nearby.

Thorin charged forward with Dawnbringer raised high, landing a powerful blow against the robed figure before them. Manshoon fought back with cunning, protected by illusory duplicates that absorbed some of the party's strikes, sending fists and blades through false images rather than flesh.

Zalthir moved with terrifying precision, shifting his magical darkness over Manshoon and activating the eldritch power bound into his tattoo. Tendrils of living darkness reached fifteen feet through the gloom, struck the wizard, and seized him. Zalthir then dragged Manshoon bodily into the anti-magic barrier and battered him with further attacks. Manshoon, desperate, used a legendary burst of movement to wrench himself free and reposition near the tower. After the party clarified exactly how far Zalthir could have dragged him, Manshoon still had to run to escape, allowing Daz to slash him with a short sword as he passed.

Manshoon retaliated with Ward Eater, a devastating wave of magical energy affecting creatures outside the anti-magic field. Zalthir and Daz were protected by their position inside the field, but Gyrgum, Thorin, and Edvaldo were caught. Thorin and Edvaldo suffered the full force of the effect and were immobilized, while Manshoon siphoned vitality from his victims and regained twenty-five hit points. Edvaldo's continued survival immediately looked wrong: by straightforward calculation, the Avowed researcher should have been dead.

Gyrgum answered by summoning a floating spectral sword at fifth level. The weapon struck Manshoon for a heavy blow and remained hovering nearby for the rounds that followed. Seeing Edvaldo howling in pain despite having survived impossible punishment, Gyrgum called reassuringly across the battlefield, “Don't worry, if you die, I can bring you back.”

Daz left the anti-magic field long enough to cast *Fireball*, centering and shaping the blast so that it caught Manshoon and Edvaldo without striking Gyrgum or Thorin. Manshoon partially evaded the flames but was left with only a sliver of strength; immobilized Edvaldo took the full blast and still did not fall. Thorin, unable to move but still able to fight, hurled a javelin at Manshoon. The wizard's remaining duplicate spared him from death. Thorin considered throwing Dawnbringer next, but dwarf and sentient sword shared a moment of mutual judgment before he chose a dart instead—and missed.

### 069.02 The Melting of the Simulacrum

Zalthir pressed the assault. Manshoon raised a magical *Shield* to deflect the first blow, bringing his defenses just high enough to survive it, but the protection bought him only a moment. Zalthir's next strike landed true, and the great Manshoon died—only to revert instantly into snow and melt in the heat of the magma chamber. An Arcana check confirmed the truth: the party had been fighting a simulacrum, a partially real construct of ice and snow with Manshoon's abilities but only a fraction of his vitality.

Daz scrutinized the performance closely and found it convincing enough to stay Zalthir's hand, although Edvaldo's impossible survival and earlier inability to state the tower's precise height continued to trouble the party.

Glabbagool earnestly asked why Zalthir had not simply thrown Manshoon into the lava. His hero-worshipping delivery made the tactical criticism especially difficult to ignore. Zalthir explained that the party had needed to give the wizard a chance because killing him before he cast a single spell would not have been “sporty,” then asked Glabbagool to remind him in the future whenever he overlooked an available instant-death trap.

The spell, with its thousand-foot range, confirmed that another duplicate was upstairs.

The prophecy also mentioned the lords of the dwarves and an unlit forge. Daz recognized that the voice was conjured rather than human because of its slight fluctuation in timbre and its lack of breathing between enunciations.

### 069.05 The Obsidian Door and the Wizard's Bargain

The party ascended into a plain circular chamber. Dawnbringer was the principal source of illumination. Shelves rose from floor to ceiling along the eastern wall, holding a hundred of the most dangerous books in the world. Each showed the faint visual distortion of a magical glyph. A desk stood in the middle of the room with a note upon it, and an open velvet tray on the far wall held ten prismatic gemstones, four of them dark.

At the far end stood a door of black stone with no handle, keyhole, or visible seam. Eleven scorch marks formed a rough circle at chest height, evidence of repeated unsuccessful attempts to force it open. Before the shelves stood another Manshoon, his hands clasped behind his back. He looked exactly like the simulacrum the party had just killed, but his gray robes were noticeably cheaper.

This Manshoon reminded the party that the chamber contained hundreds of *Glyphs of Warding*. An explosion could trigger a catastrophic cascade among the warded books. He had already solved part of the door's mystery but could do nothing with it: “I've been sitting here since then with 100 books I cannot read and a door I cannot open, hoping somebody would come. And you have.”

Then a recorded echo of Alaundo's voice filled the chamber: “To the last door I gave no lock, for a lock is a promise that a thing can be stolen. I gave it a question instead.” The party recalled the decoded instructions from their earlier discoveries: those well-versed in Candlekeep's lore might fearlessly pass the Obsidian Door; the answer concerned something consumed to make light; it had to be spoken in the dark, offered as a gift rather than a demand, and said only once. Because Gyrgum had answered the guardians' questions and been verified as the reader, he was the person who would need to speak it.

The second Manshoon proposed a bargain and pointedly asked the party to notice that it was not a threat. He lifted one of the prophetic stones and explained the exchange: “If you want the dwarf's prophecy, I want a door. These are not the same object. Your cleric says one word, the door opens. You take the tray, I take the book.” Manshoon offered the tray in exchange for opening the door. The implied threat was that Manshoon could destroy the prophecies if the party refused.

### 069.06 Questioning Edvaldo About the Riddle

Gyrgum connected the chamber to the previously decoded line stating that those well-versed in Candlekeep's lore could fearlessly pass the Obsidian Door. The party considered bringing Edvaldo upstairs, though Zalthir remained suspicious that the researcher might somehow be the real Manshoon. ## NPCs
### Manshoon (Simulacrum)
A magical duplicate of the ancient and powerful wizard Manshoon, created from ice and snow. The first duplicate fought the party with spells and legendary maneuvers, using *Mirror Image*, *Shield*, Ward Eater, and extraordinary movement. Ward Eater damaged and immobilized Thorin and Edvaldo while restoring twenty-five of the simulacrum's hit points. Zalthir finally destroyed it, causing it to revert to snow and melt instantly.

The party learned that a simulacrum is partially real, has only half the original creature's maximum vitality, cannot learn or regain expended spell slots, and turns back into snow at zero hit points. Manshoon himself was reportedly killed 170 years ago but has continued to reappear “like a bad penny.”

A second simulacrum in cheaper gray robes was found waiting patiently in the upper tower chamber. It had spent six hours surrounded by warded books it could not read and an Obsidian Door it could not open. This second duplicate proposed a bargain: Gyrgum opens the door; the party receives the prophetic gemstones; Manshoon enters and takes one book. Although he insisted the proposal was not a threat, his handling of the gemstones made the danger to the prophecies clear.

### Edvaldo
A man presenting himself as the Avowed researcher Edvaldo, who accompanied the party into the tower because his claimed research concerned Candlekeep lore. His commoner token appeared as a “Babushka lady,” causing some initial confusion about his identity.

Edvaldo survived damage that straightforward calculation suggested should have killed him, raising the party's suspicions. He had also behaved strangely earlier when he knew the tower was approximately five hundred feet high but could not provide a precise measurement.

When Zalthir attacked and grappled him, Edvaldo pleaded innocence and demonstrated knowledge of Candlekeep lore, speaking fluently about the Endless Chant and the Word Lattice. Daz's investigation found his account convincing, but the party remained suspicious. Edvaldo later asked to avoid further magical violence, then agreed to remain involved if he received second authorship on the eventual paper about the combat. He was bound and left in the anti-magic zone.

Asked what is consumed to make light, Edvaldo first answered “Books!” and then “Candles,” insisting the latter should be obvious in Candlekeep. The party concluded that he did not actually know how to open the Obsidian Door.

### Glabbagool
An intelligent gelatinous ooze who accompanies the party and projects the earnest, hero-worshipping energy of the main character from *Kung Fu Panda*. Glabbagool questioned why Zalthir had not simply dropped Manshoon into the magma, forcing the party to explain that immediately eliminating the wizard would not have been “sporty.”

Zalthir instructed Glabbagool to point out any forgotten instant-death traps in the future. Glabbagool was also highly impressed by the speed and thoroughness with which Zalthir bound Edvaldo.

### The Flying Iron Owlbear
A hovering winged iron guardian encountered before the party entered the tower. It previously questioned the adventurers to confirm their identities and later watched their fight with Manshoon.
