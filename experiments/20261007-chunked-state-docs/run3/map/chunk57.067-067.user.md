# Chapter 67 Ninety Seven Steps and a Fall

Date: 2026-08-17

## Scenes

### 067.01 The Aftermath at the High Tower

With Manshoon's simulacrum already through the shattered door of Candlekeep's inner sanctum, A'lai Aivenmore sat in the party's custody and watched in trembling horror as the truth settled over him like a shroud: Manshoon had never come for him. After eleven years of service, he had not even been worth the effort of a single spell.

#### With Manshoon's simulacrum already through the inner sanctum's door, the party deals with a broken A'lai Aivenmore and learns it is now racing Manshoon for what lies beneath Candlekeep.

- A'lai Aivenmore realizes he was merely a pawn in Manshoon's long-term plans, expressing despair at his own insignificance — Manshoon did not even bother to come for him.

- A'lai explains that for eleven years Manshoon's only contact with him came through an anonymous courier: “He sent a courier 11 years and the courier wear a glove. And I never saw the face. And I told myself that was discretion. It was not discretion. It was that I wasn't worth the face.”

- Gyrgum bluntly summarizes A'lai's predicament: “It's not even worth killing.” The party jokes that he was not worth the price of a bullet — or even a spell slot.

- Tadric the Watcher is identified among those present during the chaos in the High Tower.

- The breach creates a race: Manshoon is already pursuing whatever is hidden beneath Candlekeep, while the party must decipher A'lai's clues and intercept him before he obtains it.

### 067.02 The Prisoner's Bargain

“He's not looking for me,” A'lai admitted. For eleven years, Manshoon had sent a gloved, faceless courier, and A'lai had mistaken that anonymity for discretion. Now he understood: “It was that I wasn't worth the face.” Broken and desperate, A'lai offered the party everything he had left. He revealed the existence of a secret room beneath Candlekeep holding artifacts of extraordinary power, guarded by a cryptogram he had spent years quietly researching. He knew the answers to two of its riddles — “Fustilugs,” the base insult carved into the black marble knight in the Philosopher's Court, and “Bow,” the reply of the legendary halfling bard Batbayar's statue when applauded in the School of the Drama Library.

In exchange for this information, A'lai begged not for freedom but for a cell in Candlekeep's null magic prison, the one place he believed no wizard — not even Manshoon — could reach him. Gyrgum pressed him for everything he knew about Manshoon's purpose, and Daz asked who was funding the forces surrounding him. When asked about those forces, A'lai admitted he could not trace the payments, only the patience behind them, and concluded with quiet certainty that only one house in Menzoberranzan could afford to wait eleven years for nothing: House Baenre. Gyrgum appealed to the Watcher Tadric, invoking the party's record of service and their deputization by Kalan Strongbranch. He also reminded Tadric that he had founded an entirely new philosophy and jokingly negotiated a jointly authored monograph. Tadric agreed to the arrangement and marched a visibly relieved A'lai toward the prison.

#### The party interrogates A'lai Aivenmore, who trades secrets about a hidden vault and the forces pursuing Daz in exchange for protection in Candlekeep's null magic prison.

- A'lai Aivenmore, trembling and broken, requests to be placed in Candlekeep's null magic prison — the one place he believes he might survive what is coming.

- He asks what guarantee he has that the agreement will be honored. The party notes that he has little bargaining power, but Tadric's presence offers a way to formalize the arrangement.

- The party negotiates with A'lai, demanding everything he knows about Manshoon's true purpose and the forces surrounding Daz. Gyrgum emphasizes the urgency: “We need to defeat Manshoon or the entire tower is at risk. We're in a hurry here and we've got to make compromises that we otherwise might not want to make.”

- Gyrgum appeals to Tadric, invoking the party's deputization by Kalan Strongbranch and their record of service, including defeating Moziqodo and saving Tadric's life.

- Gyrgum adds, “Look, and I founded an entire new philosophy.” When Tadric suggests writing a monograph, Gyrgum promises, “Yes, yes. We can do a two-person paper. It seems like I've got enough prestige here that it'll likely be accepted. But you never know with the whole anonymous submission thing, but I've got a pretty good track record.”

- Tadric signs off on the bargain. A'lai is visibly relieved, looking like a man who has finally understood that he was never a kingpin, merely a pawn in someone else's plan.

- A'lai reveals that a secret room beneath Candlekeep holds artifacts of extraordinary power. Access is protected by questions whose answers feed a letter-substitution cryptogram; once inside the room, the nature of the puzzle should become obvious.

- A'lai provides the answers to two riddles: “Fustilugs” — the base insult of a Black Knight — and “Bow” — the reply of Batbayar when given a hand.

- A'lai explains that “Fustilugs” was magically inscribed centuries ago beneath an oversized black marble knight in the Philosopher's Court. Successive keepers of the relevant tome had ensured that the knight was never replaced.

- A'lai describes discovering “Bow” after applauding Batbayar's statue late one night. The statue came alive and bowed deeply from the waist in acknowledgment. He presents this discovery with considerable academic pride, claiming it demonstrates what one acquires “after a lifetime of serving the institution of Candlekeep.”

- Regarding Daz, A'lai says, “I could not trace the payments. I could only trace the patience.”

- He reasons that no one could safely sustain the operation for eleven years without demanding results unless they possessed extraordinary power and resources: “There is one house in Menzoberranzan that can afford to be patient. Baenre. It must be Baenre. No one else can afford to wait that long for nothing.”

- A'lai's conclusion is a deduction, not proof that he successfully traced the money to House Baenre.

- Tadric takes A'lai Aivenmore into custody and marches him off toward the null magic prison.

### 067.03 Deciphering the Vault Riddles

The party regrouped in the investigator's office, conveniently near the prison, where the cryptogram questions — transcribed in full from A'lai Aivenmore's photographic memory — were spread across the floor for every sharp mind in the room to examine. Sylvira Savikas arrived shortly after, grateful for the party's efforts and eager to help Candlekeep in its moment of crisis. Unknown to her, the party had killed her son Moziqodo; when she thanked Thorin for saving Tadric, he carefully replied, “I do what I can for those that I can.”

Sylvira provided the true name of Miirym, the Sentinel Wyrm and dragon guardian of Candlekeep, as the answer to the first riddle. She explained that she knew Miirym's true name from a discussion about demonic possession and the power conferred by knowing such names. Sylvira also identified the Eastern Light of Mystra's Mantle as an obscure star recorded in the charts of the Orrery of the Astronomicon — though she noted with quiet unease that three people who did not work there had been inside the Orrery when she passed it.

Working together, the party decoded the letter-substitution cipher of the cryptogram, with Thorin leading the effort through an external cryptogram aid while Gyrgum independently arrived at almost the same solution. The full text revealed a sequence of instructions: feed the quill of Alaundo the Seer, tread as many steps as he lived in years, utter the original prophecy to unseen ears, sprinkle dust of Mechanus on dormant gears, one last guardian of knowledge remains to verify the chosen Reader's claims, and that those well-versed in Candlekeep's lore may fearlessly pass the obsidian door. The assembled academics were stunned that dwarves had solved the puzzle, adding fresh fuel to Candlekeep's scholarly debates.

Kalan Strongbranch arrived huffing and puffing moments later, eager to contribute a clue he had solved — only to find the party had already cracked the entire cipher. After a moment of stunned silence, he recovered with, “Yes, well, I'm relieved to see that.” Determined to contribute something, Kalan explained that the first instruction referred to the statue of Alaundo in Founders Court.

- Sylvira initially laughs at the list of questions and calls them almost too easy, reflecting her deep familiarity with Candlekeep's secrets.

- Sylvira provides the true name of Miirym, the Sentinel Wyrm — **Vydykyq** — as the answer to the first riddle.

- She explains that she learned Miirym's true name during a conversation about demonic possession and the importance of true names, which can grant power over demonic beings.

- Sylvira identifies the “Eastern Light of Mystra's Mantle” — **Limniz** — as an obscure star that can be found on the star charts in the Orrery of the Astronomicon.

- Sylvira mentions that when she passed the Astronomicon, she noticed three people inside who did not work there — potentially three Avowed conducting research, but also a possible warning of enemy activity.

- The party works together to decode the letter-substitution cipher. Thorin enters the symbols into a cryptogram-solving site to keep track of substitutions, while Gyrgum independently reaches nearly the same solution.

- The assembled scholars are astonished by Thorin's role in the solution. Following another dwarf's success in persuading Protanther, Thorin's deciphering of an ancient academic puzzle threatens to become part of an enduring scholarly debate.

- Gyrgum remarks that the hardest part was typing the original cipher while still listening to everyone else.

- Kalan Strongbranch arrives late and out of breath, eager to contribute the answer to another clue, only to hear, “Sorry, we already solved it.”

- After a stunned silence, Kalan recovers: “Yes, well, I'm relieved to see that. I'm relieved to see that.”

- Determined to contribute, Kalan explains that his familiarity with Candlekeep's points of interest allows him to interpret the first instruction. The party gives him the acknowledgment he is clearly seeking.

- Kalan identifies the House of Alaundo in Founders Court as the next destination and describes the brass statue, quill, ink pot, and dais found inside.

### 067.04 The Statue of Alaundo

The party made their way to the House of Alaundo, where a great brass statue of the ancient seer stood upon a dais, bearing a quill and an empty ink pot. The quill's tip had been worn smooth by scholars touching it in hopes of producing well-reviewed academic work. After a brief debate over whether ink, blood, or even the poisoned ink previously encountered was the appropriate offering, the party chose fresh ink borrowed from the assembled scholars. Gyrgum made certain that the scholar who supplied it received proper credit and that everyone knew the correct spelling of his name.

When the ink was poured into the pot, the statue slid forward with the grinding of stone, revealing a long, unlit staircase descending into darkness. Thorin immediately observed that the statue had stood there for ages and yet apparently no one had ever tried putting ink in its ink pot. The assembled scholars recoiled at the very idea of defacing Alaundo's monument, neatly explaining why centuries of reverence had concealed the obvious solution.

To know how many steps to count, the party needed to determine how long Alaundo had lived. A biography in the Hall of Momentous Deeds could have supplied the answer, but a scholar proposed the simpler course of consulting his grave. The party traveled to the grove where Candlekeep's keepers of tomes were buried and learned that Alaundo had died at the age of ninety-seven. The original prophecy posed no further obstacle: every Candlekeep scholar knew it by heart, and the many friends the party had made by helping people ensured their cooperation.

#### The party locates the brass statue of Alaundo the Seer in Founders Court and triggers a hidden mechanism to reveal a secret passage beneath it.

- The party gathers at the House of Alaundo in Founders Court. Its ground floor stretches to the top of the building and is overlooked by a gallery forming a mezzanine.

- A large brass statue of Alaundo stands upon a dais, bearing a quill and an empty ink pot.

- According to a common Candlekeep superstition, touching the statue's quill ensures that one will write a well-critiqued academic work. The brass at the tip is worn smooth by generations of scholars.

- The party interprets “feed the quill” as an instruction to fill the empty ink pot, though they briefly debate whether the appropriate substance is ordinary ink, blood, or the poisoned ink sample taken during an earlier investigation.

- Thorin suggests blood. Gyrgum agrees it is possible but elects to begin with ink: “I'm not actually evil, so I'm going to start with ink.”

- Ink is easy to obtain from the crowd of Candlekeep scholars, all of whom carry their own supplies. Gyrgum chooses the freshest available ink.

- Upon pouring ink into the statue's ink pot, the party hears grinding stone. The statue slides forward on its dais, revealing a long set of stairs leading down into an unlit passage.

- Thorin asks how long the statue has stood there and points out that no one in all that time ever thought to place ink in its empty pot.

- The watching scholars recoil in horror at the suggestion, regarding such an act as defacement. Their reverence for Alaundo appears to be the reason the mechanism remained undiscovered.

- The GM acknowledges Thorin's observation as a “good plot hole discovery.”

- The party realizes they need to know the exact age at which Alaundo died to determine how many steps to descend. His age is not inscribed on the statue, and the plaque is judged deficient.

- A copy of *Alaundo of the Wonders* in the Hall of Momentous Deeds could provide the answer, but a scholar suggests the more direct option of checking Alaundo's grave in the grove where Candlekeep's keepers of tomes are buried.

- A growing retinue of scholars follows the party, scribbling notes and debating discoveries. The events — especially Thorin's solution of the cipher — are already expected to be studied for years.

- Gyrgum makes a short speech to ensure that at least some account of the discovery is recorded correctly.

### 067.05 The Grave of Alaundo and the House of Mechanus

One final requirement remained: dust of Mechanus, to be sprinkled upon the dormant gears mentioned in the decoded instructions. After considering whether the party might need to travel to another plane or kill an extraplanar creature, they learned that Candlekeep had its own House of Mechanus. The large rectangular building, topped with a blue slate roof and clock tower, housed a library devoted to construction, engineering, and technology. There, the gnome librarian Spanner directed thirteen modrons — strange, box-like mechanical creatures from Mechanus — through perfectly ordered routines.

Spanner explained that dust from Mechanus was the most perfect lubricant in existence. One method of obtaining it would be to kill one of his modrons, which was absolutely unacceptable. The safer alternative was to borrow specialized tools the modrons had created. After the party convinced Spanner they needed the tools only temporarily, he agreed on the condition that Zalthir and Glabbagool later submit to a study of their mechanical symmetry. Zalthir consulted Glabbagool and accepted, provided Spanner offered “constructive feedback, such that we may better symbiotically serve our Zen cause.” The interview was deferred until after the expedition. Spanner then addressed the modrons in a mechanistic language no one else could understand, and they deliberated through an eerie, synchronized dance of moving arms and bodies.

#### The party visits the keepers' grove to learn Alaundo's age, then negotiates with the gnome librarian Spanner and his modrons for the Dust of Mechanus.

- The party travels to the grove where the keepers of tomes are buried and locates Alaundo's grave, discovering he died at the age of 97.

- Thorin confirms the implication: the party must descend ninety-seven steps before reciting the prophecy.

- Before anyone starts down, Thorin sensibly asks whether they know the original prophecy, noting that otherwise they will have to climb all ninety-seven steps back up.

- Scholars from Candlekeep provide the party with the text of the original prophecy required for the next step of the riddle. Every Candlekeep scholar knows it by heart, and the goodwill the party has accumulated makes obtaining it easy.

- Gyrgum makes certain that everyone knows which scholar supplied the ink and how to spell that person's name, ensuring proper credit and future citations.

- Asked who knows Candlekeep's own history best, the party recruits a little-known scholar familiar with the area. The scholar is thrilled by the opportunity to accompany them and asks for time to collect a few personal items.

- The party still requires Dust of Mechanus. They consider whether it might mean literal dust from the plane, the remains of a slain extraplanar creature, or merely a specialized dry lubricant for old gears.

- Thorin asks Glabbagool whether it has any Dust of Mechanus stored away. Glabbagool replies that it does not.

- Zalthir suggests that someone could plane shift the party to Mechanus so they could kill something and return. Once the party learns that thirteen modrons already live in Candlekeep, Thorin immediately objects to killing any of them because they are librarians.

- Gyrgum suggests simply asking the modrons whether they have advice or a bag of dust to spare. Thorin adds, “I mean, we don't kill librarians. They're our friends,” while acknowledging that librarians who attacked the party first may have received different treatment.

- The party arrives at the House of Mechanus, a rectangular building with a blue slate roof and a clock tower, dedicated to engineering, construction, and technology.

- They meet Spanner, the gnome librarian, and thirteen modrons — box-like mechanical creatures from the plane of Mechanus — working in precise, tightly ordered patterns.

- Spanner confirms that dust from the plane of Mechanus is “the most perfect of all lubricants.”

- Gyrgum asks how they might obtain some and prematurely guesses that one method is “asking politely.” Spanner is momentarily stunned at the interruption before continuing his carefully structured explanation.

- Spanner explains that the only direct way to obtain the dust would be to kill one of his modrons, which is absolutely unacceptable.

- His second proposal is to lend the party unique tools created by the modrons. Because the tools are valuable, he initially requests collateral.

- Thorin points out that the party does not need to keep the tools, only borrow them. This practical observation helps persuade Spanner.

- Spanner agrees to lend the tools on the condition that Zalthir and Glabbagool submit to a study of their “perfect symmetry” and mechanical construction.

- Zalthir consults Glabbagool before agreeing: “We're game as long as you give us constructive feedback, such that we may better symbiotically serve our Zen cause.”

- Spanner agrees but defers the complicated interview until after the party returns from the hidden passage.

- Spanner speaks to the modrons in a mechanistic language the party cannot understand and can barely imagine a gnome producing. The modrons respond by moving their arms and bodies through an eerie, synchronized mechanical dance, apparently deliberating among themselves.

### 067.06 The Descent into the Vault

Armed with the modron tools, the original prophecy, Alaundo's age, and the assistance of a local-history scholar, the party descended the hidden staircase beneath the statue. Daz positioned himself farther up the stairs to preserve a clear line of sight for ranged magic, while the others gathered around the ninety-seventh step. Zalthir, still injured from the previous fighting, asked for healing, and Gyrgum restored forty-four hit points — considerably more than the twenty-seven Zalthir needed.

Then a ten-foot section of the staircase, spanning the area around the designated step, abruptly hinged downward into a vast circular well. Zalthir used his strength and wings to help hold companions aloft while Daz cast **Telekinesis** in an attempt to arrest the collapsing stairs. Gyrgum and Zalthir managed to catch themselves and scramble clear. Thorin was less fortunate: he lost his footing and plummeted into the dark well below. His fate remained uncertain when the session ended.

#### The party descends the hidden staircase beneath Alaundo's statue, but a section of the stairs suddenly collapses, sending Thorin plummeting into a vast circular well.

- The party descends the hidden staircase, counting toward the ninety-seventh step to match the years Alaundo the Seer lived.

- The GM repeatedly asks each character for an exact position, immediately making the players suspicious. Daz observes, “I think anytime you ask questions like this, it's followed by roll for initiative.”

- Gyrgum plans to stand on the ninety-seventh step and recite the prophecy. Zalthir joins him there, relying on his wings to hover if necessary.

- Daz takes a position higher up the stairs to maintain a clear line of sight for ranged attacks and to guard against an assault from behind.

- Thorin chooses to remain near the front, reasoning that if anything happens, he is best equipped to withstand it.

- At Zalthir's request, Gyrgum heals him before the descent. Zalthir needs twenty-seven hit points, but Gyrgum restores forty-four.

- As the party reaches the designated area, a ten-foot section of the stone staircase suddenly hinges open and drops into a vast circular well below. The collapsing portion catches those positioned between roughly the ninety-third and one-hundred-second steps.

- Zalthir tries to grab his companions and spread his wings. His strength helps hold Gyrgum and the others long enough for them to react.

- Daz, positioned safely above the collapsing section, casts **Telekinesis** in an attempt to seize the falling stairs, arrest their descent, and pull them back into place.

- Gyrgum and Zalthir manage to hold on and scramble to safety.

- Thorin loses his footing and begins plummeting into the darkness of the well. Even after attempting to recover, he remains in free fall.

- Everyone except Thorin manages to hold onto the stairs and scramble clear.

- Daz's **Telekinesis** cannot stop Thorin's descent.

- Thorin continues to fall as the session ends on a cliffhanger.

## Locations

### High Tower

The site of the brutal battle that ended Chapter 65, and the location where Manshoon's simulacrum breached Candlekeep's inner sanctum using a wall of force to shatter the magically protected door. The party began this session just outside the breached chamber with A'lai in custody and Tadric nearby.

### Null Magic Prison

A specialized containment area within Candlekeep designed to negate all magical abilities. A'lai Aivenmore desperately requests to be placed here, believing it is the only place he can survive Manshoon's reach. He values confinement there more than freedom because neither he nor an attacking wizard should be able to cast within it.

### Vault of Secrets

A hidden chamber beneath Candlekeep, unknown to most scholars, containing artifacts of extraordinary power and protected by a cryptogram and a series of riddles. Manshoon seeks something within it, but A'lai deliberately withheld the truth about its contents from him.

### Investigator's Office

A room near the prison where the party and Sylvira Savikas gather to analyze the transcribed cryptogram and solve the riddles. Its open floor provides space to spread out the notes and bring together Candlekeep's best minds. Kalan Strongbranch arrives only after the cipher has been solved.

### Philosopher's Court

An area within Candlekeep containing oversized black marble chess pieces that see regular use. The base of the black knight piece has the word “Fustilugs” magically etched into it — one of the answers to the vault's riddles. Successive keepers of the relevant tome ensured that this knight was never replaced.

### School of the Drama Library

A library within Candlekeep featuring a statue of the legendary halfling bard Batbayar. The statue comes to life and bows deeply when applauded, a response A'lai discovered late one night after feeling moved to congratulate the bard.

### Orrery of the Astronomicon

A chamber within Candlekeep containing star charts, including the obscure constellation called Mystra's Mantle and its easternmost star, Limniz. Sylvira noticed three suspicious individuals inside who did not appear to work there.

### House of Alaundo

A building in Founders Court whose ground floor stretches to the top with a gallery mezzanine. It features a large brass statue of Alaundo the Seer on a dais, which conceals a hidden staircase triggered by filling the statue's empty ink pot. Generations of scholars touched its quill for academic luck without discovering the mechanism.

### Founders Court

A central area in Candlekeep where the House of Alaundo is located, also near the House of Mechanus and the Immortal Chambers.

### The Grove

A burial ground within Candlekeep where the keepers of tomes are laid to rest. The party visits Alaundo's grave there and learns that he died at the age of ninety-seven.

### House of Mechanus

A large rectangular building with a blue slate roof and a clock tower, situated between the Immortal Chambers and Founders Court. It serves as a library dedicated to construction, engineering, and technology and is home to Spanner the gnome librarian and thirteen modrons. Everything within operates according to precise, tightly organized routines.

### The Hidden Staircase

A long, unlit passage revealed beneath the statue of Alaundo the Seer. Around the ninety-seventh step, a ten-foot section hinges downward into a vast circular well. Daz remains above the affected section, but Thorin falls when the trap opens.

## NPCs

### A'lai Aivenmore

A high-ranking scholar who turned traitor, only to realize he was a disposable pawn for Manshoon. For eleven years, Manshoon communicated through a gloved, faceless courier; A'lai mistook the anonymity for discretion but now understands that he was not important enough to be shown a face.

Devastated by this revelation, A'lai trades secrets about the Vault of Secrets and his deduction about which drow house has been funding the operations around Daz in exchange for placement in Candlekeep's null magic prison. He possesses a photographic memory, takes intense pride in his academic discoveries, and cannot resist explaining how he solved each clue even while being escorted to prison.

### Manshoon (Simulacrum)

A legendary and immensely powerful wizard. What reached Candlekeep is his **simulacrum**, arriving alone and visibly weakened after expending enormous magical resources. It conjures a wall of force and shatters the magically protected door to breach the inner sanctum, seeking something of extraordinary value. Its complete disregard for A'lai exposes the traitorous scholar's true insignificance.

### Tadric

A Watcher and lieutenant to Kalan Strongbranch who is present during the aftermath of the High Tower battle. He recognizes the party's deputized authority and their history of aiding Candlekeep. After Gyrgum argues for the necessity of A'lai's bargain — and agrees in jest to coauthor a monograph — Tadric accepts responsibility for delivering A'lai to the null magic prison.

### Glabbagool

An intelligent grey ooze serving as a companion to the party. It stores the High Tower key inside itself for safekeeping and seems proud of the responsibility. At the House of Mechanus, Glabbagool agrees alongside Zalthir to be studied by Spanner and the modrons in exchange for the tools needed to address the dormant gears.

### Kalan Strongbranch

Candlekeep's Gatewarden, who has deputized the party. He arrives late and out of breath at the investigator's office hoping to contribute a clue, only to find the cipher already solved. After recovering from the disappointment, he uses his familiarity with Candlekeep's grounds to identify the statue of Alaundo as the destination described by the first instruction.

### Sylvira Savikas

A powerful and knowledgeable scholar at Candlekeep who arrives at the investigator's office and assists the party in solving the vault's riddles. She provides the true name of Miirym the Sentinel Wyrm and identifies the Eastern Light of Mystra's Mantle, also warning of suspicious figures in the Orrery of the Astronomicon.

Sylvira is weakened by illness but believes it is her responsibility to help Candlekeep during the crisis. She is also Moziqodo's mother and does not yet know that the party killed her son. She warmly thanks Thorin for saving Tadric, prompting a carefully guarded response.

### Batbayar

A legendary halfling bard whose statue dominates the entrance hall of the School of the Drama Library. The statue comes to life and bows deeply when applauded — the answer to one of the vault's riddles.

### Miirym

The Sentinel Wyrm of Candlekeep, described as a dragon who dwells within the castle. Her true name, **Vydykyq**, is the answer to the first riddle guarding the Vault of Secrets. Sylvira learned it during a conversation with Miirym about demonic possession and the importance of true names.

### Spanner

A gnome librarian who oversees the House of Mechanus and directs thirteen modrons in their mechanical tasks. He confirms that Dust of Mechanus is the most perfect lubricant in existence but refuses to contemplate killing one of his modrons to obtain it. Instead, he lends the party specialized tools in exchange for permission to study Zalthir and Glabbagool's mechanical symmetry after the expedition.

Spanner is methodical and inclined to lengthy explanations. Interrupting one of those explanations with the suggestion of “asking politely” leaves him momentarily stunned.

### Modrons

Thirteen box-like mechanical creatures from the plane of Mechanus who work under Spanner in the House of Mechanus. Some resemble single boxes with limbs, while others appear like stacked geometric forms. They communicate in a strange mechanistic language and perform a synchronized, mechanized dance while deliberating among themselves.

## Items

### High Tower Key

One of two keys required to access the inner sanctum of Candlekeep. Stripped from A'lai Aivenmore after his capture, it was quietly passed to Zalthir and then stored inside Glabbagool for safekeeping. The party takes care not to bring the two keys together.

### Vault Cryptogram

A letter-substitution cipher containing the instructions needed to access the Vault of Secrets beneath Candlekeep. Two answers were supplied by A'lai Aivenmore and two by Sylvira Savikas. Thorin tracked the substitutions using an online-style cryptogram aid while Gyrgum independently verified much of the solution. Kalan Strongbranch arrived only after it was solved.

Its decoded instructions direct the Reader to feed Alaundo's quill, descend the number of steps matching his lifespan, recite his original prophecy, attend to dormant gears with Dust of Mechanus, satisfy a final guardian of knowledge, and pass through an obsidian door.

### Black Marble Knight

An oversized black marble chess piece in the Philosopher's Court. The word “Fustilugs” — the base insult of a Black Knight — is magically etched into its base, serving as one of the answers to the vault's riddles. Candlekeep's scholars deliberately preserved the original piece instead of replacing it.

### Statue of Alaundo

A large brass statue of the famous seer Alaundo, bearing a quill and an empty ink pot, mounted on a large dais in the House of Alaundo. Scholars traditionally touch the well-worn tip of its quill for luck with academic criticism. When ink is poured into the ink pot, the statue slides forward to reveal a hidden staircase leading toward the Vault of Secrets.

### Dust of Mechanus

A legendary and perfect lubricant sourced from the plane of Mechanus, required by the vault's riddle to be sprinkled on dormant gears. One theoretical source would be the remains of a slain modron, but Spanner considers that absolutely unacceptable. The party never obtains any: Spanner lends them a modron-made tool instead, and the GM rules the tool an alternative to the dust.

### Modron Tools

Specialized mechanical instruments created by the modrons of the House of Mechanus, lent to the party by Spanner as an alternative to obtaining Dust of Mechanus by killing a modron. Their loan is conditional upon Zalthir and Glabbagool returning for a detailed study of their symmetry and construction.

### Potion of Flying

A magical draught belonging to **Zalthir**, concealed inside Glabbagool. Raised as one of Zalthir's options during the positioning discussion before the descent into the hidden staircase, though Zalthir's own wings also allow him to hover.
