### 068.01 The Long Descent

Zalthir immediately dove after Thorin, plunging into the darkness at tremendous speed to catch up with his falling companion. Because a falling creature dropped roughly five hundred feet in a round, Zalthir realized that trying to outfly Thorin would not work and simply plummeted after him. Some five hundred feet below, Zalthir finally reached Thorin and caught him, the two clinging together in the dark while Glabbagool, who had accompanied the fall with characteristic calm, wondered philosophically aloud whether there was perhaps too much hugging involved. Thorin reassured the party above that he was “still kicking,” and Zalthir decided that, since they were already halfway down, they might as well scout the bottom.

After another long drop, Thorin and Zalthir entered a feather-fall zone and shouted for their companions to follow. The rest of the party leaped after them one by one. Gyrgum needed a prayer and a little encouragement before taking the plunge, while Daz observed that his attempt to hold the staircase together and save everyone had not worked and decided he did not want to be separated from the party. The scholar Edvaldo Sedanur came as well, insisting that this unknown region beneath Candlekeep demanded “a very long monograph.” The entire party landed safely some thousand feet beneath the surface of Candlekeep.

#### Thorin plummets down a massive shaft after the staircase trap triggers, leading to a rescue by Zalthir.

- Zalthir immediately dives after Thorin. When questioned about his flight, Zalthir confirms that his fifth-level dragonborn ability allows him to fly; he also has a potion of flying as a fallback.

- The established party joke is that Zalthir’s characters can always fly: “Canonically, Zalthir’s characters fly. And if they can’t, they immediately find an artifact that allows them to fly.”

- Because Thorin falls roughly five hundred feet in a six-second round, Zalthir realizes that flapping downward will not let him overtake Thorin. He instead drops freely alongside him before closing the remaining distance.

- Zalthir successfully catches Thorin mid-air about five hundred feet down. The rescue leaves Thorin holding tightly to him while Glabbagool shares the embrace.

- Glabbagool accompanies the falling pair with characteristic delight and calm, initially screaming, “Wee!” and later advising, “One should always be Zen.”

- Dawnbringer is considerably less enthusiastic, crying, “Not this again!”

- Glabbagool wonders, “Is this too much hugging?” Zalthir answers, “There are worse things, Glabbagool.”

- When asked whether they are all right, Zalthir answers, “So far,” while Thorin shouts, “I’m still kicking.”

- Although Zalthir could return Thorin to the stairs, they decide to continue downward. Zalthir reasons, “I mean, we’re already halfway down. We could just go scout a bit.”

- After another substantial drop, Thorin and Zalthir enter a feather-fall zone. Learning that the bottom is safe, they shout for the others to jump.

- Gyrgum is reluctant to step voluntarily into the shaft: “Oh, God, that’s going to be hard for me.” He says a quick prayer and trusts that the feather-fall effect will work for him as well.

- Daz follows because he does not want to be separated from the party, noting, “My whole trying to hold the thing up and save us all didn’t work.”

- The party jokes about the familiar warning against following friends over a precipice. When Gyrgum asks, “Look, if all your friends jumped off the stairs, would you?” the immediate answer is, “Absolutely.”

- Edvaldo Sedanur accompanies them. Since the region is unknown even to Candlekeep’s historical scholar, he announces, “I intend to write a very long monograph.”

- The entire party safely lands in a single tunnel approximately one thousand feet beneath the surface.

### 068.02 The Lava Chamber and the Obsidian Tower

The tunnel led them into a massive cylindrical cavern, two hundred feet high and a hundred feet across, thick with acrid, sweltering air. A steaming lake of molten lava churned a hundred feet below the ledge where the party emerged. Rising from the lava on a thin pedestal of glassy black obsidian stood a three-story stone tower, similar in style to some of the older spires in the great library. Edvaldo immediately declared it a classic example of the style invented by a Candlekeep Avowed architect and began explaining how many such examples had been destroyed by “needless rehabilitation and expansion” within the crowded library.

The tower’s base was ringed by a narrow walkway, and a stone bridge on its far side led toward a second tunnel. No bridge initially connected the party’s ledge to the tower. Mechanical components and cogs were embedded in the stone before them, and Daz quickly determined that they formed a system for extending such a bridge. He also noticed a warning that the nearby airspace was protected by an anti-magic field, preventing the party from simply flying across. Using the tools borrowed from Spanner, the party activated the mechanism, and the bridge ground outward across the molten lake with a deep, resonant groan.

#### The party enters a massive cylindrical cavern containing a lake of molten lava and a mysterious stone tower, then navigates their way across to it.

- The landing tunnel leads straight into a cylindrical cavern two hundred feet high and one hundred feet across.

- The party emerges on a ledge halfway up the chamber, roughly one hundred feet above a steaming lake of molten lava.

- The cavern’s air is “thick, acrid, and sweltering hot.”

- A three-story stone tower stands in the center of the chamber, mounted on a thin pedestal of glassy black obsidian rising from the lava.

- The tower resembles some of Candlekeep’s oldest spires. Edvaldo identifies it as a classic surviving example of a style created by an Avowed architect.

- Edvaldo explains that many other examples were destroyed by “needless rehabilitation and expansion of towers” as Candlekeep struggled with limited space. He declares that seeing the intact tower was, by itself, worth the dangerous journey.

- The base of the tower is surrounded by a narrow walkway. On the far side, a flat stone bridge connects the tower to a second tunnel opposite the party’s entrance.

- The party’s own ledge extends only five feet into the cavern and initially has no visible connection to the tower.

- Several cogs and mechanical components are embedded in the stone ledge. Gyrgum suggests examining them before anyone tries to jump across the lava.

- Daz investigates the mechanism. With his exceptionally high passive Investigation, he recognizes it as a system designed to extend a bridge.

- Daz summarizes the discovery: “So if we use the tool, we can basically expand the bridge.”

- Daz also notices a warning indicating an anti-magic field in the surrounding airspace. The GM later clarifies that the field covers only the first ten feet near the bridge rather than the entire thirty-foot gap.

- The anti-magic field is distinct from Candlekeep’s larger ward, which protects against teleportation and suppresses fire magic. Candlekeep’s own teleportation ward had been damaged by Manshoon earlier.

- Because of the anti-magic field, flying across by magical means would not have worked reliably. The mechanism and its tools are therefore necessary.

- Using the specialized tools obtained from the House of Mechanus, the party activates the machinery and extends the bridge across the lava.

- The bridge grinds outward with a noise supplied by what the GM calls “the Mark I vocal cord.”

- The party crosses into the tower. Its unadorned stone walls hold sconces lit with *Continual Flame*, and four mounted crossbows guard the interior.

### 068.03 Riddles of the Iron Guardian

Inside the tower’s first chamber, a tiny iron statue of a winged owlbear sat atop an eight-foot stone pedestal, with a staircase running behind it to the next floor. The moment the party entered, its large eyelids opened to reveal white glowing eyes, its wings unfolded, and it flew up to hover twelve feet above the ground. It declared, “Halt, seekers,” and demanded that they prove themselves worthy keepers of secrets by answering three questions. It also admitted that it had been waiting a very long time to tear failed petitioners to shreds.

Daz correctly identified the Hearth as the only refuge for flames within Candlekeep. Gyrgum named the holy triumvirate that patroned the great library—Oghma, Deneir, and Milil—while barely resisting the temptation to explain their history at length. For the final question, the guardian demanded the height of the High Tower of Exaltation above the sea. Edvaldo explained that every Avowed had once been expected to memorize the answer, although the practice had fallen out of fashion, and supplied that it was over five hundred feet. Together Thorin and Daz determined the required answer. The iron owlbear permitted them to pass. Later, after meeting the dragon, the party noticed a previous owlbear statue lying damaged on the floor—clear evidence that Manshoon had not been so patient.

#### An iron owlbear statue comes to life and challenges the party with three riddles to prove their worth.

- The first room’s only notable feature is a tiny iron statue resembling a winged owlbear, positioned atop an eight-foot stone pedestal. A staircase runs behind the pedestal to the tower’s next floor.

- As soon as the party enters, the statue’s large eyelids open to reveal glowing white eyes. Its wings unfold, and it rises to hover twelve feet above the floor.

- The guardian threatens to tear the party to shreds if they fail. It admits, “I’ve been waiting a long time to do this. This is something I have trained for in my head.”

- Its first question is, “Where is the only refuge for flames in Candlekeep?”

- Daz remembers that the Hearth is Candlekeep’s only permitted refuge for ordinary flame—and the only place in the keep where he had not been cold.

- At the first riddle Edvaldo says he knew the answer, then steps away rather than give it: “If you’re going to answer the questions, I’m going to just… over here.”

- When the guardian begins introducing its third and hardest question, Thorin quips that it sounds more like a comment than a question. The guardian apologizes for accidentally reading from the answer key.

- The party turns to Edvaldo Sedanur. He explains that every member of the Avowed was once expected to know this fact, but memorizing such dry details has fallen out of fashion.

- Edvaldo can narrow the answer only to “over 500 feet,” reducing the difficulty of the party’s deduction rather than directly solving it.

- Thorin and Daz successfully determine the tower’s height above sea level, satisfying the final challenge.

- The guardian declares, “You have answered all three questions correctly. You may pass.”

- Thorin briefly wonders whether they could have defeated the guardian anyway. The party decides to save “doing something stupid for later,” possibly attacking it on the way back if circumstances demand.

- When asked what happens after its duty is completed, the guardian does not get the chance to answer before a cough sounds from the lava chamber.

### 068.04 The Spectral Silver Dragon's Trial

Before the party could ascend further, they heard a cough from the lava chamber. Outside floated a spectral silver dragon, her ethereal form so insubstantial that molten rock flowed straight through her. She was grouchy and imperious and wasted no time revealing that Manshoon had entered her keep and was still inside. Daz concluded aloud that the protections had fallen when he entered.

The dragon demanded that the party identify which of them was the Reader and which were witnesses. Gyrgum identified himself as the Reader and proved his knowledge of Candlekeep by describing its books and the lore the party had recently investigated, satisfying riddle line 5: the verification of the chosen Reader’s claims. His devotion to Bahamut impressed the dragon besides. When she asked where he stood “on the question of the age,” Gyrgum answered, “I founded a new school that holds that there are certain fixed points among which we may make our own paths.” She replied, “Why, wisdom, wisdom truly flows from Bahamut into you, cleric of Bahamut.”

The dragon offered the party a boon. The possibilities discussed included holding the door so that nothing could leave—which would also keep Manshoon from teleporting away—revealing what he had taken, determining whether everyone present was who they claimed to be, or widening the bridge’s anti-magic field. The party briefly asked whether she could fight beside them, but she answered, “I was bound to be witness, not weapon. If I could have stopped him, I would have stopped him at dawn.” They ultimately asked her to extend the anti-magic field surrounding the bridge to thirty feet for ten minutes and hold that effect in reserve until called upon. The dragon approved their choice with a measured, repeated “Well chosen.”

### 068.05 The Vault of Dangerous Secrets and the Confrontation with Manshoon

The party ascended to the vault, a plain circular room lit with continual flame, its windows sealed shut and its eastern wall lined with a single bookcase. A desk and chair occupied the center. A note written in archaic Common explained that the vault contained Candlekeep’s most dangerous secrets, instructed its keepers to consult whatever might benefit civilization but leave the tomes on the shelves, and warned that the chamber above held the means to destroy the collection if it were ever in danger of falling into foul hands.

Around one hundred of the most powerful and dangerous magical tomes imaginable lined the shelves. Seated calmly at the desk, reading one without initially looking up, was Manshoon—in truth one of his simulacra, though the party has not been told. He greeted the party with cool composure: “By all means, browse. The trick is leaving with what you came for. Did you come for the book or did you come for me?” Gyrgum answered that they had come for him and had not known about the books; when he added that the books were a bonus, Manshoon sighed and prepared for battle.

Gyrgum, with complete earnestness, proposed that it would be a shame to damage any of the priceless books in the coming fight and suggested moving their inevitable duel to a lower chamber. Manshoon paused, considered the point, and agreed, noting that clearly one of them would ultimately leave with the book they wanted. Gyrgum suggested they leave all the books in place until the party returned after killing him; Manshoon politely acknowledged the alternative possibility that he might kill them instead. He sent the party down the stairs ahead of him and followed last, weaving a protective enchantment around himself on the way so that shimmering mirror images surrounded his form.

The party took up positions near the bridge and its anti-magic zone. Manshoon stood menacingly over Daz and declared that they had found a place for their magical duel. The owlbear guardian remained neutral, its assigned test already completed, though it appeared ready to provide color commentary.

Combat erupted with Daz launching a devastating psychic assault at Manshoon, weaving a terrifying mental attack designed to manifest the wizard’s deepest fears. Manshoon weighed burning a *Counterspell* and chose not to, then made the save through sheer force of will—an 8 on the die carried to 17 by his Wisdom. Daz called the half damage, the GM briefly took it back, and the ruling that stands is 13 psychic damage: the made save halves the 26 and ends the spell.

Daz then moved onto the bridge, within the first ten feet of the active anti-magic band. The party’s broader strategy was already taking shape: strike Manshoon, retreat into the anti-magic zone where his formidable spellcasting would be rendered useless, and use mundane ranged attacks to punish him if he followed. With Thorin preparing to act and the battle only just beginning, the fate of Candlekeep’s most dangerous secrets—and the legendary wizard who had come to claim them—hung in the balance.

## Locations

### The Great Shaft

A massive vertical drop beneath the statue of Alaundo, descending roughly one thousand feet into the depths beneath Candlekeep, with a feather-fall zone near the bottom. A creature falling freely can descend roughly five hundred feet in a combat round, forcing Zalthir to plummet after Thorin rather than attempt to overtake him through ordinary flight. A single tunnel waits at the bottom.

### The Lava Cavern

A sweltering cylindrical chamber two hundred feet high and one hundred feet across. The party enters on a narrow ledge halfway up the cavern, approximately one hundred feet above a steaming lake of molten lava. The air is thick, acrid, and oppressively hot. The central Obsidian Tower rises from the lake, while a second tunnel lies beyond it. Miirym, the spectral silver dragon guardian, manifests here with lava flowing harmlessly through her incorporeal body.

### The Obsidian Tower

A three-story stone structure standing on a thin pedestal of glassy black obsidian rising from the lava lake. Its style resembles Candlekeep’s oldest spires and is sufficiently rare that Edvaldo regards the intact structure as worth the entire journey. It contains a guardian chamber, a vault of dangerous tomes, and an upper chamber holding the means to destroy the collection. Its stone walls are largely unadorned, but its interior is lit by *Continual Flame* sconces and guarded by four mounted crossbows.

### The Vault of Dangerous Secrets

A plain circular room on an upper floor of the Obsidian Tower, lit with *Continual Flame* and featuring sealed windows, a desk and chair, and a bookcase mounted against its eastern wall. Approximately one hundred of Candlekeep’s most hazardous and powerful magical tomes are stored here. An archaic note permits consultation of knowledge that may benefit civilization but forbids removing the books. It warns that the means to destroy the collection are stored in the chamber above if the vault is threatened by foul hands.

### The Obsidian Bridge

A stone walkway extended by a mechanical system embedded in the party’s entrance ledge and activated using the tools from the House of Mechanus. It leads across the lava to the tower and its outer walkway. The first ten feet lie within an anti-magic band that suppresses spellcasting. Miirym can temporarily widen that band to thirty feet for ten minutes; the party has banked this boon for use against Manshoon but has not activated it. Daz ends the session standing inside the existing ten-foot field.
