### 055.02 The Road Through the Silver Marches

The party emerged from the depths of the Underdark through Blingdenstone's surface exit into the Silver Marches on a mid-spring afternoon, blinking in the light of a world they had long been separated from. It was here that Eldeth said her goodbyes, parting ways with the group as they set their faces south. The land around them bore the unmistakable scars of war — burned hamlets, a broken standing stone, and a defiled Tempus shrine that drew Gyrgum's quiet reverence. A refugee family trudged past them on the road, three generations sharing a single ox and a handcart, heading south with everything they had left. A local at a wayhouse noted an eerie stillness that went beyond the destruction — fewer bears, pixies no longer singing in the sown lands — something that felt older and stranger than the war alone could explain.

### 055.03 A Detour to Mirabar

The party made a detour to Mirabar, the great dwarven mining city whose forges painted the horizon red and filled the air with smoke for miles around. The city had held during the Uthgardt raids, but the cost was visible everywhere — a massive refugee population slept in shifts at the Smith's Gate while the hammers rang through the night, half the orders being for militia armor. Thorin, a dwarf who had grown up far from his people's traditions, found the city overwhelming and unfamiliar — a fish out of water in the very place that should have felt like home. A bronze Stroud bust stood at the Smiths Guildhall, and a tavern called The Knight Beneath the Knight bore his name. A traveling Stroud School polemicist spotted Gyrgum in the market and immediately attempted to recruit him to the cause of the "human agency hypothesis" — the argument that mortal will, not gods or dragons, had decided the great conflicts of the age. The polemicist introduced himself as one of the Stroudite school, rejecting both the Bahamutian hypothesis and the idea that Protanther had simply allowed events to unfold, insisting that this was all about mortal agency. When Gyrgum replied that he stood for strength and luck, the preacher pressed on, asking pointedly, "Are you walking the road for Stroud?" After a brief and fruitless exchange, the preacher pressed a well-formed pamphlet into Gyrgum's hands, titled *Stroud and the Human Agency Hypothesis: A Critique — Dragons and Gods Do Not Rule*, which Gyrgum, noting it was at least divided into paragraphs rather than a wall of text, pocketed for later reading.

Within Mirabar, the party also visited an Order of the Gauntlet shrine that housed a sculpted bronze frontispiece reproduction of Milo Goodbarrel's Account, Volume 1, and a nearly full-height painting of Malfire, the Vindicator of Nature — a weathered figure in green at the forest's edge, longbow lowered, an enormous gray wolf at his side, stars rising over his shoulder. The painting had been made by Milo Goodbarrel, the famous chronicler of the War of the Dragons, and two half-orcs tended the shrine in shifts. Thorin began quietly considering whether the city's renowned smiths might be able to outfit him with something worthy of the road ahead.

### 055.04 The Ruins of Triboar

The party pressed on to Triboar, a caravan town that had been devastated by the barbarian raids, with one-third of it still in ruins. Before an inn with recently laid flagstones, a cairn of silver fieldstones named 243 of the Triboar dead. The town buzzed with caravans staging, traders, pilgrims, Concordant Order knights, the occasional Lord's Alliance courier, and those carrying rebuild permits — the party blending into the churn of it all. It was here that the party first heard whispered tales of a figure called the Ardragon — a Kenku hooded in black who hovered a foot off the ground, appearing at a distance along the caravan trails north of Yartar, mimicking a hawk, taking nothing, and vanishing before anyone could approach. One drover offered the fuller story: "The Ardragon — my granddad called him — the one who used to fly the routes for the Black Network. Used to steal anything not nailed down. Then something happened out east. Now he's a… a ranger?" The man paused, then added: "We don't pay him. We leave a coin on a rock, and he never picks it up." A half-orc named Kestler, who kept a small Bahamut shrine at the Mountain's Mouth inn, approached the party with a worried look and declared: "You're going south! You oughta know, there's a temple! The brother who keeps it stopped writing five years ago. I keep a small house down the road from him. I have not seen him in three years. The temple fire still burns. I don't know what that means. I would very much like to know." He asked them to carry a written note to this brother, and Gyrgum immediately agreed: "Tis an omen! I shall certainly visit the temple, and I will bring your note."

That same evening, Thorin bought a drink for a local man sitting alone at the inn, speaking in two voices — his own, and a younger one that answered questions the first voice never asked. A neighbor explained quietly: "That's Eldred. He ran courier, Triboar to Sundabar in the war, came back from his last run two summers past, and he was both of them. We feed him, and we don't ask." Gyrgum and Zalthir, listening carefully with insight checks above 14, noticed that both voices were Eldred's — the second one a younger version of himself, as though something else were speaking through him in his own register. Gyrgum observed that the madness was familiar: it was the same broken, fractured quality they had witnessed throughout the Underdark, and the realization settled over them like a cold fog — whatever had been festering below the surface was bleeding through into the world above. Glabbagool, mercifully unaware of the weight of the moment, was gifted an entire wheel of cheese by the curious innkeeper as a novelty. The intelligent grey ooze dissolved it entirely and declared, "This is very good" — then paused and asked with genuine curiosity, "Why wouldn't you be good with dairy? Is dairy bad for people?" — his first encounter with cheese. Throughout the stay, Glabbagool continued pestering Zalthir about shadow monk training, periodically attempting to demonstrate that he had made progress in walking — painful to watch, but enthusiastic.

## Locations
### Silver Marches
A region on the surface where the party emerges from the Underdark near Blingdenstone's exit during mid-spring. The area has been devastated by Uthgardt barbarian raids following the War of the Giants. *Canon timeline:* the War of the Dragons (~1490 DR — the conflict Milo Goodbarrel chronicles, fought by Stroud, Protanther, Malfire, and Whistler) ended first; the War of the Giants (1491 DR, restoring the Ordning under King Hekaton) followed; the Uthgardt raids that scarred this region were a knock-on consequence of the giant war's disruption. Burned hamlets, displaced refugees, a broken standing stone, and a defiled Tempus shrine line the road. I'm not certain."

## NPCs
### Eldeth
A companion who had traveled with the party through the Underdark. She says her goodbyes at the Blingdenstone surface exit as the party continues their journey south. She had previously told Thorin about Mirabar, which is part of why the party detours there.

### Stroud School Polemicist
A traveling human preacher encountered in Mirabar who promotes the "human agency hypothesis" — the idea that mortal agency, not gods or dragons, determined the outcome of historical conflicts. He immediately spots Gyrgum and attempts to convert him: "Brother, have you heard the witness call? Are you walking the road for Stroud?" He identifies as of the Stroudite school, rejecting both the Bahamutian hypothesis and the Protanther doctrine. After failing to win Gyrgum over (Gyrgum rolls a 6), he hands him a pamphlet and declares the debate unresolved. He is one of many indicators of a broader religious factionalism surrounding the person of Stroud.

### Malfire
Known as the Vindicator of Nature, depicted in a large nearly full-height painting at an Order of the Gauntlet shrine in Mirabar. The painting shows him as a figure in weathered green at the edge of a forest, longbow lowered, with an enormous gray wolf at his side and stars rising over his shoulder. The painting is by Milo Goodbarrel. Gyrgum asks what it means to vindicate nature; Thorin suggests that if Gyrgum has to ask, he's clearly not connected to nature.

### Kestler
A half-orc encountered at the Mountain's Mouth inn in Triboar who maintains a small shrine to Bahamut. His face lights up when he sees the party heading south: "You're going south! You oughta know, there's a temple! The brother who keeps it stopped writing five years ago. I keep a small house down the road from him. I have not seen him in three years. The temple fire still burns. I don't know what that means. I would very much like to know." He asks the party to deliver a written note to this brother on his behalf.

### Eldred (The Dual-Voiced Local)
A man in the Mountain's Mouth inn in Triboar who speaks in two voices — both his own. The first is his current voice; the second a younger version of himself, answering questions the first voice never asks. A neighbor explains he ran courier routes from Triboar to Sundabar during the war, came back from his last run two summers past, and "was both of them." The locals feed him and do not ask questions. Gyrgum and Zalthir, both rolling above 14 on insight, recognize the broken, fractured rhythm of the second voice as the same quality of madness they witnessed throughout the Underdark — Eldred's voice in his own register, but not him. The madness of below is bleeding into the world above.

### Glabbagool
An intelligent grey ooze traveling with the party. In Triboar, he is gifted a wheel of cheese by a curious innkeeper, promptly dissolves it, declares it "very good," and then asks whether dairy is bad for people — his first encounter with cheese. He is forced to remain hidden in a bag throughout the party's time in Waterdeep due to the city's intolerance of oozes, peering out with two small eyeballs. He has been persistently pestering Zalthir about shadow monk training and periodically attempts to demonstrate that he has made progress in walking — painful to watch, but deeply enthusiastic. Zalthir's advice: "Give it time, you just have to level up."

### Rishaal
The proprietor of Rishaal's Pageturners bookshop in Waterdeep. He explains how a painting by Milo Goodbarrel of the war's heroes saved the print run of Volume Three: "That painting saved the print run! Milo finished it two days before the signing. We had it framed. People come here for the painting and left with a book." He describes real-world fiction enthusiasts as "exasperating, but they buy three copies of everything."

### Whistler
A Kenku hero from the War of the Dragons whose redemption arc and supposed death at Glazhael's lair are subjects of intense public debate. Some factions have declared him fallen, with a sign in Waterdeep reading *Whistler, Kenku of the First Faction, fallen at Glazhael's lair, the year of the Alliance, redeemed in life and death. May his silence speak.* Fans are split between the "Whistlerites" — who debate whether Milo's account of his morally confused post-lycanthropy stage is too generous — and the "Miloites," who argue that the "operational expenses, cost of living" framing represents a philosophical breakthrough about the impossibility of pure heroism. The rumors of the Ardragon (a black-hooded Kenku on the caravan trails who takes nothing and mimics a hawk) circulate separately from the literary debates, though the party recognizes the connection.

### The Field Ward Street Preacher
A ragged and sincere street preacher in Waterdeep's Field Ward who addresses any crowd that passes, repeatedly chanting: "The kraken under the keep! The kraken under the keep! The chant will break, and the kraken will rise!" When his eyes land on Daz, he breaks from the chant: "You came up! You came up, you will go again. You will go down again. Do not—" before cycling back. Thorin, rolling a 16 on insight, gets the distinct impression he is right about something he cannot name, and notices that the preacher's broken, looping speech rhythm matches that of Eldred in Triboar. The pattern is becoming hard to ignore.

### Ardragon
A figure of legend discussed by caravan drovers in Triboar and beyond. Described as a Kenku hooded in black who hovers a foot off the ground, appears at a distance on caravan trails north of Yartar, mimics a hawk, takes nothing, and vanishes before anyone can approach. One drover offers the history: formerly known as the Ardragon, he flew routes for the Black Network and used to steal anything not nailed down — then "something happened out east," and now he stops banditry, takes nothing, doesn't speak, mimics a hawk, and rides the next thermal. Locals leave a coin on a rock for him, which he never picks up.

### Milo Goodbarrel
A famous author and painter whose multi-volume accounts of the War of the Dragons have created a massive following and spawned intense fan factions. His painting of Stroud, Malfire, Whistler, and himself saved the print run of Volume Three, with Rishaal crediting it directly for renewed sales. A reproduction of his account's bronze frontispiece is found at an Order of the Gauntlet shrine in Mirabar. Fans debate the variations between printings of his books, the travel physics implied by his accounts, and whether his treatment of Whistler's redemption arc was too generous or philosophically groundbreaking.

### Khell-Vire
A shadow monk master based at Candlekeep, described by Waterdeep contacts as "the best of the best" for shadow monk training. Zalthir is directed toward him and obtains a letter of introduction from a local monastery — after Daz mends the monastery's leaky roof to satisfy the monks' financial demands.
