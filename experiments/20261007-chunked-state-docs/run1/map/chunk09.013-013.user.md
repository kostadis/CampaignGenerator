## Scenes
### 013.01 The Battle with the Two-Headed Giant

The streets of Gracklstugh erupted into chaos as the party found themselves alongside a squad of duergar militia, facing down a rampaging stone giant with two heads, a grotesque sign of the madness that had overtaken him. Two of the duergar guards swelled to enormous size, and two of them unleashed sprays of fire that scorched the giant's rocky hide for 23 points of damage. The party pressed the attack from every angle. Gyrgum called upon his divine power through his holy symbol. Thorin landed a critical hit with his short sword and then used Action Surge to strike again. Daz struck with unerring magical force. Through it all, the giant swung his great club in wild arcs, hitting one duergar for 19 damage and narrowly missing Zalthir.

#### The party and a group of duergar engage a crazed, two-headed stone giant in the streets of Gracklstugh.
- The duergar "act very official" and are on the party's side.
- Two duergar guards magically enlarge themselves to confront the stone giant, and two others do not.
- Two duergar unleash fire sprays at the giant. The damage rolls come to 13 and 10, for 23 points total.
- The stone giant, crazed and two-headed, swings his great club twice. One duergar takes 19 damage.
- The giant's second swing targets Zalthir, but with disadvantage it rolls a 14 against Zalthir's AC 15 and misses.
- Gyrgum uses his holy symbol to channel divine energy against the giant. The giant "once again burns at the end of the day."
- Thorin makes a nature/history/survival check (16) and recognizes the giant has likely gone mad. Stone giants are particularly unforgiving of those who kill their kind.
- The GM rules that dropping the giant to zero with a weapon knocks him out, while dropping him with a spell like Magic Missile kills him. The giant is at 34 hit points at this point.
- Daz strikes the giant with Magic Missile but rolls a one, dealing 6 damage ("every little bit counts").
- Zalthir declares non-lethal damage and attacks three times, spending a focus point on the third. He misses, hits for 5 damage, then misses.
- Thorin scores a critical hit with his short sword, then uses Action Surge and hits again for 6 more damage.

### 013.02 The Fall of Rihuud

In the heat of battle, Thorin drew on his deep familiarity with giant-kind and made a successful check (a 16) that revealed the terrible truth: this creature had not chosen violence but had gone mad. Stone giants are particularly unforgiving toward those who kill their kind, and particularly forgiving of those who spare them. Thorin urged the party and the duergar militia alike to bring the giant down without killing him. The party switched to non-lethal weapon attacks and wore the giant down from 34 hit points to 8. Zalthir attacked with a focus point, Thorin followed with his crit and Action Surge, and Gyrgum charged in with his mace. Thorin's plea to the militia failed, however. The militia, bound by their rigid code and unable to tell friend from foe inside Zalthir's magical darkness that blanketed part of the street, delivered the killing blow. The giant, later identified as Rihuud, crumpled to the stone floor of the city. Thorin spoke funeral words over the body to save face in case the giants took offense, and the darkness lifted.

#### The party makes a desperate attempt to subdue the maddened stone giant without killing him, but the duergar militia delivers the killing blow.
- Thorin, drawing on his experience with giants, realizes the stone giant has gone mad. He urges the party and the duergar to use non-lethal force to avoid offending the stone giant clans.
- With the giant down to 8 hit points, Gyrgum is "more daring than Gyrgum normally would be." He moves out of the darkness into melee range to strike with his mace.
- Gyrgum's mace hits for 1 damage. He then uses two-weapon fighting with the flat of his hand axe and misses.

### 013.03 Aftermath and the Arrival of Dorhun

In the aftermath, the duergar soldiers shrank back to their normal size with an audible pop and declared the operation a complete success. They added that any future support for the local militia and police would be "greatly appreciated and rewarded." The party pressed them about the wave of strange behavior sweeping the city. Once the questions were phrased literally enough for the duergar to parse, the militia conferred and confirmed what the adventurers had begun to suspect: incidents of citizens acting in erratic, self-destructive, and inexplicable ways had been increasing sharply, and the clan militias were struggling with the extra work. They knew no cause and suggested the party ask a clan leader.

Before the conversation could go further, a new figure arrived: Dorhun, a stone giant and fellow apprentice to the revered Stonespeaker Hgraam. After the long silence typical of his kind, he identified the fallen Rihuud as a comrade who had gone mad and sprouted a second head while communing with the stone deep in Cairngorm Cavern, then stormed out into the city. The militia honestly confirmed that the party had tried to spare Rihuud's life. Dorhun's expression softened, and he invited them to visit Cairngorm Cavern before they left Gracklstugh. He ordered the Stone Guard to escort them, much to the guards' weary irritation.

### 013.04 An Invitation from the Keepers of the Flame

Barely had Dorhun departed when a duergar materialized out of thin air nearby. The party was learning that this was a disconcerting habit of the city's inhabitants. He introduced himself as Gartokkar Xundorn of the Keepers of the Flame and flashed a badge that sent curious onlookers scattering. He explained that his order's sacred duty was to tend to the red dragon Themberchaud, who kept the city's forges burning in the lightless depths of the Underdark, where wood was nearly impossible to find. He also said the Deepking treated the Keepers as his most prized advisors. Gyrgum drily offered that they had some kindling available if the order was running short on dragons. Gartokkar laughed, then dissected the joke with such analytical precision that every trace of humor was methodically extinguished. He invited the party to his quarters by the following day. In exchange for their help with a problem he was not yet ready to name, he promised city passes and what Thorin summarized as "a limited get-out of jail-free card." Between scenes, the GM reminded Zalthir of his "I know a guy" list: one favor per city, usable to learn about the factions or obtain something useful.

### 013.05 Whispers at the Darklake Brewery

Seeking to understand the tangled web of Gracklstugh's politics before committing to anything, the party made their way to the Darklake Brewery. More precisely, they went to the kiosk outside it, since the brewery itself was strictly duergar only. Gyrgum tried to peek inside and rolled a 3 on stealth. He still glimpsed quiet duergar drinking cheaper beer over very serious conversations. Among the non-duergar outside were a halfling, a human, and a drow. Gyrgum bought a round for the talkative halfling, who was grateful and apparently well-informed. The halfling revealed that Deepking Horgar Steelshadow himself had reportedly descended into madness. This was not rampaging violence but paranoia and increasingly arbitrary laws. He said the Keepers of the Flame and Themberchaud were "in a bit of a pickle with the Gray Ghosts and the crown," and that all the factions were at each other's throats. As a non-duergar who stayed uninvolved, he was a "civilian" free to talk. He warned that attending an official meeting with a faction leader would turn the party from civilians into soldiers in the eyes of rival groups. He confirmed that the pending invitation came from the Keepers of the Flame. He also explained that the Keepers periodically replace their red dragon with a new one raised from a purchased egg, which Gyrgum noted could also be stolen. A drow patron seated nearby chimed in with contemptuous amusement. He lamented that duergar conspiracies lacked the drama and artistic cruelty of Menzoberranzan's political intrigues and amounted to little more than blunt, graceless violence.

### 013.06 A Proposition from the Stone Guard

The party had barely finished processing this flood of information when Captain Errde Blackskull of the Stone Guard arrived from the direction of the prison with a small escort. She made it politely but unmistakably clear that she expected them to accompany her. When the party bristled, she reminded them that if she wanted them arrested she would have brought a whole platoon. To ease the tension, she loudly announced to the surrounding air, and to any invisible listeners, that this was merely a friendly discussion and not "that kind of meeting."

In her front office, over pastries, she commended the party's initiative, clever tactics, and "brutal efficiency," and singled out Thorin's attempt to spare the stone giant's life. She then laid out her request. A derro named Droki had been evading Stone Guard patrols with suspicious ease, as though he always knew in advance where they would be. He lived in the volatile West Cleft District, where the guards entered only in force. She asked the party to follow him and report back, or better, to seize him for questioning, or failing that to kill him and bring evidence of his activities.

Errde spoke of a deeper conspiracy she believed was festering beneath the surface of Gracklstugh, involving corruption within the Council of Savants and among the clan lairds. She promised that exposing it would earn each of them 350 gold pieces' worth of equipment from her armory, plus safe passage out of the city when their work was done. Thorin pressed her twice on whether this bound them to the Stone Guard. She insisted it would "of course, be for the city." Daz, whose passive perception told him the bazaar's strange merchants broke every rule of this authoritarian society, agreed the market was "definitely full of corruption." He then rolled an 18 on persuasion, and Errde advanced 175 gold pieces' worth of equipment to each of them, with the remaining 175 to follow on completion. Almost as an afterthought, she revealed that she was already aware drow were hunting the party, and offered her protection against them as an additional incentive. Nothing she said forbade them from also hearing out the Keepers of the Flame, though the party came away suspecting she might be a little mad herself.

Thorin priced splint (too expensive at 200 gp) and plate (1,500 gp), then settled on chain mail and twenty darts. The others deferred their scrolls and potions to next time. The session ended just before the GM was about to run an encounter. The party now faced two faction leaders vying for their services, the stone giants of Cairngorm Cavern expecting a visit, and a city slowly unraveling at the seams.

## NPCs
### Rihuud
A stone giant apprentice to Stonespeaker Hgraam who sprouted a second head and went mad while communing with the stone in Cairngorm Cavern. He rampaged through the streets of Gracklstugh swinging a great club and was ultimately slain by the duergar militia, despite the party's attempts to subdue him non-lethally. The party had worn him down to 8 hit points before the militia killed him. Dorhun remembered him as "a good giant."

### Dorhun
A stone giant and fellow apprentice to Stonespeaker Hgraam. Somber and deliberate in the manner of stone giants, he waited in long silence for someone else to speak first. He arrived to identify the fallen Rihuud, flatly rebuffed Thorin's attempt to find a personal connection, and questioned the militia directly about the party's conduct. He expressed appreciation for the party's attempt to spare his comrade's life, invited them to visit Cairngorm Cavern before leaving the city, and ordered the reluctant Stone Guard to escort them there.

### Gartokkar Xundorn
A duergar cleric and high-ranking representative of the Keepers of the Flame. He is analytical and literal-minded, capable of dissecting a joke to death. He wields significant legal authority in Gracklstugh, and a flash of his badge sends onlookers away. He claims the Deepking treats the Keepers as his most prized advisors. He sought to recruit the party for an unspecified task, asking them to come to his quarters by the following day. In return he offered city passes and limited legal protection, hedged with "a standard set of disclaimers."

### Themberchaud
A pampered red dragon kept by the Keepers of the Flame to rekindle the city's forges. He was observed flying over the city during the session. The party learned he is kept satisfied with a large hoard, but that the Keepers must periodically replace aging dragons with new ones raised from eggs (purchased or stolen) when they become too difficult to control.

### Horgar Steelshadow
The Deepking of Gracklstugh. A halfling informant rumors that he has descended into paranoid madness, resulting in increasingly arbitrary laws and general instability in the city. This is not the rampaging kind of madness but "the kind of madness that you see when a person in power gets disconnected from society." The halfling publicly wished him long life and glory before lowering his voice to share this.

### Errde Blackskull
The captain of the Stone Guard in Gracklstugh. Observant and pragmatic, she arrived with four guards and noted she would have brought a whole platoon if she meant to arrest anyone. She commended the party's efficiency and Thorin's attempt to spare the stone giant. She tasked the party with tracking a rogue derro named Droki, suspecting a conspiracy involving the Council of Savants and the clan lairds. She offered equipment, gold, safe passage out of the city, and protection from pursuing drow in exchange for their help. She insisted the work was "for the city," and the party suspects she may be a little mad herself.

## Locations
### Gracklstugh
A grim and industrial duergar city in the Underdark characterized by paranoia, invisible sentries, strict literal laws, and a rigid penal code. The city is currently plagued by a rising tide of sudden madness among its citizens. Its clans maintain separate militias, and its factions are at each other's throats. Non-duergar who stay uninvolved are treated as civilians, "not relevant to the city."

### Cairngorm Cavern
The residence of the stone giants within Gracklstugh, where Rihuud reportedly went mad while communing with the stone before rampaging through the city streets. Dorhun invited the party to visit before they leave the city.

### Darklake Brewery
A strictly duergar-only establishment where patrons drink in serious, humorless silence, and where beer is cheaper inside. Non-duergar are restricted to a kiosk outside, where the party gathered intelligence from a halfling and a drow. A human was also drinking there. It sits near the city prison, some distance from the Blade Bazaar.

### Blade Bazaar
