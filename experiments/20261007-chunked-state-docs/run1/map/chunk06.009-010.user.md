### 009.01 The Rise of the Prince of Demons

The nightmare began without warning. Demogorgon, the Prince of Demons himself, erupted from the dark waters of the subterranean lake, his two monstrous heads roaring in unison as his massive tentacles smashed the surface into a frothing chaos of blood and foam. He waded toward the kuo-toa village of Sloobludop with terrible purpose, his very presence unraveling the minds of all who dared witness him. He was bent on slaughtering his former worshippers, and he was very distracted by this "opportunity for feasting." Around the shore, pools of blood marked where demonic-looking fish were leaping in and out of the water, tearing apart the kuo-toa who tried to flee into it. Every party member had to resist the Prince of Demons' presence with a DC 13 Charisma saving throw. Several were struck by sudden, overwhelming madness: Daz, Eldeth, Topsy, Turvy, and Shuushar all lost their grip on sanity. Daz collapsed into hysterics, incapacitated and screaming, laughing, or weeping. Their companions had to carry them toward any hope of escape. Thorin also rolled poorly, but he used his Second Wind to augment the roll and held on.

### 009.02 Escape from Sloobludop

With the village descending into screaming pandemonium, the party spotted a small boat near the docks and made a desperate break for it. Zalthir believed the Darklake was both the safest and the fastest way through the Underdark. Jimjar hoisted the incapacitated Daz over his shoulder and ran. Thorin, with his dwarven strength of 17, took the small, light twins Topsy and Turvy, and others scooped up the rest of the fallen. Sarith urged the group not to abandon Eldeth, pointing out that she was the only one keeping them hidden. The kuo-toa around the boat had been incapacitated by their own madness. As they recovered, they stood frozen between the water and the demon lord, uncertain and terrified. The boat could not hold both the party and the four kuo-toa.

At Thorin's suggestion, Gyrgum stepped forward, since Thorin protested that his own charisma was "for shit." Gyrgum addressed the kuo-toa with a boldness that surprised even his companions and convinced them they were natural heroes destined to stand against the darkness. They were already predisposed to heroism, and they were terrified of the water and the fish in it. The kuo-toa charged toward the demonic forces, buying the party the precious moments they needed. The adventurers crammed themselves into the small, barely-floating vessel, with two paddles among them, and paddled furiously away from the shore as Sloobludop was torn apart behind them. The screams of the dying faded into the dark as the boat pushed deeper into the vast underground sea known as the Darklake.

### 009.03 Navigating the Darklake

As the horror of Sloobludop receded, Shuushar cried out that this was why his people had to reach enlightenment: they had summoned Leemooggoogoon. One of the escapees slapped him. No, that was Demogorgon, the Prince of Demons himself, loose on the Prime Material Plane. When someone protested that this was impossible, the answer came from Thorin: "I don't care what's possible. I saw what I saw." The party connected the dots they had been ignoring for weeks: the rumors of demons loose in the Underdark, the demons at Velkynvelve, the demonic encounters since, and now a demon lord walking the Underdark. Whatever had caused this was no accident. It was far beyond the work of any ordinary spellcaster: "not something a fifth-level magic user does by reading a scroll." Whoever did it had brought through an avatar rather than the whole being. After a few miles the water was clean enough to drink. The group set their course for Gracklstugh, a proper city where they might resupply and find answers. Buppido suggested it; Hemeth insisted, "I know people," and the lost tomb of Khaem was set aside as the other option. Gyrgum and Thorin were keen to buy better equipment now that they had money, and Zalthir was convinced the Darklake was the fastest road. The GM waved off a day-by-day voyage. Food was not an issue, thanks to the lake's fish, but tempers were. Crammed together, the passengers whined about stepped-on toes, made threats to throw each other overboard, and took bets.

The journey was not without peril. Daz stayed vigilant in the bow, ready with Shape Water, and his keen eye spotted a field of submerged stone teeth that would have shredded the hull to splinters. With Shape Water smoothing the current, the group threaded through them with a DC 12 group Dexterity check. Thorin and Gyrgum both succeeded, and the party felt "pretty smug." Days later, a cavern ceiling dropping to just three feet above the water forced everyone to duck. Gyrgum, Thorin, and Daz failed their saves and were knocked tumbling into the cold, black water below; of the PCs, only Zalthir kept his seat. Before they could even cry out, three demonic manta-ray-like creatures surged up from the depths. They were the same things that had torn up the fleeing kuo-toa, servants of Demogorgon, and their barbed tails slashed through the dark water toward the flailing adventurers.
