### 056.01 Arrival in Daggerford

The party arrived in Daggerford during their journey south toward Candlekeep, and the town greeted them with an unsettling quiet. The townsfolk moved carefully through the streets, offering no explanation for their wariness, and the group settled in for the night at the River Shining Tavern. Before they did, they passed through the village square and found a bronze cast of a severed forearm and hand mounted atop a stone pillar — a monument to Travis Houlin, a paladin of Bahamut who had died three years prior fighting the Cult of the Dragon. A Dwarven inscription on the base read "The move is yours," mirroring a phrase tied to the legendary figure Stroud back in Waterdeep's Castle Ward, and a smaller inscription on the back noted that Milo Goodbarrel, an Order of the Gauntlet member whose work was published by Rishaal, had witnessed and recorded the monument. The local village priest arrived soon after, selling small pewter reproductions of Travis's hand cast to raise donations, and explained that the monument had been funded by the Metalworkers Guild — possibly with Stroud-school sponsorship — though he himself declared proudly that he was a "pure Bahamutian" and did not believe in the heresy of the Stroudites. When pressed about why a Dwarven Stroudite inscription had been allowed to remain, he conceded that since the casting had been a gift, the inscription could stay — after all, wasn't Travis trying to save everyone, regardless of what happened in that game of chess?

### 056.02 The Silent Child of Maerith

That evening at the River Shining Tavern, a weaver named Maerith of the Ford approached the party with desperation in her eyes. Her seven-year-old daughter Elin had been silent for six days, drawing the same shape on every flat surface she could find, and speaking only in her sleep — saying that it was only the shape that held. The party attempted to examine the girl, failing to identify the cause medically, and Dawnbringer, Thorin's sentient sun-sword, pompously declared she would bring the light to bear before performing a lesser restoration. The cure was only partial: Elin spoke briefly, uttering the haunting words "The wells are not wells anymore," before falling silent once more. The party concluded that this was no ordinary disease — something was twisting the girl's mind from within, causing auditory hallucinations that no conventional healing could reach. Gyrgum noted that what was happening felt interior rather than exterior, something that could not be slapped or grappled away, and the party recognized the madness of the Underdark as appearing to seep into the surface world.

### 056.03 The Burning Wizard Inn

The road south brought the party to Beregost, the last meaningful stop before Candlekeep, where they took shelter at the Burning Wizard Inn. The gnome innkeeper, Festrum, recounted the inn's grim namesake — a wizard burned alive during a dragon cult uprising decades past — and pointed to a permanent soot mark on the south wall, refusing to paint over it and reciting his grandfather's words: "The wall remembers." A traveling bard performed that evening, with her closing number for the season being a four-verse drinking song about a Kenku thief who had learned to steal no more, with the whole inn singing along on the chorus of "Black Hood." After Gyrgum pressed a silver piece into her hand, she sang a winding love song about Stroud and the famous game of chess — winding it, as Gyrgum observed with gentle cynicism, into the usual claptrap about love, emotions, flowers, and drinking. Meanwhile, Daz noticed something deeply unsettling about a young human scholar nearby: the man was reciting a chant, and while the words were almost right, his breathing and cadence were wrong by a hair — the same eerie, syncopated pattern Daz had observed in Eldred, a man who had gone insane earlier in their adventure, and in a Field Ward preacher. He quietly took note of the chant, intending to bring it to the scholars of Candlekeep. Thorin sat near the sea that night, contemplating how strangely life moved and deciding simply that you just had to go with it, while Zalthir climbed to the inn's roof to meditate and found himself curiously — and perhaps not entirely disappointingly — at peace with nothing interrupting him.

### 056.04 Arrival at Candlekeep and the Prisoner Question

The party arrived at Candlekeep the following morning, traveling along the Way of the Lion as the great fortress-library came into view from miles away along the cliffs above the Sea of Swords. As they drew closer, Daz noticed that the pressure behind his eyes — a persistent ache of uncertain origin — had grown sharper since leaving Beregost, intensifying with each step toward the keep. Gyrgum confirmed that something tenuous seemed to connect Daz's headache to what had been afflicting Elin, though she lacked the knowledge to name it. Inside, they were received by Bookwyrm, the First Reader — a dragonborn sage who had clearly heard every joke about her name and was entirely unbothered by it. She assigned each member of the party to a specific mentor within the library, but before she dismissed them, the Gatewarden Kalan Strongbranch was summoned to address the matter of the shrunken House T'sarran spy currently residing in the party's bag of holding. Kalan explained that Candlekeep did not keep prisoners — they held them in comfortable, localized antimagic cells in the Bell Tower and then exiled them at the gate with a warning rather than delivering them back to their house or executing them — and left the decision of what to do with her entirely to the party.

The miniature drow spy was furious, demanding to be restored to her proper size and handed over to the Candlekeep guards. Glabbagool, the sentient grey ooze who had been serving as her jailer, cheerfully explained that she kept trying to kill him, so he would simply expand to trap her whenever she ran, and then attempt to teach her meditation — and that they had, in his estimation, become great friends. When Thorin asked what exactly she provided in the way of entertainment, Glabbagool clarified that she kept attacking him, he would slap her around, she would try to escape, he would expand to catch her, she would scream at him, and he would try to explain the way of the shadow monk — and then she would scream about the meditation, and then they would talk. Zalthir confirmed that watching the prisoner was part of Glabbagool's pre-monk training, which Glabbagool took with great seriousness, solemnly asking Zalthir whether he would be more or less of a monk for watching her. Zalthir confirmed it was absolutely pre-monk training. The party ultimately decided to leave her in Glabbagool's many-eyed care, and Glabbagool fixed all six of his pseudo-eyes on her to demonstrate his commitment.

Khell-Vire challenged Zalthir with a cryptic monk-style question — "What were you sent toward?" rather than "Why were you sent here?" — before rephrasing it more gently when Zalthir's patience with the oblique approach showed its limits, asking whether the phrase meant anything to him through lived experience. It did not. Khell-Vire sent Zalthir to the Trial of the Broken Mirror, stating plainly: "I will not be there. This is your work, not mine."

The monk set a final challenge: shadow stepping to intercept and smash a thrown brick mid-air. Zalthir succeeded, shattering the brick completely. The monk declared that while Zalthir's forms were wrong in every scroll — still skipping breaths, still not entering his ki in the hara — his combination of shadow stepping and striking was something special that redeemed everything else. Zalthir then demonstrated his shadow tentacles and eldritch tattoo, which the monk found less impressive, but agreed that combining those two elements into a single strike in an interesting way was the next frontier. A sage of xenobiology wandered over to ask whether a mindless digestive system could truly achieve enlightenment, and Zalthir defended Glabbagool's intelligence and progress with conviction, noting that he was by no means mindless, was already walking, and was already admirably tormenting the pocket drow. Zalthir received a vial of resonant slime from the sage. Master Kenshi ultimately acknowledged Zalthir's unconventional mastery and taught him one of three specialized shadow techniques; Zalthir chose option C.

### 056.06 Therapy for a Sentient Blade

Thorin was brought to Master Archivist Philemon, a clinical specialist in sentient artifacts who immediately began assessing Dawnbringer with the detached enthusiasm of a scholar examining a rare specimen — a framing Thorin immediately pushed back on, clarifying that this was his friend Dawnbringer, not a specimen. Philemon identified her as a late Netherese-era artifact of sun worship origin that had suffered profound arcane stress from millennia of darkness exposure, and noted with some surprise that the bond between Thorin and the blade was stronger than he had feared. When Philemon asked Dawnbringer whether the corpse she had been found with was her wielder — the sword faltered and went silent. Philemon gently reframed what the blade needed: not polishing (which, he realized midsentence, was not even physically possible for her), but the chance to remember what daylight felt like without needing to fight — and then, when she was ready, to grieve. He outlined a two-phase recovery process, beginning with the work of light and moving to the work of mourning, and Thorin found himself unexpectedly signed up for something that felt far more like therapy than archival research.

### 056.07 The Sealed Records of Menzoberranzan

Daz was taken to the Hall of Divination, where spectral mage hands drifted through the air fetching tomes for researchers, and where Sister Yvenne — a meticulous and authoritative keeper of the public record — waited for him. She established that information in Candlekeep was exchanged reciprocally, and Daz told her everything: the amnesia, the jail, the Underdark, the mercenaries trying to protect him and the ones trying to kill him, and the headache that had been growing worse since he arrived. Yvenne explained the brutal politics of Drow noble houses — the matron mothers, the rule of intrigue, the law that betrayal was upward-rewarded if it strengthened Lolth's web, and crucially the rule that if even one member of a targeted house survived, the attacking house was forfeit and destroyed. She illustrated this with a story of a noble house that tried to wipe out a rival and left a "blithering idiot child" alive, only for the rest of the drow community to come and tear the attacking house to the ground, leaving a black puddle behind. Daz asked what happened to the surviving child. Yvenne did not know.

She then read him a list of houses extinct within living memory — the second seat, the fourth seat marked "struck," the eleventh seat, and the twenty-third seat — and Daz, rolling an exceptional investigation check, noticed that the word "struck" was strange and out of place. It implied censorship rather than elimination. Yvenne admitted there was one house she could not speak of in that room, not because she refused, but because the public record was sealed and she was the public record: "If you want what is behind the seals, you will have to want it through other doors." When Daz asked for the full list of houses that had ever existed, she handed him a document — but the word "struck" appeared again, and she noted that the fourth seat held ritual archive privileges in Menzoberranzan, making a struck-fourth seat a very political and expensive erasure. Digging deeper into a two-hundred-year-old heraldry book, Daz discovered that an entire appendix had been mechanically removed with a razor at least fifty years ago — a cut, not natural wear. A surviving footnote referenced the name "Daz'issin," which Yvenne explained was a softening name that mothers used for boys they wanted to live, and that the houses which had used it most had not lasted. As Daz turned the implications over in his mind — the sealed records, the razored appendix, the mercenaries, the name — he began to sense that he was piecing together something far more dangerous than a forgotten past. Yvenne's careful silences and precise body language told him he was close to something she could not say aloud, and the session ended with Daz pushing gently: "But what if they weren't successfully all gone?" The truth of who he was, and why someone had gone to such lengths to erase it, waited somewhere behind a door that Candlekeep itself could not open for him.

## Items
### Bronze Cast Forearm Monument
A bronze sculpture of a severed forearm and hand mounted on a stone pillar in the Daggerford village square, dedicated to Travis Houlin, a paladin of Bahamut who died there three years prior fighting the Cult of the Dragon, which had been actively recruiting in the town. It bears a Dwarven inscription reading "The move is yours" — the same phrase found beneath the Castle Ward statue associated with Stroud in Waterdeep — and a smaller Common inscription on the back noting it was witnessed and recorded by Milo Goodbarrel, an Order of the Gauntlet member whose work was published by Rishaal. The casting was funded by the Metalworkers Guild, possibly with Stroud-school sponsorship, though the local priest considers that incidental.

A drow-made Cloak of Elvenkind held by Daz that assists with stealth but cannot be used in daylight. The transcript does not establish a separate piwafwi identity or an attunement ruling.

## Spells
### Lesser Restoration
Cast by Dawnbringer on the afflicted girl Elin in Daggerford, after the party failed their medicine checks to diagnose the condition conventionally. Mechanically, the spell originated from Dawnbringer's own sentient-weapon properties and was channeled through Thorin as her attuned wielder — not from any spell slot of Thorin's (Fighter 8, Battle Master, no spellcasting). Dawnbringer performed it with pompous ceremony — "Let me bring the light to you." While it allowed Elin to speak briefly — uttering "The wells are not wells anymore" — it could not fully cure the profound mental twisting she was experiencing, as the condition was determined to be something interior and beyond a standard disease or magical effect.

### Shadow Step
A teleportation ability used by Zalthir during his Trial of the Broken Mirror at Candlekeep. The blind half-elf monk immediately criticized Zalthir's form — "You skip the third breath. You don't enter your ki in the hara. You let it bleed into your fingertips like a poison. It is technically wrong in every scroll" — but then acknowledged Zalthir arrived at his destination three seconds faster than the grandmaster. Zalthir later combined shadow step with a precise strike to smash a thrown brick mid-air (rolling 29 to hit), which the monk declared redeemed his lack of traditional skill in every other area. This combination — shadow step plus precise strike — was previously demonstrated in the Underdark when Zalthir created a precise puncture, and forms the basis for the specialized technique Master Kenshi ultimately teaches him.

## NPCs
### Maerith of the Ford
A weaver by trade in Daggerford who spots Gyrgum's holy symbol and approaches the party at the River Shining Tavern seeking help for her seven-year-old daughter Elin, who has been silent for six days, draws the same shape on every flat surface, and says in her sleep that "it is only the shape that holds."

### Elin
A seven-year-old girl suffering from a mysterious mental affliction that causes her to draw repetitive shapes and hear things that do not exist. After Dawnbringer's pompous lesser restoration, she briefly speaks the words "The wells are not wells anymore" before falling silent again. The party determines her condition is interior — not a disease, and not something that can be conventionally healed — and connects it to the Underdark madness seeping into the surface world.

### The Village Priest
A cleric in Daggerford who sells small pewter reproductions of Travis Houlin's hand cast to raise donations for the Order of the Gauntlet. ### Festrum
An old gnome innkeeper at the Burning Wizard Inn in Beregost who recounts the story of the wizard burned alive during a dragon cult uprising to anyone who buys a second pint, always pointing to the permanent soot mark on the south wall and citing his grandfather's words: "The wall remembers."

A traveling performer at the Burning Wizard Inn whose closing number this season is a four-verse drumming drinking song about a Kenku thief who learned to steal no more, with the whole inn singing along on the chorus of "Black Hood." A young man in Beregost whose strange, syncopated chanting and breathing pattern catches Daz's attention during the bard's performance. ### Bookwyrm
The First Reader of Candlekeep, a dragonborn sage who has "heard them all" regarding her name and is entirely unbothered. ### Kalan Strongbranch
The Gatewarden of Candlekeep — summoned from the Arcanium to reception to address the miniature drow spy discovered in the party's bag of holding during inspection. He explains Candlekeep's position with precision: "The bell tower has cells. Comfortable, localized anti-magic. They are very rarely used. Anyone we find guilty of a major offense is exiled rather than imprisoned. Which is to say, we hold prisoners. We do not keep them. If you give her to us, what you are giving us is a delay. We will not deliver to House T'sarran, and we will not execute her. Eventually, we will release her at our gate with a warning." He leaves the choice entirely to the party.

### House T'sarran Spy
A drow spy who was shrunken and kept as a prisoner in the party's bag of holding, watched over by Glabbagool. She is furious about her situation — she thinks it is "outrageous and unacceptable" — constantly tries to kill Glabbagool, runs for it whenever possible, and demands to be restored to her normal size and formally imprisoned by Candlekeep's guards rather than left in Glabbagool's custody. She has, despite herself, been subjected to Glabbagool's meditation lessons.

### Glabbagool
A sentient grey ooze (transformed from his original gelatinous-cube form in Blingdenstone) who has been acting as jailer for the miniature drow spy. He shapes fake eyes out of his pseudopods as an affectation — the recurring "six pseudo-eyes" are his own choice. He describes their daily routine with cheerful equanimity: she attacks him, he slaps her around, she tries to run, he expands to catch her, she screams, he explains the way of the shadow monk, she screams about the meditation, and then they talk. "We've become great friends!" He asks Zalthir seriously whether watching the prisoner will make him more or less of a monk, accepts Zalthir's confirmation that this is pre-monk training, and commits to the task with all six pseudo-eyes fixed on his charge. He is also very pleased when the xenobiology sage's interest in him is defended by Zalthir.

### Brother Khell-Vire
A 200-year-old half-elf and former monk of the Garden of Shadow who now serves at Candlekeep. He left the Garden eighty years ago after refusing an order, and wears a faded shadow monk under-robe beneath the avowed gray of Candlekeep. He has a note on his desk reading "Zalthir, the Underdark, until the Bone King sings" that he cannot explain. He opens with a cryptic monk-style question — "What were you sent toward?" — that Zalthir finds predictably annoying from long familiarity with the type, and sends him to the Trial of the Broken Mirror with the instruction: "I will not be there. This is your work, not mine."

### Master Kenshi (the blind monk)
A blind half-elf monk who tests Zalthir's combat abilities through the Trial of the Broken Mirror. He immediately identifies the flaws in Zalthir's shadow stepping — skipping the third breath, bleeding ki into the fingertips like a poison — but notes Zalthir arrived three seconds faster than the grandmaster. He is deeply unimpressed by every formal skill demonstration (acrobatics: 3, athletics: 1, stealth: 11), turns to walk away, and is surprised when Zalthir lands a powerful hit on him from behind — "Perhaps not all is lost." He sets the brick challenge, and when Zalthir smashes it with a 29, declares that while the forms are wrong in every scroll, the combination of shadow step and strike redeems everything else. He observes wryly that Zalthir is "beginning to remember, I think, all those shadow monks that drove you nuts."

### Master Archivist Philemon
A clinical specialist in sentient artifacts at Candlekeep who initially greets Dawnbringer as "a remarkable specimen" until Thorin corrects him. He identifies her as a late Netherese-era sun worship artifact suffering profound arcane stress from millennia of darkness, notes mid-sentence that she cannot actually be polished, and outlines a two-phase recovery process: the work of light (first) and the work of mourning (second). He asks Dawnbringer whether the corpse she was found with was her wielder and she falters and goes silent. He is gentle and precise, reframing what seemed like an artifact assessment into something much closer to grief counseling.

### Sister Yvenne
An authoritative librarian at Candlekeep who serves as "the public record" — a phrase she uses with quiet weight when explaining why she cannot speak of a sealed house in that room. She provides Daz with information on drow noble house politics, the rule of intrigue, the matron mother system, and the law of forfeiture when a targeted house is not fully destroyed. She reads him a list of extinct houses whose Fourth Seat entry alone is marked "struck," explains that "Daz'issin" is a softening name mothers used for sons they wanted to live, provides the heraldry book with the razored appendix, and communicates through careful silences and precise body language that Daz is close to something she cannot say aloud. Her final explicit statement on the matter: "It is possible your name is related, but it is unlikely. The houses that had those names have all gone."
