# Chapter 49 A Sign from Lolth (or Just a Spider?)

Date: 2026-03-16

## Scenes
### 049.01 Departure from Blingdenstone

The weary adventurers gathered their resolve in Blingdenstone, studying their maps and steeling themselves for the long-awaited journey to the surface. The route marked "escape" bore a white hole on the map to show it led up to the surface rather than deeper into the dark. The locals call that surface "the Overbright," and the DM teased the perspective: "Oh, that's what you call it. We call it home." Zalthir brooded over the disappointing lack of any meaningful boon from the enigmatic Jimjar. The party had spent what felt like weeks partying with a deity, and all he got was "a damn used token." Gyrgum, chill in the presence of a god that wasn't his own, sought guidance from his patron, the Platinum Dragon Bahamut. On a Religion roll of 17, the great dragon god made his presence known with a draconic eye-roll. He muttered that a gnomish god had gone into the Abyss and somehow ended up in the prime material plane. He was pleased to have helped the party, and he closed with a pointed reminder not to forget "who's buttering your bread." Under the updated rules, clerics can change their gods, so the gods are always recruiting. Bahamut, patient like all good dragons, believes in the inevitable triumph of dragonkind and sees no need to rush it. Daz, meanwhile, cast a wary eye toward the shadows. Lolth had once vanished and left her clerics powerless, and he was troubled that with gods slipping loose, Lolth herself might be stirring once more in Menzoberranzan.

#### The party prepares to leave Blingdenstone, aiming for the surface and reflecting on their recent encounters with deities.
- The party reviews their map in Blingdenstone, noting the path to escape the Underdark. The route is marked with a white hole to show it leads to the surface, not deeper down.
- Thorin notes that escaping has "been the goal the whole time"; Gyrgum jokes he'll be "blinking forever" once they get out.
- The first day of travel is relatively painless; the party feels refreshed.
- Zalthir expresses disappointment over the lack of a significant boon from Jimjar, wondering whether he was really an avatar and whether flipping a coin and making "annoying bets" was all he could do.
- Gyrgum reflects on the presence of deities in the material plane. The Forgotten Realms once let gods walk the prime material freely until an overarching deity forbade it, so a god appearing there usually implies a massive ritual. Thorin guesses Jimjar is a trickster god, "neither evil nor good."
- Gyrgum rolls Religion (17) and seeks insight from Bahamut. Bahamut rolls his dragon eye, notes that a gnomish god went into the Abyss and ended up in the prime material plane, and reminds Gyrgum "don't forget who's buttering your bread."
- Gyrgum promises to spend time contemplating, reading the prayers, and performing the appropriate rituals.
- The DM notes that under the updated rules clerics can change gods, so deities compete for followers. Bahamut, believing in the inevitable triumph of dragonkind, is in no rush and thinks the other dragons should "just sit down and chill out."
- Daz expresses concern regarding the potential return of Lolth's power in Menzoberranzan. Lolth once disappeared and her clerics became powerless before switching to other gods.
- Thorin is simply relieved that he and Glabbagool are still around and that they are finally heading toward the sunlight and his friends, the giants.

### 049.02 The Fungal Stalker

The party had not traveled far through the winding tunnels before they noticed something was wrong. Faint spores drifted through the stale air, stones lay disturbed along the path, and somewhere behind them came the labored sound of breathing that did not belong to any of them. Daz quietly conjured a spider and left it behind as the group pressed forward, watching through its eyes as a young, lean drow male stumbled along in their wake. A History check of 19 let Daz recognize the scout's symbol as that of House T'sarran, a minor house loyal to Ilvara. The figure was in a sorry state. His once-pale skin was marbled with fungal veins, mushrooms had taken root in patches of his white hair, and his eyes were an unsettling mix of drow red and fungal yellow. What struck the party most, however, was not his grotesque appearance but his staggering incompetence. For a drow, a people renowned for their supernatural stealth, he was doing a spectacularly terrible job of following anyone, a scandal his matron mother would never live down. An Investigation check (with advantage) confirmed he had been part of Ilvara's own party.

#### While traveling through the tunnels, the party discovers they are being followed by a corrupted drow scout.
- The party notices signs of being followed, including faint fungal spores in the air, disturbed stones, and the distant sound of labored breathing ("Is it Gollum?").
- Daz casts Find Familiar to summon a spider, leaving it behind as the party continues walking so his telepathic link will reveal who is following them.
- Gyrgum casts Bless on the three party members at the front.
- Through the familiar's eyes, Daz rolls History (19) and identifies the drow's symbol as that of House T'sarran, a minor house loyal to Ilvara.
- The party observes that the drow is heavily infected with fungal growth, with marbled skin, mushrooms rooting in his patchy white hair, and eyes a mix of drow red and fungal yellow.
- The scout hasn't noticed the familiar. He appears to have been trailing the party for some time and is waiting to see which way they turn.
- The party debates whether to capture, parley with, or ambush the inexperienced and poorly hidden stalker, noting with embarrassment how badly the drow is concealing himself. Daz: "I feel embarrassed for him."
- Gyrgum suggests playing on drow hierarchy ("we're your supervisors"), and the DM agrees that acting like the boss can help.
- The party discusses the fungal patterns they've seen before: the spore-zombies of the Neverlight Grove, which relied on Zuggtmoy's nearby presence, and the laughing, incoherent spore victim in Blingdenstone. This drow's condition is new.
- Zalthir clarifies that the foe they defeated was the Pudding King, on the slime demon's side, not the fungal queen, and the table recalls foreshadowing that this victory strengthened her: "It's like you knock down one warlord and another one takes his place."
- Daz rolls Investigation with advantage and recognizes the scout as one of Ilvara's party. Zalthir had assumed Ilvara was dead, but the DM confirms she is "very much not dead."

### 049.03 Confronting and Interrogating Valen

The party doubled back and restrained the hapless scout before he even knew they were upon him. Daz's Maximilian's Earthen Grasp held him fast after he failed a DC 15 Strength save, and Daz chose to restrain rather than crush. He immediately surrendered in a panic: "I, I, I, I surrender." He introduced himself as Valen of House T'sarran. Between desperate pleas and frightened glances at his own infected hands, Valen poured out everything he knew. The drow priestess Ilvara, the very woman from whose prison the party had once escaped, had been hunting them relentlessly across the Underdark with a force of twenty. That number had since been whittled down to eight through a series of disasters. First came the fiasco with the kuo-toa. Then the Duergar of Gracklstugh ambushed them, having mistaken Ilvara's party for the Ember Vanguard. The Ember Vanguard is in fact the party itself, but the Derro High Council, to avoid the embarrassment of a band of 5th-level adventurers defeating them, had recast it as a powerful drow strike force led by a 19th-level wizard who "casts wishes as if they were going out of style." Several scouts sent to the Neverlight Grove never returned. Worst of all, the survivors had begun to succumb to a creeping fungal infection. Ilvara herself had descended into madness, communing with a vast, pulsing fungal mass she believed to be the goddess Lolth and emerging from each trance more fanatical and more dangerous than before.

Valen revealed that Ilvara now spoke of an entity she called "the bride," who demanded that the party be hunted down and killed. The party exchanged knowing glances. They had attended the wedding rehearsal of Zuggtmoy, the Demon Queen of Fungi, in Neverlight Grove, and recognized exactly who this "bride" truly was, though they kept that to themselves. Valen also described a fracture running through what remained of Ilvara's followers. The junior priestess Asha Vandree still worshipped Lolth and accused Ilvara of heresy. The scarred warrior Jorlan Duskryn, a former lover whom Ilvara had discarded after his disfigurement, seethed with barely contained resentment. Gyrgum's Medicine check of 22 revealed that Valen had only days before becoming a docile spore servant, and that a lesser restoration could buy him another week. Thorin used the restorative power of Dawnbringer to treat the infection, halting the fungal growth and buying Valen precious time. Thorin couldn't resist quipping that it was "clear that you're a fun guy," and Valen gave the nervous laugh of a man laughing at a mob boss's joke. Grateful for the food the party shared, and terrified of being turned into a mindless spore servant, Valen agreed to guide them directly to the Fungal Altar in exchange for protection.

- The party rolls Medicine: Daz 13, Thorin 12, Gyrgum 22. The party's potion of greater healing and scroll of remove curse won't help; only a greater restoration would cure him, perhaps findable on the surface.
- Thorin rolls to see whether his impulsiveness takes over. On a 9 he shows restraint and defers to the group.
- The party agrees to heal Valen before making demands, to show good faith. Thorin uses Dawnbringer to cast lesser restoration on Valen, with the quip "it's clear that you're a fun guy," shrinking his fungal growths and granting him another week.

### 049.04 Planning the Ambush

Rather than race blindly for the surface and be hunted from behind, the party chose to strike first and on their own terms. As Gyrgum put it, they could "choose the place." Valen led them to a treacherous cavern where Ilvara had made camp. Elevated stone platforms were connected by bridges spanning a floor of mud and difficult terrain fifteen feet below, where anyone above held the high ground. Poisonous vents cycled beneath the bridges with a 15-foot radius, so crossing meant timing your run and hoping nobody grappled you mid-span. Corrupted pools of fungal liquid made the lower ground even more hazardous. Dominating the center of the camp was a massive, roughly thirty-foot-wide creature of living fungus, a monstrous heart-like growth that answered to Ilvara's command and was the reason everyone feared her. Valen's map of the area, rendered in a feverish and psychedelic scrawl, left something to be desired, but it was enough. The party realized they could reach only one of the two dissidents, Asha or Jorlan, without tipping someone off.

#### The adventurers coordinate with Valen to locate the Fungal Altar and prepare for a tactical engagement.
- The DM lays out the options: race for the Overbright, strike the Fungal Altar, or find a third plan. A fight would be tough.
- The party decides to launch a preemptive strike on the Fungal Altar rather than be hunted during their escape to the surface. Gyrgum argues that they can "choose the place," and Daz suggests planting traps.
- Daz proposes psychological warfare with his noise-making spells, "whispering" to shake the drow before any fight. Gyrgum offers Thaumaturgy for distractions.
- Zalthir points out that there are "a billion slimes not too far behind us."
- The group discusses using Glabbagool's near-invisibility when motionless and Daz's familiar for scouting and psychological warfare.
- The party prepares to exploit the divisions between Ilvara, Asha Vandree, and Jorlan Duskryn to cause chaos. Gyrgum notes that turning two of them would even the odds.
- Zalthir asks whether Valen is contagious. After lesser restoration his growths are less dangerous, since the fungus must reach a certain size before it spores. The DM reminds Zalthir that he blew his monastery gardening class ("I was meditating, but sure, I was there").
- Infection is manageable: Dawnbringer's restoration works once per long rest, and Gyrgum has lesser restoration as a second-level spell with many slots, though he'd "like to use my spells for other things." The DM: "illnesses don't have to be cured. They sometimes have to be managed."
- Eldeth is relieved she won't be among the walking zombies.
- Valen leads the party to a complex cavern system featuring elevated platforms, bridges, poisonous vents, and corrupted pools of fungal liquid.

### 049.05 Infiltrating the Drow Camp

On Valen's advice (a priestess of Lolth might kill any other creature, but perhaps not a spider), the party sent a spider familiar skittering silently across the camp to observe Asha Vandree on her platform. They found her in a state of barely contained fury, obsessively scraping fungal growth from her robes and muttering darkly about the theological absurdity of Ilvara's claims. "Lolth is not a bride," she grumbled. A closer look (Investigation 22) revealed that Asha had been carefully maintaining a private stash of sealed, uncontaminated food, a precaution that had kept her free of the infection spreading through the rest of the camp. The party checked the rules and found that a familiar can only deliver touch spells, so Daz couldn't speak through it with Minor Illusion. An Insight check told him that Asha, on the far edge of sanity, might be persuaded that the spider was an avatar of Lolth. Sensing an opening, Daz conjured a shimmering form of dancing light shaped like Lolth, fuzzy at the bottom, and let it fall like a column into the tiny spider before blinking out. As the session drew to a close, Asha Vandree seemed convinced that the goddess Lolth herself had sent her a sign. The DM awarded Daz an Inspiration point for the idea.

#### The party arrives at the treacherous drow camp and uses magic and deception to begin manipulating the fractured inhabitants.
- The party reaches the drow camp, a cavern with elevated platforms connected by bridges fifteen feet above a floor of mud and dirt. The floor is difficult terrain, and anyone above has the high-ground advantage.
- Valen provides a crude, psychedelic map of the area (what you get, the party is told, when you ask a fungus-crazed drow to draw), highlighting poisonous vents and corrupted pools of fungal liquid beneath the bridges.
- The vents cycle, so crossing requires timing. Being grappled on a bridge "could get awkward." The vents have a 15-foot radius. Gyrgum compares it to the Rick and Morty "Vats of Acid" episode.
- The drow on the platforms may be immune to poison. Five others are on the platform with Ilvara.
- The party observes a massive, roughly 30-foot-wide heart fungus creature in the center of the camp, under Ilvara's command. It is the reason everyone fears her: "it's one thing to turn into a zombie spore. It's another thing to be consumed by a spore demon."
- The party realizes they must choose between approaching Asha and approaching Jorlan; trying both would tip someone off.
- Daz lists his options (Minor Illusion, Dancing Lights, Unseen Servant, an unprepared Invisibility), and Gyrgum offers Thaumaturgy.
- Daz summons a spider familiar to scout the camp. Valen advises a spider because a priestess of Lolth might kill any other creature. Zalthir adds that she will "be more likely to listen to us" if it's a spider.
- The spider observes Asha Vandree on her platform, obsessively cleaning herself of fungal growth and muttering about the theological errors of Ilvara's "bride" claims: "That's the whole point of being a priestess of Lolth. You don't get married." Looking at her hands, she says: "well, at least I haven't been turned into fungi."
- The party discovers (Investigation 22, plus a Medicine check) that Asha has a stash of sealed, clean food, which has likely protected her from the fungal infection affecting the others.
- Zalthir asks whether spells can be cast through a familiar. The party checks the rules: a familiar within 100 feet can deliver touch spells only, so Minor Illusion (30-foot range) can't be used through it.
- Daz rolls Insight (12) and senses that Asha, on the edge of madness, might accept the spider as an avatar of Lolth and follow it.
- Daz casts Dancing Lights (confirming the party is within 120 feet), shaping the lights into a medium, drow-like form of Lolth with a fuzzy lower half, then letting the light fall like a column into the spider and blink out.
- The session ends as Asha appears to be convinced she is witnessing a sign from her goddess. The DM awards Daz an Inspiration point: "That was a great idea."

## NPCs
### Glabbagool
A sentient grey ooze (formerly a gelatinous cube) who travels with the party. The DM fixed his visual token at the start of the session. He can be practically invisible while motionless, which the party noted as a tactical asset.

### Bahamut
The Platinum Dragon deity who communicates with Gyrgum. He offers insight into how a gnomish god went into the Abyss and ended up in the prime material plane, is pleased to have helped the party, and reminds the cleric not to forget "who's buttering your bread." Like all good dragons, he believes in the inevitable triumph of dragonkind and sees no reason to rush it.

### Valen
A young, lean drow scout from House T'sarran. His skin is marbled with fungal veins, mushrooms grow from his patchy white hair, and his eyes are a mix of drow red and fungal yellow. He was sent by Ilvara as an advance scout, did an embarrassingly poor job of tracking the party, and surrendered immediately when caught. He is disillusioned with Ilvara's madness and fears becoming a "sporish thingy-moblop." After Thorin's lesser restoration via Dawnbringer bought him a week, he shared the party's food, drew them a map, and agreed to guide them to the camp in exchange for protection.

### Ilvara
A drow priestess who has been relentlessly hunting the party across the Underdark since their escape from her prison. She had made promises to two powerful people in Menzoberranzan that the party's escape ruined. According to Valen, she found a mushroom in the Neverlight Grove, communes with a giant pulsing fungal mass she believes is Lolth, and is now fanatically devoted to an entity called "the bride," who demands the party's death. She commands a massive heart fungus creature and can track the party through the Underdark's pervasive fungi. She discarded her lover Jorlan after he was scarred.

### Asha Vandree
A junior drow priestess (under-priestess) in Ilvara's camp who remains loyal to Lolth and accuses Ilvara of heresy for embracing the fungal corruption, insisting that Lolth is no bride. She is obsessively clean, scraping fungus from her clothes, and maintains a secret stash of sealed food that has kept her uninfected. She is on the far edge of sanity. The party attempts to manipulate her with a false divine vision of Lolth, and she appears convinced.

### Jorlan Duskryn
A maimed drow warrior and former lover of Ilvara, cast aside after being scarred ("It was very awkward"). He feels dissed by the breakup and, pushed further by the fungus, has turned homicidal toward Ilvara. Valen identifies him as someone who might sabotage her mission out of spite.

### Eldeth
A companion traveling with the party who expresses relief upon learning that the party's restorative magic can manage the fungal plague.

## Locations
### Blingdenstone
A settlement in the Underdark where the party finalizes their plans to reach the surface, reviewing their map and noting the path to the exit.

### Neverlight Grove
A fungal-heavy location where Ilvara found a mushroom through which she now communes with a giant pulsing fungal mass, and where she began her descent into fanatical devotion to "the bride." Earlier victims there were fed evil spores and turned into demon-possessed spore creatures.

A cavernous area featuring elevated stone platforms connected by bridges fifteen feet above a floor of mud and dirt that counts as difficult terrain. Fungal gardens tended by docile spore-servants have sprouted in the camp.

### The Overbright
The surface world above the Underdark, which Underdark dwellers call the "Overbright." It is the party's ultimate destination for this leg of their journey.
