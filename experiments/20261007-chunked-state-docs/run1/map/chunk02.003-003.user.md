### 003.01 Planning the Great Escape

The morning — if such a word could even apply to the lightless depths of the Underdark — brought with it a rare and precious gift: the cell door had been left open. Everything beyond remained “dark and miserable,” but the prisoners had a brief window in which to act. Their minds were linked in silent communion through the rapport spores of Stool, the small myconid sprout who had become an unlikely ally. The spores allowed the prisoners to communicate telepathically with selected creatures near them, letting the conspirators coordinate without openly announcing their intentions to every paranoid captive in the pen.

The group debated whether to make a straight dash for freedom or risk a detour to the guard tower’s armory. Their confiscated personal belongings were held elsewhere, in Ilvara’s quarters, but the armory offered weapons and armor that might make the difference between escape and recapture. Thorin, the dwarf warrior, felt the absence of his family’s memento — a pendant fashioned from a giant’s tooth — like a wound. “My character has a personal motivation to retrieve his memento of his family,” he insisted, though the immediate decision was to arm themselves first and recover their own property later.

The outpost’s interior chambers were dimly illuminated by lanterns containing phosphorescent fungi, while the exterior remained dark. The drow in the nearby barracks were inattentive conscripts accustomed to uneventful duty, and the guards at the lookout watched outward rather than back toward the prison. Ordinary footsteps might pass unnoticed, but a scream, a burst of fire, or the sound of the massive cell door being forced would bring the outpost down upon the prisoners. The group therefore settled on a cautious infiltration of the guard post, with everyone else ready to charge if the attempt failed. Prince Derendil offered muscle rather than subtlety, while Buppido argued that the “divine plan” required somebody else to secure the weapons first.

Zalthir, the group’s most capable infiltrator, went forward with Daz, whose magical tricks might salvage the situation if they were discovered. Fate had other ideas. Zalthir tripped as he entered, and Daz’s attempt to catch him only kicked a can across the floor. Two drow who had been idly playing cards looked up from their game. “That went well,” Zalthir observed as the hope of a quiet escape shattered.

### 003.02 Skirmish in the Guard Tower

With no weapons and no armor, the prisoners found themselves face to face with hardened drow soldiers and the most dangerous of them all: Imbros, a seasoned Elite Warrior whose formidable resilience was immediately apparent. Thorin rushed into the room and attempted to sucker-punch and shove the Elite Warrior, hoping to remove the greatest threat before the drow could react. His fist missed. Imbros struck him with his short sword, attempting to subdue rather than kill such a valuable prisoner. Thorin’s second wind briefly kept him upright, but another drow soon cut him down. He slipped toward death, succeeding on one death save before failing another.

The struggle became a desperate, chaotic brawl in the cramped lower floor of the guard tower. Zalthir repeatedly attacked and tried to force the Elite Warrior away from the doorway, only to suffer an extraordinary streak of failed strikes. Daz tried to attack one of the ordinary guards with Mind Sliver, but the drow resisted the intrusion. Above them, the other prisoners exploited the distraction: Eldeth, Turvy, Jimjar, Sarith, Buppido, Topsy, Shuushar, and others disengaged from the melee, scrambled upstairs, and armed themselves with short swords and hand crossbows. Prince Derendil remained below to hold the enemy in place, taking a short-sword wound as he entered the fighting and answering with both claws.

Sarith’s hatred for his former captors proved lethal. Once armed, he descended upon one of the ordinary drow, cursing the guard as he struck twice and killed him. A barrage of hand-crossbow bolts followed from the upper floor. Eldeth’s shot missed, as did Buppido’s despite his loudly asserted expertise, but one of the twins, Topsy or Turvy, and Jimjar found their marks. Ront then squeezed into the room and killed the second ordinary guard with a short sword. Shuushar the Awakened turned his own crossbow on the Elite Warrior and struck him as well.

Gyrgum fought through the press of bodies to reach the fallen Thorin. After weighing the merits of merely stabilizing him against restoring him to the battle, the cleric placed a hand upon the dwarf and cast Cure Wounds. The spell restored Thorin completely, and Gyrgum ensured that a short sword was passed down to him. Thorin then disengaged and climbed to the armory in search of armor rather than remain trapped beneath the crush of combatants.

### 003.03 The Fall of the Prince

Daz helped turn the tide by abandoning the uncertain Mind Sliver for Magic Missile. The first volley of three glowing darts dealt twelve points of damage to the Elite Warrior; a second volley left Imbros at six hit points. The spell’s unerring force proved ideal in the constricted guard room, where ordinary ranged attacks were difficult and the Elite Warrior’s defenses were formidable.

Yet Imbros remained deadly. Cursing the revolting slaves in Undercommon, he concentrated his poisoned blade on Prince Derendil. The Quaggoth suffered a devastating sequence of wounds and was slain. The self-proclaimed cursed elven prince fell where he had held the doorway, and the escaped prisoners were left one companion lighter.

Zalthir finally landed a blow and pushed the Elite Warrior into the doorway. Sarith, burning with personal hatred, was tempted to begin shouting abuse at his former captor, threatening to draw the attention of the rest of the outpost. Gyrgum answered with an unconventional religious appeal: “Think of your spirituality and how much better it would be if all this came out.” Sarith considered the suggestion and replied, “Never thought of that.” Steadied by this newfound spiritual focus, he transformed his fury into action and delivered the killing blow to Imbros.

### 003.04 Arming the Rebellion

The guard tower fell silent save for the ragged breathing of the survivors. The party had prevailed largely because the prisoners they had befriended joined the fight; without those allies, the breakout would have ended in a swift and inglorious defeat. They wasted no time stripping the tower of everything useful: short swords, hand crossbows, armor, one hundred feet of rope, and six bags of caltrops that could be scattered to obstruct pursuit.

Thorin claimed a short sword, studded leather armor, and a shield. Gyrgum kept rope and a bag of caltrops, though the available martial weapons were unfamiliar to him. Daz briefly considered leather armor before realizing that he lacked the training to cast effectively while wearing it. Zalthir declined the available weapons because they would make him less effective rather than more. Battered, bloodied, and mourning Prince Derendil, the prisoners nevertheless stood armed at last, ready to continue their flight into the darkness of the Underdark.

#### Following the skirmish in the guard tower, the party and their allies scavenge for equipment and discuss the tactical limitations of their current skills.

- The survivors inventory the remaining short swords, hand crossbows, armor, rope, and caltrops, distributing equipment among the escaped prisoners.

- Thorin claims a short sword, studded leather armor, and a shield.
- Gyrgum gives up the hand crossbow he had considered taking because his spells and cantrips are more effective.
- Gyrgum takes one hundred feet of rope, declaring, “I like rope.”
- Six bags of caltrops are added to the group’s shared supplies. They are intended to slow or obstruct drow pursuing the escapees.
- Gyrgum personally takes a bag of caltrops “for fun.”
- Daz initially takes leather armor, then learns that he has no armor proficiency and abandons the idea.
- The group discusses how armor proficiency affects spellcasters. Wizards are no longer categorically prohibited from wearing armor, but doing so without proficiency creates serious limitations.
- The conversation turns to multi-classing. A level of fighter would grant armor training and Second Wind, potentially improving a wizard’s durability.
- Gyrgum is proficient only with simple weapons, making the captured short swords poor choices for him, though still preferable to complete helplessness.
- Zalthir determines that none of the recovered weapons suit his fighting style and that using them would make him less effective.
- The party leaves the tower better equipped but still without its original belongings, including Thorin’s giant’s-tooth pendant.
- With the immediate guards defeated, the prisoners are armed and positioned to continue the escape when the session resumes.

## NPCs

### Stool

A small, sentient fungus creature known as a myconid sprout. He happily fills the cell with rapport spores, allowing the party and other prisoners to communicate telepathically and coordinate their escape without speaking aloud. His spores can connect multiple intelligent creatures for an hour, though communication remains limited by proximity. The party’s discussion establishes that linked creatures can direct thoughts selectively rather than broadcasting every thought to everyone.

### Buppido

A talkative derro prisoner who is reluctant to lead the escape and suggests that the “divine plan” requires somebody else to secure the weapons first. Once armed, he loudly insists that he is an expert in all matters related to hand crossbows. His confidence is not entirely justified, as his bolt misses the target. During the division of equipment, the group jokes that Buppido should receive one of everything “because he’s magnificent.”

### Prince Derendil

A Quaggoth who claims to be an elven prince transformed by a curse. Most Quaggoths are described as violent creatures bent on destruction and mayhem, making Derendil’s identity and behavior notably strange. He freely admits that he is not stealthy but offers his strength to the breakout, proposing to follow once the others obtain weapons and then “start bashing heads.”

When the stealth attempt fails, Derendil remains in the lower guard room while many of the other prisoners rush upstairs. He accepts a short-sword wound to enter the battle and attacks with both claws. His presence helps occupy Imbros and keeps the route to the armory open, but it also leaves him exposed to the Elite Warrior’s poisoned blade. Imbros ultimately kills him during the final phase of the fight.

### Imbros, the Drow Elite Warrior

A formidable and deadly drow commander stationed in the guard tower, armed with a poisoned short sword and possessing far greater endurance than the ordinary guards. The party recognizes immediately that defeating him while unarmed will be extremely difficult.

He initially attempts to subdue Thorin rather than kill him because the prisoners are valuable. Once the escape becomes a full revolt, he curses the slaves in Undercommon and turns his attention to Prince Derendil. He survives unarmed attacks, short-sword blows, volleys of crossbow bolts, and repeated Magic Missiles before finally being overwhelmed. He wounds Thorin, slays Derendil, and nearly stops the escape before Sarith delivers the killing blow.

### Jimjar

A deep gnome prisoner who participates in the coordinated rush to the armory while the party distracts the drow below. Once armed, he fires a hand crossbow from the upper level and lands a shot on one of the ordinary guards for five points of damage. His accurate covering fire contributes to leaving the guard vulnerable to Ront’s finishing attack.

### Sarith Kzekarit

A drow prisoner with a deeply personal grievance against his former captors. Once he reaches the armory and acquires a short sword, he attacks one of the guards while hurling bitter insults, striking twice and killing him.

During the final confrontation, Sarith is tempted to shout at Imbros loudly enough to compromise the escape. Gyrgum calms him with an appeal to spirituality and meditation. Sarith accepts the unexpected advice, focuses his fury, and delivers the killing blow to the Elite Warrior.

### Eldeth

A shield dwarf scout and fellow prisoner who uses the initial confusion to reach the armory. She returns with a hand crossbow and takes position at the opening between floors to provide covering fire. Her first shot at a drow guard misses, but she remains part of the coordinated barrage that pins down the remaining enemies.

### Ront

A hulking orc prisoner who charges into the lower guard room once enough space has been created. He squeezes past the other combatants, attacks the already wounded second drow guard with a short sword, and kills him, leaving the party free to concentrate on Imbros.

### Shuushar the Awakened

A kuo-toa prisoner who arms himself with one of the limited hand crossbows. He joins the assault from above and successfully strikes the Drow Elite Warrior for five points of damage, contributing to the accumulation of wounds that finally brings Imbros down.

## Locations

### The Slave Pen

A dark and miserable holding area where the party and several other prisoners are kept. The cell is secured by a door too heavy to break without producing an enormous racket. The drow assume escape is impossible because the prisoners possess neither weapons nor the ability to cast their way through the barrier.

When the door is unexpectedly left open, the prisoners gain a brief opportunity to act. Stool’s spores fill the pen, connecting the captives telepathically while they finalize their route and decide who will approach the guard tower first.

### Velkynvelve Guard Tower

A hollowed-out hanging stalactite tower within the drow outpost, reached by rope bridges, where the party confronts its captors. Its lower chamber serves as a cramped guard room containing a table, three chairs, a smaller side table, and spider-silk webbing fitted with equipment hooks. Two drow are playing cards here when Zalthir and Daz stumble into view.

A stairway connects the lower room to the armory above. The confined floor plan and blocked doorway complicate movement, forcing combatants to squeeze past one another or disengage to avoid attacks. Once the prisoners reach the upper floor, its elevation provides an effective vantage point for volleys of hand-crossbow fire into the chamber below.

The tower’s interior is dimly illuminated by phosphorescent fungal lanterns. After the death of Imbros and the two guards, it becomes a temporary staging ground where the escapees arm themselves and prepare for pursuit.

### Armory

Located on the upper level of the Velkynvelve Guard Tower, this room holds the outpost’s spare arms, armor, and useful supplies. Its contents include short swords, six hand crossbows, studded leather armor, rope, and six bags of caltrops.

The prisoners’ own confiscated gear is not stored here, requiring a later detour if they intend to recover it. During the battle, most of the captive allies scramble into the armory, seize weapons, and fire down into the lower chamber while the party and Prince Derendil hold the drow in place.

## Items

### Giant's Tooth Pendant

A personal memento belonging to Thorin and reminding him of his family. It was taken by the drow upon his capture and stored with the party’s other belongings outside the guard-tower armory. Recovering it gives Thorin a personal reason to risk reaching those quarters before escaping Velkynvelve entirely.

### Spider Silk Webbing

Durable webbing used by the drow to create hooks and equipment storage within the lower chamber of the guard tower. It hangs near the tables and chairs in the room where the card-playing guards discover Zalthir and Daz.

### Short Sword

Standard drow weaponry carried by the guards and stored in the armory. Imbros uses a poisoned short sword to incapacitate Thorin and kill Prince Derendil. Sarith acquires a short sword upstairs, kills one of the ordinary guards with it, and later strikes down Imbros. Ront uses another to kill the second guard.

After the battle, Thorin claims a short sword. Gyrgum and Daz note that they are trained only in simple weapons, making the captured martial blades less useful to them.

### Hand Crossbow

Small ranged weapons stored in the upper armory. Six are available, forcing the prisoners to keep track of who has one during the fight. Eldeth, Topsy, Turvy, Jimjar, Buppido, and Shuushar use them to fire into the lower guard room. Several shots miss, but the bolts that land badly wound both an ordinary drow and the Elite Warrior.

The group does not track individual crossbow bolts after the battle.

### Studded Leather Armor

Light armor reinforced with metal studs and stored in the guard-tower armory. Thorin claims a suit along with his short sword and shield. Daz briefly considers wearing leather armor but discovers that he has no armor proficiency, making it unsuitable for him without additional training.

### Caltrop

Six bags of small iron spikes recovered from the armory. The party recognizes that they can be scattered behind the escapees to injure or slow pursuing drow. Gyrgum claims one bag personally, while the remaining bags are added to the group’s shared inventory.

### Rope

One hundred feet of sturdy rope recovered from the guard tower. Gyrgum takes all of it, anticipating that it will prove useful for navigating the bridges, drops, and difficult terrain of the Underdark.

## Spells

### Rapport Spores

A natural myconid ability used by Stool to establish telepathic communication among intelligent creatures gathered near him. The effect lasts for one hour, which the party observes is effectively an entire adventuring day when measured against six-second combat rounds.

The link allows creatures to communicate silently with selected participants rather than forcing a universal broadcast. It enables the prisoners in the cell to know when Zalthir and Daz are discovered and to coordinate their immediate rush toward the guard tower.

### Minor Illusion

A simple magical trick known by Daz and proposed as a way to create a distracting sound after the failed stealth attempt. Daz considers using it when a kicked can alerts the card-playing guards, but the drow have already seen the intruders, leaving no opportunity for the false noise to explain away their presence.

### Cure Wounds

Divine energy channeled by Gyrgum through physical contact. Gyrgum chooses it over Spare the Dying and Healing Word because he can reach Thorin directly and wants to restore more than the minimum amount of vitality. The spell heals Thorin for sixteen points, returning him to full strength after he succeeds on one death save and fails another.

### Magic Missile

Three glowing darts of magical force conjured by Daz. The spell cannot miss, cannot be blocked by ordinary defenses, functions at close range without suffering the limitations of a ranged weapon attack, and proves ideal against the heavily defended Elite Warrior.

Daz’s first casting inflicts twelve points of damage. His second leaves Imbros at six hit points and helping turn the battle decisively in the prisoners’ favor.

### Toll the Dead

A ranged cleric cantrip available to Gyrgum. It forces its target to resist the sound of a dolorous bell and deals greater damage to a creature that has already been wounded, increasing from a d8 to a d12. Gyrgum turns to it after recognizing that Tasha’s Caustic Brew would also strike an ally in the crowded lower chamber.

### Mind Sliver

A psionic attack used by Daz against one of the ordinary drow guards. The target makes the required Intelligence save, so the spell deals no damage. Daz subsequently abandons the uncertain attack in favor of Magic Missile’s guaranteed force damage.
