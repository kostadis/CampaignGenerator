### 019.01 Return to Whorlstone Caverns

The DM laid out the party's "quest log." The Keepers of the Flame want only the egg, and want no one, including the dragon Themberchaud, to learn it was ever lost. Errde, head of the Stone Guards, wants two things: proof that the derro are behind the chaos in the city, and to know who the assassins are. Werz wants the assassins off his back, and in return offers better trade deals with the gnomes. Errde's reward is support, the return of the party's weapons, and "a pat on the back." If the party fled, the Keepers had promised revenge. The Stone Guards might tell the drow where the party was, or even help hunt them down. The DM's advice: "once you've got one homicidal group of crazed lunatics chasing you, what's another, right?" Thorin proposed lying to everybody and escaping on the dragon. The DM stressed that the real challenge would come later: deciding who gets the information once the party has it.

Returning to the winding tunnels of the Whorlstone Caverns, the party moved northward toward the obelisk chamber where the egg was rumored to be held. Zalthir chose the route: the same path as before, then north toward the egg. Their allies came along this time, including Flumph, Pelek's ghost, Jimjar and Spiderbait. Their path was cut short when two duergar sentries stepped from the shadows, declaring the hallway the exclusive territory of the Empty-Scabbard Killers. The quieter of the two, a grunting and largely silent duergar named Grimgrim, whom the party quickly nicknamed Bob, stood impassively. His partner, Brobrek, who asked to be called by his nickname Rust, did all the talking. When the party mentioned their shared hatred of the Demogorgon cultists lurking deeper in the caverns, Rust's eyes lit up with sudden interest.

### 019.02 Negotiating with the Empty-Scabbard Killers

Rust escorted the group to meet the assassins' boss, a formidable duergar soulblade named Eldgrim, who presided over a chamber guarded by several more of his deadly kin. Eldgrim was pragmatic and businesslike, cutting through pleasantries with the efficiency of a man who had long since stopped pretending to be anything other than what he was. His first offer was only free passage through his territory to reach the derro. Daz then pressed him on Werz. Eldgrim quoted 200 gold pieces to "whitelist" Werz, or 20 gold pieces if the party dealt with his Demogorgon problem. Daz protested paying to solve someone else's problem, and Eldgrim relented. A deal was struck: if the party eliminated the Demogorgon worshippers plaguing the caverns, Eldgrim would personally see to it that a merchant named Werz was removed from the assassins' list of targets — free of charge, as a gesture of professional goodwill. Eldgrim scoffed at the idea of proof. The assassins carry no talismans, and he warned that even a signed paper would be dismissed as a forgery.

### 019.03 Theft and Fire in the Assassin's Lair

With the deal in place, Daz seized on an opportunity. He had already spotted papers on Eldgrim's desk. Flattering Eldgrim with talk of writing a book and immortalizing the fame of the Empty-Scabbard Killers, he persuaded the assassin leader to walk over to his desk and sign his name on a piece of parchment. Meanwhile, Thorin — genuinely baffled by the logic of a group of killers who carried empty scabbards — blurted out his confusion aloud, prompting one of the soulblades to manifest a shimmering psionic weapon in wordless explanation. The distraction was all Daz needed. While the room's attention was pulled away, he rifled through the papers on Eldgrim's desk. Aided by Eldgrim's "awful" passive perception, he slipped away a document bearing the royal seal of Gracklstugh and the signature of the Deepking himself — proof that the crown was secretly in league with the assassins.

The theft could not go unnoticed forever, and the party knew it. The DM warned that "there's a clock," though the assassins would not work it out "like tomorrow." In a desperate bid to destroy the remaining evidence and cover their tracks, Daz staged a violent coughing fit, and when Zalthir came over to help him he thumped the dragonborn hard in the chest — Zalthir's cue to breathe a gout of fire directly onto the desk. The remaining papers went up in flames. Eldgrim erupted in fury, cursing the party and ordering them out of his territory under threat of death, warning that if they ever returned, the Empty-Scabbard Killers would deal with them the way they should have from the start. The adventurers made their exit with as much dignity as they could muster, passing a pair of invisible guards on their way out. The guards fist-bumped them as they went. The DM credited the table with "some very elegant role-playing there and some very successful dice rolls," and Thorin proposed "Plausible deniability" as the group's long-needed name.

### 019.05 The Obelisk Chamber

The chamber that greeted them was vast and breathtaking. Faerzress energy pulsed through the air alongside the soft glow of bioluminescent fungi, and shafts of pale white light fell upon a great rocky mesa at the cavern's center. Crude ramps wound upward to the mesa's topmost tiers, which were connected by a sturdy bridge. Atop one tier sat the dragon egg, and atop the other loomed a fifty-foot-tall obelisk of smooth black metal, its surface marred by only a handful of imperfections, as though pieces of it had been chipped away over time.

Beside the obelisk stood a female derro named Plinki, stroking its surface with the reverent intensity of a scientist on the edge of a great discovery. She squealed with delight each time the obelisk flashed, scribbling notes in a small book and paying no attention whatsoever to the armed group that had just walked into her chamber. She had a crowd of minions, some possibly invisible or hidden. The DM pointed out that the party didn't strictly need the egg; they could destroy it and report that to Themberchaud. Gyrgum, drawn irresistibly toward the egg by his devotion to Bahamut, strode boldly forward and announced himself as a cleric of the dragon god come to worship at the egg. Plinki's eyes went wide with ecstatic belief — she declared that Demogorgon himself had sent this holy man to acknowledge the greatness of her achievement, and she scrambled down from the mesa to greet him.

Plinki eagerly explained her work as she led Gyrgum toward the egg. The egg had been stolen from the Keepers of the Flame, she said, and she had spent untold hours using the obelisk's power to infuse it with demonic energy in honor of Demogorgon. She showed him the intricate incantations she had inscribed across the egg's surface, practically vibrating with pride. The party exchanged uneasy glances — they had already suspected the egg was corrupted, but hearing it confirmed so cheerfully by its architect made the situation feel considerably more dire. Earlier, Daz had rolled a natural 20 on Arcana and read the egg's etchings: it would hatch a two-headed dragon, likely possessed by some demonic power. Thorin had taken the same etchings for festival decorations.

### 019.06 Confrontation at the Obelisk

The fragile peace shattered the moment Plinki revealed what she truly needed to complete her ritual: the beating heart of a cleric of Bahamut. Gyrgum's cover evaporated in an instant. The table briefly debated what a cleric of Bahamut would do about a demon-corrupted egg. The DM consulted Gemini, which named four camps of Bahamut's clerics: destroy it, cleanse it, study it, or seek guidance while holding it. Thorin wondered aloud about a dragon omelette. Gyrgum grandstanded loudly, warning Plinki and her followers that Daz would not hesitate to smash the egg if they did not stand down — a drow, after all, could never be trusted. The cultists hesitated, but Plinki's madness won out, and she screamed for her followers to attack.

### 019.07 Ambush at the Mesa

Zalthir moved first. He had already jumped up the tiers to stand right beside Plinki. He seized her in an iron grip, pinning her in place before unleashing a rapid and brutal series of strikes that left the derro reeling. His first attack totalled 27 against her AC 13, and he spent a focus point for another strike, dealing 11 damage. Gyrgum, his earlier warmth toward the egg curdled into righteous fury at the betrayal, channeled a surge of devastating necrotic energy directly into Plinki. This was a 2nd-level Inflict Wounds; she succeeded on a Constitution save and still took 12 damage. She managed to partially resist the worst of it, but the force of the blast still left her badly wounded and struggling in Zalthir's grip. The battle for the obelisk chamber had begun in earnest, and the fate of the corrupted dragon egg — and perhaps the city of Gracklstugh itself — hung in the balance. The session paused mid-combat.

## NPCs

### Errde

The head of the Stone Guards who seeks proof of derro involvement in the city's chaos and information about the mysterious assassins. Her reward for success is support and the return of the party's weapons. The DM expects that proof of the Deepking's ties to the assassins would likely send her "all homicidal on the Deepking."

### Themberchaud

A dragon whose potential replacement is the very egg the party is currently tracking. The Keepers of the Flame do not want him to know about the egg's existence. The DM noted the party could destroy the egg and tell him about it.

### Werz

The merchant the Empty-Scabbard Killers have been targeting. He wants the assassins off his back and offers better trade deals with the gnomes. Eldgrim agreed to "wipe the slate for Mr. Werz" in exchange for the party dealing with the Demogorgon cultists.

### Rust

A duergar member of the Empty-Scabbard Killers, also known as Brobrek, who intercepts the party, explains their territory, and escorts them to meet the assassins' boss Eldgrim. He insists on his proper name or the nickname "Rust," and is visibly excited by the chance to strike at the Demogorgon-allied derro.

### Grimgrim (“Bob”)

A quiet and non-talkative duergar assassin and partner to Rust, guarding the halls of the Empty-Scabbard Killers. He demonstrates his faction's unique capabilities by manifesting a psionic blade when questioned by Thorin about the empty scabbard name. Thorin also called him "Dim."

### Eldgrim

A powerful duergar soulblade and leader within the Empty-Scabbard Killers. He is pragmatic and business-minded, willing to negotiate deals for the right price or service. He proves susceptible to flattery regarding his fame, which allows Daz to orchestrate a successful distraction and theft from his desk. The DM notes he is "tougher than your average Soul Blade" and is backed by several more soulblades. He took a liking to Thorin's bluntness, was dismissive of any "proof" the party might carry away, and after the fire banished the party on pain of death.

### Flumph

A strange, telepathic Underdark creature accompanying the party. It feeds on psychic energy and expresses dark amusement regarding the party's safety, refusing to enter the Empty-Scabbard Killers' lair because a mind flayer was inside, preferring to wait outside and pick up the party's traumatic vibes. Flumph knows only the routes where there was psychic energy to eat, and was disappointed that no one's brain got devoured: "There's still time."

### Plinki

A fanatical female derro mad scientist dedicated to Demogorgon. She has been using a massive black obelisk to infuse a stolen dragon egg with demonic energy. She is easily swayed by Gyrgum's clever deception but turns violently hostile upon revealing that the ritual requires the beating heart of a cleric of Bahamut. She is ambushed, grappled, and severely wounded by the party during the ensuing confrontation. She shows off her inscriptions with fannish glee and commands a crowd of minions, some possibly hidden or invisible. Her AC is 13.

A duergar city in the Underdark filled with political intrigue and dangerous factions, including the Keepers of the Flame and the Stone Guards.

### Whorlstone Caverns

A complex underground network beneath Gracklstugh filled with competing factions, including duergar assassins and derro demon worshippers. The party navigated its tunnels, uncovered a royal conspiracy, and ultimately confronted a mad cultist near a corrupted dragon egg. An intersection past the assassins' territory offers two routes to the obelisk chamber. One is the main entrance, behind big double doors. The other runs north past the chambers of the Gray Ghosts and the Council of Savants. An unexplored northwest passage remains unknown even to Flumph.

### Empty-Scabbard Killers' Territory

A restricted area within the Whorlstone Caverns under the protection of duergar assassins who utilize psionic weaponry. The party negotiated here and covertly stole a document linking the Deepking to the assassins. The territory is declared "off limits to all other factions" and is patrolled by invisible soulblades. The party is now banned from returning.

### Obelisk Chamber

A massive, well-lit cavern within the Whorlstone Caverns featuring a central rocky mesa with crudely formed ramps and a sturdy bridge connecting its topmost tiers. It houses a shrine to Demogorgon, a 50-foot-tall smooth black metal obelisk, and a stolen dragon egg undergoing a dark ritual. The party's confrontation with Plinki and her cultists erupts into open combat here. The mesa tiers stand 10 feet high, too tall to attack across from the floor without climbing.

## Items

### Psionic Blade

A shimmering weapon manifested through mental will by the duergar assassins of the Empty-Scabbard Killers, explaining why their scabbards remain perpetually empty. One was demonstrated by Bob when Thorin questioned the logic of the faction's name. Gyrgum wondered why anyone would carry a scabbard for a psionic blade.

### Empty-Scabbard Killers Hit List (the Royal Contract of Gracklstugh)

A highly sensitive document bearing the royal seal of Gracklstugh and signed by the Deepking, proving a clandestine alliance between the monarchy and the Empty-Scabbard Killers. It was covertly stolen from Eldgrim's desk by Daz while Thorin created a distraction. The DM warned that the assassins will eventually discover it is missing, though not right away.

### Corrupted Dragon Egg

A large red dragon egg stolen from the Keepers of the Flame. It sits atop one of the rocky mesa's topmost tiers in the obelisk chamber and is covered in dark incantations, being infused with demonic energy by Plinki to birth a two-headed servant of Demogorgon. Daz's natural-20 Arcana check showed it will hatch two-headed and has probably been possessed by some kind of demonic power. The DM notes red dragons are "notorious for being difficult young dragons" and "this one's going to be particularly difficult."

### Black Metal Obelisk

A towering 50-foot-tall structure of smooth black metal with minor imperfections, as though parts of it had been chipped away. It flashes with power and causes the surrounding faerzress energy to flare when interacted with. Plinki has been using it to channel demonic energy into the corrupted dragon egg.

## Spells

### Darkness

Employed by Zalthir to create a mobile sphere of magical shadow. It was used first to conceal the party's approach as they snuck through the caverns, and later to obscure the battlefield and gain a tactical advantage over Plinki and her cultists. Zalthir precast it to hover behind the group, and Thorin asked for it to be centered on him since he can still see within it. The DM wryly noted that a big black moving blob "like that's not like ominous in the underdark." Zalthir attacked Plinki with advantage from it; the DM ruled the cultists' darkvision does not see through it.

### Bless

A divine blessing cast by Gyrgum upon his companions just as combat began, bolstering their focus, accuracy, and resilience during the chaotic skirmish at the obelisk. Gyrgum explained it as a d4 bonus on attack rolls and saving throws for the next minute or while he holds concentration. He framed the casting to Plinki as a blessing "in my magnanimity," and Zalthir added the d4 to his second attack.

### Inflict Wounds

A divine spell cast by Gyrgum to channel destructive necrotic energy directly into Plinki. She partially resisted the brunt of the magic but still suffered severe injuries from the attack. Cast at 2nd level, it forced a Constitution save, which the table noted is new to this edition. Plinki made the save and still took 12 points of damage.
