# Chapter 59 Questions, Answers and a Key

Date: Jun 8th, 2026

## Scenes

### 059.01 Reporting to Bookwyrm

The party returned to the Great Chapter House to report their findings to Bookwyrm, the First Reader of Candlekeep. Moon elf scribes moved to block their entrance before Bookwyrm herself waved them through. They found her in conference with Kei Tigersteel — a slender human-elf of around fifty years, his bald scalp framed by sharp black side whiskers chiseled into a goatee — who served as the spiritual leader of the library and priest of the Temple of Oghma. The party had attempted to listen in on the conference beforehand but failed to exceed the perception threshold, hearing nothing. When Bookwyrm questioned them, they shared only the broadest strokes of their investigation, declining to name suspects and deflecting her pointed follow-up — "was there nobody that you wished to start your investigation with?" — by noting she should read nothing into their choices of whom to see next. Bookwyrm dismissed Kei Tigersteel with instructions to take Janussi's body to the Shrine of Oghma for purification. She then led the party to Deneir's Sanctum, a shrine dedicated to the art of writing, and offered it as their private office, assigning the eager acolyte Fembris Lancer to fetch suspects and the moon elf Vooshadi Moonriver to stand guard at the door. The party noted that Bookwyrm appeared to have a strong preference for them to speak to Sylvira Savikas, though she never said so directly.

- The party recalls the various reactions of the Council of Great Readers: A'lai Aivenmore's push for new leadership and an immediate vote; Fheminor Scrivenbark's concern over whether Janussi had named a successor; Kazryn Nyantani's insistence on finding the murderer first; Alkrist's demand to dismiss the Gate Warden.

- The investigators debate the reliability of physical evidence versus trying to parse who is lying, leaning toward the physical evidence as more reliable.

- The party considers whether Bookwyrm should be on the suspect list, but agrees that any accusation would require strong evidence beforehand.
- The party divides responsibilities: Gyrgum will research the poison in the library; others will pursue suspect interviews including Fheminor Scrivenbark, Daral Yashenti, and Kalan Strongbranch.

### 059.03 A Secret Meeting with Kalan

Before the party could fully settle into their new headquarters, Kalan Strongbranch appeared in the corridor and pressed a small brass key into Gyrgum's hand with quiet urgency, presenting it as one of the High Tower's two keys. His eyes swept left and right before he spoke: *"Whatever Bookwyrm is telling you, listen for what she isn't. The High Tower has two keys. Janussi wore one, I held the other. I have given it now to someone who is not me. If I and I do not see you tomorrow, do not look for me at my post."* He said Janussi had worn the second High Tower key in a locket around his neck and that the locket was now missing from the crime scene. Kalan said he feared being targeted for the key and made clear that, if he were killed, it was better the key not be found on him. He also shared his forensic conclusions: the Keeper had been poisoned by something he touched or held in his left hand that interacted with his saliva, and the heart had been hacked out after death, most likely to prevent resurrection. Kalan provisionally suggested that Knock had opened the safe. When the party challenged his assumption that a single culprit had both murdered Janussi and rifled the safe — offering their theory that one person knew where the locket was while another did not — Kalan paused and conceded: "Perhaps I was a little bit too hasty. Perhaps it was two different people." He suspected one of the Great Readers was the murderer and that Bookwyrm may have been manipulated into removing him from the case to protect the killer. His initial assessment of the party was bluntly unflattering: *"I believe you're only being employed so you're likely to bungle the case, or else be silent if you do happen upon the right scent. You don't matter. You're outsiders."* But when the party revealed that someone had been impersonating Sylvira Savikas on the night of the murder — a detail Kalan had not previously known — he conceded with as much grace as he could muster: "You are very good at this." He agreed to pool findings that evening, proposing the arrangement in strikingly corporate terms: *"Why don't we all go off and do offline chat… have offline meetings, and come back and have a meeting where we go over all of our findings in a group setting where we'll examine everything one by one."* Gyrgum asked if they could divide it into work streams; Kalan embraced this enthusiastically, proposing five; the exchange concluded with a mutual commitment to "merge all the things." Before departing to conduct his own parallel investigation, he warned the party to be wary of those who could change their face.

### 059.04 Research in the Flora Theca

The party split their efforts. Gyrgum made his way to the Flora Theca, a botanical subsection of the Pavilion Naturalis, guided by Fembris. A roll of 17 on his investigation check surfaced a tome called *Fey-mous Flowers & Fungi* by Archibald Roots, Jr. Within its pages he identified the poison as midnight tears: a highly toxic pale ochre dust harvested from a plant usually found only in the Feywild, deliverable through food or liquid, with no effects before midnight and potentially lethal poison damage when it takes effect. The book also described how sap from a particular Feywild tree neutralized the poison and produced a powerful pain reliever. This matched the timeline, while Kalan's claim that the substance had interacted with saliva offered one explanation for the black discoloration on Janussi's fingers and lips. Gyrgum also researched the stolen sapphire and identified it as the costly component for Drawmij's Instant Summons, a spell that marks an object so it can later be recalled to the caster's hand. The party immediately theorized that the sapphire had been connected to retrieving the stolen High Tower key.

### 059.05 Interrogating Daral Yashenti at the Hearth

Meanwhile, Fembris was sent to summon Daral Yashenti; he returned with a two-word reply. Daral was not coming to them. When the party arrived at the Hearth, they found him drunk, boisterous, and in conspicuously good spirits — a mood that Daz's drow instincts explained immediately: in the Underdark, attacking your superior and failing meant death for you and your bloodline; up here, people merely got fired. Daral greeted the party with a mocking toast — *"Oh, look, it's Thorin's most famous detectives! Bookwyrm's Brown-Nosers!"* — and ordered spiced brandies at his own expense. He admitted freely to his 11 AM end-of-year meeting with Janussi, during which the Keeper had rejected his proposal to preserve certain priceless works of poetry, intending instead to sell them to fund ritual materials for Sylvira, whom Daral referred to as "his horned tart" — one of several racially tinged remarks that drew pushback from the party on behalf of tieflings and lizardfolk alike. Furious, Daral had returned to Janussi's chambers that evening, found him reading *The Golden Ass* — Daral's Deadwinter gift to him — and taken it back: *"In defending myself, I may have caught him lightly on the nose."* When the party pointed out the contradiction between the gift and Daral's outrage at finding Janussi reading it, Daral's only explanation was "I was drugged. I was upset. I don't know what I was thinking." He then repaired to the Hearth, and in the telling of all of this, casually flipped through the pages of that same book, licking his fingers to turn each page. The theory crystallized in real time: midnight tears had been applied to the pages, and Janussi had ingested it exactly this way. Daral, doing the same thing in front of them, had potentially exposed himself as well. When the party warned him, he dropped the book in a panic: *"You've got to do something!"* They secured the book as evidence and resolved to keep Daral close so they could test and, if necessary, treat him before midnight — noting that if he suffered the poison's midnight effects, that would strongly argue against his being the poisoner. In his rattled state, Daral offered up his suspicions freely: Teles Ahvoste had always believed himself first in line; A'lai Aivenmore was "spoiling to rule the roost, the preying cock"; Fheminor Scrivenbark was seething over the dissolution of her interests and had a "steely nature." As for Bookwyrm: *"There's no telling what's going on in her lizard brain"* — a remark that immediately produced an indignant response from a party member on behalf of lizardfolk everywhere. Daral quickly backpedaled, calling it barroom chatter.

### 059.06 Glabbagool's Trial at the Whispering Dome

While all of this unfolded, Glabbagool made his way to the Whispering Dome, with Zalthir following. There Glabbagool asked, "What is the art of movement?" and went still for roughly sixty seconds. Then he answered his own contemplation: *"The body that mimics nothing is already shaped by what it watches."* The monk smiled and nodded. Glabbagool announced he had been waiting a very long time for this moment, flowed up onto Zalthir's arm, and declared a *"Glabbagool-Zalthir combo action."* The trial formalized his existing apprenticeship and combat partnership with Zalthir: Glabbagool became an eighth-level sidekick capable of Shadow Step between points in dim light or darkness, performing a flurry of blows, forcing acidic shadow-ki into wounds, and executing a corrosive embrace against an enemy grappled or restrained by Zalthir. A former shadow monk watching from the side observed dryly that Zalthir would have understood exactly what the exchange meant if he had paid more attention in the relevant training sessions — which had apparently been the day Zalthir was asleep.

The central administrative hub of Candlekeep where the Council of the Great Readers meets and where Bookwyrm holds her conferences. Moon elf scribes stood at the entrance and briefly halted the party before Bookwyrm waved them through.

A location within Candlekeep where Kalan Strongbranch conducts his private research and duties. He stated he returned here after visiting Sylvira Savikas on the morning before the murder and continued his private research until the alarm was raised.

A subsection of the Pavilion Naturalis within the Great Library, dedicated to the study of plants, herbs, and fungi. Gyrgum conducted his poison research here with a roll of 17, surfacing *Fey-mous Flowers & Fungi*.

## Items

### Brass Key Presented by Kalan
A small brass key that Kalan Strongbranch presented as one of the two keys required simultaneously to access the High Tower. He secretly pressed it into Gyrgum's hand, saying it was safer away from him if he were killed. The party understood that both High Tower keys were needed at once and that one alone opened nothing.

### Janussi's Locket
A personal item torn from the victim's body during the murder. Kalan said it had contained Janussi's High Tower key. The party theorized that the person who took it knew exactly where to look, in contrast to a possible second actor who rifled the safe without knowing the key was already gone.

### Poison Sample
A substance recovered from Janussi's fingers and lips, serving as a primary piece of physical evidence. Kalan concluded that the poison had interacted with Janussi's saliva, and the black discoloration helped the party connect the residue to midnight tears.

### Fey-mous Flowers & Fungi
A botanical tome authored by Archibald Roots, Jr., son of the legendary gnome botanist Archibald Roots Sr., discovered by Gyrgum in the Flora Theca with a roll of 17. It provided the critical identification of midnight tears, its Feywild origin, its ochre appearance, its midnight-activation property, its delivery method, and the Feywild-tree antidote.

### Midnight Tears
A highly toxic pale ochre dust made from the pollen of a plant usually found only in the Feywild. The victim suffers no ill effect before midnight, when the poison deals potentially lethal damage. It can be delivered in food or liquid. When mixed with the sap of a specific Feywild tree, midnight tears loses its toxicity and becomes a powerful pain reliever. The party theorized it was applied to the pages of *The Golden Ass* and ingested when Janussi licked his fingers to turn pages.

### The Golden Ass
A book Daral Yashenti gave Janussi as a Deadwinter gift and later forcibly reclaimed during their altercation. It is the suspected delivery mechanism for the midnight tears poison: Janussi was found having read it while licking his fingers to turn pages, and Daral was seen doing the same thing at the Hearth. The book is now in the party's custody as evidence.

### Sapphire
An expensive gem stolen from Janussi's chambers. Gyrgum's research revealed that a sapphire is the costly component for Drawmij's Instant Summons, a spell that marks an object so it can later be summoned directly to the caster's hand. The party theorized that the stolen sapphire was connected to retrieving the stolen High Tower key.

## Spells

### Speak with Dead
A necromancy spell discussed during the investigation. Kalan confirmed he can cast it and offered to assist the party should the need arise; the session did not establish that he had already attempted to cast it on Janussi.

### Knock
Kalan provisionally suggested that Knock had been used to open Janussi's safe. His rhetoric did not establish an unusually powerful version of the spell or that it had bypassed the chamber's other locks.

### Lesser Restoration
Mentioned as a potential remedy for Daral Yashenti's possible exposure to midnight tears. Because the poison produces no effects before midnight, the party considered testing and preventive poison-curing treatment rather than waiting for symptoms.

A powerful wizard spell requiring an expensive sapphire as a component, identified during Gyrgum's library research. It marks an object so that it can later be summoned directly to the caster's hand. The DM noted that the recall would not function while the object was being worn or carried by another creature, including on a lanyard.
