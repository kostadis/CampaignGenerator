The original offset-only Sol trial confused numbered orc references. Explicit target excerpts improved the final result while source spans and hidden expectations stayed unchanged. Both runs are preserved; the comparison uses the final marked-target trial.

Three exploratory cases are excluded from the 13-case score. On q16 (three hired orcs versus the entire allied group), Sol proposed membership, Terra retained ambiguity, and schema-constrained Qwen equated the groups. The source does not explicitly establish membership. On q14 (ranch corpses versus Big Al's account), Terra also abstained where Sol proposed coidentity. Terra's caution can avoid unsupported links while also missing plausible continuity.

Historical approvals can remain internally valid while their source bytes have drifted. The provenance graph detected this for Chapters 59 and 60; it did not establish semantic error or revoke approvals. Missing registry dependency bindings remained an explicit coverage gap.

The evidence supports a narrow, rebuildable relationship layer and further testing of contextual proposals. It does not justify automatic canon merging, general model rankings, or claims that we outperformed NVIDIA Auto Ontology. Production sources, registry schema and publication behavior were unchanged by the experiments.
