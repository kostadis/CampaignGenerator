## Soma — Harpers Behind the Wall

The tea is long gone. Several hours of a doorway, a wall to lean my shell against, and a street that never quite sleeps. I have done worse waits on the coast for worse fish.

I use the time. The rat I lost earlier comes back when I call it, the way Meril said such things do — a little more of me spent, a warm shape in my palm, whiskers already going. I put it in the crook of my arm. Old joints, new rat. A fair enough trade for the morning.

Vukradin turns up at the corner looking as though he has walked out of something and would like to tell us about it. He has been getting our messages through the stone all night, which means he has been talking to a rock more than to people, which for him counts as restraint.

"You've been giving me updates through the Sending Stone, so I should probably join you," he says. "It's been a few hours."

"Yeah, yeah, we can have you come along." I shift the rat to my other arm. "I can tell you about Denvar."

"Why don't I tell you about the local wererat mafia?"

Of course he has found one. "There's wererats all over, isn't it?" I say, and then, because he is waiting for me to be impressed, "I expect them to be such a big player."

He tells it anyway — Rsolk, tunnels, spiders in the tunnels — and I can see the moment the story turns into a ledger behind his eyes. "We just gotta clear out these spiders, and it's more money for the studio. This is great. Finally a business opportunity." He lets it sit for a beat, so we can appreciate it properly. "Fair Trade Gold."

Spiders are not a business opportunity. Spiders are spiders. But he is happy, and it is early, and I have learned to let the bard have his mornings.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: after several hours, Denvar wakes and sets out, and the party trails him to a bare stone wall where he looks around carefully, opens a stone, takes a bag from the hiding place, and closes it again.]

"It's a dead drop," Vukradin says, low.

I look past Denvar's shoulders at where he has been working. "Just a bare stone wall, like, on the edge of the…" I don't finish. I want to know what it is the edge of.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: the wall backs onto a building.]

"Is it like a two-way doctor's-office window," Vukradin says, hands making a little hatch in the air, "where you put it in on one side and close the door, and then they…"

"We'd have to go inspect it," he answers himself, before anyone else can. Then, with the decisiveness of a man who has already decided: "Alright. It's effectively a dead drop, so we should let him go and inspect it."

"Yeah."

Valphine has not moved. She is watching the man, not the wall — the way she watches anyone with something in his hands. "I'm gonna look at him just before he goes," she says.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Denvar looks at the chit he has collected and smiles.]

"Payday!" I say, under my breath, in what I imagine is his voice. "I'm going to the casino!"

He walks off happier than he arrived. Good for him. I turn the practical side over. "Barring coming back the next evening before he leaves his house — but we don't know exactly how early he has to leave to make these drops, so…" I let the rat climb onto my shoulder. "Denvar might be a dead end."

"Excellent," I add, because it is. A dead end for him is a live one for the wall.

"Valphine, you wanna do your…" Vukradin waves at her whole face.

"Infinite perception," I supply.

"You mean, like, on him, or on the…"

"On the wall," Vukradin and I say together.

"Could take a little look, then, at the rock." She goes to it. Her fingers find the seam quicker than mine would, and she does not touch more than she needs to. Menzoberranzan manners, I have decided. You learn early not to leave prints.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: the compartment is a two-way drop, and it cannot be told from the street whether the payment was placed from outside or from within the building.]

"Oh, okay. Well, let's loop around," Valphine says.

Vukradin is still frowning at the masonry. "Or is it earth, or…"

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: the building is a stone dwelling, built of stone throughout.]

"I'm trying to think whether we have something that can help us without going into the house," he says.

"I mean, we have Mold Earth," I say. "But I know you won't."

"It's not earth, it's stone."

He is right, which is irritating. Valphine is already moving. "Let's just quickly look around the house and see what it is."

"The outside of the house," Vukradin says, to be clear about what he is agreeing to.

We go along the wall. It is a good wall. Someone mortared it who cared. "Yeah, it's gotta be a Margaster residence," I say, "or something like that." That is a guess and I say it like one. Everything in this city that smells expensive has smelled of Margaster so far.

Valphine's head tilts. "It's not a residence, it's not a shop."

"You don't think?"

"That's what I'm questioning. Is it a residence or a shop?"

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: rounding the building, the party finds it is a tavern, the House of a Thousand Faces.]

"Great," says Valphine, in the voice she uses for things that are not.

"So many taverns," Vukradin says. "Well, maybe we can just walk right in. Are they open?"

"Yeah," I say — meaning let's find out.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: though it is early morning, the tavern is open.]

"Somebody's obviously in there paying off shady debts," I say. The rat agrees, or at least its nose is going.

"Okay. Sure, we can hit the tavern," Vukradin says. "It's early, but why not?" He considers the sky, which is the colour of dishwater. Somebody starts the old saying and I finish it for them — it is always that hour somewhere — and that is as much argument as a tavern door has ever needed from anyone in my bale.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Brewbarry has already gone inside; he had heard of this tavern when he first came to Neverwinter and could not afford it then.]

"Yeah," I say, "he was born in the tavern."

"Oh, there you go," Vukradin says, and I can see him fitting it to a story about the man. "Right. It's more of a high-end establishment. He was more of a Common Chord slum, and this was a high-end establishment."

"He's a man of class now," I say. "He can buy his way into high society."

Valphine, dry as a shed skin: "Isn't it obvious? It's the quality of your talent."

"Yeah," I agree, to help her.

"Not," says Vukradin.

"It's totally not, you sure."

"It definitely is not." He has the tone he gets when he has counted something and the count is wrong. "There is a force behind our fame. Some groups are maneuvering… We're being…"

"Manipulated," I say.

"…to their advantage for some reason."

He is not wrong. Nobody knows our name because of a lute. I file it with the other things I do not have an answer for and go back to the wall, because the wall at least has a seam.

"I have an idea for investigating without going inside," Vukradin says. "The Knock spell. So we could open our side, and we can cast Knock on the other side. Do you want to try to do that, or do you want to just go inside the tavern?"

Left to myself I would already be inside. Doors are for going through. But the rat is warm on my shoulder and I have a bard with a plan, and one ought to at least hear it. "Sorry, how does that work? You use Knock to open the two-way dead drop?"

"Right. We can't physically open the other side of the two-way hole, but I think we could open it magically."

"If you propose it, I think it's worth a shot."

"And then we could look in and see, like, what room are we looking at, try to find the right room — or we could even send one of your rats in there to look around."

"I like that combo."

Valphine has been standing with her arms folded, letting the men lay bricks. "I have a different strategy, which is, we go in there and offer them a business proposal."

"Yeah, Vukradin could play," I say.

"Your booking agency," she adds, and I swear the corner of her mouth moves.

"Getting backstage would give you access to staff-only areas," I tell him, "where you could do your music preparation. And by music preparation…"

"There you go," Valphine says.

Vukradin weighs the two the way he weighs everything, out loud. "We could cast two spells, or we could have an—"

"Or we can get paid!"

"—extended contract process, and several performance roles, and hours of time." A pause. "I do like to perform, though."

"Yeah, see, I mean… These are tough choices." I look at the wall. I look at the door. I have raised hatchlings; you do not make them choose between two good things when both fit in the day. "Let's do both. Why not both."

"Exactly. Why not?" He straightens. "The idea is that we open our side, cast Knock on the other side, and then send the familiar rat through to look around the room on the other side."

"It's a simple strategy," Valphine says. From her that is close to praise.

"Before we go inside," Vukradin says. "We're all together."

"Yeah. We want to at least not walk in completely blind."

So: our side first. Valphine's fingers, the stone coming out of its bed with a scrape I feel in my own shell. Then Vukradin's spell, spoken close and quiet against the inner closure, and the sound a lock makes when it has no say in the matter. I set the rat down at the lip of the gap. It looks at me once. Then it goes, and I go with it, behind my own eyes.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: the rat slips through the opened drop into a windowless back office holding a desk, papers, and supplies, where it finds the symbol of the Harpers; the room is a headquarters for the local Harpers.]

"Harp?" Vukradin says. He has heard it from me and does not trust his ears.

"Oh, wow," I say. It comes out plainer than I mean it.

"Okay, so is it, like, a storage room, or, like, what other…"

I give him what the rat gives me. "Desk, papers, supplies?" he repeats, checking the list against himself.

Someone says he wouldn't steal anything anyway. "No, no, it's just a rat," he says, and I say, "No, they're right, I can't carry anything," which is true — I have tried, with a rat, to bring a bean home — and he keeps on: "No. Definitely wouldn't steal anything." Sometimes you poke the bard just to hear him confirm he is still the bard.

I let the rat sit a moment longer with the paper smell and then I call it back to the gap. Whatever this is, it is not a Margaster parlour.

"Harper, so that's… weird," I say, and then the obvious, because someone should: "The Harpers are… smuggling?"

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: the question is raised whether the Harpers are themselves smuggling or merely arranging for the ninth crate.]

"Clearly that ninth crate involves some smuggling," I say, "because it's being directly delivered. So yes, the Harpers are smuggling in some capacity." Arranging it or receiving it or doing somebody else's errand — I don't know which. None of those are innocent ways to handle a crate.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: the Harpers of this city are not good people; the only good people are the Order of the Gauntlet.]

"Debatable," I say.

"Are there?" says Valphine, at the same time.

"Reformed," Vukradin says, coming to the Gauntlet's defence the way he comes to anyone's. "They're reformed. Well, they had that whole thing with the barbarians, you know."

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: the Order was misled by its political masters in that affair, has since rejected those masters, and, having recognised it was used by powerful forces, now looks out for the little guy.]

"Exactly, that's what I'm saying," Vukradin says. "They've been past that, they're reformed now. Basically, they're the Reformed Order of the Gauntlet."

"Truth, justice, and the little fellow," I say, flat, and leave it there.

We put the stone back the way we found it. Valphine checks the seam twice. The rat goes into my sleeve, warm and smug. Then we go around to the front and in, because a man of class is in there alone and someone should be near him when he learns what the drinks cost.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: the House of a Thousand Faces is a comfortable tavern of mirrors, mannequins dressed in antiquated fashions, lush couches, and a fire that draws properly — a high-end place in the low-end Bluelake District, with something subtly off about it.]

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: the office the rat found lies below the ground floor, at the basement level.]

"Went down, alright," I say, mostly to myself, fixing where the desk sits under my feet. Stairs somewhere. Staff only.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Brewbarry is inside, and the party knows the place for a Harper establishment.]

I look around at what I am now standing in and at the man who has been dreaming of it since he was poor.

"Yep," I say. "It's a good place. I need to leave it."
