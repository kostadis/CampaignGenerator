# The Dead Drop at the House of a Thousand Faces

The rat's whiskers twitch against the stone, and I feel the scrape of mortar through the bond. My familiar. My eyes in places my shell can't follow.

We'd watched Denvar do his little dance—look around, look around, oh so discreet—and pull his bag from the wall like a merchant retrieving a loaf from the oven. The stone went back. The smile stayed on his face as he walked away, clutching his payment like a child with a sweet.

Payday. Good for him.

Now we're all standing in the morning damp, staring at a wall that isn't a wall.

"It's a two-way rock," Valphine says, and she's right, of course. The problem is which side fed it. Denvar took his coin from the street, but someone put it there. Someone on the inside of whatever building has the nerve to back onto this alley.

Valphine circles the perimeter like a cat testing a fence. I follow, because my knees need the movement anyway. The building resolves itself: a tavern. Early hours, but open. There's a sign out front—the House of a Thousand Faces.

"You didn't plan for this eventuality?" I ask the air.

No answer from anyone who'd admit to it.

Brewbarry's already at the door. Of course he is. He's heard about this place since before he could afford it, back when the Common Chord was his idea of a night out. Now he's a man of class. He can buy his way into high society.

It's something, watching my bale grow up.

"The quality of your talent," Valphine says, answering a question nobody asked, "is obviously what's carrying you."

"Not," Vukradin says.

"It's totally not. You sure."

"It definitely is not."

He's onto something. We've been famous for weeks, and none of us asked for it. Someone's been playing us like an instrument, and Vukradin can feel the strings even if he can't see the hand.

"We're being manipulated," I finish for him.

"to their advantage for some reason."

Yes.

"Alright," I say, because the wall isn't getting any less wall-like. "Here's the plan. We open our side. Vukradin casts *Knock* on the inner closure. We send the rat through."

Vukradin's been fidgeting with the idea for the last five minutes. I can see him assembling it like a chord progression. "We could do that," he says, "or we could go inside, offer them a business proposal—"

"Your booking agency," Valphine supplies.

"—extended contract process, several performance roles, hours of time—"

"Or we can get paid!"

"I do like to perform, though."

These are tough choices.

"Let's do both."

So we stand in the alley like a pile of nervous cats, Vukradin's fingers tracing the seam of the loose stone. It comes free with a scrape that sounds louder than it should. Behind it, darkness. A slot, a chute, a channel through the wall's thickness. And at the far end, a closure we can't reach.

Vukradin mutters the words. The *Knock* spell goes off like a polite cough in a library, and somewhere on the other side, something clicks.

My rat squeezes through.

The senses arrive in a wash—small body, low perspective, the world made of floorboards and scent. Through the bond, I feel sawdust and old paper. Lamp oil. A room that doesn't want daylight.

"Through the rat, you see a back office with no windows." I'm narrating for the bale as much as for myself, because I need them to know what I'm seeing before I lose the thread. "It looks around and notices—"

A symbol. Painted on the wall like a claim of ownership.

The harp. The moon. The watching eye of people who think they're the good ones.

"Oh, wow."

"What is it?" Vukradin asks.

I make myself say it. "A symbol of the Harpers."

For a moment, nobody speaks. The rat keeps moving because rats move, and I let it. There's a desk. Papers. Supplies, stacked like the office belongs to someone who expects to come back. Maybe tonight. Maybe tomorrow. A working space, not a bolt-hole.

"This is a Harper headquarters," comes the announcement, and I can hear the weight of it.

"Harp?"

"The local Harpers," I confirm.

Vukradin's already asking the practical questions—storage room, what else, what can we see—and I answer because that's my job when I'm the eyes. Desk. Papers. Supplies.

"Desk, papers, supplies?"

"Would you steal anything?" the question comes, and Vukradin's answer is immediate.

"No."

"It's just a rat," I say. "They're right, I can't carry anything."

The joke lands sideways, and I let it go. There are bigger shapes moving behind this.

"Harper." I taste the word. "The Harpers are smuggling?"

"Are the Harpers smuggling, or are they arranging for that ninth crate?"

"Clearly the ninth crate involves some smuggling because it's being directly delivered." My voice comes out flat. "So yes, the Harpers are smuggling in some capacity."

The ninth crate. The one we've been chasing like a rumor with legs. Every path we follow curls back to it, and now it's led us to a hidden office in a high-end tavern that has no business being in this district, a dead drop that runs both ways, and a symbol I was raised to trust.

Weird. That's the word. Weird and getting weirder.

"The only good people are the Order of the Gauntlet."

"Debatable," I say.

"Are there?" Valphine's voice has an edge that could cut bread.

"They're reformed," Vukradin puts in. "They had that whole thing with the barbarians."

"They were misled by their political masters."

"Exactly. They've been past that, they're reformed now."

"They've now rejected those political masters. They recognized that they were used by powerful forces, and now they're all about the little guy."

I pull back from the rat's senses just enough to stand in my own body again. The morning is cold. The alley smells like wet stone and the kitchen vent of a tavern that opens too early for honest reasons.

Truth, justice, and the American Way, I think, but I don't say it. The joke would die out here.

"Okay," I say instead. The rat is still in the office, still seeing, because the spark of the spell lasts longer than the moment. "It's a good place."

I need to leave it. Not because the office is dangerous—it's just paper and furniture and a symbol on a wall—but because I've seen enough of this shape before. The pattern is the same everywhere: people with good intentions building compartments, and the compartments fill with things they'd never sign their names to.

The tavern is still open. Brewbarry's inside somewhere, probably feeling very classy. The others are still here, waiting for direction.

"We need to go in," I say. "And we need to pretend we never saw that symbol."

Vukradin nods. He's already composing the business proposal in his head. I can see it in the way his fingers move, like he's tuning an instrument.

Somewhere in the basement of the House of a Thousand Faces, a rat sits under a desk and watches a harp-and-moon symbol on the wall.

Not a clue. Not an accusation. Just a fact, waiting to be used.
