# Harpers Behind the Wall

Hours of waiting have turned my shell into a static weight against the alley wall. Denvar sleeps off whatever night he had, and I've had time to count every crack in the plaster across the street and reconsider my life choices. At some point my familiar's time runs out, so I burn the spell again, another slot gone, and the rat reappears in my palm, blinking its little black eyes like nothing happened.

"Ratio, you're costing me my old age," I mutter, tucking it into my collar.

Vukradin comes around the corner with that walk he has when he's carrying news he thinks is good. "You've been giving me updates through the Sending Stone, so I should probably join you."

"Yeah, yeah, we can have you come along," I say. "I can tell you about Denvar."

"Why don't I tell you about the local wererat mafia?"

"There's were-rats all over, isn't it." I expect them to be such a big player, and yet here we are, tailing a courier through the Bluelake District at dawn like the world's most patient predators.

"Right, well," he says, and there's a shift in his voice, that same tone he uses when the ledger balances, "we just gotta clear out these spiders, and it's more money for the studio. This is great. Finally a business opportunity."

"Sure."

"Fair Trade Gold, Soma. Fair. Trade. Gold."

I've learned not to argue with the phrase. It's load-bearing for him. The studio is going to happen with clean money or not at all, and arguing about it is like arguing about the tide.

Denvar stirs. We go quiet.

He wakes like a man with somewhere to be, stretches, checks the street with practiced casualness—the kind that's trying too hard to look casual—and starts walking. We follow at distance.

He stops at a stone wall adjoining a building. Looks around again, that same overdone discretion, then his fingers find a particular stone. It comes away in his hand. He reaches into the gap, pulls out a bag, and the stone goes back. He looks at something in his hand—a chit, small and white—and he smiles.

Payday.

He walks off, and the smile lingers in my head. The way he looked at that payment. The ninth crate's money, changing hands in a hole in a wall.

"It's a dead drop," Vukradin says.

"Just a bare stone wall, on the edge of the—"

"It's a stone that backs into a building."

Vukradin's already building the metaphor. "Is it like a two-way doctor's-office window, where you put it in on one side and close the door, and then they—"

"We'd have to inspect it."

"We'd have to inspect it," he agrees.

"Alright. It's effectively a dead drop, so we should let him go and inspect it."

"Yeah."

Excellent.

"Barring coming back the next evening before he leaves his house," I say, "but we don't know exactly how early he has to leave to make these drops, so—" I don't like loose threads. "Denvar might be a dead end."

"Yeah," the voice from behind me says, and it's the GM's tone of voice that means I'm probably right.

I eyeball the wall. It's a good stone, fitted tight, the sort of construction that says money even before you see what's behind it. Vukradin turns to Valphine. "Valphine, you wanna do your—"

"Infinite perception," I supply.

She levels a look at me. "You mean, like, on him, or on the—"

"On the wall."

"On the wall."

She watches Denvar's retreating back before he disappears around the corner, then turns her regard to the stonework like it's personally offended her. "Could take a little look, then, at the rock."

The mechanism is clever. It's not a hole with a plug—it's a channel, built into the wall's thickness, with closures on both sides. The side we're on opens easy. The other side, inside whatever building this is, is locked up tight from our angle.

"It's a two-way rock," the GM says. "You don't know whether the person who put the payment inside was on this side or inside the house, so you'd have to enter the house to find out."

"Oh, okay. Well, let's loop around," Valphine says.

"Or is it earth, or—" Vukradin starts.

"It's a stone dwelling."

"The stone dwelling."

"Right. It's built out of stone."

He's already thinking it through, the way he does, fitting pieces together. "I'm trying to think whether we have something that can help us without going into the house."

"I mean, we have mold earth, but I know you won't."

"It's not earth, it's stone."

"Right." I should have known. He's impossibly principled about what spells he'll use for what. Mold earth moves dirt, and this wall is stubbornly, aristocratically stone.

"Let's just quickly look around the house and see what it is," Valphine says.

"The outside of the house."

"Yeah, it's gotta be a Margaster residence, or something like that." House Margaster, the noble family with fingers in every pie that isn't nailed down. The ninth crate has their smell on it.

"It's not a residence, it's not a shop," Valphine says.

"You don't think?"

"That's what I'm questioning. Is it a residence or a shop?"

The GM pauses, and I catch the flicker in his eyes. He's building this as he goes. I can't help myself. "You didn't plan for this eventuality?"

"No."

"I'm teasing."

"As you walk around," he says, and I can hear the decision landing, "you realize the house you've found is actually a tavern."

"Great," Valphine says, in the tone of someone who's just been handed a fork when she wanted a knife.

"The House of a Thousand Faces."

"So many taverns," Vukradin says. "Well, maybe we can just walk right in. Are they open?"

"Yeah."

"It is early in the morning, but no, they are, in fact, open," the GM says.

"Somebody's obviously in there paying off shady debts."

"Correct."

"Okay. Sure, we can hit the tavern. It's early, but why not?"

"It's 5 o'clock."

"Clock somewhere," I finish.

"The House of a Thousand Faces," he repeats, like the name matters.

Brewbarry is already moving before anyone else can get a vote in. He makes for the front door like it owes him money—or like he's owed it a visit. I watch him go, this huge man who worships Vukradin's music and carries a halberd like other people carry umbrellas, and something about the way he walks in catches me.

"He was born in the tavern," I say.

"Actually, to be fair," the GM says, "Brewbarry has heard about this tavern for some time. When he first came to Neverwinter, he couldn't go there because he didn't have the money."

Vukradin brightens. "Oh, there you go. Right. It's more of a high-end establishment. He was more of a Common Chord slum, and this was a high-end establishment."

"Yeah. I think he'd heard about this place."

"He's a man of class now, he can buy his way into high society," I say.

"Isn't it obvious? It's the quality of your talent," Valphine says, and there's acid in the sweetness.

"Yeah."

"Not."

"It's totally not," she says, insisting, "you sure."

"It definitely is not." Vukradin shakes his head. "There is a force behind our fame. Some groups are maneuvering—we're being—"

"Manipulated," I finish.

"To their advantage for some reason."

Yeah. We've all felt it. Lathander's Death, a band that came out of nowhere, and suddenly everyone in Neverwinter knows our name. Someone's been amplifying it, deliberately, and I've been waiting to find out what they want for the favor.

There's a pause while we all look at the front of the House of a Thousand Faces. Then Vukradin gets that particular look, the one that means he's about to be clever.

"I have an idea for investigating without going inside: the *Knock* spell."

"Knock?"

"So we could open our side, and we can cast Knock on the other side." He's building it as he talks, the way he does. "Do you want to try to do that, or do you want to just go inside the tavern?"

"I assume Soma would just go inside," I say. "She's not exactly a magic wielder or familiar with all of that."

"Sorry, how does that work? You use *Knock* to open the two-way dead drop?"

"Right. We can't physically open the other side of the two-way hole, but I think we could open it magically."

"If you propose it, I think it's worth a shot."

"And then we could look in and see, like, what room are we looking at, try to find the right room, or we could even send one of your rats in there to look around."

The shape of it lands in my head. The rat goes through the hole, into whatever room's on the other side, and I see through its eyes. No one goes in, no one's noticed, and we know what we're dealing with before we commit.

"I like that combo."

Valphine has been quiet, and that's never a good sign with her. "I have a different strategy, which is, we go in there and offer them a business proposal."

"Yeah, Vukradin could play."

"Your booking agency," she says.

"Getting backstage would give you access to staff-only areas where you could do your music preparation—" I pause, let the words sit, "—and by music preparation—"

"There you go," she says.

"We could cast two spells," Vukradin says, already counting it out, "or we could have an extended contract process, and several performance roles, and hours of time."

"Or we can get paid!" I can't help it. The studio fund is a running joke and a real ledger all at once, and the idea of Vukradin performing his way into the secret room while also getting paid for it is too clean to pass up.

There's a pause in him. Then, quieter, almost to himself: "I do like to perform, though."

"Yeah, see, I mean—" I wave a hand. "These are tough choices. Let's do both!" The phrase comes out in two languages. "Porque no los dos."

"Exactly. Why not?"

Vukradin restates the plan, because he always has to have the whole shape in words: "The idea is that we open our side, cast *Knock* on the other side, and then send the familiar rat through to look around the room on the other side."

"It's a simple strategy," Valphine says.

"Yeah."

"Before we go inside. We're all together."

"Yeah, we want to at least not walk in completely blind."

"Alright," the GM says. "Brewbarry's already in the tavern."

I look at the front door, where our resident half-giant has already disappeared into the polished interior. Born in a tavern, that one. Or close enough.

The wall's mechanism is simple once we're at it. One stone on our side slides back to reveal a channel maybe a foot deep, with a second door at the back. That inner closure is the thing we can't move by hand—I try, and it's solid, locked tight from the other side.

"Ready?" Vukradin asks.

"Go."

He sums the spell the way he does everything: clean, precise, a few words of power that sound almost conversational. The inner door clicks open.

I close my eyes and find my little one, curled safe in the pocket of my robes. Like slipping into water. The world changes: my body is small, four feet on stone, whiskers tasting the air. The channel is a dark passage lined with dust and the faint smell of old mortar. Through the rat, I slip inside.

The GM's voice comes to me distant, through my own ears, but I'm seeing through the rat's eyes now. A back office, windowless, lit by something I can't quite identify from the floor. A desk with papers, ink, the paraphernalia of bureaucracy. Supplies stacked in the corners. The room has a particular quality, the kind of room that belongs to someone who's here a lot and intends to keep being here.

And there, on the wall—

The symbol. Crescent moon holding a harp in its curve. The emblem of the Harpers.

"Oh, wow!" That's my own voice, coming from a body that's standing outside while my senses are six inches off the floor.

"Harp?" Vukradin says.

"It's a Harper office. The whole place is a Harper front." I pull back from the rat's senses, just enough to speak, but I'm still seeing the room through its eyes. The desk. The papers. The symbol.

"Okay, so is it, like, a storage room, or, like, what other—"

"There's a desk. Papers." I keep describing while the rat creeps forward, whiskers working. "Supplies. A back office."

"Desk, papers, supplies?"

"Well, you wouldn't steal anything, so yeah," the GM says.

"No, no, it's just a rat," Vukradin says.

"No, they're right, I can't carry anything." The rat couldn't lift a paper if it wanted to. "But I can look."

"I meant that Vukradin wouldn't steal anything."

"No," Vukradin says, and then again, with feeling, "definitely wouldn't steal anything."

Harper. The word is sitting in my head, and it doesn't fit clean. The Harpers are supposed to be the good ones. The ones who fight the good fight, who keep the balance, who are definitely not the villains in the stories. But Denvar came to their wall to pick up payment, and Denvar is a courier for the ninth crate, and the ninth crate is secret enough that people are being paid through two-way rocks at dawn.

"Harper, so that's—weird."

"The Harpers are—smuggling?" Vukradin finishes what I don't want to say.

"Well, the Harpers aren't—are the Harpers smuggling, or they're arranging for that ninth crate?"

"Clearly that ninth crate involves some smuggling because it's being directly delivered. So yes, the Harpers are smuggling in some capacity." It's direct delivery. Covert payment. A hidden office in a classy tavern that doesn't belong in this neighborhood. The pieces fit.

"There's no—the only good people are the Order of the Gauntlet."

"Debatable," I say.

"Are there?" Valphine asks.

"You know, they are."

"Reformed," Vukradin says. "They're reformed."

"Why?"

"Well, they had that whole thing with the barbarians, you know."

"Well, that was—they were misled by their political masters."

"Exactly, that's what I'm saying. They've been past that, they're reformed now."

"Right, and so they've now rejected those political masters."

"Basically, they're the Reformed order of the Gauntlet."

"Yes. They recognized that they were used by powerful forces, and now they're all about the little guy."

"Truth, justice, and the American Way," I say.

"Different comic book. Alright."

I pull myself back into my own body, and the rat's senses fade to something I can put away for later. The wall is stone again. The street is gray dawn. The House of a Thousand Faces stands in front of me, warm light in its windows, and Brewbarry is in there, and the Harpers are in there, and I'm not sure which of those facts is the one that bothers me most.

"It's a comfortable tavern," the GM says, almost apologetically. "Mirrors, mannequins in antiquated fashions, lush couches, a fire that actually draws."

"High-end place in the low-end Bluelake District."

"Makes sense."

"There's something off about it."

And there is. I felt it even before the rat found the symbol. A place this nice, sitting in a neighborhood this poor, with a wall that opens from both sides and a basement full of Harper paperwork—it's a pretty face on a locked door.

"And as for the—where the rat was, it wasn't in the first floor, but it kind of went—"

"Level," Vukradin supplies.

"Yeah, the basement level."

"Basement level."

"Went down, alright." Into the dark, into the locked room, into the place where the Harper symbol watches over files and payment records.

"Brewbarry walks in—"

"Yep, it's a good place, I need to leave it."

But I don't leave. I stay there, in the street outside the House of a Thousand Faces, watching the windows, thinking about a man who smiled at his payday and a wall that passes money both ways and a symbol I thought I could trust.

Brewbarry is inside, warm and comfortable, drinking in a place he used to not be able to afford. He doesn't know yet that the comfortable room he's sitting in is a front for Harper business. He'll find out when I tell him, and I'm not looking forward to the look on his face.

The ninth crate. The Harpers. And us, standing in the street with our familiar's eyes full of secrets.

Yeah. It's a good place. I need to leave it.
