## Soma — Harpers Behind the Wall

The rat comes back to me the way they always do, a little pile of warm fur assembling itself out of nothing on the flagstones at my feet. I spent a slot to get him back, then another to make the shape hold. Meril would have had something to say about spending power on a rodent twice in one morning. Meril isn't here. The rat climbs my sleeve and settles against my neck where the shell doesn't reach, and my joints tell me exactly how many hours I have spent sitting on a cold step across from a sleeping man's door.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: several hours pass before Denvar wakes.]

Vukradin arrives before Denvar does. He has been talking to us through the Sending Stone all morning, the way he does, little bursts of news dropped into my head while I was trying to watch a window. Now he comes up the street whole, lute on his back, looking pleased with himself.

"You've been giving me updates through the stone," he says, "so I should probably join you. It's been a few hours."

"Yeah, yeah, we can have you come along." I shift my weight off the bad hip. "I can tell you about Denvar."

"Why don't I tell you about the local wererat mafia?"

He does. Rsolk. Tunnels. Spiders in the tunnels, more spiders than anyone wants. I listen with the part of me that listens to weather reports. "There's were-rats all over, isn't it," I say when he pauses for breath. "I expect them to be such a big player."

He hears the dryness and sails straight past it. "We just gotta clear out these spiders, and it's more money for the studio. This is great. Finally a business opportunity." He lets it land. "Fair Trade Gold."

Spiders. I have been a spider. I decide not to say so.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Denvar leaves his house and the party follows him to a stone wall that backs onto a building, where he looks around discreetly, pulls a stone loose, takes a bag from the hollow behind it, and closes it again.]

"It's a dead drop," Vukradin says, low.

I look at the wall. Mortar, weathered, one stone that sits a hair proud of the rest now that I know where to look. "Just a bare stone wall, like, on the edge of the—"

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: the stone is set into a wall that forms part of a building.]

"Is it like a two-way window," Vukradin says, "where you put it in on one side and close the door, and then they—"

"We'd have to go inspect it."

"Alright. It's effectively a dead drop, so we should let him go and inspect it."

"Yeah."

Valphine has not moved since we arrived. She is watching Denvar the way I watch a gull deciding whether my fish is worth the trouble. "Valphine," Vukradin says, "you wanna do your—"

"Infinite perception," I supply.

"You mean on him, or on the—"

"On the wall," Vukradin and I say together.

"I'm gonna look at him just before he goes," she says, and does.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Denvar examines the chit from the bag and smiles before walking away.]

"Payday," I say to his back. "I'm going to the casino."

He's gone around the corner and I let the thought finish itself. We could come back tomorrow before he leaves his house, but we don't know how early he has to be out to make these drops, and I am not spending another morning on that step for the chance. "Denvar might be a dead end."

"Could take a little look, then," Valphine says, "at the rock."

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: the hollow opens from either side, so there is no way to tell from the street whether the payment was placed from outside or from within the building; learning that means going in.]

"Oh, okay," she says. "Well, let's loop around."

"Or is it earth, or—" Vukradin presses a palm to the wall.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: the building is a stone dwelling.]

"I'm trying to think whether we have something that can help us without going into the house," he says.

"I mean, we have Mold Earth," I say. "But I know you won't."

"It's not earth. It's stone."

That is true, and I knew it was true, and I said it anyway. Some mornings you take the small pleasures.

"Let's just quickly look around the house and see what it is," Valphine says.

"The outside of the house," Vukradin agrees.

"Yeah, it's gotta be a Margaster residence, or something like that." I say it because that is where every rotten thing in this city seems to lead, not because I know.

"It's not a residence," Valphine says. "It's not a shop."

"You don't think?"

"That's what I'm questioning. Is it a residence or a shop?"

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: walking the perimeter reveals that the building is a tavern called the House of a Thousand Faces.]

"Great," Valphine says, in the voice she uses for things that are not great.

"So many taverns." Vukradin is already looking at the door. "Well, maybe we can just walk right in. Are they open?"

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: though it is early in the morning, the tavern is open.]

A tavern that lights its lamps before the bakers do. "Somebody's obviously in there paying off shady debts."

"Okay. Sure," Vukradin says. "We can hit the tavern. It's early, but why not?"

"Five o'clock somewhere," I say. It's a coast saying. It travels.

He stops with his hand halfway to the door. "I have an idea for investigating without going inside. The Knock spell. We could open our side, and cast Knock on the other side. Do you want to try that, or do you want to just go inside?"

I would just go inside. Doors are for going through; that is what they are. But I am new to this craft and he is not, so I ask. "Sorry, how does that work? You use Knock to open the two-way dead drop?"

"Right. We can't physically open the other side of the hole, but I think we could open it magically."

"If you propose it, I think it's worth a shot."

"And then we could look in and see what room we're looking at. Or we could even send one of your rats in there to look around."

The rat's whiskers tickle my jaw. "I like that combo."

"I have a different strategy," Valphine says, "which is, we go in there and offer them a business proposal."

"Yeah, Vukradin could play."

"Your booking agency," Valphine says, and it is not quite mockery.

"Getting backstage would give you access to staff-only areas," I say, "where you could do your music preparation. And by music preparation—"

"There you go," Valphine says.

Vukradin is weighing it. I can see him weighing it, the way he sets one thing against another with his whole face. "We could cast two spells," he says, "or we could have an—"

"Or we can get paid!"

"—extended contract process, and several performance roles, and hours of time." A beat. "I do like to perform, though."

"Yeah, see. These are tough choices." I let him suffer a moment. "Let's do both."

"Exactly. Why not." He straightens. "The idea is, we open our side, cast Knock on the other side, and then send the rat through to look around. Before we go inside. We're all together."

"Yeah. We want to at least not walk in completely blind."

"It's a simple strategy," Valphine says.

We are not all together. Brewbarry is not here. Brewbarry has been not here for some minutes, and there is only one door on this street he could have gone through.

"He was born in the tavern," I say.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Brewbarry has heard of the House of a Thousand Faces for some time; when he first came to Neverwinter he could not afford to go in.]

"Oh, there you go," Vukradin says, with real warmth. "It's more of a high-end establishment. He was more of a Common Chord slum, and this was a high-end establishment."

"He's a man of class now," I say. "He can buy his way into high society."

"Isn't it obvious?" Valphine says. "It's the quality of your talent."

"Yeah."

"Not," Vukradin says.

"It's totally not, you're sure."

"It definitely is not." He has gone still the way he does when something has been bothering him a while. "There is a force behind our fame. Some groups are maneuvering. We're being—"

"Manipulated," I say.

"—to their advantage, for some reason."

Nobody argues. We have all felt it: strangers who know our names, rooms that go quiet when we come in, a barbarian who could not afford a drink here a year ago strolling in like the place was built for him. Somebody is lifting us up, and you do not lift a thing that heavy unless you mean to set it down somewhere.

Vukradin works the stone. I hold the rat in both hands and feel the moment the far side gives. There is a draught out of the hollow, cool, dry, a cellar smell.

"Go on," I tell him, and I go with him.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: the rat slips through the opened compartment and sneaks around a windowless back office, and on the wall it finds the symbol of the Harpers.]

The rat's world comes to me sideways and close, all whisker and floor. Paper edges. A leg of a desk taller than a tree. And on the wall, plain as a shop sign, the harp.

"Harp?" Vukradin says beside me, hearing only what I'm muttering.

"Oh, wow."

"Okay, so is it like a storage room, or—"

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: the office holds a desk, papers, and supplies.]

"Desk, papers, supplies?" Vukradin repeats.

"You wouldn't steal anything," someone says, and I hear Vukradin start to bristle before I can answer for the rat.

"No, no, it's just a rat."

"No, they're right," I say. "I can't carry anything."

"Definitely wouldn't steal anything," Vukradin says, to be clear.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: the office appears to be the local headquarters of the Harpers.]

I bring the rat back toward the light and let go of his eyes. My own street comes up around me again: cobbles, Valphine's silhouette, Vukradin's hand still on the stone.

"Harper," I say. "So that's… weird." Denvar's bag came out of a Harper wall. "The Harpers are… smuggling?"

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: there is a distinction between the Harpers smuggling outright and the Harpers arranging the ninth crate, and what was seen does not settle which.]

"Clearly that ninth crate involves some smuggling," I say, "because it's being directly delivered. So yes, the Harpers are smuggling in some capacity."

Arranging it, receiving it, doing it for somebody else — I don't know. What I know is a harp on a cellar wall and a man walking away smiling.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: the Harpers in this city are not good people; the only good people are the Order of the Gauntlet, who were misled by their political masters, have since rejected those masters, and are now concerned with the little guy.]

"Debatable," I say, of the good people.

"Are there?" Valphine says, of the same.

"Reformed," Vukradin insists. "They're reformed. They had that whole thing with the barbarians. They've been past that. Basically, they're the Reformed Order of the Gauntlet."

"Truth, justice, and the little guy," I say. It is not a compliment and Vukradin takes it as one, which is how it usually goes.

We put the stone back. I make sure it sits flush. Then there is nothing left but the door.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: the House of a Thousand Faces is a comfortable tavern with mirrors, mannequins in antiquated fashions, lush couches, and a fire that draws properly — a high-end place in the low-end Bluelake District, with something subtly off about it.]

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: the room the rat found was not on the ground floor but below it, at the basement level.]

"Went down, alright," I say, and file it. Under my feet, then, while I sit on a couch.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Brewbarry is already inside as the party enters, and they know now that this is a Harper establishment.]

Brewbarry is at the bar looking like a man who has finally been let into the room he stood outside of, hungry, a year ago. I would not take that from him for anything. The fire is honest, at least; I can tell a chimney that draws from one that only pretends. Everything else in here is a face on a mannequin.

"Yep," I say, to nobody. "It's a good place. I need to leave it."
