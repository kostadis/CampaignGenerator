# Locations — Lost Tomb of Khaem

## Exact Source Appendix

### Chapter 007 — Lost Tomb of Khaem

- Source: `/home/kostadis/out-of-the-abyss/out-of-the-abyss/docs/summaries/007-session-untitled.md`
- Original heading: `Lost Tomb of Khaem`

An ancient floating tomb from the era of Netheril that plunged into an Underdark crevasse after the fall of the Empire of Netheril and its flying cities. Fargas revealed it contains the remains and treasures of the half-elf sorcerer Brysis. The decoy sarcophagus rests on a black marble tier, and the true treasure is hidden behind a secret passageway from the servant's tomb. Fargas places it somewhere on the northeastern edge of the Darklake, and Sarith believes it is in one of three or four possible places.

### Chapter 028 — Lost Tomb of Khaem

- Source: `/home/kostadis/out-of-the-abyss/out-of-the-abyss/docs/summaries/028-session-untitled.md`
- Original heading: `Lost Tomb of Khaem`

An ancient Netherese burial site deep in the Underdark. A mysterious telepathic feminine voice called out to the party upon their arrival. Its features include:
- A stone diorama of the sorcerer Brysis of Khaem.
- Murals of floating cities.
- A long descending staircase flanked by empty stone torch sconces.
- A Netherese calendar stone.
- A defaced shrine with a cracked marble altar and doors leading east and south.
- A chamber of four stone sarcophagi.
- A hidden lower vault.

Despite several thousand years, its contents have oddly not crumbled to dust.

### Chapter 029 — Lost Tomb of Khaem

- Source: `/home/kostadis/out-of-the-abyss/out-of-the-abyss/docs/summaries/029-session-untitled.md`
- Original heading: `Lost Tomb of Khaem`

An ancient burial site in the Underdark containing a stone sarcophagus where the mummified remains of Brysis were interred alongside a sentient magical sword. A second, southern chamber served as a decoy tomb filled with canopic jars and a heavy sarcophagus decorated with mosaics of floating cities. The decoy sarcophagus rests on a black marble bier. Its canopic jars sit in niches cut into stone blocks along the western and eastern walls.
