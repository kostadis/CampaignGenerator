### Chapter 054 — Vareth

- Source: `/home/kostadis/out-of-the-abyss/out-of-the-abyss/docs/summaries/054-session-04-28.md`
- Original heading: `Vareth`

An elderly, frantic, and officious half-elf scholar and member of the Avowed. He specializes in orc conversions to the worship of Bahamut — a field that was previously so obscure and ignored that colleagues had been ignoring him for years. Since the legendary events of Stroud and Protanther, his research has exploded: he is now receiving funding, conference invitations, and serious academic attention. He rushes in furious that the First Reader did not immediately summon him upon a cleric of Bahamut's arrival, grabs Gyrgum's hand, and practically drags him toward his office, describing him as "a real living orc cleric of Bahamut" and "a living data point." He has secured a record of the legendary chess game, and when Gyrgum identifies it by name as "Stroud's Gambit," Vareth declares: "This is all novel news! I can't wait to take advantage of it! The field of Bahamut orc conversions will benefit tremendously from your interview."

### Chapter 057 — Brother Vareth

- Source: `/home/kostadis/out-of-the-abyss/out-of-the-abyss/docs/summaries/057-session-candlekeep-s-cold-case-the-gyrgumite-gambit.md`
- Original heading: `Brother Vareth`

An elderly scholar who has kept his corner of the Theology Library at Candlekeep for forty-one years. His narrow specialty is the puzzle of why a half-orc would convert to Bahamut, given that orcs and dragons are traditional enemies who occupy similar ecological niches and that a sufficient mass of orcs can mathematically bring a dragon down. Stroud's impossible chess move three years ago — despite knowing no chess — dragged this niche question into the centre of the Question of the Age after Protanther accepted the outcome. Vareth laid out all three existing schools of thought in a prepared speech to Gyrgum, but was electrified by Gyrgum's proposal of a fourth: fixed points around which choices become constrained. He immediately labeled Gyrgum a "weak Stroudite and strong Bahamutian around fixed points," declared the concept a philosophical quadrangle and the "strong-weak hypothesis," and rushed off to begin his own research — barely pausing to hand Gyrgum the Stroudite materials and hear Gyrgum's promise to return them marked up.
