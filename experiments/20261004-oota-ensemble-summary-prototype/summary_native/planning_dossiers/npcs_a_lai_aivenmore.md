### Chapter 064 — A'lai Aivenmore

- Source: `/home/kostadis/out-of-the-abyss/out-of-the-abyss/docs/summaries/064-session-untitled.md`
- Original heading: `A'lai Aivenmore`

A powerful drow wizard and long-time scholar of Candlekeep who orchestrated the immediate conspiracy to protect his academic position. Janussi had given him ten days before taking away his chair, the only home or institutional identity A'lai believed he possessed after thirty-one years at Candlekeep.

A'lai admits that he did not initially intend to become either a murderer or an exile, but discovered he would do “a great deal” to retain his chair. His crimes reach much further back than the current crisis: for eleven years he has removed books selected by lists delivered on Manshoon's behalf.

He is self-preserving, desperate, and tactically formidable. He uses Counterspell, Misty Step, Mage Armor, and repeated Arcane Bursts, but consistently prioritizes escape and survival over fighting to the death. During the confrontation, Daz repeatedly forces him to spend magic defensively and ultimately steals the sapphire from his hand.

A'lai possesses a strange mixture of vanity and fatalistic candor. He pauses during combat to compliment the party's thoroughness and seems pleased when Gyrgum recognizes the academic grievance beneath the bloodshed. His attempt to justify Manshoon's theft as the version where nobody needed to die collapses under the weight of the bodies around him, and he revises it to the version where “fewer people had to die.”

### Chapter 065 — A'lai Aivenmore

- Source: `/home/kostadis/out-of-the-abyss/out-of-the-abyss/docs/summaries/065-session-08-03.md`
- Original heading: `A'lai Aivenmore`

A wizard who has lived at Candlekeep for thirty-one years, with no house, family, or country—only his academic chair. In the previous session (ch64) he was revealed as a traitor who secretly removed specific books from the library for eleven years on behalf of Manshoon, whose courier always arrived wearing a glove over a metal hand, and he attempted to smash the stolen sapphire before being outmaneuvered by the party.

During this session, A'lai killed the last acid-wounded Zhentarim thug and proposed a pause that he initially distinguished from surrender: “I'd like to stop, but not surrender.” He claimed to know the two important lines of a cryptogram, the location of a room beneath Candlekeep, and the identity of whoever had been paying to keep the party’s drow alive. He wanted to deliver that information behind a locked door before the people ascending the stairs arrived.

He surrendered before the assassin appeared and willingly allowed Zalthir to bind him. He then betrayed the party by directing the assassin toward Daz, presenting his capture as “not a loss” but “a position.” When the assassin failed to find the sapphire and was killed, A'lai broke down weeping.

A'lai desperately wants confinement in Candlekeep’s magic-nullifying prison, both because it offers protection and because even imprisonment there would allow him to remain within the institution that defines his identity. Manshoon’s successful breach has made him doubt that even those cells are safe. He withheld key information from Manshoon about the depository’s contents and intends to trade that secret for protection.

### Chapter 067 — A'lai Aivenmore

- Source: `/home/kostadis/out-of-the-abyss/out-of-the-abyss/docs/summaries/067-session-08-17.md`
- Original heading: `A'lai Aivenmore`

A high-ranking scholar who turned traitor, only to realize he was a disposable pawn for Manshoon. For eleven years, Manshoon communicated through a gloved, faceless courier; A'lai mistook the anonymity for discretion but now understands that he was not important enough to be shown a face.

Devastated by this revelation, A'lai trades secrets about the Vault of Secrets and his deduction about which drow house has been funding the operations around Daz in exchange for placement in Candlekeep's null magic prison. He possesses a photographic memory, takes intense pride in his academic discoveries, and cannot resist explaining how he solved each clue even while being escorted to prison.
