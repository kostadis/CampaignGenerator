### Chapter 032 — Jimjar

- Source: `/home/kostadis/out-of-the-abyss/out-of-the-abyss/docs/summaries/032-session-untitled.md`
- Original heading: `Jimjar`

A deep gnome companion with a compulsive gambling habit who insists he knows the way to Blingdenstone. He pays five gold pieces to Gyrgum on a previously settled bet, although he first claims not to have cash available.

After Rasharoo’s scouts leave, Jimjar promises to guide the party and offers to bet that Thorin will praise him upon their arrival. When a cave-in traps the group in unfamiliar passages, he argues that an unavoidable tunnel collapse should not count against his record.

Jimjar tries to distract the party with an elaborate bet that Zalthir’s next emotion will be like chipped obsidian — black, dark, and sharp — provided Thorin does not see something first. Spiderbait calls the wager weak, and Jimjar admits it is not his best work. He ultimately confesses that he was trying to conceal the fact that he got the party lost, then encourages them to search the nearby cavern for something special.

### Chapter 034 — Jimjar

- Source: `/home/kostadis/out-of-the-abyss/out-of-the-abyss/docs/summaries/034-session-the-wet-bet-and-the-wondrous-cube.md`
- Original heading: `Jimjar`

A deep gnome guide who recently made a navigational error, which he frames first as an unavoidable consequence of shifting Underdark passages and later as an unexpected shortcut to Blingdenstone. He is confident in his abilities and almost compulsively prone to making bets, particularly with Thorin.

Jimjar treats even imminent drowning as an opportunity for wordplay, describing the rising flood as an especially appropriate fulfillment of the wager over “tears.” Despite his bravado, the appearance of the first gray ooze makes him admit, “I really wished I had lost this bet, Thorin.” After meeting Glabbagool, he asks whether the party can declare a mulligan because the detour produced such a charming friend.

Once the party escapes, Jimjar insists, “See, it did not end in tears,” and recasts the entire disaster as proof of his skill: “I did not get us lost. That’s the important thing is that I found us a shortcut, right?” He accepts the promise of ten slightly slimy silver pieces as settlement of the wager.

### Chapter 035 — Jimjar

- Source: `/home/kostadis/out-of-the-abyss/out-of-the-abyss/docs/summaries/035-session-prejudice-promises-and-the-prince-of-puddles.md`
- Original heading: `Jimjar`

A gnome companion known for turning almost every situation into a wager. Delighted to be nearing Blingdenstone, he correctly predicts that the exhausted party will demand a rest, although he neglects to secure anyone to take the other side of the bet.

Jimjar knows Blingdenstone’s history, leaders, and present dangers in extraordinary detail. Mention of Juiblex causes him to look as though he is recalling something painful. He then predicts that the party will need to equip an army with acid-resistant gear and recover the necessary ingredients from a fungal wedding celebration. His companions increasingly suspect that his wagers are a disguised means of revealing information or directing their actions.

Jimjar claims to be someone who collects information and makes bets. Daz observes, “If you make enough bets with enough people, they start telling you things.” Nevertheless, Blingdenstone’s gnomes treat him with unusual deference, and at least one guard recognizes him without remembering why. The party concludes that he may be some kind of hidden dignitary or VIP.

### Chapter 048 — Jimjar

- Source: `/home/kostadis/out-of-the-abyss/out-of-the-abyss/docs/summaries/048-session-the-knucklebone-of-truth.md`
- Original heading: `Jimjar`

A mysterious deep gnome with a penchant for gambling who used a bone die carved from a saint's knucklebone to pacify the ooze army. He revealed a philosophy of 'world math' and probability before departing. When Thorin used Know Your Enemy to inspect him, he was found to be immune to poison and psychic damage, resistant to radiant and non-magical physical attacks, and unable to be charmed or frightened, before winking and vanishing entirely.
- Has an "uncanny ability to show up just when you really don't want him to make a bet."
- Kept the wager small on purpose: "10 platinum pieces, that's just chump change for guys like you."
- Frames reality as probability; the demons' arrival was an "unbelievably unrandom event" to be countered with a saint's die.
- Gives off the air of knowing "way too much and much too little."
