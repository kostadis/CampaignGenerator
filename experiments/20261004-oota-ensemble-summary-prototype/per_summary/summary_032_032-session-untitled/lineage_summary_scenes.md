# Chapter 032

### The Rehearsal March of Zuggtmoy 032.01

The party found themselves standing in the shadow of the Majestic Mushroom, a colossal fungal structure that stretched from the cavern floor nearly to the ceiling, its hollow trunk carved with slitted windows that poured an eerie phosphorescent glow into the darkness. Thousands of smaller fungi clung to its vast central stalk, which split into lesser stalks whose caps were each broad enough to crown a great tower. A bridal procession of singing, marching fungal creatures swept through the grove, moving with the imperious indifference of royalty — the Demon Queen of Fungi, Zuggtmoy herself, leading the march as though the party were invisible. The procession’s bearing reminded the party of a queen crossing a fair or Darth Vader entering the Death Star: everyone moved aside, and no one dared attract notice.

Pooling their knowledge of arcane lore and religious texts, the group pieced together a terrifying truth. Gyrgum’s peculiar combination of Bahamut’s teachings, orc wedding customs, and “one too many hits over the head” let him recognize the display as a rehearsal march rather than the true ceremony. Daz, Thorin, and Gyrgum then combined fragments of lore concerning Araumycos, an immense sentient fungal growth occupying an epically large region of the Underdark. Zuggtmoy intended to ritually mate with — and potentially possess — this entity in a ceremony involving magic and power beyond anything the party understood. The intended groom, however, was nowhere to be found, and the procession eventually retreated into the hollow mushroom. For now, the wedding could not begin.

The implications were far larger than Neverlight Grove. Demogorgon had already appeared in the Underdark, and now a second demon lord was preparing to seize a powerful native entity. The party briefly considered whether they might disrupt the ceremony, delay the missing participants, rig the site to explode, or simply sell popcorn to anyone watching. The reality was less amusing: Zuggtmoy was so far beyond them that a direct confrontation would mean rolling new characters. Their most realistic choices were to escape, warn someone capable of responding, and save any friends they could.

### The Mock Wedding and Fungal Vision 032.02

Before the creatures fully withdrew, the party witnessed something even more disturbing: a mock wedding ceremony played out before them, with a chamberlain and bridesmaid standing in for Araumycos and Zuggtmoy. Three spore servants rose from the garden and carried the recently slain Yestabrod’s corpse like a grotesque puppet, forcing it to play the priest. Then, without warning, a vision overtook them all. The mock wedding vanished, replaced by the interior of a great tower whose walls were carved with spiraling stairs and balconies. Phosphorescent mold grew across it in glowing whorls. At its center floated a woman-like figure made entirely of fungi and mold, three times the height of the fungal bridesmaids tending her. They sang a strange, soothing song while weaving her substance into delicate lichen veils and a long mycelium bridal train.

As the party crept closer to the Majestic Mushroom, a stench of rotten decay wrapped around them like a shroud, seeming to threaten not only their flesh but their souls. The same cacophony of atonal music they had heard before echoed from within. Thinking quickly, they wetted strips of cloth and covered their mouths and noses against the spores drifting through the air — a wise precaution, though not entirely effective. Daz was overcome by short-term madness and experienced vivid hallucinations of happy elf families, dwarves and elves living in peace, countless pictures of dogs, and, most horrifyingly for a drow, a city without spiders. He screamed, hid, and struggled to distinguish the hallucinations from reality.

Zalthir fared no better. When the party collected equipment piled near Xinaya’s burial place, including finely crafted studded leather armor and magical scrolls, a maddening greed seized him. He hoarded the scrolls and snapped, “These are mine. I found them,” refusing to share them with anyone. The others remembered that both Zalthir and Daz had inhaled spores released by Yestabrod during their earlier battle. Thorin used Dawnbringer’s restorative power on Zalthir, reasoning that the affliction might be a disease. The magic worked, breaking his madness and leaving him bewildered and apologetic. He admitted that he did not know what had come over him, only that possessing the treasure had suddenly felt critically important.

### Escape from Neverlight Grove 032.03

The party refused to venture through a passage that would take them deeper into Zuggtmoy’s realm and expose them to still more madness. They returned to Basidia and Rasharoo with grim news, describing the “whole nine yards” of the wedding rehearsal and the fungal demon’s plans. Both myconids were horrified. Basidia insisted that Phylo had to be told, while Rasharoo argued that they should simply run.

When the group returned to Phylo, they tried to hide in plain sight and preserve the fiction that they would still attend the following day’s celebration. Phylo cheerfully asked Basidia how the tour had gone and whether their guests had enjoyed all the changes. Daz answered cautiously that the changes appeared to be causing problems for some while others remained unaware of them, but Gyrgum was effectively jumping up and down signaling danger. Dawnbringer’s light also made the surrounding myconids visibly uncomfortable. Basidia could no longer contain herself and accused Phylo of betraying the circle and allying himself with corruption.

The confrontation rapidly threatened to become a battle among the myconids. The party failed to persuade Basidia to retreat at first, but Gyrgum managed to convince Stool and Rumpadump that Neverlight Grove was no longer what it had been and that they needed to leave immediately. As Phylo and Basidia began to fight and the grove’s trees moved to attack her, Rasharoo shouted, “If you want to live, come with me.” Loobamub and his circle followed him toward the escape route.

Zalthir then physically seized Basidia before she could throw herself into a hopeless fight with Phylo. Although his preferred method of grabbing people involved punching, he successfully dragged her away. Seeing Stool, Rumpadump, Rasharoo, and everyone she cared about fleeing finally convinced Basidia that this was not the time to stay and die. The last thing the party heard from Phylo was his cheerful, deluded voice calling after them, promising that Neverlight Grove would be better than ever and that the Great Cedar would make them all happy. He even lamented that they had refused the hospitality of the Circle of Welcome — a “welcome” that would have transformed them into heads rooted in the ground.

As they fled, the once-beautiful grove fell fully into Zuggtmoy’s rot behind them. Basidia, grateful and heartbroken, apologized for never finishing work on the party’s pet grick and promised that, given a few days, she could prepare additional potions. For the moment, she pressed potions of greater healing and a box of treasure into the party’s hands as a reward for saving what few members of her circle could still be spared. The recovered treasure totaled 975 gold pieces.

### Departure and the Road to Blingdenstone 032.04

Stool and Rumpadump departed with Basidia and the surviving myconids to remain among their own kind. Before leaving, Rumpadump delivered Zalthir an exhaustive, highly detailed, and allegedly constructive list of feedback through rapport spores, which remained in Zalthir’s head for roughly fifteen minutes. Jimjar’s previous bet was also settled: contrary to his initial recollection, Gyrgum had won five gold pieces from him. Jimjar first claimed he lacked ready cash, then relented and paid.

With Sarith dead and no longer able to guide them, the party turned toward Blingdenstone as their best hope of eventually reaching the Overbright. Jimjar confidently claimed he knew exactly where they were going and immediately tried to turn that claim into another bet. Rasharoo could not provide a guide for the entire two-week journey, but he supplied myconid scouts who could lead them safely through the first third. He also warned that Ilvara and her drow hunters remained at large. The party speculated that their pursuers might be usefully diverted by Neverlight Grove, the Circle of Welcome, or even an invitation to Zuggtmoy’s wedding.

After a long rest, the party set out once more into the deep darkness of the Underdark. Their companions were relieved to be alive, happy not to have become mushroom food, and resigned to another long journey through hostile tunnels. Seven days of largely uneventful travel passed before Rasharoo’s scouts bid them farewell. Jimjar again asserted that he knew precisely where they were going and offered to bet that Thorin would praise him once they arrived.

### Battle with the rocktopus 032.05

Soon afterward, the party entered a large cavern where a heavy, toxic, and combustible gas had settled into a deep depression in the floor. Those familiar with dwarven mines and the Underdark recognized that the gas could poison them and explode if exposed to flame. Fortunately, the party carried no open flames, and a five-foot ledge ran around either side of the depression. They began edging around the hazard, only to discover a giant rocktopus waiting on the ledge between them and the exit. The stone-like predator regarded them as convenient food.

Thorin stepped to the front and struck the beast twice with Dawnbringer, badly wounding it. The rocktopus lashed out with a tentacle, striking Thorin hard and trying to grapple him, but he shook off its grip. Zalthir closed in, restrained the creature, and battered it with a flurry of punches. Daz followed with a volley of magical force. Finally, Gyrgum softly chanted a True Strike invocation, circled into reach, and brought his mace down on the wounded monster. The final blow killed it, prompting Gyrgum to exalt as “the octopus slayer” — or, more accurately, the rocktopus slayer.

### The Cave-In 032.06

The celebration was short-lived. A sudden tremor shook the tunnels and caused a cave-in that blocked the party’s original route, trapping them in an unfamiliar maze of passages. Thorin began mapping their surroundings with cartographer’s tools while Gyrgum used calligrapher’s supplies to ensure that the resulting chart was clearly labeled in a tasteful font. Jimjar immediately argued that the cave-in did not count as his failure to find Blingdenstone because an unexplored passage still remained and they were not yet trapped beyond hope.

Feeling the pressure of his claims as a guide, Jimjar attempted to deflect with an increasingly cryptic bet that Zalthir’s next emotion would be “as if it was made of a chipped piece of obsidian,” provided Thorin did not look at something first. When pressed, he explained that the emotion would be “black and dark and sharp,” which did little to clarify matters. Spiderbait dismissed the bet as weak, and even Jimjar conceded, “It’s not my best work.” He finally admitted that the wager was a distraction from the fact that he had gotten them lost.

Jimjar quickly pivoted to suggesting that the nearby cavern might hold treasure, magic, and power worth exploring, even betting that the party would find something special. With their old route sealed and a passage opening to the north, the party agreed to investigate. They ended the session poised to go deeper into the unknown, while Jimjar continued trying to recover from the awkward collapse of his reputation as their infallible guide.
