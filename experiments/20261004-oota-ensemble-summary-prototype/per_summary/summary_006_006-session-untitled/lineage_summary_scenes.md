# Chapter 006

### Foraging in the Narrow Tunnels 006.01

The party pressed deeper into the Underdark and on the third day came to a narrow tunnel whose walls shimmered with a soft, eerie green glow. The light came from a moss called Ormu. Jimjar, the deep gnome with an incurable gambling habit, cheerfully identified it as harmless. He said it grows in warm, damp places and can be harvested and dried into a glowing powder or pigment. When asked whether painting themselves with it would help them hide, he said no, "but it would mean that we looked better." The tunnel was beautiful in its own alien way, but the group soon realized their food and water were dangerously low. The party and their allies spread out to forage and gathered ten gallons of water. At one gallon per person per day, that was enough for all ten travelers (four adventurers and six allies) for one day. They had no containers, so Daz used Shape Water to freeze the water into one-gallon blocks. Party members could chip at the blocks with their daggers and let the ice melt in their mouths as they marched. Daz would refreeze the blocks every hour. They would melt overnight, which meant a fresh search for water every morning.

#### The party navigates a narrow, phosphorescent tunnel while searching for essential supplies to survive the Underdark.
- On the third day of travel, the tunnel narrows. The party enters a narrow tunnel lined with a green, phosphorescent substance that sheds dim light.
- The group discusses the marching order for navigating the tight passage:
  - Gyrgum wants the middle: "As the cleric, I think I should go in the middle."
  - Thorin is happy to go first or last.
  - Zalthir heads toward the front.
  - Daz takes second or third: "I can't tank, but I can maybe from a person or two back help."
- A search for food and water begins as the party realizes they are low on supplies. Foraging uses individual Survival checks at DC 15; on a success, the forager rolls d6 plus their Wisdom modifier.
- Daz notes that he has Shape Water, not Create Water, so he cannot conjure more water himself.
- The party successfully collects ten gallons of water, enough for the entire group for one day. The rules call for one gallon per person per day, and the group numbers ten: six allies plus the four adventurers.
- The GM points out that the party has no container for water.
- Daz uses magic to freeze the collected water into portable ice blocks, solving the problem of having no containers:
  - He freezes one-gallon blocks and hands one to each person.
  - He refreezes them every hour.
  - People chisel off pieces with their daggers to drink on the move.
- The ice melts overnight, so the group will have to find water each morning.
- Zalthir raises a rules question from last session. The party had found a hiding spot, and the chase rules call for the quarry to make stealth checks at initiative count zero to escape, which was not done. The GM confirms "that was a mistake" and thanks him for catching it.

### Survival and Trail Covering 006.02

Food was harder to find. Only two of the allies turned up anything, but the party's own rolls were strong, and together they gathered about seventeen pounds of food. That covered the ten pounds the group needed for the day, with roughly eight pounds to spare. Eldeth, the most capable survivalist among them, took charge of covering the group's tracks, with the party helping her. Her result was a 21, and the pursuit level dropped to three, giving the group a small, welcome sense of relief. At the table, Zalthir noticed that the stealth checks for escaping a chase had been missed at the end of the previous session. The GM confirmed the mistake and clarified the rules: a true chase begins only when pursuit reaches five, and the hunt ends when it reaches zero.

#### The party manages their meager resources and works to obscure their trail from pursuers while trekking through the Underdark.
- The party and their allies forage for food, successfully gathering enough supplies to sustain the group for more than a day.
  - The group needs ten pounds of food per day.
  - Gyrgum's first roll fails. Everyone searches.
  - Thorin arrives late, rolls a 7, and finds nothing. His Tactical Mind would add 1d10 to a failed check but is only expended if the check then succeeds.
  - Only two of the allies find food.
  - The GM tallies seventeen pounds total, eight pounds extra.
- The GM reminds the party that they cannot search while running.
- Jimjar identifies the glowing green substance lining the tunnel as Ormu, a moss that grows in warm, damp areas, sheds dim light in a five-foot radius, and can be harvested, dried, and made into phosphorescent pigment. The GM had first started to attribute this to Sarith before correcting to Jimjar.
- Thorin asks whether Ormu paint would make them harder to see. Jimjar says no, but it would make them look better.
- The party harvests some Ormu.
- Eldeth leads the effort to cover the party's tracks, successfully reducing the pursuit level from four to three.
  - Zalthir insists on helping: "She's the best. We're helping her."
  - The roll comes to a 21.
- Asked when the stealth-escape rules apply, the GM explains that the chase rules only kick in when pursuit reaches five, and the hunt ends when pursuit reaches zero. Thorin: "So it's a zero base counter, not a one base counter."
- The party discusses blocking the narrow passage with a boulder or collapsing it to further delay any pursuit.
  - Zalthir asks about "a convenient boulder nearby."
  - Gyrgum asks whether anyone has spells that could bring the roof down.
  - The GM notes that a blockage wouldn't stop someone very determined unless they spent several days on it or had magic, but it would buy time.

### A Gambler's Blockade and Whispers in the Dark 006.03

After passing through the narrow tunnel (uneventfully, apart from admiring the moss), the party discussed blocking it behind them. They wanted to disguise the blockage so pursuers would not realize the passage had been sealed on purpose. That raised the question of who would do the deceiving, and all eyes fell on Jimjar and his +5 Deception.

As usual, Jimjar refused to lift a finger without a wager. He asked for five gold pieces. Zalthir countered with a pound of food, and Jimjar valued that at a single copper. Daz pointed out that Zalthir had once had a gambling problem and had vowed never to gamble again, so Gyrgum took the bet instead. Gyrgum admitted afterward that he didn't even know which side of it he was on. Jimjar disguised the blockage successfully, which meant he lost the bet. He now owes Gyrgum five gold pieces he does not have. The party slowly understood something about him: Jimjar did not bet to win. He bet because the wager itself was the point. "For him, if there's no bet, what's the point of doing it?" He was not a fool about it, though. When Zalthir bet that he couldn't fly to the roof of the cave, Jimjar declined, explaining that anyone can decline to do something stupid; what matters is whether he can do the important thing when it counts. That night, as the group settled in to rest, Daz noticed the deep gnome twins Topsy and Turvy huddled together, talking quietly. Daz could not make out a word, and an insight check revealed nothing about their intentions.

#### The party attempts to block a narrow tunnel to hinder their pursuers using Jimjar's deceptive talents, and later notices suspicious behavior in camp.
- The party discusses disguising the tunnel blockage so it does not appear intentional. Gyrgum: "We'd block it in a way that didn't make it clear it was intentional." The GM calls for Deception.
- Daz has +0 Deception, and Zalthir has +0 Persuasion. Jimjar has +5, so the job falls to him.
- Jimjar refuses to help unless a wager is made, prompting a discussion about his bizarre gambling obsession.
  - He asks for five gold pieces.
  - Zalthir offers a pound of food. Jimjar values the pound of food at "a copper piece."
  - Zalthir tries appealing to survival ("Does he care about not dying?"). Jimjar replies, "do you want to bet on that?"
  - Zalthir wonders aloud whether leaving him behind would help the pursuit number.
  - Jimjar presses: "come on, just one bet, Zalthir."
- Daz explains that Zalthir once had a gambling problem and vowed never to get back into it.
- Gyrgum takes the bet; Jimjar, who bet against himself, disguises the blockage successfully and now owes Gyrgum five gold pieces.
  - The GM, as Jimjar: "congratulations, you won the bet."
  - Jimjar will pay "when Jimjar has five gold pieces."
  - Gyrgum admits, "I didn't even know which side of the bet I was taking."
- The party realizes Jimjar is not in it for the money but for the thrill of the bet itself. "He's not interested in winning. He's interested in betting."
- Zalthir tests the theory by betting Jimjar can't fly to the roof of the cave. Jimjar declines: anyone can decline to do something stupid; what matters is whether he can do the important thing when it counts.
- While settling down for the night, Daz notices the twins Topsy and Turvy whispering secretly to one another, though their motives remain unclear.
  - A d4 decides randomly who notices.
  - Daz's insight check reveals nothing.
  - Stool's spores had worn off after an hour, so nothing was overheard through them.

### Arrival at the Silken Paths 006.04

After two more days of grueling travel, the party found ten gallons of water (Thorin led the search) and, with Zalthir's help and a strong second roll from Gyrgum, twenty pounds of food. Then Sarith, the drow who had been guiding them, broke into barely contained excitement. Before them yawned an enormous chasm, five hundred feet deep and two thousand feet wide, strung with a shifting network of enormous spider webs. "Yes, I'm very excited. This means we're in the right direction. All we have to do is cross this cavern." Sarith explained that the place was known as the Silken Paths. The major highways were easy to travel, but old webs disintegrate and the giant spiders are always spinning new ones, so the paths across are always changing. His advice amounted to: cross your fingers.

#### After days of travel, the party reaches a massive web-filled chasm and negotiates with two eccentric goblin guides.
- Two more days of foraging and hiking; on the fifth day Thorin finds ten gallons of water.
  - The allies find no food.
  - Zalthir helps and finds five pounds of food.
  - Gyrgum's second food roll brings the total to twenty pounds, "Ten extra for tomorrow."
- Sarith successfully navigates the group to a massive, two-thousand-foot-wide chasm filled with a shifting network of giant spider webs, known as the Silken Paths. The chasm is also 500 feet deep.
- Sarith expresses excitement at having found the right direction, explaining that the webs are constantly being rebuilt by giant spiders and the paths are always changing.
  - Sarith: "This means we're in the right direction. All we have to do is cross this cavern."
  - He adds that the major highways are very traversable, but old webs disintegrate, so everyone should cross their fingers.
- Gyrgum: "We crossed a cavern with spider webs in it before. Why not again?"
- Two goblins, Yuk Yuk and Spiderbait, emerge and offer their services as guides through the Silken Paths for two gold pieces per day. They pop up just as Sarith is about to step onto the web.
- Daz: "No. We're destitute and without funds."
- Gyrgum points at Jimjar: "I'm owed five gold pieces by this guy." Jimjar begins listing all of his debts, "both public and private."

### A Wager with Goblins 006.05

Just as Sarith was about to step onto the web, two goblins popped up and introduced themselves: "Hi, my name is Yuk Yuk," and "mine is Spiderbait." They had lived in the Underdark for a long time and offered their services as guides through the Silken Paths for two gold pieces per day. Daz told them the party was "destitute and without funds." Gyrgum added that Jimjar owed him five gold, and Jimjar started listing all of his debts, public and private. Thorin proposed, as a sidebar, that the goblins be made to prove they could cross, and Gyrgum suggested Jimjar simply bet them. Jimjar did: "I'll make you a bet that you don't know how to travel this." His persuasion came to 15. A very annoyed Yuk Yuk countered with a bet of twenty gold pieces that he could get "from here to over there." Gyrgum's insight told him Jimjar would not be paying, but he played along. He said Jimjar was honest and would pay when he had the money, and he offered to sign over Jimjar's five-gold IOU as well. The two goblins put something strange on their feet and proceeded to slide and surf across the strands.

#### Jimjar strikes a risky bargain with the goblin guides to secure their services for the crossing.
- The party, being entirely destitute, brainstorms ways to negotiate with the goblins using Jimjar's love of betting.
  - Thorin (sidebar) suggests making them prove they can cross: "How do we know that you know how to cross? Why don't you show us?"
  - Gyrgum suggests Jimjar "could just bet them that they can't do it."
- Jimjar challenges Yuk Yuk to a bet, wagering that the goblins cannot successfully navigate a section of the webs.
  - His persuasion roll (d20+5) comes to 15.
  - A very annoyed Yuk Yuk counters: "well, I will bet you 20 gold pieces that I know how to get from here to over there."
- Gyrgum rolls insight (14) and realizes Jimjar isn't going to pay. He tells the goblins Jimjar is honest and will pay when he has the money, and he offers to sign over Jimjar's five-gold IOU to them as well.
- Yuk Yuk and Spiderbait demonstrate their skills by sliding and surfing across the strands, dodging spiders with impressive agility.
  - They apply "something strange on their feet" first.
  - The GM swears off web puns: "I will not start breaking up all sorts of web references."
- The goblins win the bet, but Jimjar reveals he has no money to pay them.
  - The goblins come back and say "see?"
  - Jimjar replies, "yep, you won the bet."
  - Yuk Yuk is very annoyed.
- Spiderbait proposes a new deal: they will guide the party in exchange for a share of future gold to cover their fee plus the twenty gold Jimjar now owes them. His reasoning: "the bet was entertaining and fun. Plus, we need to follow you guys to get the money."
- The party agrees, gaining Yuk Yuk and Spiderbait as guides through the Silken Paths. Gyrgum: "This is about as good as it could have come out of it."
- Zalthir asks whether the goblins seem trustworthy. The GM says they give positive vibes, like "two goblin surfer dudes."
- The goblins brag after the run:
  - Yuk Yuk describes his "twist and flip."
  - Spiderbait says Yuk Yuk "kind of took the easy route" compared with how he himself went around a spider and through its legs.
  - They keep an informal score of who does the coolest thing.

### Traversing the Silken Paths 006.06

The goblins returned triumphant. Jimjar cheerfully conceded, "yep, you won the bet," then admitted he had no money. Yuk Yuk was very annoyed. Spiderbait paused and made a counter-proposal: the bet had been entertaining, and the goblins needed to follow the party to get paid anyway. So they would act as guides in exchange for a share of any gold the group acquired, up to their fee plus the twenty gold Jimjar now owed. The party agreed at once. Asked whether the goblins seemed trustworthy, the GM described them as "two goblin surfer dudes," and they spent the rest of the crossing bragging about twists, flips, and dodging spiders, and keeping score of "who does the coolest thing." They explained that the party would not be surfing ("that's advanced techniques. We'll just walk"). They also said the webs could not be burned down: a crazy wizard had tried, but there were simply too many spiders rebuilding faster than he could burn. Thorin set the terms plainly: if the spiders attacked, the deal was off. The party tied themselves together with rope, went without light, and set off along the five-foot-wide strands over the void. Along the way the goblins sang about web surfing and brought up the time their friend Bobby was eaten by a spider, a detail quickly shouted down for killing the mood.

#### The party prepares for a dangerous trek across a massive network of spider webs suspended over a deep chasm.
- The guides explain the dangers of the Silken Paths, noting that the webs are constantly being rebuilt by giant spiders and that large spiders inhabit the chasm.
  - Asked about burning the web down: "oh, you can't burn it down. So many people have tried." A crazy wizard once tried, but "there's just a lot of" spiders rebuilding faster than he could burn.
  - The chasm is a massive connection node with endless entrance points, some opening and closing over time.
  - On vibrations alerting spiders: "There's so much other food around here. They're probably not going to notice us."
- Thorin sets terms: if the spiders attack, "the deal's off because you haven't delivered what you promised."
- The guides tell the party not to try surfing: "That's advanced techniques. We'll just walk."
- Movement and falling rules:
  - Creatures with a climbing speed walk the webs normally. For everyone else they are difficult terrain, so the party moves 15 feet per round.
  - Taking damage, or having the web break underfoot, forces a DC 15 Dexterity saving throw.
  - On a failure, a creature falls 10 to 100 feet, or ends up restrained in webbing lower down.
- The party decides to travel without light sources to avoid attracting predators. Carrying light attracts hostile creatures, and everyone has darkvision.
- To prevent fatal falls, the group ties themselves together using rope, ensuring they can catch one another if a strand breaks.
  - The rope grants advantage on the save.
  - If someone fails, the next person in line makes a Strength check.
  - Gyrgum has eight ropes, enough for everyone.
  - Daz suggests spacing out beyond 30 feet so that one fall doesn't drag others into the same saving throw.
- From the goblins' warnings about fire, Zalthir sums up the danger: fire makes several strands break, and everyone within thirty feet must save or fall. The GM confirms the 30-foot area is a radius.
- The party begins crossing the five-foot-wide web strands spanning the chasm.
- The goblins regale the party with stories of amazing jumps across the chasm, singing about web surfing, until one brings up Bobby getting eaten by a spider: "Dude, why are you bringing us down, man?"

### The Cocooned Halfling 006.07

Before long someone spotted a cocoon in the webbing, and it was moving. Stool, the small myconid sprout who was endlessly eager to be helpful ("I'm really helpful, aren't I?"), used his spores to establish a connection. A voice emerged: "My name is Fargas Rumblefoot. Can you please get me out of here?" Fargas said his adventuring band had been searching for a long-lost tomb when a pack of mad gnolls attacked. He described gnolls as demon-possessed creatures that implant their spawn in living bodies, where it later erupts. He had escaped and fled deeper into the Underdark, only to be caught by the spiders and "placed here as a snack." He insisted he was not infected. Thorin answered, "That's what I used to say in the zombie apocalypse."

Yuk Yuk cut the deliberation short: "dudes, I have a really bad feeling about this, but I think there are some spiders that are coming here." Fargas, sensing that he might be left behind, played his last card: "I know the location of the tomb, and more importantly, I know the location of the treasure." The cocoon was tough but vulnerable to slashing weapons (AC 10, five points of damage). Thorin drew his short sword and cut through it in one stroke, "the first good roll I've gotten since we started playing." Fargas tumbled free and initiative was rolled, with Thorin at 17 and Zalthir at 5.

### Tactical Stand at the Silken Paths 006.08

With the spiders closing in, the GM drew the major web strands on the map. Each strand was five feet wide, the gaps between them were empty space, and cutting a strand dropped the segment between two junctions. Zalthir stepped to a junction and cut one segment, forcing the pursuing spiders to spin a new bridge before they could follow. He then struck a second strand, which frayed but held by a single hit point. Daz floated an Indiana Jones–style plan to cut everything and swing to safety, and Thorin suggested crossing one bridge and severing all the rest. The GM realized aloud that he had "created a graph problem for a bunch of computers." The session paused mid-combat, with Zalthir's bonus action still waiting for the next round and the far edge of the chasm still very far away. The group agreed to resume after New Year's.

#### The party navigates the precarious web network while being pursued by giant spiders, attempting to sabotage the path to slow their enemies.
- The GM draws the major strands onto the map. Strands are five feet wide, the gaps are empty space, and the party cannot step off a strand without jumping.
- The party analyzes the structural integrity of the massive web strands, discussing how severing segments could block the spiders' path.
  - Cutting a strand takes an action against AC 10 and five points of damage.
  - A cut collapses the segment between its two junctions, and anyone on that segment falls.
  - Spiders can spin new webs, but that slows them.
- Zalthir weighs his options, noting his fire breath, his bonus-action Dash, and his speed of 20. He observes: "I feel like there's some interesting graph theory here."
- Zalthir steps to a junction and cuts one segment of web, so the spiders will have to spin a new bridge.
- On his turn Zalthir strikes a second strand, which frays but holds by one hit point.
- Daz pitches an Indiana Jones–style plan: cut all the strands but one, grab on, and swing out. Thorin proposes crossing one bridge and then cutting all the others behind them.
- The GM realizes aloud: "I created a graph problem for a bunch of computers. I didn't even realize I was doing it."
- The party coordinates their movement to avoid falling into the void as the spiders close in.
- Zalthir notes he still has a bonus action to open the next round.
- The session ends on a cliffhanger with the spiders approaching and the web strand not yet fully severed. With holiday absences, the group agrees to resume after New Year's.
