# Chapter 038 Wererats, Wisdom, and the War on Ooze

### Aftermath in the Traders' Grotto 038.01

The aftermath of the battle with the gelatinous cubes in the Traders' Grotto left the party facing a crowd of questioning merchants and citizens. Despite having saved almost everyone, the adventurers worried that destroying part of the market might complicate their reception. Instead, the public was more disturbed that outsiders had handled the crisis more effectively than Blingdenstone’s own guards. One citizen complained, “Taxes, we pay taxes,” while a sergeant attempted to control the narrative by declaring, “Chief Dorbo Diggermattock hired these adventurers to solve our problems. Chief Dorbo Diggermattock is on top of everything.”

The crowd’s relief quickly exposed political tensions. A citizen praised Senni Diggermattock as an advocate for ordinary people while accusing Chief Dorbo of caring only for “the gold from the gems from the mines.” According to the critic, “Everything is sacrificed on the altar of wealth,” and Dorbo had been obsessed with “money, money, money, money, money” since leading the reconquest of the area. When the party suggested that Dorbo use some of that wealth to rebuild the damaged businesses, the increasingly nervous sergeant called it an excellent suggestion—then strongly encouraged them to leave the market and pursue their assigned mission. The party correctly recognized that Blingdenstone’s political class was trying to claim credit for their victory while keeping their “revolutionary tactics” away from the crowd.

Glabbagool, the intelligent gelatinous cube traveling with the party, wondered whether he had also been hired and whether he would be paid. Asked what one buys for “the gelatinous cube that has everything,” he decided that the answer was friends. The party assured him that he could come with them. Amid the wreckage, they also recovered a magical short sword, which Daz ultimately claimed as an upgrade after the group discussed weapon proficiencies and whether anyone else needed a magical weapon.

### Discussion on Ooze Problem and Pathfinding 038.02

The sergeant directed the party toward a rubble-choked route leading out of the Traders’ Grotto and reminded them of their bargain: Blingdenstone would provide directions out of the Underdark once the adventurers dealt with the city’s ooze problem. Before they departed, a merchant recommended the Foaming Mug, both for its Darklake Stout and because it had some of the only beds in town large enough for the comparatively enormous adventurers. The Chamber of Commerce also granted the party a ten percent discount on purchases. When the offer sounded suspiciously like an advertisement, the merchant literally wrote them a coupon.

The route through the rubble led toward the disputed Goldwhisker Warrens. Blingdenstone’s authorities described Clan Goldwhisker as wererat squatters who were not even a legitimate clan, while merchants offered a more nuanced warning and noted that Senni held a more favorable view of them. The party recalled their former wererat companions Topsy and Turvy and recognized that the dispute was rooted in competing claims to territory abandoned after the drow occupation: the Goldwhiskers had settled there before Clan Diggermattock returned and began to “gentrify” the area.

Rather than search for another route, the party proposed using Glabbagool as a living bulldozer. He repeatedly moved into the rubble, engulfed loose stones, backed out, and released them elsewhere. After about an hour, he had created a usable passage. His work impressed the watching deep gnomes, one of whom proposed hiring him for mining operations. Glabbagool delighted in the possibility of having “a job and a role in society” and imagined people greeting him each morning with, “Glabbagool, we need you.” The party reminded him that they already needed him. He especially identified with Zalthir, whom he considered a fellow ooze and therefore a kindred spirit.

### Residential Cave Exploration 038.03

Beyond the rubble, the party entered an abandoned residential cave. Homes had once been dug into the walls at various heights, but a foul-smelling pile of refuse now dominated the chamber. Zalthir’s first inspection revealed nothing, but Daz found decomposing matter and two slimy, partially digested deep gnome corpses. The remains had unmistakably passed through an ooze. Daz then traced an ooze trail into a hallway.

Glabbagool began moving toward the trail with unusual purpose. He admitted that he could hear voices calling to him and became excited by the prospect that others of his kind might be able to speak. The party feared that the voices represented demonic influence rather than friendly oozes. Zalthir persuaded Glabbagool to let his friends go first and protect him from corruption. Glabbagool agreed, declaring, “You’re my friend, so we should all go together,” and praised the party for protecting him from “the bad oozes.” Thorin led the way with Zalthir beside him; Zalthir detected an oddly placed pit trap, and the party crossed it safely. At the next junction, Glabbagool indicated that the voices came from the south.

### The Ooze Cavern and the Pudding King 038.04

The southern passage opened into an immense cavern carpeted with more oozes than the party had ever seen—an “ooza palooza” so overwhelming that a direct battle would certainly kill them. Green slime coated the cavern walls, and a great spherical structure stood upon stone pillars at the center. Dozens of oozes crawled around those pillars, extending grasping pseudopods, while black, yellow, and gray slime swirled across the sphere in patterns that strained the eyes and tugged at the mind.

A disembodied voice addressed the visitors and proclaimed the coming of the “Faceless Lord,” invoking the Faceless Lord and all of Blingdenstone. The surrounding oozes urged Glabbagool to join them, but he refused. He liked the party and sensed “something not nice” about the assembled oozes. More importantly, “Oozes don’t talk. Something’s weird.” Gyrgum recalled that Juiblex was also known as the Faceless Lord and connected the scene to an earlier ghost’s warning: devils create contracts, while demons infuse creatures or objects with power to generate chaos and mayhem. The party concluded that Juiblex was probably working through a local proxy rather than appearing personally.

Because the cavern could not be crossed safely, Daz summoned a bat familiar and named him Batman. Batman conducted a breadth-first reconnaissance of the surrounding passages. He found abandoned remnants of the old gnomish settlement, dripping caves occupied by scattered oozes, wreckage scoured clean, and the ruins of a fungal partition. In another cave, phosphorescent lichen illuminated pools and hanging drapes of green slime. A slime-covered throne faced east at its center.

A small gnome sat upon that throne, holding court like an insane monarch. He spoke of cleansing Blingdenstone of everyone who had irritated him and declared that the Pudding King would become king of Blingdenstone. Glabbagool and the party recognized his voice as the one projected through the great ooze cavern. The Pudding King himself did not appear particularly formidable, but hundreds of oozes and the slime covering his chamber made a direct attack impractical. Daz’s use of Batman had allowed the party to identify the enemy without exposing themselves, earning him inspiration.

### Planning the Pudding King's Capture 038.05

Zalthir nevertheless devised the core of a workable plan. In dim light, he could Shadow Step up to sixty feet, reach the Pudding King, grapple him, and fly away with him. Green slime clinging to the ceiling complicated the escape because it could fall onto Zalthir as he passed underneath. More seriously, the Pudding King’s incapacitation could release hundreds of uncontrolled oozes to wander through Blingdenstone in search of food. A large, coordinated distraction—and defenders ready to contain the ooze army—would therefore be necessary.

The party considered fireballs, collapsing rubble, and sealing the caves, but Glabbagool warned that oozes could squeeze through extremely small gaps. Gyrgum offered to motivate Blingdenstone’s troops with a speech, imagining a rallying cry that began, “We will fight them in the pudding court. We will fight them on the rubble. We will fight them by the glowing pool.” They concluded that they needed the city’s army and possibly Clan Goldwhisker. The common war against the oozes might even provide “an opportunity for reconciliation.”

### Presenting the Plan to Blingdenstone's Leadership 038.06

The party returned to Blingdenstone, where bystanders pretended to work while straining to overhear their report. Chief Dorbo demanded, “Has my ooze problem been dealt with?” The party answered that they had found the means to solve it but required his help. After hearing about the Pudding King and the hundreds of oozes, Dorbo called the situation awful, terrible, catastrophic, and cataclysmic.

Gyrgum presented the plan plainly: “The plan is I make a motivational speech to your army, and they distract the oozes while we kill their king.” Dorbo called it “the dumbest plan I’ve heard ever,” but Senni told him that he had spoken enough and asked whether he possessed a better one. He did not.

To demonstrate the plan’s viability, Zalthir asked for dim light. The assembled deep gnomes eagerly extinguished the lights, allowing him to teleport around the chamber, perform flips, and throw punches in the air. Senni understood immediately: Zalthir would infiltrate the throne room, seize the Pudding King, and kill or extract him while Blingdenstone’s forces occupied the ooze army. Jimjar then made an extraordinary wager in the party’s favor, telling Dorbo, “I’ll make you a bet that by the time this is all done, we’re all going to be living happily ever after.” His willingness to bet on them helped move the skeptical chief.

Dorbo still argued that Blingdenstone’s forces were not prepared to fight oozes and that they needed allies and equipment. Senni called for a full clan meeting involving the Stoneheart Enclave and the Miners’ Guild. Dorbo attempted to exclude Clan Goldwhisker, insisting that its members were not even gnomes. Senni corrected him: they were lycanthropes who merely transformed into rats, and their immunity to ordinary weapons could make them invaluable against oozes.

When Dorbo suggested that the Goldwhiskers could participate only if they acknowledged his authority, Daz seized control of the meeting. He declared that the adventurers—not the rival gnome leaders—would command the operation: “We figured it out, and we’re going to be in charge of it.” The party would solve Blingdenstone’s problem in its own way, without the bickering of those whose political conflict had helped create the current crisis. The room fell silent at the “management coup.” Senni then accepted the arrangement and tasked the party with securing Clan Goldwhisker’s alliance.
