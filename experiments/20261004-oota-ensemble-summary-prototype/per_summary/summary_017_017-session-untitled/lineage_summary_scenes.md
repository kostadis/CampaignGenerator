# Chapter 017

### Swarm of the Centipedes 017.01

Deep within the Whorlstone Tunnels, the party was beset by writhing swarms of centipedes that crawled over Zalthir and Daz. Thorin was eager to test his newly acquired Sentinel feat, which lets him stop foes in their tracks with opportunity attacks, though he quickly learned he gets only one such reaction per round. He repositioned himself to shield his companions while Gyrgum channeled radiant energy through his hand axe to scatter the insects. Zalthir punched and stomped the centipedes clinging to his body and insisted afterward that the frantic display was a long-standing tradition of his monastic order. One by one the swarms were destroyed. Daz unleashed a volley of magic missiles, and Thorin delivered the killing blow to the last cluster. The GM cued "appropriate music" and the tunnels fell quiet once more.

#### The party is ambushed by swarms of centipedes in the fungal thickets of the Whorlstone Tunnels.
- Before play, Thorin explains his new Sentinel feat. Foes who disengage near him still provoke an opportunity attack, and so do foes who attack someone other than him within 5 feet. A hit drops the target's speed to 0. The GM clarifies that the attack must hit and that it uses his reaction, so only one per round. Thorin's reply: "Well, that's some bullshit."
- A swarm of centipedes crawls over Zalthir. The GM rules that the movement does not trigger Sentinel, but the bite does. The swarm's attack misses, and Thorin's free opportunity attack also misses ("I suck").
- The centipedes bite Daz, whose AC is only 11, for 10 damage. Another swarm attacks Gyrgum (AC 14) but fails to land a blow.
- Zalthir retaliates by punching and stomping on the insects. His first hit leaves one swarm at 1 hit point and his second blow misses. The table asks whether stepping away from a swarm Thorin had hit with Sentinel would leave it stuck; the GM confirms it would, "but for next time."
- Daz disengages from the melee to find a safer position, after the GM suggests he "might want to consider that as a lifestyle choice." Based on an earlier check, the party believes these are the last of the swarms.
- Gyrgum casts True Strike through his hand axe, making the attack with Wisdom. He deals 9 radiant damage (1d6+4 plus an extra 1d6).
- Thorin finishes off the 1-hit-point swarm and moves to protect Daz from further harm.
- The remaining centipedes swarm Thorin and Gyrgum. A swarm rolls exactly 18 against Thorin's AC 18 and hits for 10. A swarm lands a critical, but it deals only 10 damage because swarms at half hit points do half damage.
- Zalthir punches the swarm on Gyrgum for 7 damage, leaving it at 2 hit points, then destroys it with his second strike.
- Daz fires magic missiles at the final swarm for 15 damage, leaving it at 7 hit points. Gyrgum closes in and casts True Strike again but misses.
- Thorin hits the last swarm for 8 and kills it ("Ding dong"). The GM cues the appropriate music: "There are no more centipedes."

### A Moment of Respite 017.02

With wounds still fresh, the party weighed a short rest against the chase. Droki, the derro messenger they were following, had fled ahead, but the GM noted he was "a creature of ridiculous habits" who ran the same circuit, so "even a blind rat could follow his path." Nobody wanted to linger in the fungus. Gyrgum instead called upon divine power with his new Prayer of Healing. The healing warmth washed over Thorin, Daz, Zalthir, and even their ethereal ghostly companion, and granted each of them the benefit of a short rest on the spot. Restored, the group followed their quarry's route further southeast. Along the way they peered into a natural cave and saw a deeply unsettling sight. A group of myconids was dancing frantically to a silent tune, accompanied by two hulking quaggoths covered in mushroom growths and yellow mold. The party had learned from their myconid friend that myconids simply do not dance, so they chose to leave the strange scene undisturbed and move on.

#### Following the centipede attack, the party decides to heal their wounds before continuing their pursuit.
- The GM lays out the options: a passageway southwest, the quarry's passageway, and the accumulated damage suggesting a rest.
- Daz asks whether resting would lose their quarry. The GM explains he will likely come round again within a day or so on the same circuit: "He's a creature of ridiculous habits, so even a blind rat could follow his path."
- The party discusses whether to take a short rest in the dangerous fungal thicket or move to a safer location. Thorin: "I think it sounds like a good idea not to hang out in the fungus."
- Gyrgum offers his healing prayer as an alternative, since it has the effect of a short rest, so the party can "do that and keep moving."
- Gyrgum casts Prayer of Healing on up to five creatures: Thorin, Daz, Zalthir, himself, and the ghost ("I don't know whether ghosts rest"). Each gets a short rest's benefit plus an additional 2d8 healing.
- It is the first of Gyrgum's three 2nd-level spell slots. Someone at the table: "boy that's a really excellent spell."
- The party feels rejuvenated and prepares to follow the trail of their quarry further southeast.

### The Dancing Fungi 017.03

Around a bend in the passage they encountered something none of them had ever seen before: a flumph. The small floating creature spoke directly into their minds and chided them for staring at it blankly. It introduced itself as a being that fed on the background psionic energy radiated by evil creatures. Before the party could fully process what they were looking at, the flumph unleashed a torrent of vivid, distressing psychic visions upon them. They saw a shrine to Demogorgon, a broken magical shard being worked upon near a dark obelisk, and a stolen red dragon egg being subjected to cruel experiments. When the flood of memories subsided, the flumph announced cheerfully that it felt much better, having unburdened itself entirely at the party's expense. Thorin promptly named the habit: it had "trauma dumped" on them.

#### The party discovers a strange group of myconids and spore servants performing a frantic dance in a natural cave.
- The red line on the map shows the quarry continuing southeast. A cave opens off to the south, and the party decides to glance into it "carefully."
- Zalthir stealthily scouts ahead with a Stealth roll of 26 ("it's no 38 but 26 is still pretty good"). Thorin notes that his own stealth abilities "are essentially non-existent."
- In the natural cave, several fungal creatures dance to a silent tune. Three myconid adults stand five feet tall and the sprouts are half that height. One small sprout stands apart from the rest, its movement not nearly as frantic.
- The party recalls what their myconid friend taught them: "myconids don't dance." They farm and sit around in circles.
- The scouts identify two larger ape-like creatures as quaggoth spore servants covered in mushroom growths and yellow mold.
- The GM notes that myconids are not relentlessly violent and might be talked to. Daz worries they could turn the party into spore servants, and is reminded that "typically, they have to kill you first."
- The party decides to avoid the fungal creatures for now and flags them to raise with their contacts later. They then resume following the trail east.

### Meeting the Flumph 017.04

Through the flumph's psychic revelations, the party learned that a derro savant named Plinki had been torturing the stolen red dragon egg. She was attempting to mutate the embryo into a two-headed creature, a pet and eventually a grand mount for the demon lord Demogorgon. The flumph also said that about ten derro guarded the obelisk chamber. It shared the locations of the Assassin's Guild, where a mind flayer it considered "a tasty snack" also lurked, and of the Council of Savants. It pointed out a leader-type figure named Zubriska, always surrounded by four bodyguards. The shrine was a cheap, recently crafted affair heaped with papers the party could not read through the vision. The flumph lamented that its once "hippy-dippy" home had been corrupted over the past year. The trouble began when the faerzress, the glowing magical substance that blocks teleportation throughout the Underdark, blinked out and back again, and everyone since had grown crankier and crueler. Whether the party wanted it to or not, the flumph declared it would be accompanying them, and so it did.

#### A telepathic flumph approaches the party, sharing visions of corruption and the location of a stolen dragon egg.
- A curious floating creature known as a flumph appears and says "well, hello" directly into the party's minds, on a shared telepathic "party line."
- The flumph explains that telepathy is a survival trait in the Underdark, because sound travels through tunnels and attracts the wrong things. It complains that the party is "not very talkative."
- Thorin explains they are wary because nearly everyone they have met below has tried to kill them. When he asks "What is a flumph?", it looks very disappointed.
- The flumph explains that it feeds on the psionic energy of evil creatures such as the aboleth, the gith, and mind flayers. Thorin challenges how it decides who is evil and suggests it sounds like "pre-crime." He then concedes, "we're not evil, so I'm not too worried."
- Through a telepathic "trauma dump," the flumph shares vivid images of derro working on a broken magical shard in a chamber with a circular pathway and an obelisk, along with a flood of scenes of corruption and people discussing foul deeds.
- Thorin asks whether this is the kind of thing that would make a giant grow another head. The flumph doesn't know.
- The party sees a vision of a shrine to Demogorgon and the stolen red dragon egg, and infers the location of the Gray Ghost lair. The Gray Ghosts are the faction that stole the egg from the Keepers of the Flame.
- Thorin asks what color ghosts are, since their own ghost is blue. The flumph looks at him funny: "What colors are ghosts?"
- The flumph hopes the party will deal with "these evil people who have polluted my home." It shows them the route: a quick passageway, and a door further along, both leading to the "obelisk of doom."
- When the party asks about dangers along the way, the flumph admits there are "lots of evil things going on." It sinks sadly, trauma dumps again, and feels much better.
- The flumph laments the corruption of its home. It was once a "hippy-dippy, cool place" where a flumph could "chill out and shillax." Some six or seven months ago the faerzress blinked out and back, and the inhabitants have grown crankier ever since, with assassins moving in to take over an area.
- The flumph reveals that the Assassin's Guild hides down the hallway the party was already heading along, and that a mind flayer is there. It describes feeding on the mind flayer's psionic aura as a filter feeder, without ever probing it.
- The party notes that the Assassin's Guild being right here contradicts Errde's belief that it doesn't exist. Several of Errde's relatives were killed by the Guild.
- Zalthir recalls his monastic studies and recognizes a small tendril on the flumph as one known to collect psionic energy, confirming it is a psionic filter feeder. Daz rolls a 2 on Arcana and Thorin tries Athletics, with a "really shitty" result.
- The GM steps out of the fiction to note that the party does not have to clear these caves now and can return later. Their goals in Gracklstugh are equipment and escape, and magic items are not yet for sale. They could always take their boat and leave, though the drow remain on their trail.
- Asked whether time is short, the flumph says there is "no real danger" from the obelisk. It remembers the name of the savant working on it: Plinki.
- The flumph reveals that Plinki is torturing the stolen red dragon egg. She is trying to give the embryo a second head so she can present it as a pet and eventually a grand mount for Demogorgon.
- About ten derro are in the obelisk area.
- The flumph reveals where the Council of Savants meets: a psionic group it has been "feeding off of quite energetically," near the secret passage. It points out a leader-type figure named Zubriska, always accompanied by four bodyguards.
- The Demogorgon shrine is covered in papers, but the mental image is not detailed enough to read them. The shrine itself is "recently crafted, low-quality art," the work of a "Johnny come lately."
- The GM cautions the party to be careful what they tell Themberchaud, since the dragon doesn't know about the egg. Thorin restates his dream of riding out "victoriously on a back of the dragon."
- The party decides against facing roughly ten derro without a long rest and resolves to go back to its factions for backup. The flumph is its evidence.
- The flumph decides to join the adventurers, much to their mild chagrin regarding its constant psychic offloading.

### Negotiations with the Keepers of the Flame 017.05

The GM stepped briefly out of the fiction to remind the party that their real goal in Gracklstugh was to get equipment and get out, and that these caves could be revisited later. Facing ten derro without a long rest seemed unwise, so the party chose to go back for backup. They left the Whorlstone Tunnels and returned to the Darklake District of Gracklstugh with their telepathic companion floating along behind them. Their ghost was kept invisible, since people tend to panic at the sight of ghosts. They went first to the Keepers of the Flame, who were closer, and sought out Gartokkar Xundorn, the intense head of the order. He pulled them aside into a separate chamber the moment he spotted them. The flumph projected a vivid image of the egg directly into Gartokkar's mind, and he immediately demanded to know why they had not simply brought it back. He tasked the party with retrieving the egg. He also wanted a small statuette of Demogorgon from the Gray Ghost lair and any incriminating paperwork, which together with the inscriptions on the egg could persuade the Deepking to wage holy war against the Gray Ghosts.

The party, recognizing the leverage they held, negotiated shrewdly. Gyrgum first accepted the opening offer of 2,000 gold pieces far too quickly. Gartokkar himself rejected the terms and insisted on 30% up front and 70% on delivery. The party then raised a confidentiality rider ensuring that the dragon Themberchaud would be kept ignorant of the egg's existence. After a flash of murderous madness, Gartokkar agreed, muttering that what Themberchaud didn't know couldn't hurt him. Thorin demanded double for the discretion, and Daz hinted at other bidders. Gartokkar, sweating over the political disaster a leak would cause, conceded an extra thousand. A failed attempt at intimidation settled the upfront terms at 1,100 gold pieces, which the party split evenly at 275 apiece. They also received official badges that would allow their companions to accompany them on the mission. As the coins changed hands, Gartokkar fixed the party with a cold, furious stare and enumerated, in exhaustive detail, every cave, rock, tunnel, and pool in the Underdark where they might think to hide should they fail to deliver.

#### The party returns to Gracklstugh to report their discovery of the egg to Gartokkar Xundorn and negotiate payment and assistance for its recovery.
- The party exits the Whorlstone Tunnels with the flumph and the ghost. The ghost stays invisible because people freak out at ghosts, which prompts GM jokes about invisible people colliding.
- The party returns to the Darklake district. The order of visits doesn't matter, so they pick the Keepers of the Flame first because they are closer, and see the Keepers before Themberchaud, who expects to be filled in on everything.
- Gartokkar Xundorn rushes up, steers them to a chamber in a building away from the cavern, and demands to know if the stolen red dragon egg has been found.
- The flumph telepathically projects an image of the egg to Gartokkar as proof, which he finds "quite pleasant, actually."
- Gartokkar asks why they didn't bring the egg. Gyrgum points out that nobody asked them to, and that carrying the egg of a large dangerous creature makes the adults "even more dangerous." Gartokkar reminds them they agreed to be agents of Themberchaud.
- When the party asks for help, Gartokkar offers badges so their own companions can work with them: "Do they know how to use a sword?"
- Gartokkar tasks the party with retrieving the egg, a small statuette of Demogorgon, and any incriminating paperwork. Combined with the inscriptions on the egg, this would give the Deepking cause to "wage unholy war" on the Gray Ghosts. He considers the flumph's mind-reading enough to earn the party more help, but not enough to sway the Deepking.
- He offers 2,000 gold pieces, but Gyrgum's eager acceptance as a full advance offends him. He sets the terms at 30% up front and 70% on delivery.
- The party raises a confidentiality rider to keep the egg secret from Themberchaud. Gartokkar has a moment of "depraved mad insanity" and seems about to attack, then sits down and agrees.
- Thorin demands double for the discretion, Daz hints at other bidders, and Thorin adds an "exclusivity rider." Thorin's Persuasion rolls come in at 15 and 14 (Charisma 8) against the GM's 7.
- Gartokkar is "shitting bricks." A leak about the egg's survival and corruption could destroy his leverage over Themberchaud. He refuses 4,000 but offers an extra 1,000.
- The party pushes for 50% of the total up front (1,500). Thorin's Intimidation rolls a 13 and the advantage reroll doesn't help. Terms settle at 30% of 2,000 plus 50% of 1,000, for 1,100 gold up front.
- Gartokkar hands over the bag of gold and the badges for the party's companions. The party splits the gold at 275 each.
- Gartokkar delivers a fierce threat, warning of the consequences should they fail and enumerating every geological formation in the Underdark where they might try to hide.
