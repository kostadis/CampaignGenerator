# Chapter 002

### Forced Labor in Velkynvelve 002.01

The drow overseers of Velkynvelve wasted no time putting their prisoners to work, dragging them from the slave pen and dividing them into labor groups before the day had barely begun. As they passed the guard post, the prisoners spotted three drow who sneered at them, an immediate reminder that attacking their captors openly would be suicidal. Zalthir was sent to clean and operate the outpost's lift—a creaking contraption of zurkhwood and spider silk suspended high above the cavern floor—while two quaggoth guards amused themselves by dropping the basket suddenly and yanking it back up to startle him. Thorin, Ront, and Prince Derendil were marched to the waterfall to haul small barrels of water. Gyrgum was put to work in the drow dining hall, tasked with preparing meals for the sneering warriors who lounged at circular zurkhwood tables. Daz, along with Stool the Myconid and one other drow prisoner, was assigned the least glamorous duty of all—cleaning chamber pots—though the work gave him an opportunity to observe the lower levels of the outpost and the underground river flowing from the pool below.

#### The party is divided into work groups by their drow captors and assigned various menial tasks around the outpost.

- The overseers drag the prisoners out of their cells and send them to work details suited—at least in the drow's estimation—to their physical abilities.
- Thorin, Ront, and Prince Derendil are assigned to haul water barrels from the waterfall. Thorin, with his considerable strength, assures the others, “Any feats of strength you need, I'm your guy.”
- Zalthir is sent to operate and clean the lift mechanism, a winch and basket device suspended by spider silk.
- Gyrgum is assigned to the food preparation area and jokes, “I'll make sure to plate it nicely.”
- Daz, Stool, and one other drow prisoner are tasked with emptying and cleaning chamber pots. Stool's fungal nature makes him unconcerned by the filth.
- While being marched through the outpost, the prisoners see the guard post and three drow who sneer at them as they pass.
- The party takes in the confusing vertical layout of Velkynvelve: hanging chambers, webbing, rope bridges, the lower pool, the river tunnel, and ominous creatures below.
- The temptation to attack the guards is quickly checked by the realization that doing so would produce a total-party kill rather than an escape.

### Labor in the Lift and Kitchen 002.02

Each prisoner used the assignment to quietly gather intelligence. Zalthir saw that the lift consisted of a swinging arm, a hand-cranked horizontal spool, several pulleys, and a heavy woven basket supported by spider-silk cord. It appeared to be the only visible way to descend safely from the barracks level without cutting a rope or risking the tremendous drop. While removing the rubbish left by passengers, Zalthir discovered a mysterious list of people belonging to a nearby settlement. Jimjar enlivened the work by proposing wagers on everything from how many passengers would use the lift to who would step out of the basket first. Despite having no money in captivity, he was perfectly willing to extend credit, assuring the prisoners, “As long as you're willing to trust me as the bookkeeper, I'm willing to go on credit.” Eldeth periodically accepted his bets, and Jimjar displayed an almost freakish ability to remember precisely how much had been wagered, won, and lost.

More importantly, Zalthir studied the lift as a potential target for sabotage. The spider-silk cord was extremely hard to cut, and the hand-cranked zurkhwood winch could not be broken without tools, though its operation could be impeded or slowed. Daz suggested freezing part of the mechanism with Shape Water, using something as simple as frozen spit to damage or jam it. Zalthir then realized that his fire breath changed the picture: the webbing burned, and the rope would give way to flame much more readily than to a blade. The same fire could potentially damage the wooden mechanism and the extensive webs surrounding the outpost.

#### The party is split into work groups, with Zalthir assigned to the lift and Gyrgum sent to the food preparation area.

- The lift is attached to the edge of the barracks platform. A large swinging arm carries a thin spider-silk cord, which runs through several pulleys from a hand-cranked horizontal spool to a heavy woven basket.
- Two quaggoths supervise the lift. They force Zalthir to clean the basket and amuse themselves by dropping it a short distance before hauling it back up.
- Zalthir spends the day removing rubbish and debris from the basket as people and goods travel between levels.
- The lift appears to be the only visible safe way down from the barracks. Other possibilities would require cutting a rope or surviving the substantial fall to the cavern floor.
- Jimjar proposes wagers on the number of people going up or down, how often the basket will stop, and who will emerge first. Eldeth occasionally accepts.
- Jimjar has an uncanny memory for every wager, including the exact amount owed, and is willing to let the penniless prisoners gamble on credit until they escape.
- When Zalthir remarks that the betting “Seems to presume we'll get out of here alive,” someone presses Jimjar on who that “we” includes: all of them, or just “you and me”?
- Among the lift's accumulated detritus, Zalthir finds a list of people associated with a nearby settlement.
- Eldeth finds a comb, potentially valuable in the filthy conditions of the slave pen.
- The drow dining hall contains four circular zurkhwood tables, each surrounded by five chairs. Its stores include fresh and dried fungi, dried fruit, cheese, preserved meat, and jars of spices.
- A heavy iron brazier supplies cooking heat and dim light, though much of the food is served cold. Four drow are eating and entertaining themselves with dice or cards when Gyrgum arrives.
- Topsy and Turvy immediately begin looking for an opportunity to steal food.
- Gyrgum attempts to talk to the drow but cannot understand their Undercommon. Shuushar announces that he will omit many of the “colorful adjectives” for the sake of his own spiritual and mental balance—then enumerates the insults anyway.
- Shuushar explains that the drow question Gyrgum's ancestry, viability, and species, and suggest that, instead of talking to them, he make them a tasty meal.
- Gyrgum uses performance to manipulate, combine, and dramatically sizzle the ingredients. The meal's presentation and preparation impress the drow, who offer rare praise.
- Looking around the kitchen, Gyrgum notices accessible knives and realizes that only slaves perform the cooking, meaning a discreet theft might go unnoticed.
- Topsy advises that stealing should be done “carefully, quietly, and not make a big production of it,” pointedly looking at Gyrgum's massive body.
- Gyrgum asks the twins to create a distraction, but his attempt is so conspicuous that a drow catches him holding the knife up.
- Challenged by the guard, Gyrgum says, “I'm going to sharpen this knife.” His performance and the goodwill from his meal persuade the drow not to punish him.
- The drow nevertheless warn that another attempt could cost Gyrgum “various body parts.”
- On the following day, six or seven drow are present and paying close attention to Gyrgum's celebrated cooking. Rather than risk another personal attempt, he asks Topsy and Turvy to steal the knives.
- The twins successfully bring two knives back to the prisoners. The group treats them as ordinary daggers, and Gyrgum sharpens them.

### Labor and Observation in Velkynvelve 002.03

Thorin's work detail gathered small barrels from the head of the waterfall, where water vented through a crack in the cavern ceiling, poured between the stalactites, and fell into a twenty-foot-deep pool before joining an underground river. Thorin noticed that someone could slide down the waterfall into the pool below without being injured, making it a promising escape route.

Thorin's water-hauling duties took a tense turn when he and Prince Derendil were sent to Mistress Ilvara, the cruel high priestess who commanded Velkynvelve. They crossed a steep rope bridge into the upper level of the largest hanging tower and passed through a shrine to Lolth. Dark silken mats covered the circular chamber, patterned with pale web strands woven in silvery thread. At its center stood a broad zurkhwood pedestal supporting a lifelike ten-foot-tall sculpted spider. Its eight eyes were polished jet—four small and four large—and the altar was flanked by heavy silver candlesticks holding thick black candles. Pillows and cushions were heaped behind the altar.

A rope ladder led down from the shrine into Ilvara's private quarters, where she sat in smug comfort alongside her favored lieutenant, Shoor Vandree. She ordered the prisoners to bring the water to her bathtub. When Prince Derendil declared that he should be treated with dignity, Ilvara drew a knife and made it clear that “if he wants to be dealt with, he can be dealt with,” prompting the usual amount of backing down. Thorin sensed that something about the drow at Velkynvelve seemed “crazily off,” almost as though they were hovering near insanity. He also noticed a treasure chest in Ilvara's chamber and another descending passage that led toward Shoor's Quarters, though the work detail was not permitted to explore it.

Back in the kitchen, Gyrgum turned his forced labor into an unexpected triumph. The dining hall held four circular zurkhwood tables with five chairs apiece, along with stores of fresh and dried fungi, dried fruit, cheese, preserved meat, and clay jars of spices. A heavy iron brazier provided heat and dim light, though much of the food was served cold. Four drow were eating, gambling, and amusing themselves when Gyrgum arrived. When he attempted to speak to them, they answered in Undercommon, which he did not understand. Shuushar explained that, for the sake of his spiritual and mental balance, “I have chosen to remove many of the colorful adjectives they used”—and then proceeded to enumerate a whole slew of insults about Gyrgum's ancestry, viability, and obvious inferiority as a species.

Gyrgum used his performance skills to turn the preparation itself into a spectacle, manipulating and sizzling the ingredients into a meal whose presentation impressed the drow. Their translated praise amounted to the judgment that perhaps he was not “a worthless excrement.” Topsy and Turvy could not stop talking about the meal afterward, and even Shuushar declared that the cooking had penetrated his calm demeanor and might lead his own people, the kuo-toa, to imagine a god who looked like Gyrgum. Gyrgum answered, “Godlike affectation is what I aspire to, sir.”

#### The prisoners are divided into work details, allowing them to scout the layout of the drow outpost and identify potential escape routes.

- Thorin, Prince Derendil, and Ront are tasked with hauling small water barrels from the head of the waterfall. Thorin sings a folk giant song while they work.
- Water enters through a crack near the eastern wall, runs between stalactites, and plunges to the cavern floor, where it forms a natural pool.
- Thorin realizes that the waterfall would allow someone to slide safely into the pool below without suffering damage.
- The pool is twenty feet deep and drains into an underground river that travels for miles before spilling into unknown reaches of the Underdark.
- Thorin's group is ordered to carry water into the largest hanging tower. A steep rope bridge leads from the walkway to its uppermost chamber.
- The circular shrine is covered in dark silken mats woven with pale web patterns in silvery thread.
- A broad zurkhwood pedestal supports a lifelike ten-foot-tall spider idol, which Thorin recognizes as a symbol of Lolth.
- The idol's eyes are eight pieces of polished jet—four small and four large—and a pair of valuable heavy silver candlesticks flanks the altar.
- The rear of the shrine contains a semicircle of pillows and cushions.
- A rope ladder descends from the shrine into Ilvara's private quarters. Ilvara is present with Shoor Vandree and orders the prisoners to pour the water into her bathtub.
- Prince Derendil insists that he should be treated with dignity. Ilvara draws a knife and says that “if he wants to be dealt with, he can be dealt with,” compelling him to retreat from the confrontation.
- Thorin notices a treasure chest and a descending passage toward Shoor's Quarters, although the work detail does not enter it.
- Thorin also senses that the drow seem “a little borderline insane,” though he cannot determine exactly what is affecting them.
- Daz cleans chamber pots around the lower pool and underground river. Although he finds nothing specific during his search, he gains a clearer understanding of the lower cavern's layout.
- Gyrgum's cooking becomes the talk of the slave pen. Topsy and Turvy praise it endlessly, while Shuushar claims it could disturb the mental processes of his own people, the kuo-toa, enough to inspire worship of a god resembling Gyrgum.
- On the second kitchen shift, Topsy and Turvy exploit the distraction created by Gyrgum's popular cooking to steal two knives for the party.

### The Divine Plan 002.04

His first attempt to pocket a knife went badly. Topsy and Turvy tried to help with a distraction, but Gyrgum effectively held the blade up where a drow could see it. When challenged with “what are you doing there?” he claimed, “I'm going to sharpen this knife.” His performance, together with the goodwill created by his meal, persuaded the drow to overlook the attempt, though Shuushar translated their warning: “don't do that. Or next time, you may find yourself missing various body parts.” The next day, with six or seven drow watching Gyrgum cook, he sensibly asked Topsy and Turvy to handle the theft themselves. The twins successfully stole two knives and brought them back for the prisoners to use as makeshift daggers. Gyrgum sharpened them, while the group joked that they were “magical plus zero” weapons—the equivalent of a zero-ohm resistor.

When the day's labor ended, the prisoners returned to the Slave Pen and pooled their observations. Sarith rambled through insane tangents, Stool was delighted by everyone’s cleaning activities, and Jimjar continued his obsessive betting. Buppido repeatedly invoked “the divine plan” without identifying the divinity behind it. Thorin and Gyrgum gradually concluded that Buppido might not merely worship a god—he might believe that he was one.

Asked to explain his plan, Buppido announced, “It's quite simple. All of you will distract the enemy while I escape through the waterfall.” When challenged about how everyone else would escape, he admitted that he needed to consider the divine plan more carefully. Thorin pointed out that Buppido would be entering the Underdark without protection, and flattered him with the idea that the divine plan must have arranged for everyone to escape so they could protect him. Buppido eagerly accepted the logic. Looking at the prisoners’ absent swords and unavailable spells, he declared, “since you have pledged your swords … your spells … your lives, at least, to protecting the divine plan, I will grant you insight into its mysteries later this evening.”

#### Back in the slave pen, the prisoners regroup to share information and listen to Buppido's delusional escape strategy.

- The prisoners reunite in the Slave Pen and exchange what they learned about the waterfall, lower pool, lift, guard towers, shrine, Ilvara's quarters, and possible locations of useful equipment.
- Sarith continues going on insane tangents, while Stool is delighted by the day's cleaning and considers the activities “awesome sauce.”
- Buppido repeatedly announces that the “divine plan” requires everyone to escape but does not identify which god created it.
- Thorin and Gyrgum recognize that Buppido's repeated use of “divine” may refer to himself. They remain uncertain whether he actually is a god or merely believes himself to be one.
- When Gyrgum notes that most gods would not be trapped in a drow prison, Buppido's circular answer is that he is not trapped: his captivity is part of the divine plan.
- Buppido's first concrete proposal is, “It's quite simple. All of you will distract the enemy while I escape through the waterfall.”
- Asked how the others will escape, he pauses and says, “I need to consider the divine plan more carefully.”
- Thorin points out that Buppido will need guards in the Underdark and flatters him with the idea that the divine plan deliberately assembled the prisoners so they could all escape and protect him. Buppido concedes that fleeing alone would endanger the divine plan and his followers.
- The flattery works because Buppido wants to believe it. He accepts the prisoners' absent swords, inaccessible spells, and lives as pledges to his cause.
- Zalthir studies the lift mechanism. The spider-silk cord is extremely difficult to cut, and the hand-cranked spool cannot readily be broken without tools, but the mechanism could be jammed or slowed.
- Daz proposes using Shape Water to freeze moisture inside part of the mechanism, potentially damaging or obstructing it.
- Zalthir also considers burning the zurkhwood structure and the spider-silk rope with his fire breath. The group realizes that fire offers much broader sabotage options than blades.

### An Unexpected Offer of Escape 002.05

On the second day of labor, an unexpected visitor arrived during the guard shift. Jorlan Duskryn had once been Ilvara's favored lieutenant and lover, but a black pudding attack on a routine raid had melted and scarred his formerly handsome face, twisted his sword hand, and cost him two fingers. Ilvara replaced him with Shoor Vandree, leaving Jorlan bitter and eager for revenge. Bringing food during his guard duty, he quietly asked the nearest prisoner, “if I gave you a means to escape, would you take it?”

The prisoners suspected a trap and asked why a drow would help them. Jorlan answered bluntly, “hate Ilvara, and I hate Shoor. And your escape, even your attempted escape, would ruin them.” Losing valuable prisoners would shame his rivals and cause them to lose face; he did not need the prisoners to survive, only to embarrass his former lover and her new favorite.

Jorlan proposed leaving the slave-pen gate unlocked and creating a distraction during the changing of the guard. He revealed that the armory was in the chamber above the guard post, inside the hanging guard tower directly in front of the slave pen. From there, the prisoners could jump onto the webs below and then leap into the pool. When asked what they should do after reaching the water, Jorlan replied, “that is not my problem.”

The group debated its options with growing urgency. Shuushar knew how to travel toward Darklake to the west. Menzoberranzan lay to the north and was emphatically ruled out, while Gracklstugh, a duergar city where Buppido's derro kin also live, offered a possible route to the south. None of the destinations could be called friendly; as the GM put it, “In the Underdark, friendly is a privilege of the dead.” Stool seemed happy to keep the others nearby, though they could not be sure he did not merely see them as future fertilizer. Buppido, at least, believed he needed disciples and protectors.

The party also had to decide whether to flee immediately or risk retrieving weapons first. The armory might provide usable equipment, but it was not clear where their own possessions had been stored. Drawing on drow cultural habits, Gyrgum and Daz reasoned that the highest-ranking drow would traditionally claim captured treasure. Because none of the guards appeared to be carrying the prisoners' belongings, the party concluded that their gear was probably in the chest Thorin had seen in Ilvara's private chambers.

### Planning the Sabotage 002.06

Finally, Zalthir raised the threat posed by the drow's large spools of spider silk, which allowed their warriors to descend rapidly between levels. The party could not eliminate every spool, but careful scouting and a clever use of fire might neutralize those nearest the escape route. Gyrgum summarized the idea as, “light them on fire, and fling them,” and the GM summed up the goal: “Disable. Deny the enemy the weapon.” Their exact route, whether they would raid the armory or Ilvara's quarters, and how much of Velkynvelve they would burn remained unresolved as they prepared for the desperate flight ahead.

#### The party discusses tactical options for disabling the drow's mobility assets before their escape attempt.

- The prisoners identify the drow's spider-silk spools as a primary tactical threat. Guards can use them to rappel rapidly from the hanging towers and pursue escapees.
- The exact locations of the spools remain uncertain, and the group intends to use stealth and closer observation to identify those nearest the planned route.
- The prisoners accept that they cannot eliminate every spool in Velkynvelve. Their realistic goal is to disable enough nearby equipment to delay pursuit.
- Zalthir's fire breath can burn the webbing, the lift rope, and potentially the zurkhwood lift structure itself.
- Daz's Shape Water offers a second means of interfering with the lift by freezing moisture inside its mechanism.
- Gyrgum suggests, “light them on fire, and fling them,” using the burning spools both to deny the equipment to the drow and to spread chaos.
- The GM sums up the tactical purpose of the plan: “Disable. Deny the enemy the weapon.”
- Several major choices remain unresolved: whether to raid the armory, whether to risk entering Ilvara's quarters for their own gear, which route to take after reaching the pool, and how much of the outpost to burn during the breakout.
