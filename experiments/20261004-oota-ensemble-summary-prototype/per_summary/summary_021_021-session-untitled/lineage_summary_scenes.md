# Chapter 021

### Aftermath in the Corrupted Chamber 021.01

The dust of battle had barely settled when the Ember Vanguard, though they did not yet know that name, turned their attention to the corrupted chamber around them. As the DM put it, "our heroes have once again saved the day, or at the very least, created new problems for themselves." Every surface told a story of treachery. The party moved methodically through the space, gathering notes and documents that laid bare the Council of Savants' foul dealings and demonic entanglements.

The ghost Pelek, who had seized the derro spy's body at the end of the last fight, lingered in that borrowed body while the party kept careful hold of his bones, a strange arrangement that had become oddly routine. Most precious of all was the dragon's egg. It was tough enough, and could be hidden in a bag or carried unnoticed, and its demonic corruption was a secret known only to them.

The weight of what they had uncovered pressed heavily on the group as they debated what to do next. The DM laid out the factions one by one.

- Opening situation, carried over from chapter 20: the party holds the dragon's egg; Daz, who carried it through the fight unnoticed, is still at full hit points; and the ghost Pelek now inhabits the derro spy's body while the party keeps his bones.
- The adventurers discover numerous notes linking the Council of Savants to nefarious activities and corruption.

  - The derro group running the place is "clearly … up to nefariously no good deeds."
- The party deliberates on which faction to deliver their gathered intelligence to, considering the interests of the red dragon Themberchaud, the Keepers of the Flame, and the Stone Guard.
- Gyrgum asks whether there is someone paying them to find all this out. The DM answers: "everybody's paying you to find it out. The question is, who are you going to deliver the information to?"
- The egg can be hidden in a bag or carried so that nobody notices, and dragon eggs are fairly tough.

### The Weight of Information 021.02

- **Themberchaud.** The red dragon hungered for information and would likely burn the city to cinders if he learned of the egg. Gyrgum was "a big fan of not pissing off red dragons."
- **Errde Blackskull.** The Stone Guard's leader wanted proof that the Council of Savants was behind the city's corruption. She would almost certainly march on the king himself if she learned the full truth: the Assassin's Guild "that doesn't exist" was actually being supported by the king. If she were told only about the derro and their demonic dealings, she would try to kill all the derro.
- **The Keepers of the Flame.** Led by Gartokkar and close to the king, they simply wanted their egg returned. They were also looking for an excuse to go genocidal on the derro. They had made it abundantly clear they would pursue the party "to the end of time" if it was not returned.
- **The assassin gangster.** The party had already dealt with the man who wanted to be scrubbed from the assassins' list.

The DM noted that the party had enough information to turn the whole city into a bloodbath. Gyrgum wanted "an option where nobody goes nuts and tries to kill everybody."

The DM explained what the party stood to gain. People had made offers: if something happened to the Deepking, new leadership would be far friendlier to the gnome kingdoms, whose trade the current king was blocking. Zalthir asked which faction was most pro-gnome. The answer was Clan Ironhead, a weaponsmithing clan that would happily deal with the gnomes if the Deepking were removed.

Gyrgum had only a −1 in Nature, so he rolled his dragon check with Religion instead, for an 18. After checking Appendix A, the DM ruled that the egg would hatch of its own accord in forty-four weeks. That left, in Gyrgum's words, "plenty of time for us to pop potato it to someone else." Lesser restoration would not lift the demonic corruption, and removing it would take research. As a follower of Bahamut, Gyrgum resolved to consult his superiors about demonic dragons. The DM warned they would give him "12 contradictory opinions."

Thorin wished aloud for a little baby dragon of their own. That landed badly with Zalthir the dragonborn standing right there. The DM needled that Zalthir was "feeling a little bit like, you know, dissed right now," and Thorin apologised, saying he hadn't realised it came out that way.

After much deliberation, the party chose quiet pragmatism over dramatic upheaval. They would return the egg to Gartokkar. Gartokkar would probably let them leave the city in exchange, and the egg might be parlayed into rights to buy the minerals the gnomes wanted. They would tell the Keepers they had found no evidence of who was behind the theft. Telling them the egg was demonic, and letting them decide, had been floated along the way.

Gyrgum admitted some disappointment ("in a story, everybody dying is kind of fun"). But with "too many real-world parallel tracks," he advocated simply giving the egg back. The incriminating list connecting the Deepking to the Assassin's Guild would stay hidden from Errde's volatile temper. Thorin summed up the approach: "meet our responsibilities, but don't go above and beyond and don't, you know, light shit on fire."

It was not a heroic choice, nor a villainous one. It was the choice of people who wanted to leave the city alive and perhaps return someday without being immediately stabbed. Clan Ironhead was the one faction Zalthir hoped to leave in a stronger position.

### Deliberation and Looting in the Council Lab 021.03

The chamber was more a laboratory than a lair: "not the kind of place you store your treasure." Among its sparse loot the party found 170 gold pieces and one deeply puzzling object: a baby rattle. There was no baby. The rattle was heavier than any child could comfortably hold and crafted from materials far too durable for an infant's toy. It carried no gems and no inlays, but it did bear the name of Clan Thrazgad, a Duergar steelsmithing clan. The realisation crept over them slowly: this rattle had been made for a dragon hatchling to gnaw on.

Gyrgum guessed it had been commissioned. The DM agreed that was a very odd thing to ask of a Duergar smithing clan, though by whom, and why it had ended up in this chamber, remained a mystery. As a weapon it would count as a rock, doing 1d4 damage. It might interest a baby dragon, sweeten a deal with Themberchaud, or serve as a distraction for some other creature. The party tucked it away, sensing it might prove useful as leverage or evidence, though they were not yet sure against whom.

### The Ember Vanguard's Debut 021.04

Their exit from the dungeon brought them face to face with four Derro guards stationed at a heavy double door, hands hovering near an alarm mechanism. Anyone the alarm would summon was already dead. The guards had not spotted the party. Rather than cut them down, the party saw an opportunity, and Thorin let a die roll decide between killing and intimidation. It came up intimidation.

Daz conjured a swirling display of floating lights that drifted and spun around the group like orbiting stars. Gyrgum used his connection to the earth to send tremors rippling through the stone beneath the guards' feet. The Derro, wide-eyed and trembling, demanded to know who these people were. Daz stepped forward into the shimmering light and told them, with absolute conviction, "You do not know who we are." Thorin added that they should make sure to tell everybody what had happened here.

Only then did the party realise it needed a name. It was hammered out in a hasty committee vote while the guards stood frozen in awe, and the party declared itself the Ember Vanguard. The name landed with the weight of legend. On the group intimidation check Thorin failed, but Daz rolled a 20. The Derro mistook the ember-like lights for a meteor swarm and became convinced they faced a supremely powerful drow archmage and his elite retinue. They would say a 20th-level drow mage had swept through the Council's stronghold with his companions, or even "a marauding army of drow, led by a drow Archmage," and left nothing but ruin behind. The Ember Vanguard let them go, satisfied that their reputation would now travel faster than they could. The DM warned that the drow might now count among the factions who hated them.

### Choosing the Path West 021.05

The party chose to skirt the assassins' territory entirely. They had been told not to return after "sneezing" on and burning the assassins' desk, and did not want anyone wondering whether there was more to that fire. So they headed west through the dark Underdark passages. Stool's pleas about his Myconid friend were noted but set aside for another time, even though Thorin, a loyal character modelled on Amos from *The Expanse*, was on Stool's side.

### The Trapper's Ambush 021.06

The tunnels were silent and oppressive, the kind of silence that hides things. No one's passive Perception beat 18. Without warning, something struck at the rear of the party. A massive, manta-ray-shaped predator known as a Trapper wrapped its great leathery wings around Gyrgum, smothering him in darkness and acid. The creature's grip was crushing. Gyrgum was driven toward zero hit points, but he held on at a single hit point through sheer orcish stubbornness.

The rest of the party moved fast. Daz hurled bolts of magical force into the creature's bulk while weaving enchantments to distract it and bolster Gyrgum. Thorin hacked at the beast with his longsword, landing heavy blows. But it was Zalthir who ended it. He seized the Trapper with his bare hands, locking it in a counter-grapple, and beat the enormous predator into the ground with a relentless series of strikes until it collapsed, dead. It released a battered and thoroughly acid-covered Gyrgum onto the cave floor. Gyrgum scraped the caustic residue off himself with visible disgust, which the rest of the party found quietly hilarious, and the Ember Vanguard pressed on into the dark.
