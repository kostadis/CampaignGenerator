# Chapter 013

### The Battle with the Two-Headed Giant 013.01

The streets of Gracklstugh erupted into chaos as the party found themselves alongside a squad of duergar militia, facing down a rampaging stone giant with two heads, a grotesque sign of the madness that had overtaken him. Two of the duergar guards swelled to enormous size, and two of them unleashed sprays of fire that scorched the giant's rocky hide for 23 points of damage. The party pressed the attack from every angle. Gyrgum called upon his divine power through his holy symbol. Thorin landed a critical hit with his short sword and then used Action Surge to strike again. Daz struck with unerring magical force. Through it all, the giant swung his great club in wild arcs, hitting one duergar for 19 damage and narrowly missing Zalthir.

#### The party and a group of duergar engage a crazed, two-headed stone giant in the streets of Gracklstugh.
- The duergar "act very official" and are on the party's side.
- Two duergar guards magically enlarge themselves to confront the stone giant, and two others do not.
- Two duergar unleash fire sprays at the giant. The damage rolls come to 13 and 10, for 23 points total.
- The stone giant, crazed and two-headed, swings his great club twice. One duergar takes 19 damage.
- The giant's second swing targets Zalthir, but with disadvantage it rolls a 14 against Zalthir's AC 15 and misses.
- Gyrgum uses his holy symbol to channel divine energy against the giant. The giant "once again burns at the end of the day."
- Thorin makes a nature/history/survival check (16) and recognizes the giant has likely gone mad. Stone giants are particularly unforgiving of those who kill their kind.
- The GM rules that dropping the giant to zero with a weapon knocks him out, while dropping him with a spell like Magic Missile kills him. The giant is at 34 hit points at this point.
- Daz strikes the giant with Magic Missile but rolls a one, dealing 6 damage ("every little bit counts").
- Zalthir declares non-lethal damage and attacks three times, spending a focus point on the third. He misses, hits for 5 damage, then misses.
- Thorin scores a critical hit with his short sword, then uses Action Surge and hits again for 6 more damage.

### The Fall of Rihuud 013.02

In the heat of battle, Thorin drew on his deep familiarity with giant-kind and made a successful check (a 16) that revealed the terrible truth: this creature had not chosen violence but had gone mad. Stone giants are particularly unforgiving toward those who kill their kind, and particularly forgiving of those who spare them. Thorin urged the party and the duergar militia alike to bring the giant down without killing him. The party switched to non-lethal weapon attacks and wore the giant down from 34 hit points to 8. Zalthir attacked with a focus point, Thorin followed with his crit and Action Surge, and Gyrgum charged in with his mace. Thorin's plea to the militia failed, however. The militia, bound by their rigid code and unable to tell friend from foe inside Zalthir's magical darkness that blanketed part of the street, delivered the killing blow. The giant, later identified as Rihuud, crumpled to the stone floor of the city. Thorin spoke funeral words over the body to save face in case the giants took offense, and the darkness lifted.

#### The party makes a desperate attempt to subdue the maddened stone giant without killing him, but the duergar militia delivers the killing blow.
- Thorin, drawing on his experience with giants, realizes the stone giant has gone mad. He urges the party and the duergar to use non-lethal force to avoid offending the stone giant clans.
- With the giant down to 8 hit points, Gyrgum is "more daring than Gyrgum normally would be." He moves out of the darkness into melee range to strike with his mace.
- Gyrgum's mace hits for 1 damage. He then uses two-weapon fighting with the flat of his hand axe and misses.
- The GM explains that hit points represent skill at avoiding damage. Gyrgum recalls Gygax's interpretation: "100 hit points on a rhinoceros is different from 100 hit points on a human fighter."
- Thorin attempts to intimidate or persuade the duergar to stay their hand and spare the giant's life, but rolls poorly and fails. He goes with intimidation because his modifier there is +1, versus -1 on the others.
- The duergar militia ignores the plea and delivers a lethal blow, killing the stone giant.
- Thorin speaks funeral words over the giant so that, if the giants grow angry, word may reach them that the party was trying to help.
- The GM lifts Zalthir's darkness.
- The duergar soldiers shrink back to their normal size with the "usual popping sound" as combat ends and the area is cleared.

### Aftermath and the Arrival of Dorhun 013.03

In the aftermath, the duergar soldiers shrank back to their normal size with an audible pop and declared the operation a complete success. They added that any future support for the local militia and police would be "greatly appreciated and rewarded." The party pressed them about the wave of strange behavior sweeping the city. Once the questions were phrased literally enough for the duergar to parse, the militia conferred and confirmed what the adventurers had begun to suspect: incidents of citizens acting in erratic, self-destructive, and inexplicable ways had been increasing sharply, and the clan militias were struggling with the extra work. They knew no cause and suggested the party ask a clan leader.

Before the conversation could go further, a new figure arrived: Dorhun, a stone giant and fellow apprentice to the revered Stonespeaker Hgraam. After the long silence typical of his kind, he identified the fallen Rihuud as a comrade who had gone mad and sprouted a second head while communing with the stone deep in Cairngorm Cavern, then stormed out into the city. The militia honestly confirmed that the party had tried to spare Rihuud's life. Dorhun's expression softened, and he invited them to visit Cairngorm Cavern before they left Gracklstugh. He ordered the Stone Guard to escort them, much to the guards' weary irritation.

#### Following the giant's death, the party speaks with the duergar militia and then meets Dorhun, a stone giant apprentice who comes to claim his fallen comrade.
- The duergar militia thanks the party for their assistance in maintaining order and declares the operation a success.
- The militia adds that "any support you have for the local duergar militia and police will be greatly appreciated and rewarded."
- Gyrgum hangs back because he "doesn't really want to be in front."
- Thorin suggests questioning the militia but admits his negative charisma, so Gyrgum does the asking.
- The literal-minded duergar struggle with "crazy making" until Gyrgum rephrases it as attacks with "no clear motive" and "anti-social, uncivil behavior."
- The militia confirms a significant increase in chaotic and antisocial behavior across the city, straining the clans' militias. They know of no cause and suggest speaking to a clan leader.
- The militia clears the area, calling out to citizens that they can come out and clean up: "Nothing to see, nothing to see."
- A second stone giant walks down the street, surrounded by more duergar. The party notices the "Stone Guard" duergar belong to a different clan from the first group.
- The giant is Dorhun, apprentice to Stonespeaker Hgraam. As is usual for stone giants, he says nothing for an exhausting period of time. Thorin knows stone giants do not start conversations.
- Thorin opens the conversation by trying to establish a connection. Dorhun replies, reverberating: "No, we do not have any connection."
- Dorhun identifies the fallen giant as Rihuud, another of the Stonespeaker's apprentices.
- Dorhun explains that Rihuud went mad and sprouted a second head while communing with the stone in Cairngorm Cavern, then stormed out.
- Thorin explains that the party tried to subdue Rihuud and that "it was the town watch that dealt the final blow."
- The duergar militia honestly confirms the party tried to save Rihuud. They say they heard only "voices from inside of a pitch of darkness that we did not recognize" and that they acted "well within our rights under the current penal code."
- Dorhun expresses appreciation for the attempt to spare Rihuud's life and invites the party to visit Cairngorm Cavern before they leave the city.
- Dorhun instructs the Stone Guard to escort the party to Cairngorm Cavern. The guards shrug with the look of "another one of these self-important VIP types telling us to do something we don't want to do" and reluctantly agree. Dorhun then leaves with them.

### An Invitation from the Keepers of the Flame 013.04

Barely had Dorhun departed when a duergar materialized out of thin air nearby. The party was learning that this was a disconcerting habit of the city's inhabitants. He introduced himself as Gartokkar Xundorn of the Keepers of the Flame and flashed a badge that sent curious onlookers scattering. He explained that his order's sacred duty was to tend to the red dragon Themberchaud, who kept the city's forges burning in the lightless depths of the Underdark, where wood was nearly impossible to find. He also said the Deepking treated the Keepers as his most prized advisors. Gyrgum drily offered that they had some kindling available if the order was running short on dragons. Gartokkar laughed, then dissected the joke with such analytical precision that every trace of humor was methodically extinguished. He invited the party to his quarters by the following day. In exchange for their help with a problem he was not yet ready to name, he promised city passes and what Thorin summarized as "a limited get-out of jail-free card." Between scenes, the GM reminded Zalthir of his "I know a guy" list: one favor per city, usable to learn about the factions or obtain something useful.

#### A high-ranking duergar cleric materializes from invisibility and approaches the party with a mysterious job offer.
- Gartokkar Xundorn reveals himself to the party, having been invisible nearby.
- Gartokkar introduces himself as a representative of the Keepers of the Flame. Thorin asks whether that means "Keeper of the Old Flame."
- The GM notes the party knows only that Gracklstugh has clans and, in a society of spies and invisibility, many secret societies.
- Gartokkar flashes a badge to disperse curious duergar onlookers. The party gets the impression he is a legal authority, one who "has the ability to make the other authorities disappear."
- Gartokkar explains that his order tends the red dragon Themberchaud. In the Underdark, finding trees is hard and starting flames is harder, so a dragon that can restart the forges is convenient.
- He says the Keepers keep the dragon's needs "and the needs of the city are well aligned." He calls theirs "a position of power and prestige" and claims the Deepking treats them as his most prized advisors.
- Gyrgum asks whether this is another dragon cult and concludes it isn't, because dragon cults always boast about being dragon cults. Daz adds that they usually have purple robes.
- Gyrgum jokes about having kindling available if they are short on dragons. Gartokkar finds it amusing and then dissects it analytically, draining the humor from it.
- Gartokkar says the party's skill set would do "a great service to the citizens of Gracklstugh."
- He offers passes to other parts of the city. He also offers help if they run afoul of other factions or police while acting on his behalf, with "a standard set of disclaimers." Thorin calls this "a limited get-out of jail-free card."
- Gartokkar invites the party to his quarters by the following day to discuss the specific task. The only commitment is attending the meeting.
- The party worries aloud about being double-crossed if they say no in that meeting.
- The GM reminds Zalthir of his "I know a guy" list: a contact who can supply background or help, usable once per city per party. The GM also reminds the party they have already sent someone to fetch them a magical weapon.
- The party lists possible sources of information: the stone giant, the dragon himself, or asking around the Blade Bazaar. They settle on getting a drink.

### Whispers at the Darklake Brewery 013.05

Seeking to understand the tangled web of Gracklstugh's politics before committing to anything, the party made their way to the Darklake Brewery. More precisely, they went to the kiosk outside it, since the brewery itself was strictly duergar only. Gyrgum tried to peek inside and rolled a 3 on stealth. He still glimpsed quiet duergar drinking cheaper beer over very serious conversations. Among the non-duergar outside were a halfling, a human, and a drow. Gyrgum bought a round for the talkative halfling, who was grateful and apparently well-informed. The halfling revealed that Deepking Horgar Steelshadow himself had reportedly descended into madness. This was not rampaging violence but paranoia and increasingly arbitrary laws. He said the Keepers of the Flame and Themberchaud were "in a bit of a pickle with the Gray Ghosts and the crown," and that all the factions were at each other's throats. As a non-duergar who stayed uninvolved, he was a "civilian" free to talk. He warned that attending an official meeting with a faction leader would turn the party from civilians into soldiers in the eyes of rival groups. He confirmed that the pending invitation came from the Keepers of the Flame. He also explained that the Keepers periodically replace their red dragon with a new one raised from a purchased egg, which Gyrgum noted could also be stolen. A drow patron seated nearby chimed in with contemptuous amusement. He lamented that duergar conspiracies lacked the drama and artistic cruelty of Menzoberranzan's political intrigues and amounted to little more than blunt, graceless violence.

#### The party gathers outside the Darklake Brewery to collect intelligence on the political climate of Gracklstugh from a talkative halfling and a cynical drow.
- The brewery bears a duergar-only sign, and non-duergar buy beer from a stand outside. Gyrgum: "There's nothing that's going to make me more interested than telling me I can't go in."
- Gyrgum tries to peer in and rolls a 3 on stealth. He sees quiet, humorless duergar having very serious conversations, and notices the beer is cheaper inside.
- A halfling, a human, and a drow are drinking outside.
- The halfling welcomes Gyrgum: "You're new to this town."
- Gyrgum buys the halfling a refill, and later a second mug "out of gratitude."
- The halfling points out the nearby prison. Gyrgum: "like so many towns, they put the prison close to the tavern."
- The halfling has heard of a crazed giant brought down with the help of a spellcasting dragonborn.
- The halfling warns that anyone "crazy" enough to work for a faction should know "they're all trying to kill each other."
- The halfling reveals that Deepking Horgar Steelshadow has reportedly gone mad, passing arbitrary and paranoid laws: "the kind of madness that you see when a person in power gets disconnected from society."
- The halfling notes the merchants' strange behavior ("talking to brothers that don't exist") is not how duergar merchants act: "They are the most boring, unenlightened, ignorant traders on the planet."
- The halfling explains the precarious political situation involving the Keepers of the Flame, the Gray Ghosts, and the Crown, noting that all the factions are at each other's throats.
- The halfling can share all this because, as an uninvolved non-duergar, he counts as a civilian. Non-duergar are considered "not relevant to the city."
- Gyrgum asks the halfling to come to the meeting. He refuses: attending "would be immediately interpreted as me joining one of the factions. And as a non-duergar, my life would be extremely short."
- The halfling identifies the meeting as the Keepers of the Flame's.
- Themberchaud is seen flying over the city. The halfling explains he is pampered and given a hoard large enough "to keep him pleasant," but that red dragons eventually think they're in control.
- The party learns that the Keepers of the Flame periodically replace their red dragon by raising a new one from an egg when the old one becomes too difficult to control. Eggs "can be purchased or stolen," and the Keepers must raise the new dragon, deal with the old one, and keep both happy in between.
- The halfling warns the party that attending official meetings with faction leaders will mark them as 'soldiers' rather than 'civilians' in the eyes of rival groups: "If you go with the mob parlance, you go from being a civilian to a soldier."
- A drow patron who has been in Menzoberranzan (to the other patrons' eye-rolling) mocks the lack of 'finesse' in duergar conspiracies. He describes their politics as blunt violence compared to the dramatic artistry of Menzoberranzan, where "we'll torture people, we'll burn their houses down, we'll make speeches."
- The GM tells the party the only two factions they can currently attract are the Stone Guard and the Keepers of the Flame. They know nothing about the Gray Ghosts beyond their existence.
- Daz argues for talking to everyone to get a better picture. Gyrgum wonders about "a job fair kind of area."

### A Proposition from the Stone Guard 013.06

The party had barely finished processing this flood of information when Captain Errde Blackskull of the Stone Guard arrived from the direction of the prison with a small escort. She made it politely but unmistakably clear that she expected them to accompany her. When the party bristled, she reminded them that if she wanted them arrested she would have brought a whole platoon. To ease the tension, she loudly announced to the surrounding air, and to any invisible listeners, that this was merely a friendly discussion and not "that kind of meeting."

In her front office, over pastries, she commended the party's initiative, clever tactics, and "brutal efficiency," and singled out Thorin's attempt to spare the stone giant's life. She then laid out her request. A derro named Droki had been evading Stone Guard patrols with suspicious ease, as though he always knew in advance where they would be. He lived in the volatile West Cleft District, where the guards entered only in force. She asked the party to follow him and report back, or better, to seize him for questioning, or failing that to kill him and bring evidence of his activities.

Errde spoke of a deeper conspiracy she believed was festering beneath the surface of Gracklstugh, involving corruption within the Council of Savants and among the clan lairds. She promised that exposing it would earn each of them 350 gold pieces' worth of equipment from her armory, plus safe passage out of the city when their work was done. Thorin pressed her twice on whether this bound them to the Stone Guard. She insisted it would "of course, be for the city." Daz, whose passive perception told him the bazaar's strange merchants broke every rule of this authoritarian society, agreed the market was "definitely full of corruption." He then rolled an 18 on persuasion, and Errde advanced 175 gold pieces' worth of equipment to each of them, with the remaining 175 to follow on completion. Almost as an afterthought, she revealed that she was already aware drow were hunting the party, and offered her protection against them as an additional incentive. Nothing she said forbade them from also hearing out the Keepers of the Flame, though the party came away suspecting she might be a little mad herself.

Thorin priced splint (too expensive at 200 gp) and plate (1,500 gp), then settled on chain mail and twenty darts. The others deferred their scrolls and potions to next time. The session ended just before the GM was about to run an encounter. The party now faced two faction leaders vying for their services, the stone giants of Cairngorm Cavern expecting a visit, and a city slowly unraveling at the seams.

#### Captain Errde Blackskull intercepts the party and brings them to her office, where she commends their actions and offers them a dangerous assignment.
- Captain Errde Blackskull and her Stone Guard approach from the direction of the prison and insist on a meeting to discuss the recent battle with the mad stone giant.
- Her invitation looks "a lot less inviting" than Gartokkar's: "an offer we can't refuse."
- Errde: "There's no threat ... I am simply enforcing the will of the city. I am the law."
- After some initial tension, Errde publicly announces the meeting is merely a discussion so it won't be read as the party joining the Stone Guard: "they are just meeting with the stone guards because I asked them to they are not coming to a meeting." The party echoes the announcement aloud.
- The party agrees to go to gather more information, reasoning that she isn't trying to bind them before a conversation.
- The party is taken to the Stone Guard front office rather than a back room. Errde offers them pastries.
- She commends their initiative, "clever tactics," and "brutal efficiency," noting that they arrived before her guards and caused minimal collateral damage.
- Errde expresses particular appreciation for Thorin's attempt to spare the stone giant's life. She contrasts it with the Keepers of the Flame, "who have no respect for our stone giant brethren."
- Errde tasks the party with tracking down an elusive derro named Droki. He evades patrols "as if he somehow knows in advance where the duergar patrols will be invisible or otherwise" and lives in the West Cleft District, "a place where we enter only in force and where our presence would likely provoke a chaotic uprising."
- The options are to follow and report on him, seize him for questioning, "or kill him and bring evidence of his evil activities."
- Daz raises the question of payment for the risk. Gyrgum frames it as quid pro quo ("pro quo").
- Thorin repeatedly asks whether the job is for the city or for the Stone Guard. Errde finally answers: "this would, of course, be for the city."
- Errde's tone turns dark as she reveals her suspicions of a deep conspiracy involving the Council of Savants and corrupt elements among the clan lairds. Exposing it would earn her great favor with the Deepking.
- Errde promises safe passage out of Gracklstugh and 350 gold pieces' worth of weapons, armor, and common potions and scrolls.
- Errde asks whether the party has seen corruption in the stores. Daz's passive perception of 14 tells him the merchants' behavior fits no one's rules in this authoritarian city, and he declares "the market's definitely full of corruption."
- Errde describes the progression: "First, it starts with corrupt people not behaving according to the rules. Then it proceeds to grift. Then it proceeds to chaos. Then it proceeds to destruction."
- Daz makes a persuasive case, rolling an 18. Errde offers each party member a down payment of 175 gold pieces worth of equipment from her armory, with the remaining 175 to be paid upon completion.
- The credit must be claimed from her warehouse, cannot be topped up with the party's own gold, and can be lent between party members. Gyrgum: "Oh, it's a gift card."
- Errde promises to shield the party from any drow forces hunting for them while they remain in Gracklstugh. She explains, "it's not uncommon for Drow to show up looking for missing prisoners." The party had never told her about the drow.
- The party notes that Errde never forbade them from working for the Keepers of the Flame, whom she does not treat as an enemy. The GM floats working for both. The party is "quite convinced that she might be a little bit mad."
- The party coordinates their reward choices. Thorin asks about a longsword +1 (the GM notes he "could know a guy") and considers splint (200 gp, over budget) and plate (1,500 gp). He settles on chain mail (75 gp) and twenty darts (5 cp each, about 1 gp). Gyrgum and the others will list scrolls and potions for next time.
- The session ends just before the GM was about to run an encounter.
