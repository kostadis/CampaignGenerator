# Chapter 007

### Escape from the Silken Paths – First Wave 007.01

The party's desperate flight through the Silken Paths began in a storm of silk and fangs. Giant spiders descended from every direction, skittering across the vast network of web strands that crisscrossed the dark chasm. Each spider had 26 hit points and an armor class of 14, and the level-two party quickly judged them "not pushovers, but they're not impossible either." Thorin drew his short sword and slashed through a critical strand for nine points of damage, severing the five-hit-point line and sending one of the predators plummeting into the chasm below. The fall didn't kill it, but it meant the spider had to spend its next turn saving itself rather than attacking. More spiders emerged from the shadows, spinning fresh lines to close the distance, and the party realized that standing their ground was not an option.

With the spiders closing in from multiple directions, the group fell into a desperate but coordinated retreat southward, the direction their goblin guides had pointed them. Gyrgum attempted to sever another strand to cut off a pursuing spider, but his blade glanced off the swaying silk. He narrowly avoided the creature's snapping jaws as he scrambled southward, using his ability to take the Dash as a bonus action. Daz moved into position and readied his action, waiting for Zalthir and the rescued halfling Fargas to clear a critical web intersection. Then he unleashed all three darts of a magic missile into it, tearing the strands apart and severing the spiders' path entirely. Zalthir quickly confirmed that nobody intended to stand and fight, and Thorin announced he would "run like hell." When the DM warned that the spiders could deal around twenty points of damage, Gyrgum chose to disengage and flee rather than trade blows. Daz fired one final burst of arcane energy at a strand to slow the pursuers before stepping off the webbed paths to safety, buying Gyrgum just enough time to escape. Their goblin guides, Yuk Yuk and Spiderbait, watched the whole frantic escape with barely concealed amusement, boasting about the far more stylish maneuvers they would have performed.

#### The party navigates a treacherous network of massive spider webs while defending themselves against a swarm of giant spiders.
- A giant spider lunges at Gyrgum with its webbing but misses its mark.
- Gyrgum points out that the strand Zalthir and Daz are on is the critical edge, and that fleeing south and cutting it would put the party in better shape.
- The DM confirms the giant spiders have 26 hit points and AC 14, and that the party is level two. The web strand has five hit points.
- Thorin severs a critical web strand with his short sword for nine damage, sending a giant spider plummeting into the darkness below. The fall doesn't kill it, but it must spend its next turn saving itself instead of attacking.
- More giant spiders emerge from the shadows. One misses Zalthir with its webbing, and others spin new lines and climb along them to close in on the party. The DM notes the party "did not roll well on initiative."
- Gyrgum attempts to cut the strand a spider is on but misses; the silk was swaying and his blade didn't land true. He narrowly dodges a spider's snapping jaws as he retreats toward his companions.
- The DM points out that magic missile can damage the webs and suggests Daz move first and hold his action.
- Daz moves south and readies an action on an intersection, waiting for Zalthir and Fargas to cross it before blasting it to cut off their pursuers. Thorin is repositioned so he isn't standing on the doomed strands.
- Daz sends all three magic missile darts into the intersection, severing two strands and cutting off the spiders' path. "The spiders are not happy."
- Zalthir confirms that nobody intends to stay and fight. The party flees southward, narrowly avoiding the spiders as they spin new lines to give chase.
- Thorin dashes 60 feet to follow his companions ("I'm going to run like hell").
- The DM checks that no one at the table has arachnophobia. His son has a serious case of it, which is why he usually avoids spiders.
- Told the spiders could deal around 20 damage, Gyrgum disengages and moves 30 feet south.
- Daz fires a final magic missile at a strand to hinder the spiders, forcing them to spin new webs, then steps off the webbed paths to safety. The spiders, limited to about 15 feet of movement when spinning, cannot reach Gyrgum.
- With five giant spiders lining up behind him, Gyrgum disengages again and escapes south.
- Yuk Yuk and Spiderbait mock the party's frantic escape. Yuk Yuk calls it "hyper cool" but describes an implausibly crazy maneuver he would have pulled, which "people would be talking about … for years." Spiderbait suggests they go back and check it out. Daz jokes that Yuk Yuk would have "pirouetted around them while surfing."

### The Secret of Brysis of Khaem 007.02

Once the immediate danger had passed, the party turned their attention to Fargas, the halfling they had cut from a spider cocoon. He had let slip earlier that he knew the location of a hidden tomb and its treasure, and the party was not about to let him quietly forget it. Confronted with written notes of his own words, Fargas relented and revealed the history of Brysis of Khaem. Brysis was a half-elf sorcerer from the height of Faerûn's great empires of magic, and her floating tomb had plunged into a crevasse and landed in the Underdark after the fall of the Empire of Netheril and its flying cities. Fargas explained that the obvious sarcophagus within the tomb, a wide stone coffin atop a black marble tier, was a decoy. The true treasure lay hidden behind a secret passageway leading from the servant's tomb to a concealed vault. He admitted that he did not know the tomb's precise location, only that it lay somewhere in the passages on the northeastern edge of the Darklake. Gyrgum's sharp Insight check (22) convinced him that Fargas had not been lying so much as "desperate to not die." Daz examined him for signs of the gnoll spawn with an Arcana check (18) and confirmed he was clean. Sarith, who claimed the descriptions narrowed the tomb down to "three or four places," scratched a rough map with a stick and the green paint from the mushroom-moss they had gathered. He marked the Darklake, the drow outpost of Velkynvelve where they had been held prisoner, the settlements of Sloobludop and Gracklstugh, and the estimated location of the lost tomb along the northeastern edge of the Darklake. The party learned they were still midway through the Silken Paths and roughly three days from Sloobludop, with their drow pursuit level at three.

#### After reaching safety, the party interrogates Fargas about the treasure he mentioned earlier, leading to a revelation about a lost floating tomb.
- The party confronts Fargas about his claims regarding a hidden tomb and treasure. Gyrgum quotes him back to himself and shows him the notes.
- Fargas reveals the history of Brysis of Khaem: "in ages past, height of Faerun's great empires of magic, the half-elf sorcerer, Brysis of Khaem, was interred in a floating tomb." After the fall of the Empire of Netheril and its flying cities, the tomb plunged into a crevasse and has remained in the Underdark for centuries.
- Fargas explains that the obvious sarcophagus, "a wide stone sarcophagus atop a marble black tier," is a decoy. He and his team found evidence of a secret passageway from the servant's tomb to the real tomb holding Brysis's magic and treasure.
- Thorin's mishearing of "Brys" prompts Fargas to correct the pronunciation to "Brysis."
- Fargas admits: "Unfortunately, I do not know the precise location of the tomb." He knows only that it lies somewhere in the passageways on the northeastern edge of the Darklake.
- Sarith questions Fargas further and concludes: "I may not know exactly where it is, but I know probably where it is." He says there are only three or four places it could be.
- Zalthir notes it is suspicious that Fargas now claims not to know the location. Gyrgum rolls Insight (22) and reads Fargas as not boastful but "desperate to not die."

Sarith stresses that the Darklake is not one lake but a three-dimensional series of interconnected water-filled tunnels and caves.

- The party discusses their current position within the Silken Paths and notes that their pursuit level by the drow remains active, currently at three.
- Zalthir asks whether the commotion with the spiders might slow the drow. The guides muse that it depends on whether the drow have a guide, because "they don't know my secret paths through this." Yuk Yuk pulls his beanie on and proclaims them "the best guides."
- Zalthir tries to claim the party is level three; the DM replies, "That was a good try, Zalthir."

### Encounter with the Spectator 007.03

The party had barely processed this revelation when a strange, multi-eyed creature drifted into view, flying above the web strands. It reached into their minds with a telepathic voice, identifying itself as a being freed from the service of a long-dead drow wizard. It warned them that demons were rising in the dark. The creature's mind was deeply fractured, however: its five eyes did not always seem to be "on the same page," splitting off to stare at whoever cracked a joke. Its warnings quickly curdled into paranoid accusations. It declared the party to be a demon pack sent to hunt it down. Gyrgum bet Jimjar a gold piece that he couldn't talk the creature out of attacking, and Jimjar gladly took the challenge. But the persuasion roll came up a two, and the spectator attacked.

The battle that followed was chaotic and disorienting. The creature turned one of its eyes on Thorin, flooding his mind with confusion. A d6 roll sent him lurching north toward the spectator, where he struck it for nine damage and marked it with Vex. Zalthir shook off a paralyzing ray (Constitution 16) and then clambered beneath the sticky web strands to strike at the floating aberration. Dangling from the silk left him effectively facing AC 18, and he missed three times, even after spending a focus point. The spectator retaliated with a second ray that locked Zalthir's body in a rigid paralysis, and it tried to fill Thorin's heart with supernatural dread. Thorin was so unafraid that the spectator itself was almost frightened. He drove home a blow with advantage from Vex, adding a Hill Strike that dealt 11 damage in total, though the creature made its strength save and stayed aloft. Daz answered with volley after volley of magical force. When the final burst struck home against the spectator's last 10 hit points, the creature shuddered and died. Its last words, delivered as a dying telepathic whisper, were the same warning it had given at the start: the demons are coming.

#### While navigating the webbed tunnels, the party is confronted by a floating, multi-eyed creature that delivers a cryptic warning before turning hostile.
- A strange, multi-eyed flying creature appears before the group, and their goblin guides warn that it's not a real beholder, but still damn scary.
- The creature establishes two-way telepathic communication with the party ("I bring warnings"), revealing it was freed from the service of a long-dead drow wizard.
- Its five eyes stare at the party, splitting off to track whoever laughs or cracks a joke, a sign that its mind is not unified.
- The entity warns the party, "since you also were a prisoner of the drow," that the demons are rising in the dark.
- The party asks what kind of demons and where. The creature turns suspicious, asking why they want to know, then accuses them of working for the demons and of hunting it down.
- The party asks whether the myconid spores could help. The DM rules that telepathy and spores are independent channels ("that's the compute network and that's the storage network"), and that all the spectator's eyes share a single mind.
- The spectator grows increasingly unstable, accusing the party of being a demon pack sent to hunt it down. Daz protests, "Now, we're not a demon pack."
- The DM gives the party one action to decide. Thorin votes to make a break for it; Daz notes he has zero in the social skills.
- Zalthir points out Jimjar's +5 Persuasion. Gyrgum bets Jimjar a gold piece that he can't get the creature to stand down.
- Jimjar attempts to persuade the creature to calm its paranoia, but his words fail to reassure it; Gyrgum, having bet against him, rolls for Jimjar and gets a two.
- Initiative is rolled. The creature is identified as a spectator, "not the specter."
- The spectator attacks, striking Thorin with a ray of confusion that disorients him. On a d6 roll of 5 he moves north and lashes out at the creature, hitting for nine damage and applying Vex.
- The spectator attempts to paralyze Zalthir with another eye ray, but Zalthir successfully resists the effect (Constitution 16).
- The spectator has 30 hit points. Daz hits it with a magic missile for nine damage.
- Zalthir navigates the difficult, sticky webs to strike at the creature but struggles to land a blow while dangling from the strands. Because Thorin is confused and no longer counts as an ally, Zalthir cannot share his space and must crawl beneath the web, which is difficult terrain and gives the spectator an effective AC 18. He misses three times, including an extra attack paid for with a focus point.
- Gyrgum considers inflict wounds and Tasha's caustic brew but has no clear line. He settles on a hand crossbow instead.
- The spectator retaliates by paralyzing Zalthir, who can repeat the saving throw each round. It tries to magically frighten Thorin, who resists so thoroughly that "it almost causes the spectator to be frightened."
- Thorin delivers a powerful blow with advantage from Vex: seven damage plus a Hill Strike for four more. The spectator succeeds on its DC 13 Strength save and is not knocked prone.
- With the spectator at 10 hit points, Daz fires a final volley of magic missiles, striking it and ending its life.
- As the creature dies, it telepathically warns the party one last time that the demons are coming. Zalthir's paralysis ends.

### The Web-Bound Chest 007.04

Near the tunnel's end, the party spotted a chest tangled in the webs and immediately grew suspicious. Thorin declared it a mimic on sight. Zalthir suggested using the telepathic spore network of their myconid companion, Stool, to reach out and communicate with it at a distance. The DM called the tactic brilliant and something no one in all his years of campaigning had ever tried. The effort produced nothing, and the silence that answered them was answer enough. Daz asked about taking a long rest before dealing with the chest, but Yuk Yuk's cheerful assurance that he had rarely seen spiders "show up in the middle of the night and eat other groups" ended that idea. Zalthir flatly refused to become spider food. The party quietly walked around the chest and pressed on. They emerged at last from the Silken Paths and finally allowed themselves to rest.

#### Near the end of the Silken Paths, the party discovers a suspicious chest and decides how to proceed.
- As they near the end of the Silken Paths, the party spots a chest stuck within the spider webs near the tunnel exit.
- Thorin immediately calls it: "I don't believe it, it's a mimic." Daz reminisces about a past campaign where the party disarmed every statue they came across.
- Zalthir asks whether they can use the spores to communicate with it, and Daz notes that a mimic should have a consciousness to reach.
- Daz asks about taking a long rest before dealing with the chest. Yuk Yuk's reassurance about spiders eating groups in the night makes the others balk, and Zalthir declares he is not becoming spider food.
- The DM calls the spore idea brilliant and something nobody has ever tried in all his years of campaigning. While he looks up the rules, Daz teases that the lookup itself suggests it's a mimic, and Thorin compares that to breaking crypto by timing the responses.
- The DM rules, "You cannot communicate with it," so the attempt fails to establish any connection.
- The party considers firing a magic missile at it or poking it with a rock.
- Fearing the chest is a trap or a mimic, the party chooses to bypass it entirely rather than risk an encounter.
- The group successfully reaches the end of the Silken Paths and prepares for a long-needed rest.

### Debts and Divine Plans at the Path's End 007.05

When they woke, Yuk Yuk and Spiderbait wasted no time in reminding Jimjar of the twenty gold pieces he owed them. Jimjar, ever the negotiator, convinced the two goblins to stay on with the group until he could secure their payment. Thorin sweetened the deal by promising them first dibs on the next good mushrooms. Gyrgum graciously agreed to let the five gold Jimjar owed him wait so the guides could be paid first. Zalthir joked about sending the goblins back to pry open the suspicious chest. Buppido, the unsettling derro who had been traveling with them, lit up at the suggestion ("I like how you think"). He fixed the party with a meaningful look and murmured that perhaps they would like to discuss becoming part of his divine plan. No one took him up on the offer, and Zalthir wondered aloud whether Buppido might be the demon. His words lingered in the dark air of the Underdark long after the campfire had gone cold.

#### The party reaches the end of the Silken Paths to rest, where Jimjar negotiates a debt with their goblin guides, and Buppido hints at a dark alliance.
- The party arrives at the end of the Silken Paths and settles down for a long rest.
- The goblin guides, Yuk Yuk and Spiderbait, turn to Jimjar and demand their twenty gold pieces: "Where's our 20 gold?"
- Jimjar negotiates with the goblins, convincing them to join the party until he can secure their payment ("I'm good for it, guys"). The goblins shrug it off, and the party acquires two more goblins.
- Thorin promises the goblins first dibs the next time the party finds good mushrooms.
- Gyrgum agrees to defer a debt Jimjar owes him so the guides can be paid first. Jimjar puts it this way: "I do owe you five gold, but we're going to put these two guides at the higher on the priority queue if that's okay with you, Gyrgum."
- Zalthir suggests, if they are feeling evil, that the goblins retrieve the gold from the chest.
- Daz reasons that keeping the goblins motivated by their gold might get their help slaying something later.
- Buppido says "I like how you think," expresses admiration for the party's cunning, and cryptically invites them to become part of his mysterious divine plan. He gives the impression that he genuinely thinks he and the party could partner on it.
- Zalthir calls Buppido "a dubious ally, if anything" and wonders whether he's the demon.
