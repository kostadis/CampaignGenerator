# Chapter 003

### Planning the Great Escape 003.01

The morning — if such a word could even apply to the lightless depths of the Underdark — brought with it a rare and precious gift: the cell door had been left open. Everything beyond remained “dark and miserable,” but the prisoners had a brief window in which to act. Their minds were linked in silent communion through the rapport spores of Stool, the small myconid sprout who had become an unlikely ally. The spores allowed the prisoners to communicate telepathically with selected creatures near them, letting the conspirators coordinate without openly announcing their intentions to every paranoid captive in the pen.

The group debated whether to make a straight dash for freedom or risk a detour to the guard tower’s armory. Their confiscated personal belongings were held elsewhere, in Ilvara’s quarters, but the armory offered weapons and armor that might make the difference between escape and recapture. Thorin, the dwarf warrior, felt the absence of his family’s memento — a pendant fashioned from a giant’s tooth — like a wound. “My character has a personal motivation to retrieve his memento of his family,” he insisted, though the immediate decision was to arm themselves first and recover their own property later.

The outpost’s interior chambers were dimly illuminated by lanterns containing phosphorescent fungi, while the exterior remained dark. The drow in the nearby barracks were inattentive conscripts accustomed to uneventful duty, and the guards at the lookout watched outward rather than back toward the prison. Ordinary footsteps might pass unnoticed, but a scream, a burst of fire, or the sound of the massive cell door being forced would bring the outpost down upon the prisoners. The group therefore settled on a cautious infiltration of the guard post, with everyone else ready to charge if the attempt failed. Prince Derendil offered muscle rather than subtlety, while Buppido argued that the “divine plan” required somebody else to secure the weapons first.

Zalthir, the group’s most capable infiltrator, went forward with Daz, whose magical tricks might salvage the situation if they were discovered. Fate had other ideas. Zalthir tripped as he entered, and Daz’s attempt to catch him only kicked a can across the floor. Two drow who had been idly playing cards looked up from their game. “That went well,” Zalthir observed as the hope of a quiet escape shattered.

#### The party wakes up in their cell to find the door open and must finalize their plan to escape the drow outpost.

- “Morning” in Velkynvelve means that everything remains dark and miserable, but the prisoners awaken to discover that their cell door is open.
- The party recognizes that the open door presents only a brief window of opportunity and begins finalizing the escape plan discussed the previous night.
- The party debates whether to head directly for freedom, go to the guard tower’s armory for weapons, or risk reaching the quarters where their confiscated belongings are stored.
- The group concludes that obtaining weapons first will give them a chance to fight their way toward their property and then out of the outpost.
- Thorin expresses a personal need to recover a family memento: “I have a pennant made from a giant’s tooth reminding me of family.”
- Stool happily scatters rapport spores throughout the cell, establishing a telepathic link among the prisoners.
- The spores allow affected creatures to communicate for an hour, which the party observes is effectively an eternity compared with the few seconds occupied by most battles.
- The telepathy is selective: a prisoner may communicate with as many or as few linked creatures as desired, though the Dungeon Master warns that paranoid or observant captives might still overhear fragments when dramatically appropriate.
- The link initially connects creatures gathered near Stool and remains usable within roughly thirty feet, allowing the prisoners to coordinate their movements without speaking aloud.
- The Dungeon Master clarifies that magical effects need not obey ordinary physics and confirms that Zalthir’s fiery breath can ignite a five-foot area and allow mundane fire to propagate from it, though he can use the ability only twice per long rest.
- Interior chambers are dimly lit by lanterns containing phosphorescent fungi, while the exterior portions of the outpost remain dark.
- The nearby barracks house bored conscripts accustomed to routine activity. Ordinary footsteps and movement may not attract notice, but screams, conspicuous combat, or loud spellcasting certainly will.
- The guards at the exterior post are watching away from the prison rather than behind themselves, giving the prisoners a chance to pass if they remain quiet.
- The group considers sending Prince Derendil ahead because Quaggoths work in the outpost, but rejects the idea as likely to produce havoc and noise.
- Prince Derendil offers to follow once weapons are secured: “If you need someone with muscle, I could come and knock hold people down.”
- Buppido hesitates to lead, maintaining that the divine plan requires someone else to retrieve weapons first.
- Zalthir, with the best chance of moving quietly, volunteers to approach the guard post. Daz accompanies him because his illusion and utility magic might help if something goes wrong.
- Zalthir explains the fallback plan: “Maybe I’ll try going on my own first. If that fails, we all charge in and have fun.”
- Zalthir immediately rolls disastrously, followed by an equally poor attempt from Daz.
- As they enter, Zalthir trips. Daz tries to catch him but kicks a can, sending a clatter through the room.
- Two drow guards who had been playing cards look up and notice them. Daz considers using Minor Illusion to redirect the sound, but it is already too late.
- With the guards alerted and the telepathic link carrying the news back to the prisoners, the plan changes instantly from stealth to a charge.

### Skirmish in the Guard Tower 003.02

With no weapons and no armor, the prisoners found themselves face to face with hardened drow soldiers and the most dangerous of them all: Imbros, a seasoned Elite Warrior whose formidable resilience was immediately apparent. Thorin rushed into the room and attempted to sucker-punch and shove the Elite Warrior, hoping to remove the greatest threat before the drow could react. His fist missed. Imbros struck him with his short sword, attempting to subdue rather than kill such a valuable prisoner. Thorin’s second wind briefly kept him upright, but another drow soon cut him down. He slipped toward death, succeeding on one death save before failing another.

The struggle became a desperate, chaotic brawl in the cramped lower floor of the guard tower. Zalthir repeatedly attacked and tried to force the Elite Warrior away from the doorway, only to suffer an extraordinary streak of failed strikes. Daz tried to attack one of the ordinary guards with Mind Sliver, but the drow resisted the intrusion. Above them, the other prisoners exploited the distraction: Eldeth, Turvy, Jimjar, Sarith, Buppido, Topsy, Shuushar, and others disengaged from the melee, scrambled upstairs, and armed themselves with short swords and hand crossbows. Prince Derendil remained below to hold the enemy in place, taking a short-sword wound as he entered the fighting and answering with both claws.

Sarith’s hatred for his former captors proved lethal. Once armed, he descended upon one of the ordinary drow, cursing the guard as he struck twice and killed him. A barrage of hand-crossbow bolts followed from the upper floor. Eldeth’s shot missed, as did Buppido’s despite his loudly asserted expertise, but one of the twins, Topsy or Turvy, and Jimjar found their marks. Ront then squeezed into the room and killed the second ordinary guard with a short sword. Shuushar the Awakened turned his own crossbow on the Elite Warrior and struck him as well.

Gyrgum fought through the press of bodies to reach the fallen Thorin. After weighing the merits of merely stabilizing him against restoring him to the battle, the cleric placed a hand upon the dwarf and cast Cure Wounds. The spell restored Thorin completely, and Gyrgum ensured that a short sword was passed down to him. Thorin then disengaged and climbed to the armory in search of armor rather than remain trapped beneath the crush of combatants.

#### A desperate, unarmed struggle breaks out in the guard tower as the prisoners attempt to overwhelm the drow sentries.

- The lower guard room contains a table, three chairs, a smaller side table, and spider-silk webbing fitted with hooks for equipment. A stairway leads to the armory above, but there are no useful windows or alternate exits.
- Thorin rushes into the room first. Recognizing Imbros as by far the greatest threat, he attempts an unarmed strike and shove against the Elite Warrior.
- Thorin describes the attack as a high-risk attempt to “sucker punch” Imbros, but misses despite his confidence in the maneuver.
- Imbros uses his short sword to subdue rather than kill Thorin, since the prisoners remain valuable assets. He strikes the dwarf twice for seven points of damage each.
- Thorin invokes Second Wind retroactively as the bonus action he would have used before attacking, restoring four hit points and keeping himself on his feet through the elite warrior's blows.
- Zalthir attacks twice with his unarmed techniques but rolls two ones and a two, failing to land a blow.
- The other prisoners exploit the distraction. Eldeth, Turvy, Jimjar, Sarith, Buppido, Topsy, Shuushar, and others disengage, squeeze past the combatants, and climb to the upper armory.
- The Dungeon Master rules that the upper room can physically hold the prisoners while they arm themselves, though it would be extremely crowded if combat moved upstairs.
- Prince Derendil does not retreat. He exposes himself to an attack of opportunity, suffers five points of damage, and answers with two claw attacks that wound one of the drow.
- Gyrgum disengages, squeezes through the room, and heads upstairs to claim a weapon.
- A second drow targets the visibly weakened Thorin, hits him for five points, and knocks him unconscious.
- The drow continue to use nonlethal force because they want the prisoners alive. Thorin succeeds on his first death save but fails the next.
- Daz targets a wounded guard with Mind Sliver, but the drow succeeds on the Intelligence save and takes no damage.
- Zalthir refuses an opportunity to disengage — “Not my style” — and continues trying to force the Elite Warrior away from the doorway.
- Zalthir redirects his attention toward one of the ordinary drow but misses twice more.
- Once armed, Sarith attacks one of his former captors. He curses the guard with the stored fury of someone finally confronting the person who imprisoned him, hits twice for fourteen damage, and kills him.
- Eldeth takes position at the opening and fires a hand crossbow, though her first shot misses.
- Topsy and Turvy fire next; one bolt strikes the remaining ordinary guard for five damage.
- Jimjar follows with another accurate bolt for five damage.
- Buppido loudly maintains that he is an expert with hand crossbows, but his shot misses.
- Ront squeezes into the lower room and attacks the second guard with a short sword, killing him.
- Shuushar the Awakened uses one of the remaining hand crossbows against Imbros, hitting the Elite Warrior for five points.
- The coordinated action of the prisoners turns what had looked like a certain defeat into a winnable fight. The Dungeon Master notes that befriending the other captives prevented an “inglorious and brutal end.”
- Gyrgum returns to Thorin and considers Spare the Dying, Healing Word, and Cure Wounds. Because he can physically reach the dwarf, he chooses the stronger touch spell.
- Cure Wounds restores sixteen hit points, bringing Thorin back to full strength.
- Gyrgum also passes Thorin a short sword, ensuring that the revived warrior will not have to continue fighting barehanded.
- With the lower floor too crowded for him to attack effectively, Thorin disengages and climbs upstairs to secure armor.

### The Fall of the Prince 003.03

Daz helped turn the tide by abandoning the uncertain Mind Sliver for Magic Missile. The first volley of three glowing darts dealt twelve points of damage to the Elite Warrior; a second volley left Imbros at six hit points. The spell’s unerring force proved ideal in the constricted guard room, where ordinary ranged attacks were difficult and the Elite Warrior’s defenses were formidable.

Yet Imbros remained deadly. Cursing the revolting slaves in Undercommon, he concentrated his poisoned blade on Prince Derendil. The Quaggoth suffered a devastating sequence of wounds and was slain. The self-proclaimed cursed elven prince fell where he had held the doorway, and the escaped prisoners were left one companion lighter.

Zalthir finally landed a blow and pushed the Elite Warrior into the doorway. Sarith, burning with personal hatred, was tempted to begin shouting abuse at his former captor, threatening to draw the attention of the rest of the outpost. Gyrgum answered with an unconventional religious appeal: “Think of your spirituality and how much better it would be if all this came out.” Sarith considered the suggestion and replied, “Never thought of that.” Steadied by this newfound spiritual focus, he transformed his fury into action and delivered the killing blow to Imbros.

#### The battle reaches a climax as the Drow Elite Warrior makes a final stand, resulting in a tragic loss for the group.

- With both ordinary drow guards dead, Imbros is left alone against the escaped prisoners, but his resilience, skill, and poisoned short sword keep him dangerous.
- Daz initially considers another Mind Sliver, then remembers that Magic Missile cannot miss or be blocked.
- Daz’s first casting launches three glowing darts for a total of twelve force damage.
- Daz admits, “I need to read my spells better,” after realizing that the spell produces three missiles rather than a single dart.
- The Dungeon Master explains that Magic Missile remains effective at close range because it does not count as a ranged weapon attack, making it particularly useful in the cramped tower.
- Imbros curses the revolting slaves in Undercommon and focuses his attacks on Prince Derendil.
- The Elite Warrior’s poisoned blade inflicts a devastating amount of damage. One attack misses, but the successful blows leave Derendil grievously wounded.
- Sarith attacks Imbros with his newly acquired short sword and deals twelve damage.
- Six hand crossbows fire from above. Three bolts find their target, dealing fifteen damage to the Elite Warrior.
- Gyrgum considers using Tasha’s Caustic Brew but realizes that its line would also strike an ally in the packed room.
- Instead, he turns to Toll the Dead, learning that the cantrip is especially potent against an already wounded creature.
- Daz casts Magic Missile again, and the second volley leaves Imbros at six hit points.
- Imbros nevertheless remains standing long enough to kill Prince Derendil.
- Derendil’s death is sudden and final; the group briefly confirms that the Quaggoth is truly dead before returning its attention to the surviving enemy.
- Zalthir finally lands a hit for four points of damage and pushes the Elite Warrior into the doorway, helping create an opening.
- Sarith is on the verge of shouting curses at Imbros loudly enough to alert the rest of Velkynvelve.
- Gyrgum attempts a Religion check to calm him, suggesting that Sarith contemplate his spirituality and meditate instead of screaming.
- The appeal succeeds. Sarith responds, “Never thought of that,” and keeps his voice down.
- Sarith Kzekarit, fueled by a mix of vengeance and newfound spiritual focus, delivers the final blow to the Drow Elite Warrior.
- With Imbros dead, the lower chamber is secured. The surviving prisoners can finally move freely between the guard room and armory.
- The victory comes at the cost of Prince Derendil, whose strength had held the drow in place while the others armed themselves.

### Arming the Rebellion 003.04

The guard tower fell silent save for the ragged breathing of the survivors. The party had prevailed largely because the prisoners they had befriended joined the fight; without those allies, the breakout would have ended in a swift and inglorious defeat. They wasted no time stripping the tower of everything useful: short swords, hand crossbows, armor, one hundred feet of rope, and six bags of caltrops that could be scattered to obstruct pursuit.

Thorin claimed a short sword, studded leather armor, and a shield. Gyrgum kept rope and a bag of caltrops, though the available martial weapons were unfamiliar to him. Daz briefly considered leather armor before realizing that he lacked the training to cast effectively while wearing it. Zalthir declined the available weapons because they would make him less effective rather than more. Battered, bloodied, and mourning Prince Derendil, the prisoners nevertheless stood armed at last, ready to continue their flight into the darkness of the Underdark.

#### Following the skirmish in the guard tower, the party and their allies scavenge for equipment and discuss the tactical limitations of their current skills.

- The survivors inventory the remaining short swords, hand crossbows, armor, rope, and caltrops, distributing equipment among the escaped prisoners.
- The party does not track individual crossbow bolts, simplifying the division of ammunition.
- Thorin claims a short sword, studded leather armor, and a shield.
- Gyrgum gives up the hand crossbow he had considered taking because his spells and cantrips are more effective.
- Gyrgum takes one hundred feet of rope, declaring, “I like rope.”
- Six bags of caltrops are added to the group’s shared supplies. They are intended to slow or obstruct drow pursuing the escapees.
- Gyrgum personally takes a bag of caltrops “for fun.”
- Daz initially takes leather armor, then learns that he has no armor proficiency and abandons the idea.
- The group discusses how armor proficiency affects spellcasters. Wizards are no longer categorically prohibited from wearing armor, but doing so without proficiency creates serious limitations.
- The conversation turns to multi-classing. A level of fighter would grant armor training and Second Wind, potentially improving a wizard’s durability.
- Gyrgum is proficient only with simple weapons, making the captured short swords poor choices for him, though still preferable to complete helplessness.
- Zalthir determines that none of the recovered weapons suit his fighting style and that using them would make him less effective.
- The party leaves the tower better equipped but still without its original belongings, including Thorin’s giant’s-tooth pendant.
- With the immediate guards defeated, the prisoners are armed and positioned to continue the escape when the session resumes.
