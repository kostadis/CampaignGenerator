# Chapter 039 From Exasperation to Expedition: Blingdenstone's New Hope

### Daz Reads Dorbo's Political Play 039.01

With the party poised to recruit Clan Goldwhisker, Daz—whose takeover had come from exhaustion with political maneuvering rather than ambition—recognized exactly why Dorbo had become so accommodating. The chief had found a convenient fall guy: if the operation failed, he could blame the drow who had seized control; if it succeeded, he could claim that supporting Daz had been his own design. Daz remained determined to involve the Goldwhiskers so everyone would have warning if the Pudding King operation failed and because, as he argued, their political status could be settled only if they survived. Senni supported the appeal to common purpose, though Daz sharpened her rhetoric: “It's important for us to have a common outcome and a common drive to that outcome. The rest of the commonality would be a nice-to-have.” The bureaucrats began taking notes on Daz's pronouncements while papers shuffled around the chamber, each official seemingly preparing to explain how faithfully implementing Daz's leadership had produced any eventual success.

#### As the party prepares to recruit Clan Goldwhisker, Daz recognizes why Dorbo has yielded operational control and the gnome bureaucracy reacts to Daz's blunt leadership.

### Kitchen Discovery and Glabbagool's New Role 039.02

Before heading to meet the Clan Goldwhisker, Thorin's completionist curiosity got the better of him. Unable to tolerate unexplored portions of the map, he investigated the remaining doors in the bureaucratic hub. The eastern door opened into a kitchen or break room, where a gnome chef mistook the group for the newly hired heroes and Thorin asserted that their agreement included board and “unlimited access to the snack room.” The chef eventually agreed to provide a snack. Chief Dorbo insisted they eat elsewhere, at the Foaming Mug, where there were tables and chairs suitable for larger visitors.

The other unexplored door led toward Chief Dorbo's private quarters. Dorbo intercepted the party and dryly explained that he had no acid-resistant armor inside—only a desk, couch, bed, and private space. Glabbagool volunteered to squeeze through and investigate, but the party decided it was not yet time to antagonize their host that thoroughly.

The chef then noticed the intelligent gelatinous cube and asked whether Glabbagool could clean the kitchen floor. Glabbagool was delighted by the chance to be useful and productive, earning the affectionate nickname “Roomba.” This prompted a discussion of the legendary origin of gelatinous cubes as dungeon-cleaning creatures created to remove abandoned corpses and treasure between expeditions.

#### Thorin's curiosity leads the party to explore Chief Dorbo's kitchen, where Glabbagool finds a new purpose.

- Thorin immediately asserted, “Yes, the deal includes board. Unlimited access to the snack room, I believe, was one of the things I had to do.”
- The party jokingly described the space as a “micro kitchen,” prompting a misunderstanding about calling the chef's chicken “micro.”
- The chef agreed to provide a snack. Chief Dorbo then insisted that the party leave the kitchen.
- Chief Dorbo later shouted that they could not eat there and directed them to the inn, where tables and chairs were available for “big people.” After noticing Thorin's height, he clarified that Thorin's taller companions were the real concern.
- At the other door, Dorbo intercepted the group and asked, “Is it really necessary for you to go into my private quarters?”
- When asked about acid-resistant armor, Dorbo replied, “No, I do not have acid-resistant armor in my private quarters. I've got a desk. I've got a couch. I've got a bed.”
- Glabbagool offered to squeeze into the room and inspect it, but the party declined because it was not yet time to annoy their host that much.
- The chef asked Glabbagool to clean the kitchen floor. As this matched a gelatinous cube's natural talents and gave him a chance to feel useful, Glabbagool eagerly agreed.

### Approaching the Goldwhisker Warrens 039.03

The party approached the massive, monumental double doors leading to the Goldwhisker Warrens. They briefly considered sending Glabbagool underneath to scout, but rejected the idea because the inhabitants might mistake him for a hostile ooze. Someone finally proposed the radical alternative of simply knocking. Daz announced that they intended to rid the warrens, grotto, and surrounding area of the slime problem and needed the Goldwhiskers' help. A startled guard told them not to leave and scampered away to consult Chief Chipgrin.

#### The party approaches the Clan Goldwhisker's territory and announces their purpose to the guards.

- The party took the direct route to the Goldwhisker Warrens through a pair of locked double doors.
- They approached openly and confidently rather than sneaking, joking that a ladder and clipboard could get someone into most places.
- The doors were described as massive—roughly fifty feet tall—and “monumental.”
- The party briefly investigated whether there was a keyhole or enough space beneath the doors to scout.
- They considered sending Glabbagool underneath under the pretext that he was cleaning, but worried the wererats might mistake him for a hostile ooze and kill him.
- Someone finally suggested knocking, earning the response, “Oh, well, that's a remarkable idea.”

- After a short wait, a voice announced that Chief Chipgrin wanted them brought inside.

### Meeting the Wererat Guards 039.04

Four gnome guards escorted the party through the warrens. Their stringy mustaches, pronounced noses, and other subtle features appeared increasingly rat-like. Although Gyrgum had the wisdom not to bluntly point out their appearance, the party openly expressed curiosity about meeting wererats and assured the guards that they were not judgmental. The guards were thrilled to encounter visitors who had not arrived demanding the wererats' deaths. When the party observed that rats, lycanthropic or otherwise, occupied an important ecological niche, one guard triumphantly declared, “See, I told you.” The remark ignited a loud philosophical debate among the four guards about rats, wererats, and their respective ecological roles, which continued all the way to Chipgrin's court.

#### The party is led into the Goldwhisker Warrens by wererat guards, whose appearance prompts a philosophical debate.

### Negotiating with Chief Chipgrin 039.05

The party found Chief Chipgrin seated atop a rise carpeted with soft, well-manicured green moss. His stone chair was draped with a cave badger pelt, and he was flanked by two giant rats and numerous guards. Chipgrin immediately assumed that the Diggermattocks had sent the party to drive his clan out. Daz instead explained that the party wanted to escape the Underdark and had learned that helping Blingdenstone defeat its ooze infestation would secure that aid.

Daz offered to arrange the conversation Chipgrin had long wanted with Chief Dorbo, but imposed strict conditions: the meeting would be for cooperation, not political maneuvering, positioning, recrimination, or opportunities to say “I told you so.” Otherwise, the party would leave and allow the gnomes to deal with the oozes themselves. Chipgrin remarked, “I've been told that Drow are a difficult group to negotiate with, but you take it to the next level.” Daz replied, “It's not a negotiation. I'm here to ask you for your help.”

Chief Chipgrin agreed to join the fight but refused to place the Clan Goldwhisker under Dorbo's command or participate in a “grand crusade” designed to send his people to slaughter. Daz clarified that the party would formulate the plan and make the operational decisions. He personally accepted responsibility if Goldwhisker fighters died because of those choices, even acknowledging that Chipgrin could seek vengeance against him afterward. Daz had no ambition beyond eliminating the threat and leaving Blingdenstone. The blunt arrangement satisfied Chipgrin, producing a moment of “shock and awe” among his listeners before he agreed to attend a broader meeting of the clan leaders.

#### Daz engages in direct negotiations with Chief Chipgrin, securing the Clan Goldwhisker's cooperation.

- Chief Chipgrin sat atop a raised area carpeted with soft, carefully maintained green moss.
- His stone chair was draped with a cave badger pelt, and two giant rats rested beside him while guards surrounded the audience area.
- Chipgrin opened bluntly: “Let's get down to brass hobnails, then. You were sent by the Diggermattocks because they want us out, right?”
- The party warned that the Diggermattocks might get their wish in a way they would not celebrate if the oozes destroyed everyone.
- Daz explained that the party wanted to leave the Underdark and had arranged to receive help if they eliminated Blingdenstone's ooze problem.
- Daz said the settlement's leaders had wanted to spend too much time orchestrating and debating the response: “We just need some help. We need some help. From you.”
- He offered to arrange the meeting Chipgrin desired with Dorbo, but only if both chiefs were prepared to cooperate.
- Daz warned, “If you want to meet with him because you want to do some political wrangling and position yourself in a better place or say, I told you so, or generally cause confusion or chaos, then I have no interest.”
- Chipgrin replied, “I've been told that Drow are a difficult group to negotiate with, but you take it to the next level.”
- Daz answered, “It's not a negotiation. I'm here to ask you for your help.”
- Daz presented a stark choice: help eliminate the oozes or let the party leave while Blingdenstone was overrun.
- Chipgrin agreed that he had only ever wanted Dorbo to speak with him and promised that the Goldwhiskers would fight.
- He refused, however, to place his clan under Dorbo's leadership or let them be expended in a Diggermattock “grand crusade.”
- Daz made clear that the party would formulate the operation and accept responsibility for it: “If there's a mistake where we send your people off to slaughter, it's all on me.”
- He even accepted that Chipgrin could declare vengeance and kill him afterward, though he emphasized that his actual goal was simply to solve the crisis and leave.
- Chipgrin paused and concluded, “Well, then, I think we can have a deal.”
- Daz instructed Chipgrin to gather every able-bodied fighter and attend a meeting with Dorbo and Blingdenstone's other clan leaders.

### The Pudding King's Origins and Demonic Influence 039.06

Chipgrin then shared valuable information about the Pudding King. He had known the gnome when he worked in the tunnels, his kin had witnessed the gnome's return, and he knew both what the Pudding King was doing and where he could be found. The Pudding King's fall appeared rooted in vengeance, pride, madness, resentment at being ignored, and demonic power that amplified his worst qualities rather than entirely replacing his personality.

Chipgrin asked whether the party had noticed anything strange about Blingdenstone's other gnomes. Reflecting on their encounters, they recognized that greed and aggression had become unusually pronounced throughout the settlement. Dorbo's fixation on profit seemed particularly unhinged in the face of an existential threat. The party told Chipgrin about Juiblex and connected the phenomenon to the demonic corruption they had already witnessed in Gracklstugh, Neverlight Grove, and among the kuo-toa. Chipgrin agreed that the influence centered on the Pudding King but was bleeding outward into the entire community.

- The party shared its theory concerning Juiblex, the demon lord of oozes.

After Daz used a forceful appeal to prevent the returning leaders from immediately derailing the negotiations, everyone agreed to convene a moot within a few hours. The gathering included Chief Dorbo, Senni, Chief Chipgrin and his Goldwhiskers, Nomi Pathshutter, Gurnik Tapfinger, Kazook Pickshine, and the ghostly Burrow Warden Uth-Jadger. Senni asked Daz to call the meeting to order because he was now leading the effort.

Daz summarized the crisis: an amphitheater full of oozes lay beneath Blingdenstone, commanded by a Pudding King heavily influenced by a demon lord of oozes whose corruption was already affecting the settlement's leaders. The party possessed magic that might let them remove the Pudding King directly, but they needed a convincing distraction. Every able-bodied gnome would launch an apparent full-scale assault on the ooze gathering while the party moved covertly to “snatch” the Pudding King and end his control. Uth-Jadger enthusiastically praised the plan as displaying the technical brilliance once associated with the Burrow Wardens.

### Strategic Proposals and Side Quests 039.08

The leaders then began proposing strategic modifications and side quests. Dorbo suggested that the army attack from the Temple of the Steadfast Stone, redirecting the oozes away from Blingdenstone's populated areas. The temple stood within the Rockblight and would first need to be cleansed. Gurnik Tapfinger produced a red spell gem containing the Hallow spell and explained that it must be placed in the temple's menhir. Ogrémoch's Bane would then send servants to dislodge the gem, forcing the party to defend it until its power awakened three temple guardians and cleansed the site.

Gurnik and Nomi Pathshutter delivered an obviously rehearsed exchange explaining that Earth Elementals would be ideal troops against the oozes but could be driven insane by Ogrémoch's Bane. Cleansing the Steadfast Stone would allow the Stoneheart Enclave to protect summoned elementals during the battle. Daz and the others questioned why the army could not simply attack from the north, avoiding the Rockblight entirely. The room's silence revealed the truth: Blingdenstone's leaders had discovered a capable adventuring party and were attempting to use it to solve additional community problems. The leaders hastily praised the northern approach, while Gurnik looked increasingly concerned that his opportunity to have the temple cleansed was slipping away.

Earth Elementals would help the gnome army survive, and completing preparatory tasks would reduce the number of hostile encounters before the final confrontation. Kazook Pickshine warned that ooze acid could destroy the soldiers' weapons and armor. He knew how to make a protective treatment but needed ingredients from Neverlight Grove. Alternatively, the gnomes could send salt to Gracklstugh in exchange for better weapons—assuming Gracklstugh still existed and remained willing to trade after the party's departure. Uth-Jadger offered assistance from other Burrow Warden spirits if the party completed a separate task for them.

Chief Dorbo's committee sweetened the Rockblight proposal by offering the party a Stone of Controlling Earth Elementals. The group objected to the idea of enslaving an elemental and began speculating about befriending one, freeing it by giving it its own control stone “like giving Dobby a sock,” or repeatedly summoning the same elemental until it became a recurring companion. They imagined Glabbagool and an Earth Elemental getting along and joked that a sufficiently good “summoner score” might pair them with the best elementals, like an extraplanar rideshare service.

### Quest Selection and Party Division 039.10

The party seriously considered ignoring the preparations and trusting their magical “snatch and grab” plan. As a monk who did not wear armor, Zalthir was less worried about corrosion than the armored combatants, but everyone recognized that the gnome army depended heavily on its equipment. They also worried that allowing the entire army to be slaughtered would reflect poorly on them. Thorin settled the cautious-versus-reckless question with a d20: above ten meant safety, below ten meant “YOLO.” He rolled a 17, selecting caution.

The group therefore chose to cleanse the Rockblight first, earning both elemental support for the battle and the Stone of Controlling Earth Elementals. They would also revisit Neverlight Grove for the acid-protection ingredients. They explicitly rejected the Gracklstugh option because no one knew what condition the city was in or how the party would be received there.

To maximize their efforts, the party assigned Glabbagool, Eldeth, and Jimjar to assist Uth-Jadger and the other Burrow Warden ghosts while the main adventuring party handled the Rockblight and Neverlight Grove. Their initial joking description of this assignment as “fight the ghosts” was corrected to helping them, though the party compared the arrangement to defeating creatures in order to recruit them in a game.

#### The party debates which side quests to undertake and decides to split their forces.

- The party asked how much the extra preparations would actually matter if the magical attempt to seize the Pudding King worked.
- They learned that a perfect “snatch” could bypass the larger battle, but failure would force them through a succession of encounters.
- Each completed preparatory quest would remove one encounter or provide a specific advantage, such as an Earth Elemental ally, protection from corrosion, or increased combat effectiveness.
- These benefits applied to the Pudding King operation rather than carrying into the later campaign against the demon lords.
- The Pudding King confrontation was identified as the conclusion of the campaign's first major part; the second would involve confronting the demons themselves.
- The party considered ignoring every side quest and gambling on the direct magical approach.
- Zalthir noted that acid-corroded armor mattered less to an unarmored monk, but the army and armored party members would remain vulnerable.
- The group recognized that the gnome army could suffer severe casualties if sent against the oozes without protection.
- Daz observed that having the entire army killed would “not look good on us.”
- Thorin used a physical d20 to resolve the debate: “above 10 is safety. Below 10 is YOLO.”
- He rolled a 17 and accepted the safer course.
- The party prioritized cleansing the Rockblight because it would secure elemental support and the Stone of Controlling Earth Elementals.
- They also chose to collect the acid-protection ingredients from Neverlight Grove.
- They rejected the Gracklstugh route because of the city's uncertain condition and their troubled history there.
- When Uth-Jadger looked disappointed that his ghosts had not been prioritized, the party considered appealing to the Burrow Wardens' vanity and sense of duty rather than performing another task personally.
- The party ultimately decided to divide its available forces.
- Glabbagool, Eldeth, and Jimjar would assist Uth-Jadger and the other Burrow Warden ghosts while the main group dealt with the Rockblight and Neverlight Grove.
- The assignment was initially described as sending them to “fight the ghosts,” then corrected to helping the ghosts.
- The party compared recruiting allies through conflict to a game in which creatures must first be defeated before joining the player's side.
- The main party selected cleansing the Rockblight as its first task because acquiring an elemental ally would make their arrival at later objectives more impressive.
