# Chapter 014

### Shopping and Preparation 014.01

The party began by taking stock of their situation, rifling through what little they had recovered during their escape: a bedroll, rope, tinderbox, torches, and a waterskin. They used the store credit provided to them to stock up on essentials. The credit came to 175 gold pieces, given as an advance and good only at this particular store, for common potions and scrolls, and priced from the GM's "Sane Magical Prices" price book. They bought a week's worth of rations and a generous supply of healing potions to see them through whatever dangers lay ahead in the Underdark. Gyrgum first hoped to buy scrolls of Thunderwave and Heroism, but learned that he could only cast scrolls of spells on his own class list. He instead acquired a scroll containing a powerful divine bolt of radiant energy, reserving the remainder of his funds for future needs. The shopkeeper was visibly disappointed that "of all the adventures in all the world, she gets the two who don't need any magic, who don't need any weapons."

### The Mystery of the Empty-Scabbard Killers 014.02

Their patron, Captain Errde Blackskull of the Stone Guard, pulled them aside before they departed and asked them to look into a disturbing rumor circulating through the city of Gracklstugh. She introduced her subordinate, Grimholl Forgebrand, who described finding bodies with no visible wounds: no sword cuts, no burns, no magical residue, nothing at all to explain why the victims were dead. The clerics could not revive them. Errde was deeply skeptical of the legend behind the killings, a supposed order of psionic assassins known as the Empty-Scabbard Killers or the Forak-Erach-Naek. Still, she admitted that two members of her own clan had died under these same mysterious circumstances, and that she herself had found one of the bodies. She believed the myth was being weaponized by corrupt forces within the city to cover their tracks. She pointed the party toward the Whorlstone Tunnels beneath Gracklstugh as a possible hiding place for whoever, or whatever, was responsible.

### Ambush at the Pier 014.04

They never made it far. At the end of a pier on the Darklake Docks, they spotted a Duergar merchant standing alone. Two figures suddenly shimmered into visibility beside him: masked, hooded assassins who drove glowing blades into the merchant with vicious precision. Thorin immediately coveted the glowing weapons. Without hesitation, the party charged, and dubbed themselves "Firefly Tactics" on the way in.

Gyrgum rushed forward and tried to shove one of the killers into the water, but the assassin held his ground. Daz unleashed a torrent of magical energy that tore into one of the attackers for nine damage. Zalthir conjured a sphere of impenetrable magical darkness over the pier, blinding the assassins and leveling the playing field. His first strike missed on a natural 1, but he spent a focus point to strike again and killed one assassin outright. Within the darkness:
- Thorin salvaged a miss with a Precision Attack and landed a precise blow for six damage.
- Gyrgum channeled radiant holy energy through his mace.
- Daz hurled three searing rays of fire into the black void.

When the darkness lifted, both assassins lay dead. The glowing blades they had wielded had vanished entirely, dissolving back into the psychic energy from which they had been conjured, much to the party's collective disappointment.

Gyrgum knelt beside the wounded merchant and spoke a quick prayer, closing the man's wounds. Thorin cautioned that the party had gone in "guns blazing" without knowing who the villain really was. The merchant, Werz, claimed to have no idea why he had been targeted. He thanked them profusely and invited them to meet him at the Shattered Spire to receive a proper reward.

### Aftermath at the Pier 014.05

Before leaving the scene, the party searched the bodies and found eighteen silver pieces, a trinket, a pair of bloodstained gloves, and, somewhat absurdly, a box of scented candles. More importantly, they discovered a fish-skin parchment bearing a charcoal likeness of Werz himself, confirming that the attack had been a deliberate, pre-planned assassination. Gyrgum shoved the corpses off the pier into the dark water below, and the party slipped away before the city guard could arrive.

After a brief rest to recover their strength, during which Daz used Arcane Recovery to regain a second-level spell slot, the party was escorted by Duergar priests through the stone corridors of the Flowstone District toward the territory of the Keepers of the Flame. They traveled on the strength of their permit through a district inhabited by no one but Duergar.

### Audience with the Wyrmsmith 014.06

The ground trembled before they arrived, and a thunderous voice rolled through the cavern as a massive red dragon came into view. His scales were the color of cooling lava, and his bright yellow eyes gleamed in the shadows. He announced himself with the joke, "You didn't say I was having surfacers for dinner tonight." The priests introduced him with great reverence as Themberchaud, the Wyrmsmith of Gracklstugh, Father of Flame. He was enormous, and clearly well-fed, carrying the unmistakable look of a predator who had not hunted in a very long time.

Themberchaud dismissed the priests, who were visibly uncomfortable at leaving him alone with strangers, and led the party into his private lair. There he threatened to turn anyone invisible to ash, thrashed his wings about, and breathed fire in every direction before settling onto a mountain of gold. He then interrogated each member of the party in turn:
- He dismissed Gyrgum's devotion to Bahamut with a curt "Next!"
- He expressed mild disdain for Zalthir's brass dragonborn heritage.
- He showed a flicker of approval for Daz's Drow origins, from Menzoberranzan, and his capacity for secrecy.
- When Thorin explained that he had simply been minding his own business before waking up in a jail cell surrounded by Drow and strangers, the dragon seemed almost amused. He was delighted that the party had "nobody to be blackmailed."

Without waiting for the party to formally agree, Themberchaud declared them his personal agents, summoned his liaison Gartokkar, and presented each of them with a golden badge bearing his own profile.

### The Keepers' Secret Mission 014.07

Gartokkar then drew the party aside and revealed the true political landscape of Gracklstugh. The Keepers' power derived "in equal measure from our psionic abilities, the leadership of the Deepking, and our influence over Themberchaud." The dragon had no idea that they were already planning to replace him. A red dragon egg, intended to hatch his successor, had been stolen by a shadowy organization known as the Gray Ghosts, and the Keepers had been waging a quiet war against the Thieves Guild ever since.

When Thorin revealed that Themberchaud wanted the party to report directly to him, Gartokkar was grateful for the tip. He insisted that the old dragon would not be disposed of but "set free." Gartokkar also described a great psionic disturbance rippling through the Underdark, a sensation like a hole being torn through the world, which he believed the Gray Ghosts were responsible for. The party's lore checks failed to turn up any further clue about it.

He tasked the party with tracking down a derro messenger named Droki, who carried messages and supplies for the Gray Ghosts, and with recovering whatever powerful magic had caused the disturbance. To sweeten the arrangement, he gave the party a holy symbol of Laduguer and gold pins bearing Themberchaud's profile, granting them passage beyond the Darklake District. He made clear, with a thin smile, that refusal would result in the Stone Guards inventing an appropriate crime to charge them with.

The party accepted the mission, though not without pushing back. Thorin provocatively suggested that perhaps the Stone Guards ought to know about the stolen egg, sending Gartokkar into barely concealed alarm. When pressed on whether they would return the egg to the Keepers once found, Thorin simply replied that they would decide what to do with it when the time came. That answer left Gartokkar calling them "funny" through gritted teeth, and led to a brief exchange about everyone's height.

The party now found themselves operating on three fronts at once:
- as agents of the dragon who didn't know he was being replaced,
- as servants of the faction that was replacing him,
- and as investigators for a Stone Guard captain who was hunting the same derro they had just been sent to find.

Thorin was already floating the idea of escaping "in a blaze of glory on the back of a dragon." Whatever came next, it was clear that Gracklstugh's web of secrets was only going to grow more tangled.
