# Chapter 028

### Travel Through the Faerzress 028.01

The party pressed deeper into the Underdark, passing through a region thick with Faerzress, the strange magical radiation that warps the tunnels and makes teleportation all but impossible. The GM reminded the table that Faerzress is also the thing that "went blink and caused everybody to go mad." The party's working hypothesis is that the blink somehow caused the demons to show up. Scattered through the area were fist-sized chunks of quartz that shed a dim light in a 10-foot radius. Sarith explained that these crystals, when hurled against a hard surface, burst into a blinding flash in a 10-foot radius. Any creature caught in the flash must make a DC 10 Constitution save or be blinded for a minute, repeating the save at the end of each of its turns. Gyrgum concluded that you should throw it at least 10 feet, and Sarith agreed. Daz rolled a 19 on Arcana and recalled Zalthir's earlier explanation: magical darkness blocks all light, even magical light. The party set about harvesting what they could, pocketing twelve of the glowing stones before turning their attention to the rest.

### Crystals and Cover-ups 028.02

Eldeth warned that the evidence of their harvest would be plain to any Drow tracker worth their salt, so Zalthir devised a clever solution. Under the cover of his shroud of magical darkness, the party smashed the remaining crystals. They scattered destruction across the area so it would look like a battle had taken place rather than a careful harvest. Eldeth then used her considerable skill to mask the party's tracks, and by the time she was done, the Drow pursuers who had been closing in were left with little more than confusion. Even before the harvest, while Sarith was still explaining the crystals, the party had come across two wretched figures huddled in the dark: a man and a woman in tattered clothes, hollow-eyed with hunger and fear. Daz rolled a 22 on Investigation and spotted the telltale symbols marking them as former prisoners (slaves) of the Drow. The man, Fuurm, raised a glowing crystal like a weapon the moment he spotted Daz, a drow, his voice cracking as he declared that he and his companion, Baedora, were not going back.

#### The party decides to harvest magical crystals while attempting to hide their trail from Drow pursuers.
- The party discusses the risks of harvesting quartz crystals, which Eldeth warns could lead Drow pursuers to their location.
- Gyrgum is eager ("they sound like a great prank"). Eldeth explains that cutting the crystals out leaves obvious gaps showing someone was harvesting, and that it will cost them "because the mechanics said so."
- Zalthir suggests a plan to gather some crystals and smash others to make the area look like a battlefield rather than a harvest site.
- Eldeth concedes that smashing crystals left, right, and center "would be something completely different." The GM calls it "a clever workaround through the mechanical details."
- Gyrgum points out that smashing the crystals would blind them, and the idea of using Zalthir's darkness follows.
- The party strategically smashes the remaining crystals under the cover of magical darkness to avoid being blinded by the resulting flashes.
- The GM assumes no one accidentally hits anyone else, and Gyrgum is allowed to join the smashing.
- Eldeth successfully uses her survival skills to mask the party's tracks, significantly slowing the Drow pursuit.
- The party collects twelve quartz crystals in total, which the GM tells them to just call "light crystals."

### The Shrieking Cavern 028.03

Daz approached them carefully, introducing himself without giving too much away. He explained that the party had no interest in returning anyone to the Drow, since they were trying to escape the Underdark themselves. Fuurm quickly offered that "we could be of some help," and a 24 on Insight confirmed the pair were simply desperate to get out, not plants. Fuurm's story came out haltingly over a shared meal. He had followed his sister into the depths after the Drow stole her from the Overbright, only to be captured almost immediately. He spent a long and miserable stretch as a Drow slave until a demonic attack on his captors gave him the chance to flee. The GM described him as "just a farmer... with a perverse sense of loyalty who made some terrible life choices," and the fact that he had survived at all struck the party as remarkable. Daz's player joked that he might have been selectively bred for luck, like the puppeteers of Niven's *Ringworld*. The party agreed to let Fuurm and Baedora travel with them, reasoning that two more sets of eyes cost little and that the pair seemed genuinely desperate rather than dangerous.

Days of travel followed. The first break came in a tense passage through a fungus cavern, where shriekers wailed at the party's approach and threatened to bring every predator in the Underdark down on their heads. A crystal hurled at the shriekers from 10 feet away did nothing to stop the noise, but Eldeth made the DC 16 Survival check that covered their passage. The second came at a later field of crystal clusters, where the party stopped to search for more, and Eldeth once again covered their trail. By journey's end she was fairly confident she had almost shaken the Drow, and the party's pursuit level stood at one. Then, after what felt like an eternity of winding tunnels and oppressive dark, Sarith announced that they had arrived.

- Several uneventful days follow. On about the sixth day the party passes another crystal cluster with no one around, and Daz decides "Might as well search for a little bit more."
- Eldeth is fairly confident she has almost shaken the Drow off their trail but isn't certain, and the pursuit level is now one.

### Arrival at the Lost Tomb of Khaem 028.04

Before them stood the entrance to the Lost Tomb of Khaem: a set of heavy double doors, beyond which stood a stone diorama depicting the Netherese sorcerer Brysis of Khaem in her prime, surrounded by attendants and slaves and all the trappings of an empire long since turned to dust. Just before they reached the doors, Jimjar felt the irresistible pull of his gambling instincts and demanded a bet. Gyrgum obliged, wagering that the crystals would come in handy after all, and a startled Jimjar took it. Jimjar then tried, without a single taker, to bet that no one would ever agree whether the crystals had been useful.

A soft, feminine voice drifted into their minds the moment they drew close. It was faint and distant, pleading for help and begging to be freed from the dark where she had been trapped "for so long. So very long." The voice gave them a sense of the tomb's entrance, which lay in the direction they were already headed. The party pushed through the double doors, where a 24 on Arcana confirmed this was truly Brysis's Netherese tomb and that powerful magic lay within. The GM added a reminder that Netherese notions of good and evil may not map to the party's own. From there they descended a long staircase that plunged over a hundred feet into the earth, passing a Netherese calendar stone set into the wall along the way. Gyrgum took the lead, since, as the GM put it, the magic user Daz was "more fungible in a disaster."

At the bottom, they found a shrine with shredded tapestries and friezes gouged beyond recognition, and a cracked marble altar that had clearly been the target of recent violence. The party noted how oddly intact everything was after thousands of years. Gyrgum studied the damage carefully and concluded that the culprits were floating undead, creatures that left no tracks in the thick layer of dust on the floor. He remembered from his days with Bahamut that the undead don't rest but are driven to keep searching, and he suggested they tend to hate things they were associated with in life. The party also recalled Fargas Rumblefoot's tip that the tomb held a secret, false tomb.

### The Sarcophagi Chamber and the Specters 028.05

The party pressed east through a side door, a direction chosen by die roll in Thorin's honour, and entered a chamber dominated by four stone sarcophagi, each bearing the sculpted likeness of a robed figure in repose. Searching the room, they noticed that the northeast sarcophagus rested on hidden stone rollers. The hangers-on stayed outside ("You go there. We're not doing this"). Zalthir drew his shroud of magical darkness across the room and Thorin rolled the sarcophagus aside. Four specters tore free from the sarcophagi and launched themselves at the party, while the distant voice cried, "No, you must save me."

Zalthir moved with terrifying speed, striking one of the spirits down in a flurry of blows before it could react. Daz unleashed a volley of magical force at the remaining specters, but the specters struck him twice with life-draining touches. His hit-point maximum fell by 2, then by 10 more, leaving him badly weakened. Gyrgum raised his holy symbol and called upon Bahamut's power to drive the undead back, but every remaining specter shrugged off the divine energy. That left Gyrgum to wonder, not for the first time, whether the god was still displeased about the dragon egg. Thorin stepped in with his enchanted longsword and cut down another specter, and Zalthir finished the rest with a relentless series of strikes. One of them paused mid-fight to correct Zalthir that it was a specter, not a ghost.

With the specters destroyed, the party looted the sarcophagi and found a respectable haul: gold bracelets, a fancy walking stick, and a gold censer worked with platinum filigree. Daz then rolled a 27 on Arcana and made a grim assessment: these kinds of undead did not stay dead. They would reform every 24 hours, and the party could not afford to linger. Beneath the sarcophagus Thorin had rolled aside lay a four-foot square hole dropping down into the darkness below.

### The Wraith of the Lower Chamber 028.06

The party scrambled down into a lower chamber whose walls were decorated with rich pigments and inlays of semi-precious stone. A gilded sarcophagus rested atop a stone bier along one wall, with an invisible stone chest sitting silently at its foot. The moment they entered, a wraith materialized before them, cackling with theatrical menace and declaring that their deaths would free her from her prison and that her victims would serve her even in death. Daz moved to hurl a fireball at the creature, then recalled (Arcana 17), with a sinking feeling, that she was resistant to fire, and that fire was essentially the foundation of his entire magical repertoire. He briefly considered binding her with Maximilian's Earthen Grasp. He then pivoted to a third-level magic missile, five darts of pure force for 20 damage, and the wraith reeled. She screamed something incoherent about dwarves and Drow defeating her once before, lashed out at Thorin with a life-draining touch that missed entirely, and then slipped into her gilded sarcophagus to hide, forcing the party to spend an action to get at her.

Zalthir shifted his shroud of darkness into the lower chamber, knowing it would cost Daz his line of sight for Magic Missile. He then spent his action to open the gilded sarcophagus. As he did, the mysterious feminine voice that had first called out to them at the tomb's entrance spoke again, this time from within the sarcophagus itself: "in the sarcophagus, I can help you." Inside, resting atop a withered, mummified corpse surrounded by treasure, lay a magical sword. The wraith, free to slip in and out of her sarcophagus, had yet to reappear, the invisible chest sat waiting to be opened, and the voice of something ancient and bound offered its aid from the very heart of the tomb. Whatever Brysis of Khaem had left behind in this place, the party was only beginning to uncover it.
