### A Sword with a Soul 031.01

When Thorin's player returned, Thorin examined a legendary weapon unlike any other — Dawnbringer, a gilded longsword that could conjure a blade of pure radiance from its hilt. The sword was no mere tool, however. It was sentient, possessed of a kind feminine voice, and deeply scarred by millennia spent trapped in the dark. It feared the darkness with a desperate, anxious intensity, and it feared being left alone even more. Thorin and Dawnbringer came to an uneasy understanding, a kind of arranged partnership neither had asked for, but both seemed willing to honor. Dawnbringer was a plus-two weapon that inflicted radiant rather than slashing damage, dealt additional damage to undead, and could cast lesser restoration once each day. Its light was genuine sunlight — a tremendous advantage against the light-sensitive creatures of the Underdark — but Zalthir's magical darkness could still extinguish it. If Thorin tried to carry the sword into that darkness, Dawnbringer might panic and attempt to take control of him. Thorin, whose charisma was notably poor, accepted the risk: “I mean, no, we're bonded at this point.”

### Tour of the Circles 031.02

Basidia, the myconid sovereign who had been guiding the party through the Neverlight Grove, led them on a tour of the colony's various circles. They then met Hebopbe, the obsessive leader of the Circle of Growers, who proudly explained that myconids were herbivores who cultivated specific fungi for food — a distinction that left Gyrgum visibly confused before Thorin compared the arrangement to a mammal eating other mammals. Hebopbe took offense at the common outsider belief that myconids were merely ordinary mushrooms, explaining that they were a sentient species with their own varied tastes, cooking styles, and agricultural practices.

Hebopbe was indifferent to the political upheaval caused by the sovereign Phylo, viewing it as a concern for leaders rather than farmers. He understood Phylo's proposal as an organizational restructuring: specialized circles would concentrate on their own work while a smaller group of leaders coordinated the colony. Hebopbe even saw advantages in this arrangement, since it would allow growers to discuss crop yields and favored fungi without boring the rest of the colony. He considered Basidia's fears exaggerated and wondered whether Phylo might genuinely be leading myconid-kind toward a better day. What Basidia quietly revealed, however, was that Hebopbe had already been infected by the strange evil spores spreading through the colony. The party confirmed that the cultivated food itself appeared clean; it was the farmer, not the food, that had been infected.

Next, the party visited the circle mound where new myconids were spored and grown. Basidia showed them newly spored myconids only a few inches tall, not yet aware but soon to mature into myconid sprouts. The mound's leader, Yrberop, was visibly infected and swayed to discordant internal music that no one else could hear — a chaotic, painful cacophony that echoed faintly through Basidia's rapport spores into the minds of the party. Yrberop welcomed them and asked whether they had come to see the young myconids who would join the “great plan” and spread the happiness offered by the great Zuggtmoy. He spoke excitedly of sending myconids into the Overbright, something the party knew should be fatal or deeply unnatural for them.

Thorin, drawing on a dark memory of his own time spent in the presence of something deeply wrong, felt certain that the noise and the evil he had encountered before were connected. Soon afterward, Yrberop lost interest in the conversation and began crooning a lullaby to the newly spored myconids. Thorin and the others who could make it out heard the song praise Zuggtmoy, her coming wedding to a “great body,” and the joy that would supposedly spread throughout the world above and below. Phylo had previously described Zuggtmoy in reverent, specifically feminine terms and claimed that she could answer any question, but refused to explain what she was: “One cannot be told what the great Zuggtmoy is. One must experience it firsthand.” The party increasingly suspected that the promised visit to the Garden of Welcome was intended to make them sacrifices to a demon.

Their final stop before the Garden of Welcome was the Circle of Explorers, where they met Rumpadump, a nervous and energetic myconid scout who advocated passionately for a nomadic way of life. He described the explorers as the colony's scouts, patrols, pathfinders, and visionaries beyond the boundaries of Neverlight Grove. Rumpadump trusted only Basidia and, by extension, the party, and he made no secret of his fear that something terrible was unfolding in the grove. Every time he returned from an expedition, he found the colony changed further and for the worse. He had already prepared escape routes and hidden nutrient caches in the exit tunnels in case Basidia chose to abandon the grove and resume a nomadic existence.

Rumpadump dismissed the idea of moving into the Overbright as “completely insane and perverse,” insisting that myconids belonged in the darkness of the Underdark. It was Rumpadump who offered the party a way into the Garden of Welcome through a secret passageway, allowing them to investigate without waiting for Phylo's guided tour in the morning. Meanwhile, their companion Jimjar had been behaving strangely — hinting at a coming wedding and, most tellingly, placing no bets at all. Basidia confirmed that he was not infected. When challenged, Jimjar returned briefly to form by offering to bet that he had never been infected by spores, but he gave no clear explanation for his knowledge beyond observing that weddings always made someone unhappy.

### Horrors of the Garden of Welcome 031.03

The Garden of Welcome was a nightmare made real. The plateau rose high above the rest of the grove, screened by towering zurkhwood stalks, and from within came a chorus of muffled moans and cries. As the party climbed, the muttering grew into “a symphony of moans and cries and hisses.” Only a few glowing mushrooms illuminated the gloom. At the top, the party found the source: the heads of creatures from a dozen different Underdark races peeked from the soil, their bodies consumed by mold and sprouting mushrooms.

One voice rose above the rest — a drow woman named Xinaya, her face half-rotted and blooming with tiny fungi, who raised a spider medallion embedded in the fungal mass that had once been her hand. She begged the party in desperate, broken words to end her suffering and warned that Zuggtmoy, the Lady of Decay, was present. Xinaya recognized the party and laughed hysterically at the reversal of fortune: she had been sent by Ilvara to search for and capture them near the grove, only to find herself begging those same fugitives for salvation. She and her patrol had made the fatal mistake of accepting Phylo's invitation to visit the Garden of Welcome. She urged Daz, whom she recognized as being from Menzoberranzan, to warn the other Drow that the Demon Queen of Fungi had entered the Underdark.

Before Xinaya could say more, a disgusting larval creature rose from the foul ground — Yestabrod, whose vestigial fungal growths hinted that it might once have been a myconid. It welcomed the party to a wedding rehearsal in a gurgling voice heard both aloud and inside their minds. It did not care whether they were friends of the bride or the intended; all would be embraced by the “great cedar” and made one with her chosen “great body.” Two adult myconids and drow spore servants from Xinaya's former patrol emerged to join it. When the party attempted to fall back on their familiar talk of performing an audit, Yestabrod rejected the ploy: there would be no audit, only incorporation into the “coworkers of pain.”

### Battle in the Garden of Welcome 031.04

The battle that followed was brutal and chaotic. Dawnbringer cast genuine sunlight in a fifteen-foot radius, hampering the light-sensitive myconids. One adult myconid opened by stunning Jimjar with a puff of spores. Gyrgum moved in close and channeled devastating necrotic energy directly into Yestabrod, accepting the danger of touching such a corrupted creature. Yestabrod immediately demonstrated its unnatural resilience: a nearby corpse exploded, and the creature absorbed the resulting remains to heal itself.

Daz unleashed a roaring fireball that badly burned three clustered creatures, though they survived and the combat continued. A player joked that the blast had made the spores crispy, but the GM clarified that the spores had already been emitted. Yestabrod answered with another corpse eruption, showering the battlefield with gore, offal, and acid. It then moved among the party and released a thick cloud of spores. Daz and Zalthir succumbed and were each afflicted with a secret madness, the nature of which was communicated privately to their players. Daz treated the affliction as a hidden alteration to his roleplay, while Zalthir was warned that his worldview had suddenly shifted. The others knew only that the madness could be cured if its victims first recognized that something was wrong.

Most devastating of all was the fate of Sarith, a companion who had traveled with the party for their entire journey through the Underdark. In the midst of the battle, his head exploded and he rose as a mindless drow spore servant. Eldeth and Jimjar were forced to cut him down almost immediately. Thorin, wielding Dawnbringer and flooding the garden with sunlight, was briefly overwhelmed by a stunning cloud of spores and remained incapacitated until the battle was nearly over. Zalthir ultimately ended Yestabrod with a relentless flurry of strikes, preventing it from detonating any more corpses. Gyrgum withered another myconid with a second necrotic touch, Daz precisely divided bolts of magical force between two badly wounded enemies, and Zalthir stepped into Dawnbringer's sunlight to destroy the final myconid attacker.

### The Fate of Xinaya 031.05

In the aftermath, Zalthir retrieved the spider medallion from Xinaya's bloated, fungal hand while she was still alive. The dying drow warned the party that those infected by the spores had only days to find a cure before sharing her fate. Gyrgum considered lesser restoration and even revivify, but she was too far gone; only magic on the scale of a wish might have restored her. Thorin consulted quietly with Dawnbringer, and together they granted her the merciful death she had begged for. Thorin described his bond with the blade as a marriage he had not requested but intended to honor: “We're a partnership now.”

### The Wedding Procession of Zuggtmoy 031.06

Beyond the garden, a smaller cavern pulsed with a discordant, wheezing melody as the remaining victims in the soil began to groan and croak in horrible unison. Thorin recognized the same musical quality he had sensed in Yrberop's mind, confirming the connection between the infected myconids and whatever waited ahead. Through the mist, the party saw a parade of vaguely humanoid creatures, their decaying flesh covered in luminescent lichen and tumescent growths, their voices stabbing into the party's minds with equal parts madness and glee.

A chant rose from the procession — a wedding hymn celebrating the union of Zuggtmoy and Araumycos, promising that her joyous spores would spread to all the world below and above:

> “From rocky bed the toad stills rose, from chaos dark, her love she shows. Wish, yearn, laugh. The weighty lady will be wed. Crave, hunger, dance. Her joyous spores will spread. Araumycos and Zuggtmoy joined together heart to heart, becoming one till death do part. Hail, hail.”

And then, leading the procession, came Zuggtmoy herself — the Demon Queen of Fungi, the Lady of Decay, and the same demon lord Thorin had encountered before — walking toward the party with her bridesmaids as the moans of her garden's victims swelled around her.
