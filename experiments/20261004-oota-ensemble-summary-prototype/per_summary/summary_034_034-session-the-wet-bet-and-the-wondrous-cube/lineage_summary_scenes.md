### Jimjar's Navigational Error and a Bet 034.01

Jimjar, the deep gnome guide, found himself defending his navigational choices as the party emerged from yet another unexpected detour through the Underdark. He insisted that the mistake was not intentional, that shifting passages were to be expected, and that finding an exit through their current location was remarkably convenient—perhaps even a shortcut to their destination of Blingdenstone. Thorin immediately predicted, “Yeah, this will end in tears.” When Jimjar offered a wager, Thorin carefully clarified the terms: “By end in tears, what I mean is not literally that somebody will cry, but rather there will be some unfortunate event before we get back on our way.” With the definition settled, the bet was accepted.

### The Flooding Passage and the First Gray Ooze 034.02

At the first intersection, Thorin left their course to chance. “So I rolled a D2, otherwise known as flip a coin, and it came up one. So I say we go left.” The chosen route led into a 10-foot-high passage whose walls glistened with dark, dripping water. Thorin initially refused to step into the pool because “creepy crawlies will come out and get me,” but his examination revealed an even greater danger: the water was rising at an alarming rate. Daz investigated further and confirmed that the entire complex was leaking. A tremor had cracked an underground reservoir or river into the cave system, and unless the party found a way out, the temple would eventually fill completely.

The discovery prompted Jimjar to appreciate the poetic possibilities of the wager: “What an appropriate bet. Tears, water.” Thorin argued that the square, constructed chamber beyond the pool was more likely to offer an exit than the natural cave passage, and the party rushed forward. As Thorin led them through the water, a gray ooze emerged exactly as he had predicted. Jimjar admitted, “I really wished I had lost this bet, Thorin,” while Thorin observed, “Okay, so not only is the water, but there’s also something creepy crawly in the water, as I predicted.” Zalthir stepped forward and destroyed the creature with two powerful unarmed blows before anyone else could act.

### Meeting Glabbagool, the Sentient Gelatinous Cube 034.03

Farther along, the party discovered the remains of a drow, a dark metal mace, and scattered coins apparently hovering above the floor. The objects were suspended inside a gelatinous cube with two visible eyes. Rather than attacking, the cube greeted them: “Well, hello, hello, hello.” He introduced himself as Glabbagool and explained that until recently he had been “an average garden variety gelatinous cube,” moving around, cleaning floors, and decomposing whatever adventurers left behind. Now, inexplicably, he could think and see.

Glabbagool was fascinated by the implications of sentience. He did not understand whether everyone experienced awareness in the same way and readily admitted that he knew little about the world. He also had no idea which route led upward, having spent much of his brief sentient life standing in place and deciding where to go. Thorin invited him to join the expedition, promising companionship, adventure, and a reliable supply of food. Glabbagool accepted, though he politely asked, “You won’t be upset if you all die and I eat you, right?” The party agreed that once they were dead they would probably be past caring, provided Glabbagool allowed natural causes to do the killing.

As a welcoming gift, Gyrgum tossed Glabbagool a lucky foot that he had been carrying in his backpack. The cube absorbed it and declared it tasty. Glabbagool also offered a standing service to the party: “If you ever decide you want to get rid of something, just drop it on the floor and I’ll be happy to eat it.”

### Second Gray Ooze Encounter 034.04

The enlarged company continued into another water-filled passage. Thorin, expecting more oozes, threw darts into the pools before approaching. His caution was justified, but Glabbagool’s ten-foot-by-ten-foot-by-ten-foot body completely blocked the narrow corridor behind him, separating Thorin from the rest of the party. Thorin struck a gray ooze twice with darts and withdrew before it could reach his armor. Daz then cast Toll the Dead, inflicting 18 points of necrotic damage and destroying it. Glabbagool drew the obvious tactical lesson: “Perhaps I should be in the back.”

The party and Jimjar briefly reconsidered their bet. Jimjar asked whether the “tears” wager might be declared a mulligan because they had met such a charming and humorous friend. Thorin was willing to call it a draw on account of Glabbagool’s company, although the temple continued filling “sort of like a bathtub that doesn’t drain well.”

### The Oozing Temple Fountain Room 034.06

Continuing more carefully, the party reached a door leading into a temple chamber. Inside stood a stone fountain filled with shallow, brackish water. The rubble of a broken statue lay atop a central pedestal, with only a pair of clawed stone feet still recognizable. Seven niches lined the walls: one admitted water through a crack, the two beside it were empty, and four contained strange, formless sculptures. Skeletons also lay in the chamber, prompting the party to consider removing their weapons as a precaution against animation.

Worried that the door might trap them and prevent another tactical retreat, the party accepted Glabbagool’s suggestion that he scout. “Well, you know, the oozes can’t go through me,” he reasoned. Thorin told him, “We would sing your praises at the tavern once we get out of the Underdark,” while the others pointed out that his newly acquired lucky foot and the prospect of future food might assist him.

Glabbagool entered and asked what he should inspect. When he touched one of the formless sculptures, it materialized into a gray ooze. “Well, that’s rude,” he complained. Zalthir rushed in and punched the ooze apart. The party then chose the cautious approach of activating the remaining sculptures one at a time. Zalthir rapidly destroyed each creature as it appeared, while Glabbagool methodically absorbed the debris until the floor was perfectly clean.

Glabbagool next searched the fountain and discovered a hidden cache of treasure: 112 silver pieces, three gold pieces, a drow dagger, and a vial of oil of slipperiness. Unfortunately, he absorbed all of it. The party concluded that they would have to wait several days for the valuables to pass through him, joking that Glabbagool had become “a cube of holding” or a convenience-store time safe.

### Rising Waters in the Eastern Passageway 034.07

The fountain chamber proved not to be the expected exit, so the party retraced its route and explored the eastern passage. Near a rough wall they heard pouring water. Streams entered through cracks in the 10-foot-high ceiling, and the party estimated that the water was rising by roughly one foot per hour. Thorin’s dwarven knowledge suggested that the cracks were worth investigating. Jimjar bet that a massive quantity of water waited behind the stone, causing Thorin to remind him that nobody had accepted that particular wager.

Eldeth examined the site and recognized the signs of an underground river breaking through the rock. She had heard stories of people escaping such disasters by breaking through the stone and swimming through the resulting passage. She also supplied the grim counterpoint: “I didn’t hear about all the people who did that and drowned anymore.” The party compared the logic to hearing only about bombers that returned from battle.

### Escape from the Flooding Complex 034.08

With no obvious mundane exit remaining, the group debated potions, spells, ice barriers, and air bubbles. Thorin mentioned his potion of spider climb, but the chamber’s ten-foot ceiling offered little advantage. Daz proposed a more promising use of Shape Water: redirect the water inside five-foot cubes to create pockets containing no water. By maintaining two effects at once, Daz could create a moving, ten-foot-long refuge. The party would crowd into one pocket, create another ahead of it, and advance through the flooding passage in stages.

The magical bubbles would give the party time to survive the initial flood while Thorin prepared to use his considerable strength to break through the stone. Glabbagool could not fit inside the cramped air pockets, but this did not concern him. “Oh, that’s totally fine. I don’t breathe.” He also cheerfully promised, “If any of you were to perish, I will absolutely grab you and digest you.” After negotiation, he agreed to wait long enough for the surviving party members to attempt a resurrection.

Glabbagool caused more of the ceiling to collapse, doubling the volume of water entering the complex. The breach nevertheless revealed the diverted underground river that was feeding the flood. Protected by Daz’s progression of air pockets, the party endured the rising water and then swam upward for approximately thirty feet. They broke the surface in a large cavern and resumed their journey.

Jimjar immediately declared, “See, it did not end in tears.” Thorin maintained that there had unquestionably been shenanigans, but agreed to settle the wager by promising Jimjar ten of the silver pieces once they emerged from Glabbagool, even if they were “a little slimy.” Jimjar then reframed the entire detour as a convenient shortcut to Blingdenstone: “Just so we’re on the same page, I did not get us lost. That’s the important thing is that I found us a shortcut, right?” The party conceded that either Jimjar had found the shortcut or the shortcut had found him—and, more importantly, they had gained a friend. Their successful escape also brought them to level 6.
