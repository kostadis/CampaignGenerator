## Brewbarry — A Banker's Revelation

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: the party has just come out of the Spire of the Morninglord after giving the Necklace of Fireballs, Lady Alagondar's heirloom, to Perrin Alagondar, and Cullen Sharpe and House Margaster continue to grate on their nerves.]

"That's dicks," Soma says.

She is right. I do not say anything. The necklace is gone. That part is done.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: as the party is leaving, Aurelan Vance comes hurrying back from the counting house, out of breath.]

I know him before he reaches us. The coat. The way he holds his hands. Soma squints at him.

"Which one was that guy?"

"My banker friend," I tell her.

Soma looks at me the way she looks at a fish that has come up on the line the wrong way round. "Friend. The list of people Brewbarry has made friends with is very large."

"I was just making friends with him. He is doing me a favor. I will do him one."

That is how friends work. I do not see the problem.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Aurelan arrives huffing and excited, pats himself down and straightens his clothing into proper order before he speaks.]

"A greeting," I say. "Hello, my friend."

"Yes, Mr. Brewbarry, I—" He stops. "Is it Mr. Brewbarry? Is that the correct appellation? I'm sorry, I'm not familiar with Guardian norms."

Nobody has ever asked me that. Nobody in the tribe. Nobody since.

"Just call me Brew. That's fine."

"Brew!"

Vukradin turns his whole body toward me. "You're going by Brew now?"

"Yes. Well. Yeah. It's a new thing."

"Don't like you, Brewbarry," Soma says, flat, which is how she says the other thing.

"Only to his friends," says Valphine. She does not smile. She is watching the banker's hands, not mine.

"Brew," Vukradin says again, trying it. "Brew."

"To my banker friends," I say.

Aurelan bows a little. "And since we're on such friendly terms — my friends call me Ori."

"Whoa."

I did not expect that. A name is a thing you give. He gave me one. I put it somewhere I will not lose it.

"Thank you, Ori. So. Speak your piece."

"Alright. Well. You asked me to find out who the gnome is."

"Yes, I did."

"And after the performance I was thinking I would wait for you to come by the bank. But then I thought to myself: Valphine's sermon was inspiring."

"Oh, was it ever," Soma says.

"There was, yeah," I say. I remember the light. I remember people's faces.

"It was," Ori says. "And it inspired me to action."

"Great. Glad to hear it."

"Yes. Well. Last night I decided to go over some of the paperwork related to Bimble, and here's what I—"

"Enough," says Vukradin, holding up one hand like a conductor stopping the horns. "We wrote the name down. It says here, Bimble." He looks at the paper. He looks up, delighted. "Bimbo the Gnome!"

"Bimble," Ori says.

"Alright, come on," I say. "This isn't a spelling bee. Get on with it."

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Aurelan pulls out a piece of paper and shows them the correct spelling, B-I-M-B-L-E.]

"Bimble, okay," I say. I can see the letters. I do not think the letters will help my friends.

"Pronouncing the name, that's fine," Vukradin says, generous now. "Maybe he has a speech impediment."

Ori lets it go. He has the paper in both hands and the paper is what he cares about. When he talks about it his voice changes. It goes steady. This is the thing he is good at.

"The Commission carried him on maintenance roll for eleven years."

"Eleven years," Vukradin says.

"Yes. The payments stop seven tendays ago — on the day, not the tenday. No severance. No final reconciliation. No forwarding instruction." He looks straight at me. He is angry, and it is a small clean anger, the kind you keep in a drawer. "That is not how a contract ends, Mr. Brew— Brew. That is how a contract is abandoned!"

He says abandoned like I would say it about a child left on the ice. I understand him then. This is his war. Paper that stops on the day.

"And because this is the only kind of question I am any good at," he goes on, "he has drawn nothing since. He has not banked. He has not borrowed. And he has not left. Your Harper friends would tell you the same, if you had any. So somebody is keeping him. Find who feeds a man, and you have found the man."

I think about a man who stops being paid on the day. In my tribe when the drums stop for someone, it is not because they moved to another camp.

"Sounds like he's feeding worms," I say. Then I look at Ori's face, and the paper, and the hope he has carried across the city out of breath. "But we'll get to the bottom of it, my friend. Right. Thank you."

"Well, you're very welcome. If you could find who is feeding him, I am certain you will find him."

"Who's feeding Bimbo," Vukradin says, tasting it. "Who is feeding Bimbo?"

"Bimble," Ori says. "Bimble."

"Bimbo," I say. I did not mean to. It is easier.

Vukradin has already gone somewhere else. "Mmm — what should the name of this quest be? Chase—"

"—the bimbo," I finish.

"Oh, boy," Vukradin says. "I could go pretty dirty on this one."

"I'm sorry," says Valphine, cool as a well. "Undercommon is my native language."

"I'm just a pervert," Soma says.

Ori looks between the four of us like a man who has handed a good sword to children. I clap him on the shoulder so he knows the sword is safe.

"How can we go about finding this guy?" I ask them. "This gnome."

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Soma remembers the Board Laid Bare, where she used to get the best turtle soup in the whole area — good prices, good food, good atmosphere — and that its proprietor Lim, who runs the small business, might know how a hidden man could be fed.]

"I mean, I've been dying for this soup," Soma says. Something in her shell settles when she says it. "Aww — when you're here, you're family?"

She thinks about it. Her hands go to her staff.

"Why'd she stop making it?" A pause. "Well, I guess we go to the Board Laid Bare, right?"

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Lim never stopped making the soup; Soma stopped going, having left on a two-day guiding trip about a month and a half ago.]

"Yeah, well," Soma says. "Now we're back. Let us find—"

She does not finish. She has already started walking. I fall in behind her. Ori stands by the Spire holding his paper, and I lift my hand to him, and he lifts his, and that is a friend.
