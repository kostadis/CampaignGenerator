## Vukradin — The Universal Basic Treasure Proclamation

Harbin Wester's office smells of ink and the particular sweat of a man who has just been asked to do something today.

"Call the Privy Council," I tell him. "A meeting. Now."

He looks at me the way Toblen looks at a customer who has ordered something not on the board. "So soon? So quickly? Right now?"

I have laid this out for him with care. The dragon is dead. Cryovain's hoard sits in our cart as one fused, ugly lump, and the people of Phandalin are walking past it on their way to work. There is a natural harmony in this situation that any reasonable mayor should hear, and I have given him the melody twice, slowly, in my warmest register. Nothing. So I try the other register, the one I dislike, the one that requires me to lean over his desk and lower my voice and let the light of my heritage come up a little in my eyes.

It does not go as well as it does on bandits.

He straightens his collar. "Your intimidation," he says, "lands about as flatly as your sweet tongue would have landed well."

Right. I feel the hubris rising in me like the second verse of a song I have not finished writing, and I decide to let it. "Fine. I'll gather the people myself."

---

"We could put on a rock concert," I tell the others outside, "get them all out on the green, get them happy and bouncing, and *then* I make the big speech."

Brewbarry nods as though this is the obvious order of operations. "I can make my voice boom to announce that there is a gathering."

"Do that. Decent performance outside — not as good as inside, the acoustics are terrible on grass — but they'll be drunker by the time I'm done, so they'll be easier to persuade." I look at the empty green. "I guess I perform on the green, right? That's where a man performs."

Brewbarry's voice goes out over the rooftops like a bell someone dropped. I tune up. Soma finds a bench with a good view of everything and sits on it as if she has been assigned to it.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: the townsfolk and the council gather on the green, including Harbin, Petunia, Big Al, Qelline Alderleaf, Linene Graywind, Ser Kaelen, Toblen and his bar team, Elsa, Barthen, and the woodworker.]

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: the crowd is dancing and relaxed, everybody feeling cool.]

This is what the studio would be. Not this exactly — walls, for a start, and a floor that does not have goats on it — but this: a crowd that came because they heard music, faces turned the same direction, the whole town breathing on the downbeat. I hold the last chord until it has nowhere left to go and let it go.

"Ladies and gentlemen! We are adventurers! We've killed the dragon! We drove out the orcs! We saved Phandalin! *Yay!*"

They yay. It is a good yay.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Brin and Giles are in the crowd, engaged, and sunburnt again.]

"Kissed by Lathander," Soma says from her bench, not loudly.

Valphine, standing near the edge with her hands folded, does not smile, which is how I know she heard.

---

"What I want to say," I tell them, "is that your government — led by Wester, over here—"

Harbin lifts a hand, half acknowledging, half hoping I will move on.

"—believes that the way adventurers should work is that they should go do heroic things, and then claim all the treasure for themselves."

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: everybody stares.]

Good. Stare. "That treasure came from you. That is *your* treasure. It was stolen from you, by orcs, by dragons, by bandits, by whoever, and it was sitting in a cave doing nothing for anyone. I want to give it back to you."

A hand goes up. Linene Graywind, of the Lionshield Coster, who has never in her life let a number pass without checking it.

"Well, strictly speaking," she says, "it may not have been stolen from *me*. It may have been stolen from somebody else, a long time ago. And so by you bringing it into the market — hold on — by you bringing it into the market, I get to… it recirculates." She frowns at the word as though it has just arrived. "Right? When you spend that money, you're making it mine. Because when it's over there in the hands of the monsters—"

"Hang on. All your questions will be answered."

A sharp woman I do not recognize, standing a little apart from the townsfolk with the posture of someone who has come from somewhere larger, says, "This has been very well covered in the dialectics of the financials." She says it the way Valphine says *mind flayers*: as a thing she is certain of and expects nobody else to be.

I do not know who she is. I move on.

"If we can find the rightful owner of that treasure, then it needs to be returned. That is not negotiable. That is the constitution of who I am. But we have found, as adventurers — and I will be honest with you, it has been a source of great personal frustration — that many times we cannot find the rightful owners. The dead do not leave forwarding addresses. And so I swear to you, now, here, on my own gifts—" and I feel them in my chest, the ones I was born with, the ones that turn a room "—a new, universal, basic *treasure*."

"UBT," Soma says, trying the shape of it.

There is a moment where I do not know which way it will go. I have been in enough rooms to know that moment. I let my words carry it.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: the crowd cheers enthusiastically, and Jenna looks appalled.]

Yes. *Yes.* I do not have the words for this, and I have a great many words.

---

I round on the mayor while the cheering is still going, because that is when you round on a mayor. "And I would like to know what Harbin Wester thinks about this plan. Is he in favor of it, or does he need to be replaced?"

Harbin's face does a great deal in a short time. "I am *so* glad! This was a — this was — I'm —" He spreads his arms toward me. "My good friend, and member of the Privy Council — V— Vu— *Vukradin!*"

He stutters on my name. He has known me since the road was safe enough to have a mayor on it.

"Sounds about right," I say.

"Because I think that is an *extraordinary* idea," he says, recovering by talking faster, "and that is why we have declared him extraordinary, and we have declared the income that he produces as part of his adventuring career — *Vukradin's UBT!* The Privy Council heard this proposal, and we were waiting a few more days before making a formal announcement, but since we — since — well, this is as good a way to make an announcement as any. All hail Vukradin, and the—" He stops. "What is the name of your adventuring group?"

It is my name on the thing. It is also his council that apparently thought of it, days ago, in a meeting I sit on and do not remember. I would like to be outraged. I look at the crowd, which is still cheering, and I find I cannot get the outrage to take. The people are getting their money. He can have the sentence.

"Did we ever make one?" Soma asks.

"Slayers of Cryovain."

"That's — we couldn't have started with that, there was no Cryovain to slay."

"We'll have to have a slight sidebar," I tell the crowd, and then, to my friends, "I don't know. What name do you want?"

"It'd be the band's name at this point," Soma says. "It's a band."

"Well — not yet. Soma's not in the band, so—"

"No, no. I'm just the tour guide." She adjusts her staff against the bench. "I'm your manager at this point."

"Valphine and Vukradin's — death metal —"

Valphine tilts her head one degree, which is the most she will give a joke about her church in public.

"Yeah," says Harbin, giving up. "Well — all hail the adventuring group!"

The adventuring group is hailed.

"Okay. So, Wester." I turn to him properly, because this is the part that matters and I want witnesses. "We are writing into the town charter, then, that all adventurers will distribute their treasure as universal basic treasure to the citizens of Phandalin. Unless they can find the rightful owner, of course."

"I think that's a *wonderful* idea!" He turns to the green, arms up. "Huzzah! Huzzah! Huzzah!"

The huzzahs come back. "So it's written in," I say. Done.

The sharp woman — Jenna, someone calls her — looks as if the green has personally insulted her.

"Is she outvoted?" I ask.

"Democracy sucks," Soma says, without heat.

---

"And I would say," I announce, "as the first contribution to the UBT fund, we have this melted hoard of dragon gold that we can't do anything else with."

Brewbarry hauls it off the cart. It is a slab of gold the size of a small chest, coins and cups and chains all run together in the dragon's breath, and it catches the afternoon light in a way that makes several people in the front row make small involuntary sounds.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: the town thanks the adventurers but cannot use the gold, because it is fused inside a magical containment that must first be extracted.]

"Nobody told us that," I say.

Nobody told us that. We hauled it out of a dragon's lair. We hauled it over a mountain. I find Ser Kaelen in the crowd, standing very straight in the way of a man who has recently been told his chant was embarrassing.

"Kaelen. Can you take care of this?"

"Yeah," Brewbarry says. "Be useful for once."

"This seems like your kind of thing. Like — a task. A holy task." I gesture at the slab, and then at the people. "Get this money to the people. They need it."

Ser Kaelen looks at the slab. He looks at me. "Sidebar," he says. "Sidebar."

"Do it, old man!"

"Does the Order of the Gauntlet get a share? Or is this strictly for the citizens of Phandalin?" He pauses, and I can see the second question arrive behind the first like a second wave. "And what do we mean by *citizens of Phandalin*?"

Valphine says, from the edge, "Ask the crowd."

"Excellent — *excellent* idea, Valphine!" I fling it open. "Phandalin! Who is a citizen?"

Brewbarry answers first, because Brewbarry always answers first. "I would say property owners within the bounds of Phandalin."

"Landed gentry," says Soma.

"What about renters?"

"Wait," Soma says. "Do they actually have property? I thought they were all serfs or whatever."

Several people who own property make offended noises. They do have property.

"As long as you live within the bounds of Phandalin, then you're a Phandalin citizen," Brewbarry tries. "How's that? Is that open enough?" And then, to me, lower: "I need to get myself a house in Phandalin, by the way."

"But what if somebody just comes down from Neverwinter for the day," I say, "and immigrates to Phandalin, and suddenly they are entitled to the UBT?"

"That's why we need to build a wall," says Soma.

"You have to live within Phandalin's boundary at least fifty-plus percent of the year," says Brewbarry.

"Primary residence, I mean—"

"That's right, that's right."

"—and attest under oath," I add, because I want it to be honest money, "with a Zone of Truth in effect."

"And you have to pay your taxes in Phandalin too, right?" Brewbarry frowns at himself. "No — no, you can just determine if they pay taxes in Phandalin, then they're citizens."

"That doesn't mean they're *paying* the tax—" Linene begins, and then abandons the point and comes forward with a folded paper she has apparently been carrying on her person for years, waiting. "I think this is a fabulous idea. I've been working on such a proposal for a long time." She unfolds it. Candlekeep's seal, a title in a very serious hand: *The Sage's Universal Theorem for Income Distribution.* "If I could suggest, Vukradin."

"Yes, of course."

"Perhaps a subcommittee of the Privy Council, led by myself — obviously under your supervision — could provide a draft set of rules in time for next week. Maybe two weeks from now. For how the treasure would be distributed."

Two weeks. I look at the green. "The people need the money sooner than that. They're *poor!* They're *suffering!* They need *UBT!*"

The crowd is still cheering, which is the important thing. Somewhere near the front a child asks whether children get UBT, and another child answers her before any adult can.

"We never have to work a day in our lives?" Soma says, in the voice of someone repeating what a shell sprout just said.

"As long as adventurers come," I say.

"As long as adventurers come," she agrees. "Yeah. It's true."

"Until some enchantment starts doing the adventuring," Valphine says, "and takes their livelihood."

"Sounds like slavery with extra steps," says Soma.

---

Harbin has been thinking, which is a slow process visible from the outside. "Hold on. Hold on. Right — wait, hold on." He clears his throat toward Valphine. "Well, of course, Valphine is a — a member of the community, an upstanding — cleric, you know, her — her —" He is trying very hard to say that the Temple of Lathander's Searing Pain of Justice is inside the city limits without saying its name in front of the children. "And of course, Vukradin is absolutely a member of the Privy Council. But—" He turns. "The Tortle! And the — the Barbarian! Their exact ties to the community—"

Brewbarry's head comes up. "Next words very carefully, old man."

"I've been living on the outskirts all my life," Soma says.

"You are talking to the muscle of this party."

Harbin looks at the muscle of this party, and at the tortle, and at the green, and does the arithmetic of a man who has to stand for election. "But — but I think we can all agree that Brewbarry and Soma, through their unique contributions, should be considered *honorary citizens of Phandalin*." A beat. "I'll take that."

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: the crowd cheers the honorary citizenship.]

"Everybody clapped," Soma says, flat, to the bench.

Which leaves the matter I have been circling since the slab came off the cart. Because I am not a thief, and I will not sell dragon plunder for the studio; but if the fund is the people's, and my friends are the people, and an adventurer's share of what he returns is drawn from the fund by rule and not by his own hand — then that coin has passed through the town on its way to me, and the town has chosen to give it. So I negotiate. Five percent. Five percent back to the adventurers who bring the treasure in, distributed from the fund like any other citizen's portion, tax-free.

"So five percent of the blood money," I say, when they agree, "now becomes organic fair trade?"

"Well," Soma says, "it's been laundered through the UBT."

"Effectively," I say.

"Effectively, yes."

Brewbarry nods, satisfied. "Turn evil into something good for the community."

I do not love the word *laundered*. I have decided not to hear it.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Jenna, looking very concerned, mounts up and rides out of town toward Neverwinter; the members of the Lord's Alliance are very particular.]

"Don't let the door hit you on the way out," Soma says to the road.

"I don't know who you are," I tell the dust, "and I don't care."

---

Valphine has not moved from the slab. "So. Are we selling this — three thousand — blood—"

"We have to Dispel Magic first."

"Prepare that, and then do that?"

"Yeah. If we can cast Dispel Magic, then I think we can recoup it."

"I can do Dispel Magic," she says, "or Remove Curse."

"Remove Curse. You can try it."

Brewbarry has crouched down to look at the slab at eye level, as he does with anything he might have to hit. "So when you remove the curse — where does the curse go?"

"Same place the socks go," says Soma.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: the fusion is not conventional magic, and freeing the gold will require finding an expert who knows about it.]

"What better place to find an expert," Brewbarry says, "than Neverwinter?"

Exactly. It is always Neverwinter.

We each take a turn at the thing anyway, because we are who we are. Valphine lays a hand on it and comes away with a sting she does not explain. I run my fingers along a seam where two coins have become one coin and get nothing but the feeling of a wrong chord I cannot name. Brewbarry taps it. Nothing.

Then Soma gets up off her bench, heavily, and brings the staff over. Meril's staff. She does not make anything of it. She sets the butt of it against the grass next to the slab and stands there a while, the way she stands at the edge of water.

"I can detect a planar anomaly around this cube," she says. "This is no ordinary magic." She looks at me. "You remember that slag? Those weird gnome things?"

I remember.

"The whole reason we're intervening," she says, and that is all she says, and she goes back to her bench.

The people have their fund. They have a name for it, and my name on it, and a subcommittee, and honorary citizens, and a slab of gold on the green that nobody can open. Somewhere up the road toward Neverwinter, a woman I do not know is telling someone I do not know what happened here today. And I find, standing on the grass with the lute still warm in my hands, that I am not frightened of that at all. It was a good yay. Done.
