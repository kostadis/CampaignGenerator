## Soma — Arrival at the Spire

Dawn. Of course it is dawn. Nobody schedules a sermon for the Morninglord at a decent hour, and my knees have opinions about the cobbles at this time of day.

The plan, as Vukradin explained it three times on the walk over, is that he will announce the name of his new band. The impromptu performance he and Valphine put on at the Moonstone Mask has, apparently, made a stir at the Spire. Enough of a stir that they invited the drow to give the morning sermon.

"Playing first, or are we preaching first?" Vukradin asks. He has been holding his instrument since we left the inn, the way a man holds a hatchling he is not sure will bite.

"Sermon first," Valphine says. "Followed by music and sacraments." She has a folded sheaf of paper under her arm. She has written the thing out. Of course she has.

"So it's a bit of everything," I say. "A service for people who haven't decided what they believe yet."

Nobody laughs. Vukradin is excited. Brewbarry wants to hear it. I want tea, but I am here, and the bale is here, so I walk.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: It is dawn, and the party arrives at the doors of the Spire of the Morninglord to find clerics at the threshold and a whole crowd of factions already gathered around.]

I look, because looking is free.

Valphine is looking too, and she looks the way she always does — at the whole room at once, sorting it. Brewbarry looks, and sees enough. Vukradin looks and sees a great many friendly faces, because that is what Vukradin sees when he looks at anything. He beams at the doors like they are already applauding.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Among the crowd are skeptical Lathanderite clerics at the doors, watching Valphine with visible doubt.]

"A drow cleric of Lathander?" I say, low. It is not a hard guess. Doubt has a posture. Arms folded, weight back on the heels, chin a little high. These ones have it.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: There are also several Loviatarite agents in the shadows; the followers of the goddess of pain regard Valphine's searing sacrament as stolen from them, and heretical.]

"Followers of Loviatar," Valphine says, as if naming a fish.

"Your sacrament is appropriated," I tell her. It seems fair to say it out loud. Someone had pain first. Now someone else has it and is charging admission.

Brewbarry, practical for once, wants to know whether the skeptics are bunched together or spread out. Spread out, from what I can see. Valphine wants to know if there is someone they defer to. She is already counting exits and elders.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: To the party's surprise, Lord Neverember himself is among the crowd, along with a mass of the faithful and the merely curious, all of whom have arrived very early in the morning.]

"Wow," says Vukradin.

"I mean," he adds, when he has recovered, "it's a concert?"

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: There are factions within factions here; these are only the ones the party recognizes, and there are more.]

I do not need to be told that. A crowd this early with this many kinds of shoes in it is not one crowd. But nobody, that I can see, has come with a blade on. Not the clerics, not the shadows, not the lord. Even Vukradin has noticed that. He seems slightly disappointed.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Just before the party arrives, the dragonscale armorer turns up at the Spire, having spent all night assembling Valphine's white dragon scale armor; he is one of the devout and wants Lathander's blessing on it, and he sets about fitting it on her there at the door, very excited.]

"Which is a great advertisement for his business," Valphine says, turning so he can get at the buckles.

Maybe. He does not have the face of a man thinking about sales. He has the face of a man who has not slept and has prayed about it. His hands are steady on the straps though, and that is what I care about. Armor that is fitted in a hurry pinches under the arm and rubs the collarbone raw by noon, and then I am the one mending the skin.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: The armor causes an even bigger stir; whispers and commentary run through the crowd as Valphine puts on the dragon-scale, and the armorer is very proud of himself.]

"Well," she says, rolling her shoulders in it. "It shows we're not to be trifled with."

"Hold on." Brewbarry has his hand on her shoulder. His eyes go bright, two lamps in that big plain face. "Ominous whispers," he explains, to me, and from the direction of the temple doors a sound starts up, thin and wordless, as if the hinges have secrets. "Just for theatrics."

Valphine's own eyes are doing the thing they do. Everyone at the threshold can see them. She knows. She has checked.

There is a choice to be made about how to walk in. I would walk in. Valphine is not me.

She raises one hand, high, and the light comes down — not a candle's worth, not the little lamp clerics use for this. A full noon inside a stone room at dawn.

"It is time for the morning sermon," she says.

"Hey," Vukradin murmurs, at my elbow. "It wasn't Sunbeam."

"That's a lot," Valphine agrees, not sorry.

"There's no undead here," he adds, as though someone had suggested it. "We took care of that problem."

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: The crowd is quite pleased and in awe of the spellcasting — it is not often anyone sees a Daylight spell inside the Church of Lathander in daytime — and as the whole chamber lights up, some people squint.]

She admires the squinting. Just for a breath. I see it because I am watching her hands, and the hand that cast it stays raised a beat longer than it needs to.

Brewbarry leans down to her ear. "Let's make sure we collect the donations at the end of the sermon."

"Oh. I haven't planned."

"But he has many plans," Vukradin says, meaning Brewbarry, meaning himself. "I am here to serve, by the way. Anything you want me to do, just ask."

"So am I."

A cleric near the doors makes it clear, politely, that before any sermon Brother Aldric would like a word. Valphine's "Really?" has a little frost on it.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Brother Aldric receives them in a private chamber; he ponders all the liturgical and ceremonial elements Valphine has skipped, but he is in a good mood.]

"You weren't supposed to do that," he tells her, not unkindly.

"Your religion isn't his religion," Vukradin says, under his breath, on her other side.

"Yeah," I say. "It's a new religion."

That is the whole of it, really. She has walked into somebody's house and rearranged the furniture and is now being asked, kindly, whether she knew where the chairs went. She knew. She is leaning into her own Church of the Searing Pain, and she knows this room, and I can see her deciding how slowly to say so.

Vukradin, meanwhile, is offering her Hold Person. Sotto voce. "No? Okay. Just say the word. I got it ready." Nobody in the chamber is armed, and Aldric has the look of a man who would faint before he swung, but Vukradin doesn't know what anyone has planned, and Hold Person, he notes, is great for inflicting pain.

What Valphine has planned, she told us on the walk: promoting the band. Drumming up business for the rooms. Sermons are incidental.

"I've heard of the blessed temple that you've built in Phandalin," Aldric says.

"Why, yes. That's glorious."

"But I have a question. You keep talking about this blessing. What does it mean? What is the meaning of the blessing?"

"It allows us to gain a deeper appreciation of the glory and power of the Morninglord."

"So you're saying this is not about the pain. This is about redemption, and understanding the Morninglord's path."

"Why, yes, of course. Of course. We must gain a deeper spiritual communion with the Morninglord."

She says it well. Vukradin's music is in it; I can hear the lift he gave her. It does not land.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Brother Aldric is not impressed, and seems a little disappointed.]

Valphine does not blink. She turns her head a fraction. "Brewbarry."

"Brewbarry, of course!" Vukradin, delighted, as if she has picked the right chord.

Brewbarry's eyes light again, and the whispers come again — from the back of the chamber this time, behind Aldric's shoulder, where he has to half-turn to look for them and finds only wall. It is a cheap trick. It is a good cheap trick. It changes the air, and Valphine talks into the changed air, and this time the old man's face moves.

"Well," Aldric says. "I'm happy to see that you're doing this. But I warn you to stay away from this path of pain. This is not Lathander's way."

"What do you mean by that?"

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Before Aldric can answer, another sun priest among those seated around the chamber — a scarred one — interrupts.]

"Is pain the point," the scarred priest asks, "or the healing?"

There it is. I have gutted enough fish to know a hook when I see one.

"The light is the point," Valphine says. "He is the Morninglord. The Lord of Light."

"Imagine life taught you that healing can be painful," Brewbarry says, in the tone of someone who is not imagining it.

"But why do you insist on the pain?"

"But healing doesn't come cheap, my friend."

"Embrace the fullness of the light," Valphine says, over him and with him. "You must experience it in its totality. Otherwise it's just a pale shadow."

"Then what is the purpose of the healing?"

A pause. Small. I watch her hands; they do not move.

"Our Lord has great ambitions for us on this world," she says. "And therefore we must be able to see the lit path."

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Aldric smiles; it is plain now that the other cleric was there to trap her and that she has threaded the trap, and he makes the sign of the Morninglord, very pleased.]

"Well done," Vukradin breathes.

"Persuaded," I say.

Vukradin, still vibrating, mutters something about having just watched her argue before a full bench of judges, and that it was fun. I suppose it was. For him. For me it is dawn, my shell is cold, there is a crowd of people out there who did not come for the sermon, and the only one of my bale who is properly dressed for this is the one they wanted to trip.

She did not trip. That is one thing.
