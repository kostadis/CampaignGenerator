# Initial A/B reading notes, before opening the assignment key

I read both anonymous drafts in full against the supplied extraction. I know the
hypothesis and the two briefs, but have not opened their A/B assignment. The prose
can itself suggest which brief was used, so this is not a claim of experimental
double blinding.

## Initial preference

A small preference for **A's treatment of the discovery and ending**, not a
clear preference for A throughout. Both versions feel quite close. Neither
demonstrates a substantial departure from the short-beat style of the earlier
chapter experiment.

A connects the rat's size and movement, Soma settling her body outside, her grip
on Meril's staff, and her revised view of the tavern's comfort. Its recollection
that the place initially meant nothing more to Brewbarry than something he could
finally afford gives the discovery a character-specific consequence. B also
connects the discovery to Soma's concern for her bale, but its ending reaches
that conclusion more quickly and generally.

The qualification matters: A repeatedly tells us how to interpret the experience.
Its statements that the symbol does not explain their fame and that finding a
symbol is not finding a knife at Brewbarry's back preserve uncertainty, but can
sound like a factual-review rubric entering the character's thoughts. The
additional reflection does not automatically make it better fiction.

## Exchanges and comic timing

Both keep the performance-versus-spells exchange intact: the practical argument,
Soma's possibility of getting paid, Vukradin's admission that he likes performing,
and the decision to consider both. Neither turns the performance proposal into
an actual performance or deal.

A gives this exchange more pauses and interior commentary, including “We are
about to save time at some length.” That can be funny, but the later explanation
about an audience, honest money, and the studio reiterates what the exchange
already shows. B's version moves more briskly. I do not regard A's longer handling
as an unambiguous improvement.

Both preserve “Infinite perception,” the source's joint answer about examining
the wall, the fame/manipulation exchange, Brewbarry's access to the expensive
tavern, and Vukradin's defense of the reformed Order of the Gauntlet.

B additionally retains the rat/theft discussion, but the original GM tease has
become a somewhat disconnected denial from Vukradin, followed by Soma explaining
that she had not accused him. That is a candidate for literary editing rather
than a reason to require every original quotation.

Both explain Brewbarry's uncomplicated entry with a sequence beginning “No…”:
A uses “No negotiations with himself”; B uses “No contract.” Neither fully
solves the tendency to append an explanation after a character action.

## Factual review

I found no consequential contradiction with the supplied extraction in either
draft. Both preserve the familiar's restoration; the bard's reunion and spider-job
report; Denvar's collection and departure; initial uncertainty about the two-way
compartment; the tavern's identity; Brewbarry entering alone; the proposed but
unperformed business approach; Knock and rat access; the basement office and
Harper symbol; and the continuing uncertainty about the ninth crate and fame.

Both give Soma a plausible glimpse of the tavern through its entrance before she
uses the rat. Both communicate the rat's discovery to the others through her
speech. Neither sends the party into a new confrontation or brings Brewbarry out.
Both remember the campaign's Order of the Gauntlet history and avoid treating
the Harpers as automatically benevolent.

Several lines dramatize GM description as character speech or invent private
interpretations. Those are permitted by the test. The checks above are scoped
to the supplied scene, not certification against every VTT detail or campaign
document.

## Paragraph rhythm and provisional conclusion

A: 1,699 words, 117 paragraphs, median 10 words; 20 very short quote-led paragraphs.
B: 1,781 words, 115 paragraphs, median 11 words; 18 very short quote-led paragraphs.

These are almost the same broad shape. A is slightly shorter, not an inflated
version of B. Both include connected sensory passages, but repeatedly separate
individual observations or reactions into standalone paragraphs. The metrics do
not establish quality, but they do not support a claim of a strong rhythm change.

Whatever the assignment reveals, this pair supports only a **modest, localized
difference in narrative emphasis**. If the more directed brief is A, its intended
emotional emphasis is visible, but it has not clearly solved scene composition
or paragraph development. If it is B, the generic brief has independently
achieved much of the proposed result. The user's reading preference should
decide which tradeoffs are desirable.
