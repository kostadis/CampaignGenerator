Soma — Harpers Behind the Wall

By the time Denvar wakes, I have made another rat and run out of comfortable ways to stand.

The rat has the better arrangement. Four feet. Very little weight. I shift my shell and work the stiffness out of one knee while Vukradin returns to us, brought along by the updates through the Sending Stone.

“I can tell you about Denvar,” I say.

“Why don’t I tell you about the local wererat mafia?”

I look down at my familiar. A perfectly ordinary shape for a creature to have. Apparently it opens doors in Neverwinter.

“There are wererats all over, aren’t there?”

Vukradin tells us about Rsolk. There are spiders in the tunnels, directions to reach them, and money to be made clearing them out. After hours of watching a man sleep, this sounds almost restful.

“We just gotta clear out these spiders, and it’s more money for the studio. This is great. Finally a business opportunity.” His face brightens. “Fair Trade Gold.”

Then Denvar moves, and we have a payment to follow.

He leads us to a stone wall backing onto a building. He looks around before touching it. I keep still. After all this waiting, I would prefer not to become the large tortle he remembers standing behind him.

A stone opens under his hand.

“It’s a dead drop,” Vukradin murmurs.

Denvar takes out a bag and closes the stone again. Valphine watches him rather than the wall. I follow her attention long enough to see him examine a chit and smile.

“Payday,” I mutter. “I’m going to the casino.”

We let him go.

“Denvar might be a dead end,” I say. “Unless we come back before he leaves next evening. And we don’t know how early that is.”

The wall is here now. I prefer the wall.

“Valphine, you want to do your…” Vukradin gestures.

“Infinite perception,” I supply.

“On him, or on the wall?”

“On the wall,” we say together.

She gives the stone her attention. The arrangement passes through the wall, with a closure on the far side as well as ours. Someone could put a payment in and someone else could collect it without meeting. Sensible. Irritating. Nothing we can see tells us whether Denvar’s bag came from the street or from inside.

Vukradin studies the masonry. “I’m trying to think whether we have something that can help us without going into the house.”

“We have Mold Earth. But I know you won’t.”

“It’s not earth. It’s stone.”

Yes. There is that small difficulty.

“Let’s look around the building,” Valphine says.

“Got to be a Margaster residence, or something like that,” I say.

It is a tavern.

The House of a Thousand Faces, and open already. Through the entrance I catch the shine of mirrors, deep couches, mannequins dressed in fashions that have had plenty of time to reconsider themselves. A fire burns without filling the room with smoke. Someone has spent money on making people comfortable here. Rather more money than the surrounding stretch of Bluelake suggests.

“Somebody’s obviously in there paying off shady debts,” I say.

“It’s early,” Vukradin says, considering the open door, “but why not?”

“It’s five o’clock somewhere.”

Brewbarry looks at the entrance.

“I heard about this place when I first came to Neverwinter,” he says. “I couldn’t afford it.”

He can now. He goes inside.

I watch him pass into the comfortable room. No negotiations with himself. No need to establish whether a couch has an ulterior motive. For a moment I envy him.

Vukradin is still thinking about the wall.

“I have an idea for investigating without going inside. Knock.”

“How does that work? You use Knock to open the two-way dead drop?”

“Right. We open our side, and we can’t physically open the other side, but I think we can open it magically. Then we can look in, see what room it is—or send your rat through to look around.”

I look at the rat. Small enough. Already available. Unlike my knees, willing to do more work.

“I like that combo.”

“I have a different strategy,” Valphine says. “We go inside and offer them a business proposal. Your booking agency.”

“Vukradin could play,” I say.

He turns toward us.

“Getting backstage would give you access to staff-only areas,” I continue. “For your music preparation. And by music preparation…”

“There you go,” Valphine says.

Vukradin draws breath. I recognize the posture. We are about to save time at some length.

“We could cast two spells, or we could have an extended contract process—”

“Or we can get paid.”

“—and several performances, and hours of time, when we could just look through the wall and know what’s there before we go inside.”

He glances toward the door.

“I do like to perform, though.”

I let him have a moment with that.

“These are tough choices.”

They are, for him. There is a possible audience in there. Honest money. Another small piece of his studio, provided we can get through the business of finding out who is paying Denvar.

“Let’s do both,” I say. “Why not?”

“Exactly. Why not?”

“The spell first,” Valphine says. “It’s a simple strategy.”

“Yes. Before we go inside,” Vukradin says. “While we’re all together.”

“Brewbarry’s already in the tavern.”

Vukradin looks toward the entrance again.

“He was born in a tavern,” I say.

“He used to have to settle for the Common Chord. This is more of a high-end establishment.”

“He’s a man of class now. He can buy his way into high society.”

“Isn’t it obvious?” Valphine asks Vukradin. “The quality of your talent.”

“No.”

“You’re sure?”

“It definitely is not.”

The answer comes firmly enough to pull my attention back to his face.

“There is a force behind our fame,” he says. “Some groups are maneuvering. We’re being—”

“Manipulated.”

“—to their advantage, for some reason.”

We know someone is making Lathander’s Death famous. We do not know who. Or why they want so many people to recognize us.

The open tavern door seems less useful for a moment. We have a bard people know, a booking proposal we have not made, and a barbarian already enjoying the improvement in his circumstances. I would like one look behind the wall before the rest of my bale follows him.

We return to the compartment.

Our stone opens. Beyond the empty space is the inner closure, inaccessible to our hands. Vukradin casts Knock. It releases.

No contract required.

I bring the rat to the opening and settle my weight before borrowing its senses. My body must stay here, outside, with its stiff joints and its staff. I give it a firm stance to wait in.

Then the stone is enormous.

The rat slips through. Rough edges pass close on either side. The view drops and changes as it picks its way inward, down into the basement. I let it find its footing. There is no shell to scrape, no shoulder to wedge through. It is easy to forget how much of a room I ordinarily occupy.

Desk legs rise ahead. Papers sit above us. Supplies fill parts of the room. No windows. A back office, tucked below the tavern.

I guide the rat where it can see more.

There.

I hold it still.

The symbol is unmistakable. I know it before I want to know it.

I pull back into my own senses. The street returns at its proper height. My fingers are tight around Meril’s staff.

“The Harpers.”

“Harp?” Vukradin asks.

“Harpers. Their symbol’s in there.”

For a moment no one supplies another plan.

I look through the rat again. The desk, the papers, the supplies resolve into a working place. A local headquarters. The compartment opens into it. Denvar’s payment has led us straight through their wall.

I return to the others.

“Basement office. No windows. Desk, papers, supplies. It looks like the local Harpers work from here.”

“So the Harpers operate the drop,” Vukradin says.

“Yes.” I shift my grip on the staff. “The Harpers are… smuggling?”

“Arranging the ninth crate, perhaps,” Valphine says. “That does not yet tell us who receives it.”

“That ninth crate is being delivered secretly. There’s smuggling in there somewhere.”

But somewhere matters. They might be arranging it. Receiving it. Working for another client. A symbol on an office wall does not give me the person at the far end of the payment.

It also does not explain our fame. I keep the two things apart, though I dislike having them so close together.

“At least the Order of the Gauntlet has reformed,” Vukradin says.

“Debatable.”

“Have they?” Valphine asks.

“They’re reformed. The whole thing with the barbarians—they were misled by their political masters, and they’ve rejected those masters now. They recognized they were being used.”

He is quite definite about the distinction.

“Basically, they’re the Reformed Order of the Gauntlet.”

“Truth and justice,” I say.

“And looking after the little guy.”

I want that to be true. It would be convenient to have an organization whose good intentions survived contact with its accounts.

The Harpers offer no such comfort. I know enough of them for that.

Beyond the wall, the rat waits among office furniture. I check through its eyes once more, then come back to the weight of my own body and the early morning outside. Brewbarry is still in the tavern. He has not heard any of this.

I remember the couches through the doorway. The old fashions on the mannequins. Mirrors multiplying a room somebody has taken care to make pleasant. Brewbarry could finally afford to walk into it, and he did. That was all the place had needed to mean to him.

Now I cannot think about its good fire without thinking about the office beneath it.

Nothing has happened to him. I have found a symbol, not a knife at his back. I make myself keep that distinction too.

Still, I would like him close enough to hear me.

We remain outside the House of a Thousand Faces, beside the opened drop. My rat is below. Brewbarry is within. The payment trail has reached the Harpers, and the ninth crate is still a question.

Soma — Harpers Behind the Wall

By the time Denvar wakes, I have restored my rat and shifted my weight often enough to know which joints will complain when we move. All of them. The rat has the easier task. Small feet. No shell.

Vukradin rejoins us after several hours of Sending Stone updates.

“Yeah, yeah, you can come along,” I tell him. “I can tell you about Denvar.”

“Why don’t I tell you about the local wererat mafia?”

I look down at my familiar. An ordinary rat is beginning to seem an unnecessarily modest choice.

“There’s wererats all over, isn’t there?”

He tells us about Rsolk and the spiders in the tunnels. We have directions enough to find the place again. More work underground. More things with too many legs. Vukradin’s account grows brighter as it approaches the subject of payment.

“We just have to clear out these spiders, and it’s more money for the studio. This is great. Finally a business opportunity.” He gives the conclusion its proper space. “Fair Trade Gold.”

The distinction between a wererat mafia and an acceptable business associate is evidently spiders. Still, work is work. I would rather see him paid for something he can bear to take payment for. It saves my bale a great deal of discussion afterward.

Then Denvar moves, and we follow.

He leads us to a stone wall adjoining a building. Nothing welcoming about it. No counter, no door on this stretch, no person waiting with a purse. He looks around with the careful casualness of someone hoping nobody notices how carefully casual he is being.

We hold back.

“It’s a dead drop,” Vukradin says quietly.

Denvar pulls open a stone. His hand goes into the wall and comes out with a bag. He closes the hiding place again.

That is a useful wall.

“Let him go,” Vukradin says. “Then we inspect it.”

Valphine keeps her attention on Denvar. He looks at the chit and smiles.

“Payday,” I murmur. “I’m going to the casino.”

He leaves with the smile still doing its work. We let him.

“Denvar might be a dead end,” I say. “We could come back tomorrow evening before he leaves his house. But we don’t know how early he has to leave to make these drops.”

The wall, at least, will stay where we put it.

Vukradin turns to Valphine. “You want to do your—”

“Infinite perception,” I supply.

“On him, or on the wall?”

“The wall,” we both say.

She gives the stone her attention. Up close, the compartment makes more sense. There is an outer opening, where Denvar reached in, and another closure beyond it. A payment could pass through from the building without the two people ever meeting. It could also have been left from the street. Stone does not volunteer which.

“Like a little transfer window,” Vukradin says. “You put something in, close your side, and they open theirs.”

“If that’s how they used it.”

We have a bag collected from a wall. We do not yet have the hand that put it there.

Vukradin studies the surrounding masonry. “Can we get through without going inside? Is it earth, or—”

“We have Mold Earth,” I say. “But I know you won’t.”

“It’s not earth. It’s stone.”

There is that difficulty.

“Let’s look around the building,” Valphine says.

“Got to be a Margaster residence or something,” I mutter.

It is an attractive suspicion. Those are often the ones worth watching in oneself. We follow the wall around, and the building declines to be what I expected.

It is a tavern.

“The House of a Thousand Faces,” Vukradin reads. “So many taverns.”

The entrance is open, despite the early hour. Through it I catch mirrors and the rounded arms of lush couches. Mannequins wear fashions old enough to have survived their original wearers. Farther in, a fire burns without rolling its smoke into the room.

A chimney that draws properly. Someone spent money on the useful parts as well.

The place sits oddly among its neighbours in the Bluelake District. Too much comfort concentrated behind one doorway. I look back along the stonework toward the hidden payment.

“Somebody’s obviously in there paying off shady debts.”

“Sure, we can hit the tavern,” Vukradin says. “It’s early, but why not?”

“Five o’clock somewhere.”

Brewbarry looks through the entrance.

“I heard about this place when I first came to Neverwinter,” he says. “I couldn’t afford it.”

Then he goes in.

No contract. No inspection of the wall. Just the doorway, and the ability to pay for whatever is beyond it. I watch his back disappear into the comfortable room.

Vukradin is still thinking about the compartment.

“I have an idea for investigating without going inside. Knock. We open our side, then cast it on the other side.”

I turn from the entrance. “How does that work? You use Knock to open the two-way dead drop?”

“Right. We can’t physically open the inner closure, but we can open it magically. Then we look through and see what room it leads to. Or send your rat in to look around.”

The rat is already here. Unlike the rest of us, it fits into things.

“I like that combo.”

“I have a different strategy,” Valphine says. “We go inside and offer them a business proposal. Your booking agency.”

“Vukradin could play,” I say. “Getting backstage gives you access to staff-only areas. For your music preparation. And by music preparation—”

“There you go,” Valphine says.

Vukradin glances between us. “We could cast two spells, or we could have an extended contract process—”

“Or we can get paid.”

“—and several performances, and hours of time.” He pauses. “I do like to perform, though.”

“Yes. Tough choices.” I settle the matter. “Let’s do both.”

“Exactly. Why not?”

The performance remains a possibility. First, the hole.

Vukradin lays out the plan again as we return to the dead drop: open our side, Knock on the inner closure, send the rat through. A sensible sequence. I appreciate a plan with a creature chosen for the size of the opening.

“Before we go inside,” he adds. “We’re all together.”

“Brewbarry’s already in the tavern,” I remind him. “He was born in the tavern.”

Vukradin looks back toward the entrance.

“He’s a man of class now,” I say. “He can buy his way into high society.”

“Isn’t it obvious?” Valphine asks Vukradin. “The quality of your talent.”

“No.”

“You’re certain?”

“It definitely is not.”

His refusal is sharp enough to turn my attention from the stone. He is perfectly willing to believe in his talent. This is something else.

“There is a force behind our fame,” he says. “Some groups are manoeuvring. We’re being—”

“Manipulated.”

“—used to their advantage, for some reason.”

Lathander’s Death has become more famous than our own efforts explain. Someone is helping that happen. We know that much. We do not know who has decided we are useful, or what they expect us to do once enough people know our name.

I dislike being moved without seeing the hand. Especially when the rest of my bale is being moved with me.

For now, we have an actual hand-sized opening to investigate.

We open the outer stone. Beyond it, the inner closure still blocks the way. Vukradin casts Knock, and the barrier releases.

I bring the rat to the opening.

The change in sight takes a moment. My own bulk stays outside with the others, but the wall grows enormous before me. Edges I could scarcely bother to notice become obstacles. The familiar slips through.

I guide it onward.

The route takes it down, below the tavern’s first floor. A back office opens around it. No windows. A desk rises above the rat’s head, with papers and supplies arranged in the room beyond. From this height, furniture is mostly legs and undersides. I let it creep farther, turning so I can take in what is here.

Then I see the symbol.

Unmistakable.

“Oh, wow.”

I draw back into my own senses long enough to speak properly. The stone is in front of me again. Vukradin and Valphine wait beside it.

“The Harpers.”

“Harp?” Vukradin asks.

“Harpers. Their symbol is in there.”

The ninth-crate trail has brought us to a local Harper headquarters. I look at the wall with considerably less affection.

“What sort of room?” Vukradin asks. “Storage?”

“An office. Basement level. No windows. Desk, papers, supplies.”

His attention catches on the papers.

“It’s just a rat,” he says.

“Can’t carry much.”

“I definitely wouldn’t steal anything.”

I had not accused him. It is useful to have the denial prepared.

I look through the familiar’s eyes again, holding on to the plain facts of the room. An office beneath a tavern. The Harper symbol. The inner side of the compartment we watched Denvar use. No convenient explanation waiting on the floor at rat height.

“The Harpers are smuggling?” I say when I return.

They operate the drop. That is clear now. Their exact place in the trade is not. Arranging the ninth crate is different from receiving it. Both are different from arranging it for somebody else.

“Clearly that ninth crate involves some smuggling,” I say. “It’s being delivered directly. So they’re involved in some capacity.”

I leave it there. I would like a firmer answer. Wanting one does not put it in the room.

Nor does finding Harpers make any of this reassuring. Their name is no guarantee of good people behind the wall.

Vukradin brings up the Order of the Gauntlet as a better comparison.

“Debatable,” I say.

“Are there good people there?” Valphine asks.

“Reformed,” he insists. “They’re reformed. They had that whole thing with the barbarians, but they were misled by their political masters, and they’ve rejected those masters now. They recognised they were being used.”

He speaks with the determination of someone who needs us to distinguish a changed organisation from an unchanged one. Fair enough. I can distinguish them and still keep my hand on my staff.

“Basically,” he concludes, “they’re the Reformed Order of the Gauntlet. They’re about the little guy now.”

“Truth, justice, and the little guy,” I say.

There is no Brewbarry here to give that a literal examination. He is inside the House of a Thousand Faces, enjoying his new ability to enter an expensive tavern. The rest of us remain outside after scouting its basement, with Rsolk’s spider tunnels still waiting for another visit.

I shift my feet beneath the weight of my shell. The wall looks as bare as it did when Denvar approached it.

My bale is on both sides of it now. That bothers me more than the stone.
