## Soma — Harpers Behind the Wall

The rat comes back a little before dawn. It takes the better part of an hour and more of me than I like to spend before breakfast, but there it is, sitting in the palm of my hand with its whiskers going, exactly as if nobody had ever squashed it. I put it in my shell pocket. Denvar's shutters are still closed. We have been watching them long enough that I know the crack in the third one from the left.

Vukradin turns up shortly after. He has had our updates through the stone all night and looks as though he slept about as much as we did, which is not at all.

"It's been a few hours," he says. "I should probably join you."

"Yeah, yeah. We can have you come along." I shift my weight off the bad hip. "I can tell you about Denvar."

"Why don't I tell you about the local wererat mafia?"

So he does. There is a wererat called Rsolk. There are tunnels. There are spiders in the tunnels, apparently a great many, and Rsolk would like them gone, and Vukradin has directions. I listen and think that of course there are wererats. This city is built on water and rot. Something has to eat what falls through.

"I expect them to be such a big player," I say, which is only half a joke.

Vukradin has already moved past the wererats to the part that matters to him. "We just gotta clear out these spiders and it's more money for the studio. This is great. Finally a business opportunity." He says the next words like a blessing. "Fair Trade Gold."

Spider extermination. Clean money at last. I let him have it.

---

Denvar wakes when the light does. He comes out of his house with his hair still flat on one side and walks with the loose, quick step of a man who has somewhere to be and does not want to look like it. We follow at a distance. He leads us three streets over to a bare stone wall, the back of some building, no door, no window, nothing to recommend it to anyone.

He stops. He looks left. He looks right. He is being as discreet as a man can be who has never once had to be discreet about anything, which is to say he looks like someone about to do something at a wall.

"It's a dead drop," Vukradin says quietly.

Denvar works a stone loose, reaches into the gap, and comes out with a small bag. He fits the stone back. He looks at what he has been given—a chit, from the way he holds it—and he smiles, and he walks away a good deal lighter than he came.

"Payday," I say. "I'm going to the casino."

We let him go. There is no reason to follow a man with his morning's coin when the wall is right here and the wall is what paid him.

"Barring coming back the next evening before he leaves his house," I say, "but we don't know how early he has to leave to make these drops." I chew on it. "Denvar might be a dead end."

Nobody argues. So we eyeball the wall.

"Valphine," Vukradin says, "you wanna do your…"

"Infinite perception," I supply.

She had already been doing it, on Denvar, right up until he turned the corner. Now she takes a little look at the rock. It is a stone in a stone wall, and the wall backs into a building, and the stone lifts out. Behind it is a cavity with a closed face on the far side. That is the whole problem in one glance. Somebody put the bag in there. Whether they stood where Denvar stood or stood inside the building, the hole does not say.

Vukradin makes a shape with his hands. "Is it like a two-way window, where you put it in on one side and close the door, and then they…"

"We'd have to go inspect it," I say.

"I'm trying to think whether we have something that can help us without going into the house."

"We have Mold Earth," I say. "But I know you won't."

"It's not earth. It's stone."

He is correct, which does not make him less tiresome. Valphine has already lost interest in the argument and walked off along the wall. "Let's just quickly look around the house and see what it is."

"It's gotta be a Margaster residence," I say to her back. "Or something like that." I do not know that. It would fit, is all. Everything ugly in this city seems to have that name written somewhere on the underside.

"It's not a residence," Valphine says, in the tone of a woman who has been running the question for a full minute already. "It's not a shop. That's what I'm questioning."

We round the corner and there is the front of it, and the sign, and the question is answered. A tavern. The House of a Thousand Faces. The door is open, this early, with light behind it.

"Great," says Valphine.

"So many taverns," Vukradin says. "Well. Maybe we can just walk right in. Are they open?"

"Somebody's obviously in there paying off shady debts," I say. That is an open door at dawn, in my experience.

"Okay. Sure, we can hit the tavern. It's early, but why not?"

"It's drinking hour somewhere."

Brewbarry has gone very still beside me, which for Brewbarry is a kind of announcement. He is looking at the sign. He is looking at the door. Then he is walking through it, without a word to any of us, and is gone.

"He was born in the tavern," I say.

"Actually," Vukradin says, "I think he's heard about this place. When he first came to Neverwinter he couldn't go there. He didn't have the money. He was more of a Common Chord slum, and this was a high-end establishment."

"He's a man of class now," I say. "He can buy his way into high society."

Valphine, dry as a shed skin: "Isn't it obvious? It's the quality of your talent."

"Not."

"It's totally not, you're sure."

"It definitely is not." Vukradin does not laugh with her. He never does, on this subject. "There is a force behind our fame. Some groups are maneuvering. We're being—"

"Manipulated," I say.

"—to their advantage, for some reason."

He has been saying so for a while. I have stopped disagreeing. A small band of misfits does not become Lathander's Death in every tavern between here and Phandalin by accident. Somebody is stoking that fire. We do not know who is holding the bellows, or why. It is one more hole where the bag went in and the hand did not show.

---

The three of us stand outside the House of a Thousand Faces and work out how to go into it.

"I have an idea for investigating without going inside," Vukradin says. "The Knock spell. We open our side, and we cast Knock on the other side."

"Sorry," I say. "How does that work? You use Knock to open the two-way dead drop?"

"We can't physically open the other side of the hole. But I think we could open it magically."

I have had a staff for a year and a leaf for a shorter time than that. He has been throwing words at locks his whole life. "If you propose it, I think it's worth a shot."

"And then we could look in, see what room we're looking at. Or we could even send one of your rats in there to look around."

"I like that combo."

"I have a different strategy," Valphine says, "which is, we go in there and offer them a business proposal."

"Yeah. Vukradin could play."

"Your booking agency."

"Getting backstage would give you access to staff-only areas," I tell him, "where you could do your music preparation. And by music preparation—"

"There you go," says Valphine.

Vukradin looks pained in the way he gets when a good idea is also a lot of work. "We could cast two spells. Or we could have an extended contract process, and several performance roles, and hours of time."

"Or we can get paid," I point out.

"I do like to perform, though."

"These are tough choices." I let him suffer for a breath. "Let's do both. Why not both?"

"Exactly. Why not."

So it is settled the sensible way, which is that we do the cheap thing first. The whole idea is not to walk in completely blind. We are all together, near enough, with Brewbarry a wall's thickness away, and that will do.

Valphine keeps an eye on the street. Vukradin lifts the stone out. I put my hand into the cold cavity and feel the closed face at the back of it, smooth, a seam I could not get a claw into. He says his word. The seam gives with a sound like a knuckle cracking. There is a draft on my palm now, from the other side, and it smells like paper and lamp oil and a room nobody airs.

I set the rat on the ledge and send it through, and close my eyes, and go with it.

---

Down. That is the first thing. The hole lets out onto a shelf and the shelf is above a floor that is lower than the street, so the rat drops and I feel the drop in my own shell. Stone underfoot. No window anywhere. A back room, basement level, the kind of room a building keeps for the parts of itself it does not want seen.

A desk. Papers on it, in stacks, a careful hand's stacks, not a lazy one's. Supplies on shelves along the wall—ink, wax, string, the ordinary stuff of people who write things down and send them places. The rat noses along the baseboard. I am not looking for anything. I am only looking.

And then I see it, because it is meant to be seen by whoever is allowed in this room. On the wall above the desk, plain as a shop sign: a harp between the points of a crescent moon.

"Oh," I say, out loud, on the street, with my eyes still shut. "Wow."

"Harp?" Vukradin says.

"Harpers."

The rat sits up on its haunches. I make it look again to be sure. Same harp. Same moon.

"Okay," Vukradin says, "so is it, like, a storage room, or—"

"Desk. Papers. Supplies." I open my eyes. "A back office. Nobody's in it. It's their house, though, not a room they borrow. Looks like headquarters. The local Harpers."

"I definitely wouldn't steal anything," Vukradin says, to nobody, unprompted.

"It's a rat. It can't carry anything." I bring it back through the hole and into my hand and the papers and the harp go out like a lamp. The rat's heart is going fast against my thumb. Mine is not much slower.

"Harpers," I say. "So that's… weird." I say the obvious thing next, because somebody has to. "The Harpers are smuggling?"

Vukradin is careful about it, which I did not expect. There is a difference, he says, between the Harpers smuggling and the Harpers arranging for the ninth crate. Someone has to hire the cart. Someone has to receive what is on it. Someone might be doing either for someone else entirely. We know the payment went into this wall and that this wall is theirs. That is all we know.

"Clearly that ninth crate involves some smuggling," I say, "because it's being directly delivered. So yes. The Harpers are smuggling in some capacity."

That much stands. The rest is a hole with no hand showing.

I have never held the Harpers in the regard the songs do. Songs are written by people who want work. In this city the ones who wear the harp are not the kindly meddlers of the old ballads; I have seen what they meddle in. Valphine, I think, never held them in any regard at all. It is Vukradin who wants there to be a good order somewhere, and he has picked his.

"The only good people," he says, "are the Order of the Gauntlet."

"Debatable," I say.

"Are there?" says Valphine.

"Reformed. They're reformed."

"They had that whole thing with the barbarians," I remind him.

"They were misled by their political masters. That's what I'm saying. They've been past that. They've rejected those masters. Basically they're the Reformed Order of the Gauntlet." He is warming to it, the way he does. "They recognized they were used by powerful forces, and now they're all about the little guy."

"Truth, justice, and the common weal," I say, flat.

He lets it go by. Some of my jokes are for me.

---

We go in the front, since the back has told us all it is going to. I am ready for a low room with a low ceiling and a floor sticky underfoot, because that is what the Bluelake District has always given me.

It is not that. The House of a Thousand Faces is the wrong tavern for this street. The walls are hung with mirrors, tall ones, so the room goes on longer than it should and there are always more of us in it than I can count. Between the mirrors stand mannequins, dressed—old fashions, coats and gowns a hundred years out of the shops, standing about like guests who came to a party that ended before I hatched. The couches are deep and the cushions are not split. The fire is drawing properly, pulling its smoke straight up the flue, and I notice that because I have spent thirty years in rooms where it did not.

Somebody put money into this. Not the district's money.

Brewbarry is already seated, halberd against the couch arm, looking at the room as if it might be taken away from him. Vukradin and Valphine drift in behind me. We have Rsolk's tunnels for later and a harp on a wall for now, and none of it says who is hiring the cart.

"Yep," I say, low, to the mirrors. "It's a good place. I need to leave it."
