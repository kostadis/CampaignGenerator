## Soma — Harpers Behind the Wall

The waiting is the part nobody sings about. Several hours on a Bluelake stoop with a shell that has opinions about cold stone, drawing my rat back out of wherever a familiar goes when it dies. The ritual takes what it takes. I pay it twice, in fact, because the first pull of the leash doesn't hold, and I sit there afterward feeling the second slot leave me like a tooth. The rat sniffs my thumb as if none of it happened. Good. I don't need it to remember.

Vukradin arrives with the light, stone still warm in his pocket from all the messages he's been sending us. "You've been giving me updates through the Sending Stone, so I should probably join you. It's been a few hours."

"Yeah, yeah, we can have you come along," I say. "I can tell you about Denvar."

"Why don't I tell you about the local wererat mafia?"

So there is one of those. "Yeah, there's were-rats all over, isn't it? I expect them to be such a big player."

He tells it fast: a rat-man called Rsolk, tunnels, and spiders in the tunnels that somebody would like removed. I watch his face do the arithmetic before his mouth does. "We just gotta clear out these spiders, and it's more money for the studio. This is great. Finally a business opportunity." A beat, then the label he loves. "Fair Trade Gold."

Spiders don't have heirs to dispute the provenance. I'll give him that one.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: A few hours later Denvar wakes, and the party follows him to a stone wall that backs into a building, where he looks around with careful discretion, pulls a stone free, takes out a bag, and closes the hiding place again.]

"It's a dead drop," Vukradin says, low.

I look at where the man's hands went. "Just a bare stone wall, like, on the edge of the…" No. It's set into something. It's the back of a building, the kind of wall a building doesn't bother to make pretty because it faces nobody.

"Is it like a two-way doctor's-office window," Vukradin says, "where you put it in on one side and close the door, and then they…"

"We'd have to go inspect it," I say, before he finishes the picture.

"Alright. It's effectively a dead drop, so we should let him go and inspect it."

"Yeah." He goes. We don't follow. "Excellent."

Then the practical problem lays itself out in my head like a net on a dock. Denvar drops, Denvar collects, Denvar sleeps. We could come back tomorrow evening and wait outside his house again, but we don't know how early he leaves to make the drops, and I don't want to spend another night of shell on that stoop for a maybe. "Denvar might be a dead end," I say. Nobody argues.

"Eyeball the wall," I tell them.

"Valphine, you wanna do your…" Vukradin waves a hand.

"Infinite perception," I supply.

"You mean, like, on him, or on the…"

"On the wall," Vukradin says, and I say it with him. Valphine's already turned her head the other way, watching the little man's back as he leaves.

"I'm gonna look at him just before he goes."

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Denvar looks at the chit in his hand and smiles before walking off.]

"Payday," I say in his voice, flat. "Off to the gaming tables."

"Could take a little look, then, at the rock," Valphine says, and does.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: The stone is a two-way compartment; nothing about it shows whether the payment was placed from the street or from inside the building, so learning that would mean going in.]

"Oh, okay. Well, let's loop around."

"Or is it earth, or…" Vukradin is looking at the wall like it might be persuaded.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: The building is a stone dwelling, built of stone throughout.]

"The stone dwelling," Vukradin repeats, and I can see him wanting a way in that doesn't involve a door. "I'm trying to think whether we have something that can help us without going into the house."

"I mean, we have Mold Earth," I say. "But I know you won't."

"It's not earth, it's stone."

I did say I knew.

"Let's just quickly look around the house and see what it is," Valphine says, and Vukradin adds, "The outside of the house," as though somebody might have been about to start with the inside.

"Yeah, it's gotta be a Margaster residence, or something like that," I say. It has the smell of it. Money paying for silence out of a wall.

"It's not a residence, it's not a shop," Valphine says.

"You don't think?"

"That's what I'm questioning. Is it a residence or a shop?"

We go around. The wall gets longer and better dressed as we go, the way a fish looks different from the belly than from the head.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Walking the perimeter, the party finds the building is a tavern, the House of a Thousand Faces, and though it is early morning the place is open.]

"Great," says Valphine, in the tone she uses for things that are not.

"So many taverns," Vukradin says. "Well, maybe we can just walk right in. Are they open?"

They are. At this hour. "Somebody's obviously in there paying off shady debts," I say. It's the only trade I know that keeps these hours besides fishing, and nobody fishes in Bluelake.

"Okay. Sure, we can hit the tavern. It's early, but why not?" It's drinking hour somewhere. It's always drinking hour somewhere; that's how taverns stay in business.

Vukradin stops with his hand half raised. "I have an idea for investigating without going inside: the *Knock* spell. So we could open our side, and we can cast Knock on the other side." He looks at me. "Do you want to try to do that, or do you want to just go inside the tavern?"

I'd have walked in. I'm not a magic wielder, whatever the staff says about me, and my instinct with a closed door is a door. But I'd rather know what I'm walking into. "Sorry, how does that work? You use Knock to open the two-way dead drop?"

"Right. We can't physically open the other side of the two-way hole, but I think we could open it magically."

"If you propose it, I think it's worth a shot."

"And then we could look in and see, like, what room are we looking at, try to find the right room — or we could even send one of your rats in there to look around."

Now that is a use for a rat. "I like that combo."

"I have a different strategy," Valphine says, "which is, we go in there and offer them a business proposal."

"Yeah, Vukradin could play," I say.

"Your booking agency," Valphine says to him, and I can see the shape of it now. "Getting backstage would give you access to staff-only areas," I say, "where you could do your music preparation — and by music preparation…"

"There you go," Valphine says.

Vukradin weighs it with his whole face. "We could cast two spells, or we could have an—"

"Or we can get paid!"

"—extended contract process, and several performance roles, and hours of time." A pause that gives him away. "I do like to perform, though."

"Yeah, see, I mean… these are tough choices." They are not. "Let's do both. Why not both?"

"Exactly. Why not?" He runs it back for the drow. "The idea is that we open our side, cast Knock on the other side, and then send the familiar rat through to look around the room on the other side."

"It's a simple strategy," Valphine says. From her that is nearly praise.

"Before we go inside. We're all together."

"Yeah," I say. "We want to at least not walk in completely blind."

We are not, it turns out, all together.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: Brewbarry has already gone into the tavern; he had heard of the House of a Thousand Faces when he first came to Neverwinter and could not afford it then.]

"Yeah, he was born in the tavern," I say. Of course he is inside. A closed door and a barbarian is not a puzzle.

"Oh, there you go," Vukradin says, and softens. "Right. It's more of a high-end establishment. He was more of a Common Chord slum, and this was a high-end establishment."

"He's a man of class now," I say. "He can buy his way into high society."

"Isn't it obvious?" Valphine says. "It's the quality of your talent."

"Yeah," I say, because it costs nothing.

"Not," says Vukradin.

"It's totally not, you sure."

"It definitely is not." He isn't playing. "There is a force behind our fame. Some groups are maneuvering — we're being—"

"Manipulated," I say.

"—to their advantage for some reason."

Nobody has a name for the hand doing it. That's the part that sits in my shell. Fame that arrives before the fish is caught was bought by somebody, and somebody expects to eat.

We go back to the wall. I lift our stone. Vukradin puts his palm to the dark behind it and speaks the word, and I hear the inner closure give — a click and a sigh, like a jaw unlocking. Then I put the rat down at the mouth of it and close my eyes and go with him.

Small nose first. The stone smells of the last hands that touched it: sweat, oil, cheap ink. Then a drop, more than a rat likes but less than he'll refuse, and a floor that is packed and cool. Down, not across. Whatever this is, it's under the tavern, not behind it.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: The rat slips into a windowless back office on the basement level, with a desk, papers, and supplies, and among them the unmistakable symbol of the Harpers.]

I know that harp. Everybody on the coast knows that harp; it's carved into more doorframes than the shipwrights would like to admit. My eyes open in Bluelake with a rat's memory of it still hanging in front of them.

"Oh, wow."

"Harp?" Vukradin says, hearing my face rather than a word.

"Okay, so is it, like, a storage room, or, like, what other—"

"Desk, papers, supplies," I say. There's a joke in the air about what Vukradin would not take from a room like that, and he says, "No, no, it's just a rat," and I say, "No, they're right, I can't carry anything," because it's true — my rat can barely carry his own opinions — and Vukradin says "Definitely wouldn't steal anything," twice, to be sure the wall heard.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: The office is the local headquarters of the Harpers.]

"Harper," I say. "So that's… weird." I sit with it. "The Harpers are smuggling?"

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: It remains open whether the Harpers are smuggling or arranging the ninth crate; and the Harpers, as the group is reminded, are not good people in this city.]

"Clearly that ninth crate involves some smuggling," I say, "because it's being directly delivered. So yes, the Harpers are smuggling in some capacity." Whether for themselves or for a client with deeper pockets, the wall doesn't say. A wall is a poor witness.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: The reminder extends to the claim that the only good people in this are the Order of the Gauntlet, who were misled by their political masters, have since rejected them, and now stand for the little guy.]

"Debatable," I say.

"Are there?" says Valphine.

"Reformed," Vukradin says. "They're reformed. Well, they had that whole thing with the barbarians, you know. They've been past that, they're reformed now. Basically, they're the Reformed Order of the Gauntlet."

"Fair dealing and the small folk," I say. "The city's own champions." Flat as a plank. Vukradin hears it and chooses not to.

We walk around to the front, where Brewbarry already is, and I take the door the ordinary way.

[GM NARRATION — TO BE WRITTEN: The House of a Thousand Faces is a comfortable tavern of mirrors, mannequins in antiquated fashions, lush couches, and a fire that actually draws — a high-end place in the low-end Bluelake District, with something subtly off about it.]

The fire is the tell. Nothing in Bluelake draws properly; the chimneys are all built by cousins. This one pulls clean. Somebody paid a mason who knew his trade, in a district where nobody pays for that, and the couches don't have the sag of a hundred drunk backsides. Mirrors so a man can see who comes in behind him. Old clothes on wooden bodies, watching.

"Went down, alright," I say to Vukradin, quiet, about the rat. Basement. Under our feet, under the couches, a desk with a harp on the wall.

Brewbarry is in there somewhere, happy, in the place he couldn't afford once. The rest of my bale drifts in around me and pretends to look at the mannequins. It's a good place. I need to leave it.
