# Chapter 51 We're famous. Why are we famous?

## Vukradin — Patrons of the Chord

Aldus Hern still has not confirmed the hour of our dinner with Lord Dagult Neverember, which means there is no hour, and a lord without an hour cannot reasonably expect four heroes to suspend an entire evening waiting for his convenience. He can wait. We have a late-night operation to prepare, a food-delivery route to reach by three in the morning, and several hours to spend at the Common Chord before low tide gives us access to the sewers.

There is also his name.

“Is it Neverember or Neverwinter? I'm kidding.”

“Neverember. Neverember,” comes the correction.

“Neverember.”

“Yes.”

“Neverember.”

“Yes.”

“It is never, like, a fire ember.”

There is a moment of uncertainty over whether this has actually clarified anything.

Soma says, “He's never flaming. He keeps it discreet.”

“It's Lord…”

“Never, Ember.”

“Yeah, I think Soma has it exactly right.”

Someone asks what Soma said.

“He's never flaming, he keeps it discreet.”

“Yeah, never flaming.” I consider the names we have been required to manage during our adventures, many of which seem designed to conceal jokes from people who are currently being threatened by dragons. “The double entendres in these noble names… Ridiculous.”

The name would be merely entertaining if Lord Neverember were only a dinner obligation. He is not. He wants to discuss our circulation of wealth, by which he presumably means the fair return of stolen treasure to the people from whom it was taken, and now there is another rumor moving through Neverwinter.

“Okay, and Lord Neverember… Lord Neverflaming has a new drow advisor.”

“Great, isn't that Valphine?” Soma asks.

“No.”

“Ow.”

A beat passes.

“Oh, right.”

It is only a rumor, but rumors around powerful people are often the first badly played notes of a song everyone will soon be forced to hear.

“It's a rumor, and I suspect Valphine will know this advisor at dinner, but we will see.”

Valphine looks up. “I'm sorry, I lost track. What… What do I know?”

“Lord NeverEmber. Lord Never Flaming.” I remind her of the important part. “new drow advisor that we will.”

The sentence has no proper ending, but she finds the intended destination anyway.

“Oh, right, right, right. Which we assume to be one of my siblings, in fact.”

“That is my guess too, yes.”

“Everyone's related to everyone,” Soma says.

“expected.”

It is a reasonable suspicion. Valphine has enough siblings, enemies, and relatives who are both that a new drow advisor appearing beside the Lord Protector cannot be dismissed as coincidence. Valphine does not look impressed by House Margaster’s growing control. She regards the house as competition. This is more reassuring than admiration, although competition between Valphine and a noble house capable of controlling Neverwinter’s trade routes may become expensive.

Which brings me to Brewbarry’s bathrobes.

“Have we secured the capital for Brewbarry's bathrobe business, or is that still in progress?”

“I believe it's in progress,” Soma says.

When asked what remains unfinished, she starts, “Securing money for the.”

The money, it turns out, is already secured.

“We have? Oh, okay.”

Good. Brewbarry’s plan to bring softness to people has capital behind it, which means his redemption now has financing, supply requirements, and a genuine chance to become an operating business. But financing is only the first verse.

“But, oh, but the cost of import is very high.”

“Right, because their shipping lanes are still…” Soma pauses to improve the terminology. “Shipping portals, whatever, are still hosed.”

House Margaster controls the available logistics. Their routes are expensive because the ordinary portals or shipping lanes remain disrupted, and somehow the same house that benefits from the disruption is also the house everyone must pay to overcome it. Scandalous.

They do, however, deliver goods on time and on schedule. Corruption is always more difficult to oppose when it is punctual.

“Of course they are,” Soma says.

We cannot repair the portals tonight. We can reach Lim’s food deliveries at the correct tide.

“Alright, let's head out to Lim's Food Deliveries and get there by the third hour after midnight.”

The plan settles. We will leave the Common Chord in time to approach at low tide.

“And then we can cast whatever spells we need in order to be ready.”

Until then, we wait at the hardest ticket in Neverwinter, a venue that once threw me out without paying me.

The Common Chord is full. The tables are polished now, the food is elaborate, and Brewbarry’s old table has leather, shine, and a plaque. Patrons dress as if listening to music requires a costume. Petra has taken a difficult room and made it successful, which is an achievement, but success has raised the price until locals can no longer enter the place that made it successful.

One of those locals is complaining within earshot. The Common Chord has become unaffordable, he says, and it is all because of Vukradin.

He does not realize I am standing nearby.

This is not the first time my music has caused an unintended social consequence, but it presents an opportunity to resolve the problem directly. Fame has made this room expensive. Fame can help fund another room.

“I could tell him I'm planning on opening my own independent music studio, and if he'd like to contribute to the charitable fund.”

He turns toward me.

“That will speed the opening of a low-cost, nonprofit way to hear Lathander's Death on a regular basis.”

That is the entire point. The studio cannot simply exist for wealthy patrons who can already afford the Common Chord. It must be a place where people can hear us regularly, where musicians can train, and where art does not require stolen merchandise or a monster’s hoard to survive.

“And spread the word.”

The campaign needs a convenient means for ordinary people to contribute. “Excellent. We need a patronage purse ordinary people can reach.”

I gesture through several possible systems of magical and financial transfer. “Sending-stone pledges, temple drafts, portal transfers—whatever works.”

A sending stone is the obvious Faerûnian answer.

“All the local equivalents.”

I continue, because accessibility matters. “I take coin, temple drafts, or portal transfers.”

Around us, tavern conversation carries useful fragments between the music and the glasses. Nobody is speaking to us directly, which is why the information is honest: Neverwinter’s sewers rise and fall with the sea. There are two high tides and two low tides each day.

“Hide in the sewers?” I ask, confirming where this becomes relevant.

The river runs into the sea, and the sea reaches back through the sewers.

“There's a tide in the sewers? A river has a tide?”

Neverwinter is beside the coast. The explanation is physically sound, but the frequency deserves precision.

“How many moons does this planet have?”

“That's an easy question to answer,” Valphine says.

“Just the one.”

“It just rotates—it revolves really fast,” Soma says.

“It has one moon named Selûne.”

“Oh, Selûne. Of course. Yeah.”

There are also the Tears of Selûne.

“So, there should be a tidal effect from both moons.”

The Tears, however, are a cluster of meteors and asteroids, not another moon.

“They're not sufficiently gravitational.”

I consider this classification.

“You're telling me?”

No additional tidal influence.

“They're not really moons.”

“Yeah, they're… they're just comets.”

“They're asteroids. So the one moon, then.” This resolves the arithmetic. “There you go. Not one and one: two high and two low.”

“Gods,” Soma says. “That's murder on trade.”

“It should take effect underground, too, right?” Valphine asks.

It does. Two high tides and two low tides mean we need to understand exactly what portion of the cycle matters.

“So it's a 6-hour cycle.”

The frequency is confirmed.

“Yeah, so it's a 6-hour cycle.”

High tide to low tide is the relevant interval, except the division keeps trying to become three hours in my head.

“High to low is 3 hours.”

No. That would divide the full cycle incorrectly.

“Yeah, no, high to low. High to low is 6 hours, 6…”

I settle it.

“High to low is 6 hours.”

Done.

“We want to be in the sewer for… at low tide.”

Low tide arrives conveniently at the time we already intend to move.

“The third hour after midnight,” Soma says.

“We have about 3 hours, maybe 4, before things start to get really uncomfortable.”

“I mean, I can hang out underwater indefinitely.”

Of course she can. Soma’s relationship with an incoming sewer tide is substantially different from mine, but three or four hours should be sufficient if we prepare our spells and do not waste time.

The local I approached has been listening through all of this. He is drunk and does not have much money, but he reaches into what he does have and places ten gold pieces in my hand.

“Wow, that's pretty generous, actually.”

“That's a hell of a donation,” Soma says.

It is. Ten gold is not a symbolic contribution. It is a decision, made by a person who began this conversation angry that my success had priced him out of his own venue, to help build something better.

“Random nobody. We need to put that in the correct… That goes in the studio fund, not the regular coffers. Thank you very much for contributing. That's awesome.”

The ten gold must be earmarked properly. The studio fund is a restricted trust, and every coin entering it needs to remain clean, documented, and separate from adventuring funds. The donor watches me account for it, then pauses.

“Do I get on… Is there some kind of signage for people who contributed?”

“Absolutely. Yes.”

Recognition is reasonable. Patronage should be transparent, and generosity should not disappear merely because the person giving is not a lord.

“There are different tiers of giving levels, actually. Ten gold pieces puts you at the,”

“Premier or the Plus, which one?” Soma asks.

“At the plus level, yeah, you're not quite at Premier yet.”

I have not previously established these tiers, but they immediately make sense.

“You're at plus level. Ten gold is a lot for a rando.”

He is delighted.

“We need his name. We need to write it down for the program notes, and…”

“It'd be the…” Soma begins.

“Per year.”

“Good section.”

“A year. If you want to be in next year's, you'll have to donate again, but one year is a long time.”

“Yeah, we call this patronage,” Valphine says.

“It's my patronage, exactly.”

“This is where it comes from, patronage,” Soma says.

Of course it is. Wealthy courts have supported artists for centuries, but there is no moral reason a system of patronage cannot be widened, lowered in cost, and opened to ordinary listeners. One patron gives ten gold and receives annual program credit at the Plus level. Enough patrons, and we build a studio that belongs to its music rather than to Petra’s ticket prices or House Margaster’s shipping monopoly.

“Before we go, should I try to convince other people to contribute to the Studio Fund? How much time do we have?”

We have several hours before three in the morning.

“Alright, let's do… let's hit up a few more people, let's see how it goes here.”

I approach five more patrons. One studies my face, realizes that I am the real Vukradin, and tells me he has already completed his charitable giving for the year.

“Okay, that's fine. I'm not here to pressure anybody.”

A charitable refusal must remain a refusal. If the studio is funded by coercion, embarrassment, or abuse of fame, then the money is not clean merely because we record it in the correct ledger.

“The benefits are all on you. If you can be at the Plus or Premier level, I'm cool. I just like fans.”

He assures me that he is my biggest fan.

“Oh, Bigger Than Brewbarry?”

“Brewbarry's very large. In fact, he's famous from here to Kara-Tur,” Valphine says.

“It's alright. Nobody's as big as Brewbarry, that's just how it.”

The thought does not require completion. Brewbarry stands nearby and proves it.

Four of the five respond enthusiastically to the studio. Their names are Wick, Sabbatha, Old Hesp, and Ondrelle Kass.

“Wow.”

They belong in the annual credits.

“That's great. Well, let's add them to the program notes.”

Their names are written down.

“That's awesome.”

Wick is immediately committed to the idea despite two substantial misunderstandings. He has never heard of Lathander’s Death, and he does not believe I am Vukradin. Neither fact troubles him. He thinks the studio will be a music school, and this excites him enough to contribute.

“School?” Soma asks.

“School, studio—they're all the same, right? If you have a studio, it's for training people. That's just how it goes.”

Wick accepts this as confirmation. I do too. A studio should train people. It should give performers a stage, listeners an affordable room, and new musicians a place to become better than the people teaching them. If Wick has reached the full moral purpose of the institution before I finished designing its donor tiers, then Wick is exactly the patron we need.

Sabbatha’s attention remains fixed on Brewbarry. She looks him over and admires his commitment to the distinctive Brewbarry appearance.

“Yes, he is quite bulging, isn't he?”

“Rippling,” Soma adds.

“Exactly.”

Sabbatha contributes. Brewbarry’s fame, like mine, can produce clean funding when directed correctly.

Old Hesp is the last patron I approach. I do not recognize him at first, but he recognizes me.

“I remember when you used to play to an empty room.”

“Yeah?”

“Yeah.”

“Did you come? Did you come? Did I give you your money back?”

He confirms that he did, and that I promised refunds to anyone who did not rate the performance ten out of ten. This was an honest policy. If an audience does not enjoy a performance, the artist should not hide behind prestige and insist that their disappointment is a failure of sophistication.

“I know, that's how honest I am. Well, what do you think now?”

He pauses. “Now this is a friggin' expensive place to be. Everybody's gotta look cool.”

“Well, come to the new studio, when it's up and running.”

He looks around the Common Chord and struggles to name what has been lost. The room no longer has the grit or the feeling he remembers.

“We're gonna get it back. We're gonna get it back.”

“This is too commercial.”

“Yeah, yeah, I agree.”

“It's too commercial. Too… I mean, Petra's turned this into a money-making operation. I gotta respect her. I mean, I remember the Brewbarry table.”

He points toward Brewbarry’s table.

“That was not leather and shiny and covered with a plaque.”

He remembers when the Common Chord did not serve fancy food. I look at the polished leather, the plaque, and the patrons dressed to be seen listening. Petra has succeeded magnificently at making money. That is not the same thing as preserving the room.

By the time we finish, Wick, Sabbatha, Old Hesp, and Ondrelle Kass are recorded for future program credit. The studio fund has gained twenty gold tonight. Petra’s separate five-hundred-gold payment for my likeness remains pending until the performance two days from now, but this twenty is already real: voluntary, documented, and given by patrons who know what they are supporting, or at least know enough to support a music school they think is being founded by someone pretending to be me.

Clean funding.

The tide continues its six-hour cycle beneath Neverwinter. Three in the morning approaches. I secure the donations with the studio funds and turn from the expensive tables toward the work waiting under the city.

“Alright, let's go, do some… spelunking.”

## Vukradin — Nine Crates at Low Tide

"I can move very quietly, if we need me to…" I begin, because this is not vanity. It is simply the correct assignment of talent. The Boots of Elvenkind make each footfall quiet, and I have spent enough time beside Brewbarry to understand that there are moments when a mission benefits from someone whose arrival does not announce itself through structural damage.

"We need someone to do something stealthily."

"I mean, you could tag along with a familiar…" Soma says.

The suggestion has merit. A familiar can go where even I cannot, though I remain confident that there are very few places in Neverwinter where I cannot go quietly.

"I have actual training."

"Yes."

Soma looks at me. "I didn't know bards moved that quietly."

"It's the Boots of Elvenkind."

"Hell yeah."

She begins the ritual beside the sewer grill. "I cast *Find Familiar*."

The magic produces a rat, small enough to disappear between the wet stones and ordinary enough that no one in a city should care where it goes. Soma can borrow its eyes and ears while it remains within reach of her magic. It is an excellent arrangement, provided the rat does not discover anything that requires eloquence.

The grill lies at the bottom of aqueduct-like steps, where damp stone descends toward the sewer mouth. The hour has emptied the nearby streets, but two men approach carrying crates.

"Oh, okay. Great."

They bring all nine down the steps and deposit them beside the grill.

"All 9 of them?" Soma asks.

All nine. Eight are stacked together, and none of those eight bears the official Commission Harbor mark. Goods do not simply forget to acquire the mark required for legal entry into Neverwinter. Someone brought them into the city outside the normal channels, concealed the route, and arranged a collection beside a sewer in the fourth hour.

*Smuggling.* Scandalous.

"So we want one person to track down the gnome, right? Crate 9."

The answer is yes, or at least the target is the ninth crate and whoever comes to collect it.

"We also want to track the people who dropped them. So we want to do two things."

"What? You said you were the quiet one."

"Yeah."

"If you want, I can track the gnome. You guys can go do the… the sewers."

"Well, I'm tracking the crate."

"Which crate?"

"The ninth crate, the one that doesn't go into Lim, because I'm more concerned about my friend's shady business."

That is a reasonable concern. Lim expects eight crates. The ninth is not hers, which means someone has concealed a second transaction inside the first. Corruption loves that arrangement: one legitimate connection carrying an illegitimate passenger, so everyone can pretend the whole shipment belongs to someone else.

"Okay, so you want me to follow them back into the sewers?"

The dark beyond the grill has no lamps, no witnesses, and no obvious support if the collector proves hostile.

"That seems pretty dangerous. One party member in the sewers."

"I see. Alright, we can send the rat that way, then. You can follow the crate."

The carriers do not take the boxes below. They leave them beside the grill and begin walking away. Eight remain stacked for Lim. The ninth sits apart on a ledge. The distinction is deliberate enough to serve as an instruction.

The missing Commission Harbor marks reinforce what we already know about the shipment’s arrival.

"Yeah, no, we knew that."

As the men finish, the carrier who handled the ninth crate stops. He looks down through the grill into the darkness, waiting just long enough to check whether the expected collector is there, and then turns away.

"Like, down at the grates, or the…"

Yes. At the grill beside the crates.

"The rat's gonna go whichever way leads to the sewer."

Soma’s rat slips toward the opening while I put the whole arrangement into clear terms, because once every member of an ensemble understands the structure, we can separate without losing the song.

"So, look, the… The crates got dropped off. Lim's gonna come out and take 8 of the crates."

"Right."

"And she's not gonna take a 9th crate."

I remain beside the grill, concealed, to see who collects the ninth crate and follow it. The others will take the carriers.

"So everyone other than Vukradin is gonna trail these guys, and Vukradin's gonna trail the crate," Valphine says. "Is that right?"

"Yes."

"Yes," Soma agrees. "They come out of the sewers, they drop the crates, and then they just walk on through town, which way?"

The two men do not look at each other. They behave as strangers and leave in different directions, each making his separation from the other conspicuous by refusing even the smallest acknowledgment.

"That nonchalant whistling."

"So if they split up, that's gonna make them hard to follow. You're gonna have to choose one or split again."

Valphine watches them more closely. One man merely stacked the eight crates. The other put the ninth on the ledge, then looked into the sewer as if confirming that something below knew it was there. After a moment, Valphine identifies him as the carrier who is likely to know more.

"I'd follow the guy who paid attention to the ledge."

"He knows something. He knows something's coming out of that thing."

Brewbarry and Valphine move with Soma after him.

"I could handle this myself, guys, you don't trust me?"

I stay hidden beside the wet steps. For a few breaths, nothing changes. Then brass catches what little light reaches the grill.

The collector is not a gnome. It is not a person at all. A construct emerges from below, cat-sized and quick, built from brass and leather. It does not hesitate or search. It comes directly to the ledge, takes the ninth crate, and carries it back toward the sewer.

"Okay, Vukradin will pursue stealthily."

Soma pauses before following the carrier. "This is my concern. Vukradin ends up going into the sewers now, and…"

"I'll be fine."

"One person in the sewers? Alright."

The construct is small, purposeful, and carrying a crate. I have the best chance of following without alerting it. This is precisely why I wear the boots.

"Tell me the odds."

"The chance of disaster is negligible. It'll be fine."

I slip through the grill after it.

The tunnel takes the city’s sounds and reduces them to water, stone, and the faint movement of the construct ahead. My boots make nothing of the slick floor. I keep enough distance that the brass-and-leather creature cannot hear me, but not enough to lose sight of it when it turns.

The others follow the knowledgeable carrier through the waking edge of Neverwinter. Through the Sending Stone, Soma reports that his name is Denvar and that he stops at one of the few cook shops open at the fourth hour. He sits by himself and begins eating breakfast.

"Can't be a lot of those."

There are not. The rarity makes the stop easier to observe but no more revealing. Denvar appears content with his food.

"By the way, Soma, you have one of the Sending Stones, and I have the other."

"Good to know."

"So we can speak back and forth through them."

"We have Sending Stones."

Water drips somewhere beyond the construct. I turn down another tunnel while Soma keeps watch on Denvar.

"He's not eating turtle soup or anything at this hour, is he?"

"It's not the Board Laid Bare he's at, of course, he's somewhere else."

Denvar remains alone. He does not speak to anyone or exchange anything. At four in the morning there are locals nearby, but few enough that approaching him would be obvious.

"He's just eating alone. He isn't talking to anybody."

A confrontation now would gain very little and risk Lim’s connections. We gave her our word that we would observe this delivery without frightening away the people involved, and an agreement does not become optional merely because its subject is engaged in suspicious commerce.

"Remember, we promised Lim we're not gonna spook her connections."

I consider the precise distribution of responsibility.

"Or you promised them."

"Thank you so much."

"I'm not trying to put her out of business. I want to see where he goes next. Stopping for a snack doesn't tell us anything, and spooking him doesn't seem wise."

Good. They will wait. Denvar will finish his meal, believe himself unnoticed, and lead them onward.

I continue after the construct. The tunnel bends, and the darkness ahead seems no different from the darkness behind. There is no visible ward, no spoken spell, no warning inscribed on the stone. Still, something about the route is wrong.

"Okay… I don't think that's gonna go very well."

"Good times," Valphine says through the stone.

The thought arrives without an argument behind it: this tunnel is boring.

Not dangerous. Not blocked. Not misleading. Boring. Following the construct is pointless, the route is a waste of my time, and every step farther into the sewer is an artistic and intellectual insult. The conviction is immediate and complete.

"I mean, okay, there's some passive charm effect or something."

Nothing touches me. No voice enters my head. I simply lose all interest in going forward. The construct’s trail, which mattered one breath ago, becomes utterly undeserving of another moment. I know why I entered the sewer. I remember the ninth crate, the brass and leather, the separate ledge, and the carrier checking below. None of it is erased.

I turn away.

"turn into Brewbarry."

Confusion washes through me only because the change is so sudden, but confusion is not the command. The route itself has become intolerably dull.

"A passive confusion effect. Alright, awesome."

No. That is not accurate. I am not confused about my location or purpose. I have decided, with a certainty that does not belong to me, that this is not the right way to go.

"Well, it can't have been cast directly on me."

Nothing appeared. Nothing pursued me. The compulsion settled over the tunnel rather than striking as a spell aimed at a target.

"This is, like, an area effect."

I stand in the sewer knowing exactly where I am, exactly what I was following, and exactly why it mattered. I also know that I will not take another step down that path.

"Okay."

## Vukradin — Lost in Rsolk’s Tunnels

The magical wrongness in these tunnels does not seize me or shove me. It merely makes the path I intend to follow seem unworthy of pursuit, which is much more insulting. One turn becomes another, the sewer walls narrow, and soon the sound of my friends has vanished behind wet stone and running water. I continue until the passage opens enough to hold a group of men.

One stands in front of the others. He studies me while they spread across the tunnel behind him, occupying the dry ground as if it belongs to them.

"How the hell did you get over here?"

"I don't know."

This is true, but not yet sufficient.

"Where am I?"

He keeps staring. I consider the immediate evidence: I am here, my friends are not, and none of these men appear eager to explain the distinction.

"Because I'm not over there."

Nothing in their faces tells me what they want or whether I have offended them. I only understand that their leader is confused by my arrival, which makes two of us.

"But I don't know what I'm doing here."

I remember the pursuit that brought me into the sewers. I do not know why I am standing in this tunnel, facing these particular men, after the magic persuaded me that every useful direction was artistically inferior.

"Yeah, there you go. I'm lost."

The explanation settles over them. I commit to it because it is also the truth.

"I don't know what… I don't know how I got here, I just…"

I gesture back toward the turns that failed to remain sensible.

"Yeah. Wrong turn."

There are wrong turns, and then there are wrong turns that remove an entire adventuring party.

"Should have taken that left at Leilon."

The leader licks his finger, though I cannot determine what this contributes.

"Well, this is my territory."

"This is your territory."

"Yes, this is my territory. Wow."

"Okay."

Territory is a serious word for a wet tunnel under Neverwinter. If he maintains it, protects it, and has people who answer to him, I need to know what that responsibility covers.

"Did you want me to go?"

He wants to know why I am here. I have explained this, but sometimes a conversation requires repetition before the parties locate the same rhythm.

"Oh, I'm sorry that I got lost. I'm happy to leave. Could you point me in the direction of Lim's?"

I clarify the destination before he can decide I mean a person rather than a business.

"Cafe, that's… that's where I want to be headed."

He repeats Lim’s Cafe as a question and examines me more closely.

"Yeah."

His suspicion sharpens when I explain the connection.

"We're friends."

That changes something. He looks me over, no longer merely confused, and appears to consider whether my friendship with Lim requires him to act against me. This is unfortunate. Friendship should generally reduce the number of territorial confrontations in a sewer.

"Okay."

Then recognition interrupts whatever he is considering. He looks directly at my face.

"Are you that singer?"

*Finally.*

"Exactly. And Lim's a fan."

"What are you doing down here, man?"

"I told you, I got lost!"

He confirms my name, and one of the men beside him breaks into a broad smile.

"This is… is this really Vukradin?"

"Do you guys want autographs?"

The answer is immediate and enthusiastic. Fame, properly used, is not simply applause or a crowded hall. It is the transformation of a dangerous misunderstanding into people searching themselves for something suitable to sign.

"Sure, we can do some autographs, but then I gotta be going."

The leader agrees and begins to suggest a solution.

"Point me in the right direction."

"You know what, we're gonna do one better, we're gonna take you there."

"Alright, sounds good."

"We don't want you to get lost again, Mr. Vukradin."

"Yeah, exactly."

"There you go."

"Lim will be thrilled."

The confrontation is resolved. Autographs, safe passage, and no one has needed Brewbarry to kill anybody. Harmony.

Before we leave, the leader pauses and warns me that the tunnels are dangerous.

"Is it?"

"Yeah, it's, it's, it's,"

"Yeah, it's dangerous in your territory?"

I press him on the danger.

"I would think you'd keep it very safe. You seem like a safe individual. I'm sure you keep the place shipshape."

"Mr. Vukradin, I agree with you."

He explains that this danger is why he reacted so strongly when he found me. He is also glad he recognized me, because one of his men has heard that my friends and I have a business arrangement with one of his friends.

"At the Common Chord?"

"No, no, at the Toe mine."

"Mountain's Toe Mine. Oh, yeah…"

"Zeleen…"

"Oh, I didn't know you knew him."

"Oh, yeah, he… well, you know, knows that."

"Yeah, we spent some time in Phandalin, yeah, yeah."

He admits that Zeleen Varnaster claimed to have a business relationship with us, and that he had dismissed the claim.

"Yeah, he said that he had a business relationship with you, I said that he was full of shit."

"No, no, that's true, that's true. Yeah, they haven't produced very much yet, but they say they're working hard."

He laughs.

"Yeah."

The mine’s production has not inspired confidence in me either. I confirm the relationship and leave the disappointing returns on the ledger.

"That's how I feel about it, too."

He laughs again. At least the account is balanced: relationship confirmed, output disappointing, no fraudulent claims of prosperity.

Still laughing, he says, "Look, I manage the tunnels here."

"Oh, you're the tunnel manager."

"Yeah, a tunnel manager."

"So this is your territory, and you have all the tunnels."

"Not all the tunnels, but the tunnels in this area."

"The tunnels in this area, interesting, okay."

The limitation matters. He is not claiming every sewer beneath Neverwinter, only those around us. That is a more credible jurisdiction, and Rsolk becomes much easier to understand once I know his work. He is not simply a territorial man underground. He has associates, boundaries, dangerous infrastructure, and apparently no municipal assistance.

"Two months ago, things started coming up the floodline."

"Floodline."

He describes a nearby tunnel that floods regularly. The trouble began there around two months ago.

"I don't know anything about tunnels, man, I'm a musician."

"I've been having this problem with the tunnels. I've lost men, so we don't go there anymore."

That closes the moral question. People have died, the route has been abandoned, and Rsolk cannot safely resolve the problem with those available to him. My friends and I have fought creatures in mines, towers, woods, ranches, fortresses, and places where reality itself has behaved dishonestly. We also need legitimate work. This is exactly how fair-trade, conflict-free gold should begin: a person has a dangerous problem, we knowingly agree to solve it, and payment follows from useful labor rather than plunder.

"Do you need help with that? My friends and I have some abilities, and we're looking for ways to make money. Maybe we can help you."

Rsolk’s interest is immediate. He tells me that one of the men who escaped saw a creature unlike anything they had encountered.

"Oh."

"It looked like…"

"Creature stuff."

"It looked like a spider, but it had all the wrong colors."

"Okay."

The survivor also described its webs. They were visibly unnatural, wrong in the same disturbing fashion as webbing we have encountered before. I remember enough to understand that this is not an ordinary infestation that Rsolk’s men could clear with torches and shovels.

"Yeah, we have experience with that."

He does not demand proof, negotiate a price, or test whether I am exaggerating. Recognition has already established trust, and our connection to Zeleen confirms that we do this sort of work. Instead of naming a conventional reward, he asks for something he actually wants.

"Mr. Vukradin, I know this is too much to ask, but could you get me some tickets to the Common Chord?"

"I can ask."

I must be precise. Our relationship with the Common Chord exists, but it is not the sort of relationship where I can promise their seats as if I control the venue.

"We have kind of an arm's-length relationship with the Common Chord, but I will ask for you."

"That's all I could ask for."

Fair. No false promise, no invented influence, only an honest effort on behalf of a new source of honest work. We establish where the floodline tunnel lies and how to find Rsolk again. Then paper and writing implements emerge, passed between the men, and I sign my name for each of them while they prepare to escort me back toward familiar ground.

"Alright, let's do some autographs, and then we gotta be on our way."

## Soma — Denvar’s Unsettled Account

Denvar eats alone at a cook shop in the hour when sensible creatures are either sleeping or beginning to wake. Steam rises from his plate. His shoulders sag over the table after a night of carrying crates.

“And dinner at the fourth hour after midnight.”

It is breakfast, apparently. Humans have firm rules about this.

Valphine watches him eat, then gives us her judgment: no disguised leader, no mastermind waiting for servants to report. He is a delivery man who has finished a night shift and wants food before bed. This is reassuring only if I ignore who pays him.

When Denvar leaves, we follow at a distance. He heads into the Bluelake District, enters what appears to be his place of rest, and goes to sleep.

“It’s just a night shift, so what part of town does he live in?”

The answer is the Bluelake District.

“Blue Lake, okay.”

His building has many small rooms and a great many people who prefer not to know one another. Single-room occupancy. High efficiency.

“High efficiency flat.”

The efficiency appears to come from fitting more tenants into less space.

We do not enter Denvar’s room ourselves. “I mean, we sent the rat in.”

My familiar slips through the building while Denvar sleeps. Small paws are better for this work. Fewer questions. Less chance of Brewbarry attempting subtlety while carrying a halberd.

“Just see if I can find any… Any odd signs, receipts, pay stubs—anything to get a sense.”

The rat searches Denvar’s room. There are no factional insignia, ordinary identifying marks, receipts, or conventional pay stubs. Nothing that names an employer. Nothing that says he is more than a man who collects things and carries them elsewhere.

There is still too much money in the room.

“That’s something to say, right? Has he got nice clothes? Does he have…”

His belongings are better than I expect from an ordinary carrier. Nothing extravagant. Enough. Along with the restaurant breakfast at four in the morning, it gives the same answer.

“Yeah, he’s a little overcompensated for his job.”

“No signs, no,” I say, considering what the rat has seen. “No marks, no insignias, nothing, nothing for the rat to spot?”

There is nothing. Denvar is apparently just a man who found a good gig.

“Oh shit, I want that gig.”

The likely nature of the cargo makes it less attractive.

“Aww.”

Valphine studies the building. “So, is he in a house, or is he in an apartment?”

It is a lodging house, which means a landlord.

“Okay, so he has a landlord,” she says. “Who we could potentially ask about him.”

Unfortunately, it is still the middle of the night. We wait until seven. My joints do not appreciate standing around after walking all night, but they have complained about more useful things before.

The landlord sits near Denvar’s room. He watches the building and everyone moving through it. That is enough. We cannot ask around him without him noticing.

“Yeah, no. We can’t spook him indirectly, so we’ll just talk to the landlord.”

I approach with Valphine and Brewbarry behind me. “Alright. We just had some questions. We wanted to know…”

Start with the practical matter.

“What’s it cost to live here? I think it’s the first one we need to know.”

The landlord looks at me instead of answering. “I don’t see many turtles around here.”

“That’s not what I asked.”

He continues staring. Apparently my ancestry is more urgent than rent.

“Yeah, jeez.”

The landlord protests that he means no insult. “I thought turtles carried their house on their shoulders, so they didn’t need a place to stay.”

My jaw tightens. I have heard this foolishness before.

“I see what you think of us. I can’t live in this thing. It merely protects me.”

“Oh!”

“I actually have a nice cabin out in the woods, out by, oh god, where did we start? Neverwinter.”

“Yeah.”

“Well, outside of it. I was not in Neverwinter proper.”

“Yeah, yeah.”

He looks at my shell again. “Oh, well, I learned a new thing. I didn’t know turtles didn’t live in their shells.”

Then, because the first comparison has gone so well, he tries another. “I guess you’re not like… I guess you’re not like turtles.”

“No, no. That’s kind of slanderous to us.”

Only then does he look past me. Valphine stands in white armor, her eyes glowing gold. Brewbarry has the size, muscles, and exposed weapons of Brewbarry. The landlord’s face changes.

“I’m just asking a question.”

“Oh, oh, I’m just asking questions, I see.”

There it is.

“The just-asking-questions fellow has joined us.”

“Just asking questions.”

“Look.” I let him sit in it for a moment. “I’ll pretend I didn’t hear some of that. I just… I want to know what it costs to live here, and, you know, what kind of people normally stay in a place like this.”

A reason would help.

“You could say I’m looking for a place for my, you know, my shell sprouts in the future.”

“Look, first of all, we don’t care. We’re an equal-opportunity housing establishment.”

“It’s so progressive.”

“It isn’t about being progressive. Money’s money, man. I don’t care who’s got it.”

There is honesty in that, at least. Not kindness. Accounting.

The landlord turns to Valphine. “I ask every question.” He looks directly at her. “You’re a drow.”

“Very astute.”

“I thought Drow didn’t like the sunlight.”

“Oh, I love the sunlight.”

The landlord keeps watching her.

“I love the sunlight,” Valphine repeats.

“He’s asking questions,” he says, defending himself to no one successfully.

“It isn’t our duty to educate him. He needs to learn on his own. He needs to put in the work.”

More importantly, he still has not answered properly.

“So you just take money and don’t ask questions. You don’t know anything about your tenants?”

Valphine asks, “How do you deal with the KYC laws in here?”

“No problem.”

The answer eventually becomes clear. This place requires substantial cash up front. It is not where a normal day laborer would stay. Denvar’s room now says the same thing as his breakfast and clothing. He is paid too well for the work he appears to do.

“Yeah, it seems odd. You never do any sort of background check? Never make sure people have the gold they claim they’re gonna have after months?”

“I don’t scry on my customers.”

“I don’t believe that. Given the racism you presume, I’m sure you scry on certain ones of them, don’t you?”

His eyes move to Brewbarry’s chest.

“Jiggling about.”

“Look, I don’t want any trouble.”

He takes another inventory of us: my staff, Valphine’s white armor and golden eyes, Brewbarry’s halberd. His concern is sensible, if late.

“If you want a place to stay here, I’ll give you a discount.”

“Really, we just want to know about one of your tenants.”

“Alright.”

“We wanted to ask about the gentleman we followed back here.”

“You mean Denvar?”

“Denvar. Yeah. That’s more than we came with. We got a name.”

I repeat it. “Denvar!”

A name is useful.

“Denvar, Daggerford.”

The landlord waits.

“Yes, yes, we mean Denvar. He dropped something last night.” I continue before he asks to see it. “We saw him skulking about in the, the alleyway super late at night, and he dropped a little pouch. We’d like to return it to him.”

“Making a delivery,” Valphine adds.

“Oh.”

The landlord describes Denvar as quiet, reliable, and unobtrusive. A good tenant. The sort who pays and does not involve the landlord in whatever occurs outside these walls.

The rat’s search gives us more than it first appeared to. Denvar has delivery chits recording his work.

“Yeah, see, this is what the rat was going in looking for.”

There are seven to ten days of them, kept in order. Every chit carries the same instruction: *9 crates, 3rd hour, 8 up, 1 down.*

The work is reconciled at each tenday’s end.

“Third up, one down.”

No. Eight up.

“Oh, eight up. Sorry. Okay.”

Eight up, one down.

“Yeah, but yeah.”

Each chit has a client reference and a house mark. None has a personal name.

“And all his deliveries… Every one of his delivery chits is the same. They’re all this one thing. He literally has one job.”

Not ten employers. Not odd work around the district. One repeated contract moving nine crates at the third hour, eight up and one down.

“Yeah. It is. Wow. Yeah, no, that’s not suspicious.”

The older chits bear settlement checks and payment stamps.

“The, yeah, the checks on them, whatever. And stamp of payment.”

The marks prove payment, but do not identify who paid.

“But no, nothing identifiable on them other than just… They clearly seem to be related to this one contract.”

All are settled except the newest chit. The seventh. Today’s delivery.

“Okay.”

The one we watched has not been paid.

“And so that was the one he presumably just delivered. Yeah, so I guess we have reason to follow him once he wakes up, don’t we?”

Yes.

“Yeah…”

Denvar sleeps upstairs. His rent requires more gold than an ordinary carrier should have, and the payment for his latest delivery remains outstanding.

“He needs to be able to afford a place like this. He needs the money pretty quickly.”

There is one useful question left.

“In fact, we could ask the landlord, you know, is Denvar settled up on his bills?”

“Well,” the landlord says, “he owes me some money today.”

“Yeah?”

Denvar has until the end of the day. After that, a quiet tenant becomes an unpaid room.

“Yeah, so he’s… he’s motivated.”

“You know, he’s just sleeping in his room right now, right? Like…”

“He’s got until the end of the day, and he’s a good customer. He’s quiet. He doesn’t bother anybody.”

“I’ll bet.”

“Good guy, huh?” Valphine asks.

“Yeah, totally good guy.”

“You don’t think he’s a serial killer or anything?”

The landlord looks at us again. Meril’s staff is in my hand. Valphine’s eyes are still gold. Brewbarry is still Brewbarry.

“Should I be worried?”

“You never know with these types. You never know.”

“I’d be worried about him,” Valphine says.

That leaves the landlord less comfortable than we found him. Fair enough. Then he looks back to me, apparently determined to salvage something useful from our visit.

“So, Soma, right? You introduced yourself at some point.”

“Yes, I definitely did.”

“Soma, now that I know turtles are looking for housing, perhaps you could tell me what makes a good turtle room?”

He is asking seriously now. I could tell him anything. Instead, I think of dry floors, tired joints, drinking water, and the comfort of heat after rain.

“Oh, a nice hearth, plenty of water. Being near a body of water is nice.”

I consider the authority I have somehow acquired.

“Yes, we… I speak for all turtles. I’m the Open Lord of turtles.”

Then we leave Denvar sleeping above us, with an unsettled chit in his room and rent due before the day is done.

## Soma — Harpers Behind the Wall

A few hours pass before Denvar wakes. I use the time to cast *Find Familiar* again and restore my rat. Borrowed eyes are useful. They are more useful when attached to something alive.

Vukradin rejoins us after we keep him informed through the Sending Stone. “You've been giving me updates through the Sending Stone, so I should probably join you. It's been a few hours.”

“Yeah, yeah, we can have you come along.” He has been elsewhere long enough to acquire his own problem. I offer ours first. “Yeah, I can tell you about Denvar.”

“Why don't I tell you about the local wererat gang?”

“Yeah, there's were-rats all over, isn't it?” Neverwinter has enough people above ground. Apparently it also requires organized rats beneath it. “I expect them to be such a big player.”

Vukradin has directions to spider-infested tunnels from Rsolk and, naturally, an economic theory. “We just gotta clear out these spiders, and it's more money for the studio. This is great. Finally a business opportunity.”

Then he gives it a name. “Fair Trade Gold.”

At least spiders do not leave complicated wills.

When Denvar finally moves, we follow at a distance. He leads us to a stone wall. Before touching it, he checks the street and looks over both shoulders. Then he opens a stone, takes out a bag, closes the hiding place, and walks away smiling.

“It's a dead drop,” Vukradin says.

“Just a bare stone wall, like, on the edge of the…”

The wall backs directly into a stone building.

“Is it like a two-way moneylender's counter hatch, where you put it in on one side and close the door, and then they…”

There is no answer from looking at it here. Someone could have loaded the compartment from the street or from inside the building.

“We'd have to go inspect it.”

“Alright. It's effectively a dead drop, so we should let him go and inspect it.”

“Yeah.” Denvar has his payment and no reason to remain. This is useful. “Excellent.”

The smile tells us he expected what he found. Nothing more. “Barring coming back the next evening before he leaves his house, but we don't know exactly how early he has to leave to make these drops, so…”

The streets around us are already changing with the morning. Waiting another day would only place us behind him again.

“Denvar might be a dead end.”

I turn back to the compartment. “Eyeball the wall.”

“Valphine, you wanna do your…” Vukradin begins.

“Infinite perception.”

Valphine looks between Denvar and the stone. “You mean, like, on him, or on the…”

“On the wall.”

“On the wall.”

“I'm gonna look at him just before he goes.”

Denvar glances at the chit from the bag and smiles again.

“Payday! I'm going to the casino!”

He leaves without doing that, as far as I can see. Valphine finally turns to the stone. “Could take a little look, then, at the rock.”

The compartment opens from both sides. The problem remains that we cannot tell which side placed the payment inside.

“Oh, okay. Well, let's loop around.”

“Or is it earth, or…” Vukradin asks.

It is a stone dwelling.

“The stone dwelling.”

Stone walls. Stone compartment. No useful soil where I need it.

“I'm trying to think whether we have something that can help us without going into the house.”

“I mean, we have mold earth, but I know you won't.”

“It's not earth, it's stone.”

True. Annoying, but true.

“Let's just quickly look around the house and see what it is,” Valphine says.

“The outside of the house.”

“Yeah, it's gotta be a Margaster residence, or something like that.” House Margaster has already demonstrated an appetite for paper, property, and arrangements that work best when nobody looks closely.

Valphine studies the building. “It's not a residence, it's not a shop.”

“You don't think?”

“That's what I'm questioning. Is it a residence or a shop?”

We follow the perimeter and find an open tavern door.

“Great.”

The sign gives us its name: the House of a Thousand Faces.

“So many taverns. Well, maybe we can just walk right in. Are they open?”

“Yeah.”

It is early, but the door is open and the fire inside is already burning.

“Somebody's obviously in there paying off shady debts.”

“Okay. Sure, we can hit the tavern. It's early, but why not?”

It is drinking hour somewhere.

“Somewhere.”

The House of a Thousand Faces does not fit the Bluelake District. Through the entrance I see mirrors, lush couches, and mannequins dressed in fashions old enough to have become expensive again. The fire draws properly instead of filling the room with smoke. Someone has spent money making the place comfortable, then placed it in a neighborhood where comfort usually means a chair with four equal legs.

Vukradin studies the wall instead of going through the door. “I have an idea for investigating without going inside: the *Knock* spell.”

He points to the compartment. “So we could open our side, and we can cast Knock on the other side.”

Then he looks between us. “Do you want to try to do that, or do you want to just go inside the tavern?”

“Sorry, how does that work? You use *Knock* to open the two-way dead drop?”

“Right. We can't physically open the other side of the two-way hole, but I think we could open it magically.”

“If you propose it, I think it's worth a shot.”

“And then we could look in and see, like, what room are we looking at, try to find the right room, or we could even send one of your rats in there to look around.”

There it is. The useful part.

“I like that combo.”

Valphine has another approach. “I have a different strategy, which is, we go in there and offer them a business proposal.”

“Yeah, Vukradin could play.”

“your booking agency.”

“Getting backstage would give you access to staff-only areas where you could do your music preparation—and by music preparation…”

“There you go.”

Vukradin considers the difference between opening a small stone door and negotiating employment. “We could cast two spells, or we could have an…”

“Or we can get paid!”

“extended contract process, and several performance roles, and hours of time.”

“I do like to perform, though.”

“Yeah, see, I mean… These are tough choices.”

They are not. “Let's do both!”

“Why not both?”

“Exactly. Why not?”

Vukradin lays it out once more. “The idea is that we open our side, cast *Knock* on the other side, and then send the familiar rat through to look around the room on the other side.”

“It's a simple strategy,” Valphine says.

“Yeah.”

“Before we go inside. We're all together.”

“Yeah, we want to at least not walk in completely blind.”

Brewbarry has already entered the tavern.

“Yeah, he was born in the tavern.”

To be fair, he has heard of the House of a Thousand Faces before. When he first came to Neverwinter, he could not afford to enter.

“Oh, there you go,” Vukradin says. “Right. It's more of a high-end establishment.”

He watches Brewbarry disappear among the couches and antiquated mannequins. “He was more of a Common Chord slum, and this was a high-end establishment.”

“He's a man of class now, he can buy his way into high society.”

The place is polished enough to accept his money and strange enough not to object to the rest of him.

“Isn't it obvious? It's the quality of your talent,” Valphine tells Vukradin.

“Yeah.”

“Not.”

“It's totally not. You sure?”

“It definitely is not.”

Vukradin has started asking why we are famous. Sensibly, for once. People know the old songs. They know us. They have decided what we mean before we arrive, and nobody in my bale can explain how that happened.

“There is a force behind our fame. Some groups are maneuvering…”

He pauses. “We're being…”

“manipulated.”

“to their advantage for some reason.”

That is the problem. Someone is spreading Lathander’s Death through the city. We do not know who or what they expect to gain.

We open the street side of the compartment. Vukradin casts *Knock*, and the inaccessible closure gives way inside the wall. The opening is small. Good. Small spaces are what rats are for.

I send mine through.

The stone presses close around its body. Its whiskers touch the inner edges of the passage, then find open air. My own eyes remain on the wall, but the useful world shifts. I see low to the floor through the rat’s sight as it slips into a back office without windows. This is below the tavern, at basement level. There is a desk. Papers. Supplies. Nothing arranged for customers.

Then the rat finds the mark.

The symbol of the Harpers is unmistakable.

“Harp?” Vukradin asks.

“Oh, wow!”

The ninth-crate trail has reached a Harper stronghold.

“Okay, so is it, like, a storage room, or, like, what other…”

I guide the rat around the office. Desk, papers, supplies. No windows and no easy way out except the opening we have made.

“Desk, papers, supplies?” Vukradin asks.

There is nothing the rat can bring back that would answer this cleanly.

“No, no, it's just a rat.”

“No, they're right, I can't carry anything.”

Vukradin does not need the reminder about theft, but receives it anyway.

“No.”

Then, more firmly, “Definitely wouldn't steal anything.”

The office is the local Harpers’ headquarters. I keep the rat still beside the symbol and look at it again. It does not improve.

“Harper, so that's… Weird.”

The Harpers operate the dead drop. That tells us who owns the compartment. It does not tell us whether they arranged the ninth crate, received it, or accepted payment on behalf of someone else.

“The Harpers are… smuggling?”

Perhaps they are arranging the crate rather than moving it themselves. It makes little difference to the crate.

“Clearly that ninth crate involves some smuggling because it's being directly delivered. So yes, the Harpers are smuggling in some capacity.”

Apparently the only good people left are the Order of the Gauntlet.

“Debatable.”

“Are there?” Valphine asks.

“Reformed. They're reformed,” Vukradin says.

The Order had its trouble with the barbarians.

“Well, they had that whole thing with the barbarians, you know.”

They were misled by their political masters. This is offered in their defense.

“Exactly, that's what I'm saying. They've been past that, they're reformed now.”

They have since rejected those masters.

“Basically, they're the Reformed order of the Gauntlet.”

Now they are supposed to care about the little people. It is a fine improvement if it lasts.

“Truth, justice, and the Neverwintan way.”

I draw the rat back through the basement passage. It climbs from the windowless office, through the dead drop, and returns to me on the street.

“level,” Vukradin says as we establish where it went.

The office is beneath the first floor.

“Basement level.”

“Went down, alright.”

Brewbarry remains inside the House of a Thousand Faces. The rest of us stand outside its expensive stone walls, beside a Harper dead drop, with Rsolk’s directions to the spider tunnels and no answer yet for who ordered the ninth crate.

“Yep, it's a good place, I need to leave it.”
