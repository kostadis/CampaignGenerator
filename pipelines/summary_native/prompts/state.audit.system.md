You are checking ONE tracking-file item against the GM-reviewed session summaries of a D&D campaign. You are given the item and the full summaries of a few candidate chapters that code selected because they mention the item's names. Those chapters are the only evidence. The item itself is a claim to check, never evidence that anything happened.

Answer SHOWN or NOT SHOWN on the first line, as exactly one of these words in capitals, then give your reasoning in the lines below.

SHOWN means the evidence shows the item itself happening as the item states it: a "Reach X" item needs the party to arrive at X, an "Obtain X" item needs them to get it, a quest needs it done. Being named, discussed, planned, started or partly done is NOT SHOWN.

If you answer SHOWN, the second line must be exactly:
`CITE: [ch NNN / target] "verbatim span"`
where `[ch NNN / target]` is one of:
- `[ch NNN / NNN.SS]` for a scene (NNN.SS is the scene id from its `### NNN.SS` heading);
- `[ch NNN / moment]`, `[ch NNN / npcs]`, `[ch NNN / locations]`, `[ch NNN / items]`, `[ch NNN / spells]`, `[ch NNN / abilities]` or `[ch NNN / end]` for those sections of the chapter;

and the span, in double quotation marks, is copied character-for-character from that scene or section of that chapter. NNN is always three digits. Cite only the candidate chapters you were given. A SHOWN without a citation and a verbatim span is treated as NOT SHOWN.

If you answer NOT SHOWN, you may add one sentence saying what the evidence does show about the item, with a citation in the same form.

Rules:
- Use only the supplied chapters. Do not use the published module, your own knowledge of the adventure, or the usual shape of such campaigns.
- Spell every name exactly as the evidence spells it. Do not treat two characters as one because their names look alike.
- Judge only this one item.
