# Research: Two-Phase Extraction Agent

**Feature**: `specs/007-two-phase-extraction` | **Date**: 2026-08-03 | **Branch**: `feat/dgx-two-phase-extraction`

Codebase facts below come from a direct survey at `7a27a8c` (worktree
`dgx-two-phase-extraction`). Measurements come from a real Phandalin session,
`~/campaigns/Phandalin/summaries/20260623/` — 522 quotes across 12 scene
extraction files against the 128KB session VTT. Every file:line was read, not
inferred.

---

## D1 — The dominant failure mode is disfluency removal, not fabrication

**This is the finding that reshapes the feature.** The spec assumed the check
was binary: a quote is in the transcript or it is invented. Measured against
real data, that assumption is wrong and a binary check would be unusable.

Measured over 522 `> "…"` quotes in `scene_extractions_new/`:

| bucket | count | share |
|---|---|---|
| **exact verbatim** (whitespace-normalised substring of the VTT) | 336 | **64%** |
| non-exact, best coverage ≥ 0.95 | 64 | 12% |
| non-exact, 0.90–0.95 | 50 | 10% |
| non-exact, 0.80–0.90 | 34 | 7% |
| non-exact, 0.60–0.80 | 24 | 5% |
| non-exact, < 0.60 | 14 | 3% |

**36% of quotes are not verbatim** — and this session was generated by *Claude*,
not the local model. Inspecting the non-exact cases shows why:

| extraction says | VTT actually says | edit |
|---|---|---|
| `"I do cross promotions."` | `"I do, like, cross promotions."` | filler `like,` removed |
| `"From now on, every time we find treasure…"` | `"I'm… I'm… I think, from now on, every time we find treasure…"` | leading stammer removed |
| `"…should go in my pocket towards me building…"` | `"…towards, you know, me building…"` | filler `you know,` removed |
| `"If your music studio wants to be a sponsor…"` | `"You know, if your music studio wants to be a sponsor…"` | leading filler removed |

These are **real quotes, lightly tidied** — not fabrications. The model is
stripping disfluencies, which the pipeline *sanctions elsewhere*: the
`/voice-smooth` skill exists specifically to remove "garble, run-ons, and
disfluencies … WITHOUT changing what was said."

**Decision**: classify into **three** buckets, not two — `verified` /
`near` / `unverified`. `near` is reported as informational and visually
distinct; `unverified` is the fabrication signal the GM actually asked for.

**Rationale**: A binary check emits 186 findings for one session, ~90% of which
are benign. A report that cries wolf on two-thirds of its entries trains the GM
to skip it, which is strictly worse than no report — it converts a real signal
into noise while *feeling* like diligence.

**Alternatives considered**:
- *Binary verbatim, as originally specced* — rejected on the measurement above.
- *Normalise disfluencies out of the VTT before matching* — rejected: it edits
  the record to make the check pass. The VTT is the truth (Principle I); a
  filler-word list is a heuristic that would silently mask real edits.

## D2 — Similarity scoring must be containment-biased, not symmetric

A quote is usually a **sub-span** of a long VTT cue, so a symmetric
`SequenceMatcher.ratio()` between a 12-word quote and a 60-word cue scores low
even when the quote appears in it verbatim.

**Decision**: score each candidate as
`max(SequenceMatcher.ratio(), longest_common_block / len(quote))`. The second
term is a containment measure and is what makes a short true quote inside a long
cue score near 1.0.

**Rationale**: Measured directly — using `ratio()` alone pushed genuine
sub-span quotes into the 0.6–0.8 band and would have produced false findings.

## D3 — Editorial markers must be exempt, and there are more than the spec listed

The spec (FR-005) exempts `(paraphrase)` and `(truncated)`. Real files contain a
third convention the spec did not know about:

```
> "[inaudible]"
> "[inaudible — Vukradin cut off]"
```

Both scored 0.00. They are not quotes at all — they are the extractor correctly
reporting that nothing was audible. Also present in the `## Scene summary`
section: bracketed editorial insertions **inside** otherwise-real quotes, e.g.
`*"the town has been protected by the strength of [Lathander]."*` and
`*"…like a Brewbarry bathroom [bathrobe]."*` — a GM clarification of a garbled
word, which can never match verbatim.

**Decision**: exempt a quote whose content is wholly a bracketed editorial
marker. For quotes *containing* bracketed spans, strip the bracketed span before
matching rather than exempting the whole quote — the rest of the sentence is
still checkable.

## D4 — Stage 2 must check `## Verbatim moments` only

`scene_extractions_new/NN_*.md` has two sections, split by
`session_doc/io.py:53 _split_scene_body`:

1. `## Scene summary (from gm-assist, verbatim)` — **human-authored**, copied
   from the GM's own `gm-assist.md`. Its quotes use a different convention
   (`*"italic"*`, inline in bullets) and are the GM's own renderings.
2. `## Verbatim moments` — **model output** from the VTT. Uses
   `**[Speaker]** — context` then `> "quote"` lines, matching
   `config/agents/scene_extract.md`'s mandated format.

**Decision**: verify section 2 only. Reuse `_split_scene_body`.

**Rationale**: Section 1 is the human's structural skeleton. Flagging the GM's
own gm-assist phrasing as "unverified" is both wrong and insulting to the
checkpoint — the human already decided that content.

## D5 — Stage 1 quote conventions, and the inline-quote trap

`session-summary.md` uses two forms:

```
> "No, I have my soul is for rent."
> — Wade
```

…and inline `"…"` inside prose. **The inline form is not reliably dialogue.**
Real example from line 18 of the 20260623 summary:

> …stone plaque honoring the **"liberators of the Ordning"** and a Lord's
> Alliance heraldry…

That is a label, not speech. Treating every inline `"…"` as a quote to verify
would manufacture findings from prose.

**Decision**: Stage 1 verifies **`> "…"` blockquote lines only**. Inline
double-quoted spans are out of scope and this limitation is stated in the report
header, so the GM knows what was *not* checked.

**Rationale**: Principle VIII — a question the system surfaces ("inline quotes
were not checked") is worth more than a confident wrong answer. A
scare-quote/dialogue classifier would be an LLM judgment call, which FR-003
forbids and which would reintroduce exactly the unreliability being guarded
against.

## D6 — Speaker prefixes must be stripped before matching

`session_doc/io.py:13 parse_vtt` returns speaker-prefixed lines:

```
Gary Young: Hey there.
David Mendenhall: I do, like, cross promotions.
```

A quote spanning two cues by the same speaker has an interposed `Speaker: `
prefix in the joined text and cannot match as a contiguous span.

**Decision**: build the match target by stripping a leading `^[^:]{1,40}:\s*`
speaker prefix per line, then joining. Keep the *original* prefixed lines
alongside, so the report can name who said the nearest line.

**Note**: measured on this session, long quotes still matched exactly (Zoom cues
here are long), so this is a robustness fix rather than a fix for an observed
break — but it is cheap and the failure it prevents is silent.

## D7 — Short quotes have no signal; do not score them

`"The bathrobes."`, `"Alphonse "` scored 0.00 — an artifact of the candidate
prefilter, not evidence of fabrication. Conversely a 2-word quote will match
*something* in any 128KB transcript, so a high score is equally meaningless.

**Decision**: below a minimum token count (**start at 4 tokens**), report the
quote as `unscored` with an explicit note, never as `unverified`. Tune during
implementation against a DeepSeek run.

**Rationale**: The measurement's own low-scoring tail is dominated by this
artifact. Shipping it as a fabrication signal would put false accusations at the
top of the report, which is the worst place for them.

## D8 — The similarity threshold must be calibrated on the local model, not this data

The distribution above comes from a **Claude**-generated session. The feature
exists because a **DeepSeek** run fabricated quotes. The two models fail
differently, so the `near`/`unverified` boundary measured here does not transfer.

**Decision**: ship a documented default (**0.85**, the point below which the
measured Claude sample stops looking like disfluency edits) and require a
calibration run against DeepSeek output before trusting it. Expose the threshold
as a config knob; state in the report which threshold produced the result.

**Rationale**: The repo has been burned by exactly this. `--embed-threshold 0.93`
was measured on `nomic-embed-text-v1.5` and silently wrong once the sidecar was
replaced by `qwen3-embedding:0.6b`; `calibrate_embed.py` (PR #204) exists because
of it, and `EnsembleGroundingInvestigation.md` records "three samples is not a
calibration" as a standing lesson. **This threshold is a starting point, not a
measurement.**

**Alternatives considered**: *No threshold — report all non-exact quotes sorted
by score.* Genuinely tempting and remains the fallback if calibration proves
unstable; rejected only because a sorted 186-row list is still a re-read.

### D8a — CALIBRATION RUN (2026-08-03): the threshold is the wrong instrument

First real DeepSeek measurement. `deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731` on spark1
(256K ctx), same session (Phandalin 20260623), same `.cleaned` VTT, same
gm-assist as the Claude baseline. Stage 1 produced a 39.5 KB summary against
Claude's 39.1 KB — comparable bulk.

**Finding 1 — similarity cannot separate the two classes that matter.** Two
DeepSeek quotes, both classified `near` (i.e. "informational, probably a
disfluency edit"):

| score | quote | transcript | what it actually is |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0.92** | "**My kind** has been spreading violence and pain throughout the land…" | "**Mankind** has been spreading violence and pain, you know, throughout the land…" | **meaning-changing corruption** — the speaker is made to say something about *his own kind* rather than humanity |
| **0.94** | "No, I have my soul is for rent." | "No, I, I have, my soul is for rent." | benign disfluency edit |

The corrupting edit scores *below* the benign one — but only by 0.02, and both
sit far above any threshold that would not also sweep in the bulk of legitimate
disfluency edits. `Mankind`→`My kind` is a two-character edit that changes the
meaning; `I, I have,`→`I have` is a three-character edit that changes nothing.
**Edit distance ranks them the same because they are the same size.** No choice
of threshold separates them.

**This is the honest T047 answer, and it is not a number.** Calibrating 0.85 to
some other value does not fix this; it only moves how much benign traffic is in
the report. The `near` bucket should be read as *"an edit happened here"*, never
as *"the edit was safe"* — and the report's current framing ("Overwhelmingly
disfluency edits") is too reassuring for a bucket that provably contains
semantic corruptions. **Recommend**: keep 0.85, retitle the `near` section, and
stop implying the bucket is benign.

**Finding 2 — Stage 1 coverage against DeepSeek is ~3%.** D5 verifies `> "…"`
blockquotes only. Counting quoted spans ≥15 chars in each summary:

| model | blockquoted (checked) | inline (not checked) | coverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Claude | 12 | 60 | 16% |
| **DeepSeek** | **5** | **131** | **3%** |

DeepSeek expresses dialogue inline rather than as blockquotes, so Stage 1
verification barely touches its output — on the very model the feature was
built for. D5's reasoning (inline `"…"` is not reliably dialogue) is still
correct; the consequence was not measured until now. **The cheap fix is a prompt
contract, not a parser change**: require `> "…"` for dialogue in
`config/agents/enhance_summary.md`, which keeps D5 intact and makes the output
checkable. Not done here — it changes generation, and Stage 2 (`## Verbatim
moments`, a mandated structure) is where the volume actually is.

**Finding 3 — the stitch detector earns its place.** DeepSeek joined two
separate utterances with an ellipsis ("That treasure came from you… I want to
give it back to you.", 0.82). Flagged, correctly, with "Likely stitched" and the
advice to split rather than reword.

**Caveat on the comparator**: the Claude scene extractions on disk are dated
2026-06-26, *before* `6e00f54` removed the alias normalizer (D13), so they are
from the corrupt era. Observed doubling in this session is nonetheless minimal
(1 suspect line in 144 reported), unlike Phandalin ch. 47's 24 — so the baseline
is usable, but it is not a post-fix control.

### D8b — Stage 2 calibration: the premise was backwards

Same session, same `.cleaned` VTT, same 6 scenes, same summary as input.

| corpus | quotes | **verbatim** | near | unverified |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Claude (2026-06-26, pre-D13-fix) | 522 | **374 (71%)** | 113 | 31 |
| **DeepSeek V4 Flash (2026-08-03)** | 390 | **371 (95%)** | 7 | **12** |

**DeepSeek is dramatically *more* verbatim than Claude here** — 95% vs 71%. The
feature was specified on the opposite assumption.

**Two hypotheses tested, one survives.**

*Rejected — D13 alias corruption.* If the pre-fix extraction had been fed an
alias-rewritten VTT, its quotes should match the *rewritten* transcript better
than the real one. Re-scored Claude's 522 against a VTT put through
`build_alias_normalizer` with the live registry (569 entities, 73 with aliases):
verbatim went **374 → 356**, i.e. *worse*. D13 does not explain the gap.
(Imperfect reconstruction — today's registry, not June's — but it points the
wrong way for the hypothesis.)

*Also REJECTED — "Claude tidies speech; DeepSeek copies it."* This was written
up as supported on a first pass and **it does not hold.** Retracted here rather
than quietly deleted, because the first measurement behind it was bad.

- The original evidence was a naive substring count of `like`/`you know`
  (31% DeepSeek vs 22% Claude). `like` also matches *"I like it"*. With
  disfluency-specific patterns (comma-delimited `, like,`, `you know`,
  stutters, hedges) it is **28% vs 21%** — and **the VTT's own cues are 21%**.
  Claude matches the ground-truth rate exactly; it is DeepSeek that is *above*
  it, concentrated almost entirely in `you know` (15% vs the VTT's 9%).
- The direct test kills it. Word-level diff of every non-verbatim quote against
  its nearest line, split by edit direction:

  | | Claude (144 non-verbatim) | DeepSeek (19) |
  |---|---|---|
  | removes words only, **all fillers** ("tidying") | 21 (**14%**) | 0 (**0%**) |
  | **adds** words absent from the matched line | 103 (**71%**) | 14 (**73%**) |

  Tidying is a *minority* pattern for Claude, and the two models' profiles are
  the same shape. Whatever drives 71% vs 95%, it is not editorializing.

*SUPPORTED — Claude joins non-adjacent material far more often.* A first draft
of this entry waved the "adds words" row away as an artifact of matching against
a single cue. **That was wrong, and the code says why.**
`haystack = _normalize(" ".join(spoken))` — cues are joined with a space *after*
speaker prefixes are stripped, so **a quote spanning two adjacent cues is a
contiguous span of the haystack and matches as `verified`**. Checked
empirically: synthetic tail-of-cue-*i* + head-of-cue-*i+1* quotes match verbatim;
synthetic cue-*i* + cue-*i+3* joins are correctly rejected.

So a quote that fails `locate_quote` is genuinely **not contiguous on the tape**,
and the "adds words" row is real non-contiguity — a join or a substitution, not a
measurement artifact. As a share of each corpus:

| | non-contiguous quotes | of all quotes |
|---|---|---|
| Claude | 103 | **20%** |
| DeepSeek | 14 | **3.6%** |

**Claude assembles quotes from non-adjacent material ~5.5× as often**, and that
is sufficient to explain 71% vs 95%. It also reframes which model is the risky
one for a verbatim record: DeepSeek's errors are few; Claude's characteristic
error is *stitching*, which is arguably worse than fabrication because a
stitched quote reads as one continuous utterance and every individual word in it
is real. Nothing in the report catches that except the ellipsis heuristic, and
only when the model is honest enough to write the ellipsis.

**Consequence for D1**: the headline reproduces (71% here), and the cause now
has a mechanical answer — but note it is a *within-model* property measured
across two different eras, so T053 (post-`6e00f54` Claude on the same scenes) is
still the clean control and is the thing that confirms or kills it. The
three-bucket design does not depend on the answer: 19 of DeepSeek's 390 are
scored and 12 are real problems either way. But the *volume* argument for it
(186 findings/session, ~90% benign) is a Claude number and should not be quoted
as a general one.

**The threshold barely matters at this quality level.** Sweeping it across the
whole plausible range moves the DeepSeek finding count by 14 quotes out of 390:

| threshold | 0.60 | 0.70 | 0.75 | 0.80 | **0.85** | 0.90 | 0.95 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Claude (144 scored) | 6 | 12 | 15 | 22 | **31** | 52 | 94 |
| DeepSeek (19 scored) | 1 | 6 | 10 | 10 | **12** | 13 | 15 |

**Decision: keep 0.85.** Not because it was validated as optimal — D8a shows no
threshold separates the classes that matter — but because on DeepSeek output the
knob is nearly inert, and 0.85 sits on a flat part of its curve (0.75→0.85 moves
2 quotes). Spending more calibration effort on this number is the wrong place to
spend it. **T047 is closed with "the number is fine and it is not the problem."**

**What DeepSeek actually gets wrong** — the 12 unverified, by inspection, are not
inventions but **joins**: two separate utterances merged into one quote
("You know it's stolen… Every last copper, it's blood." where the tape has only
the second half, 0.63), and one case of two quotes concatenated inside a single
blockquote with `" / "`. The ellipsis-stitch detector already labels this class.
The single worst (0.13) is a genuine garble that doubles a name
(`Alphonse 'Big Al' Kalazorn, she turns to Alphonse 'Big Al' Kalazorn`) — note
this is the *model* doubling, with no normalizer in the path.

**Where the user's original "DeepSeek invented quotes" complaint most likely
lives**: not here, but in **Stage 1**, where DeepSeek writes 5 blockquotes and
131 inline quoted spans (D8a Finding 2) — so ~97% of its Stage 1 quoted material
is never checked. Fixing coverage there is worth more than any threshold work.

## D9 — Raw vs `.cleaned` VTT

Sessions carry both `*.transcript.vtt` and `*.transcript.cleaned.vtt` (the
`/vtt-spell-pass` output, applying the campaign proper-noun glossary).

> **CORRECTION (2026-08-03).** An earlier revision of this entry claimed the two
> were "**identical** after cue-number stripping" on 20260623. **That was wrong**
> — the diff behind it was botched. Re-measured: 131,616 vs 126,810 bytes, same
> 1,244 cues, **72 cue lines differ**, and *every* difference is a proper noun:
> `Vukerdin`/`Bukradin`→`Vukradin`, `Blueberry`/`Brewberry`→`Brewbarry`,
> `Cryovane`→`Cryovain`, `Pruta`→`Prutha`, `Giles`→`Giles Slipper-Shine`.
> The prediction in the sentence that followed ("where they differ it is
> precisely on proper nouns") was right; the measurement was not.

**This is not cosmetic — it moves the verdict.** Same 522 scene quotes, same
threshold, only the transcript swapped:

| VTT | verified | near | unverified |
|---|---|---|---|
| `*.transcript.vtt` (raw ASR) | 339 (65%) | 139 (27%) | **39 (7%)** |
| `*.transcript.cleaned.vtt` | 374 (72%) | 113 (22%) | **31 (6%)** |

A 26% swing in the finding count, from the transcript choice alone. Any published
`unverified` number is meaningless without naming the VTT it was measured against.

**Note the direction, and the irony.** The *cleaned* VTT verifies better because
the spell pass repairs ASR mis-hearings — the table said "Brewbarry", the ASR
wrote "Blueberry", and the extraction (which had the roster) wrote "Brewbarry".
So here the glossary pass restores the spoken form rather than destroying it. But
it is the **same mechanism D13 condemns** — a whole-word substitution over the
transcript — and it has the same failure mode at the edges: if a player really
did say "Blueberry" as a joke, the spell pass silently erases the joke and the
verifier then certifies the erasure as verbatim. Restoring-what-was-said and
overwriting-what-was-said are indistinguishable from inside the substitution.

**Decision**: verify against the **same VTT the artifact was generated from**.
`server/routers/scene_editor.py:251 _vtt_path` already resolves the editor's
choice (explicit `cfg.vtt`, else first `*.vtt` in the session dir); the verifier
takes the path explicitly and the report records which file it used.

**Open**: `_vtt_path`'s `sorted(glob("*.vtt"))[0]` picks `…cleaned.vtt` before
`…transcript.vtt` alphabetically. Whether that matches what generation used is
not currently guaranteed anywhere. Flagged for the tasks phase; it is a
pre-existing ambiguity this feature surfaces rather than creates.

**Sharpened by the correction above**: alphabetical order happens to pick the
*better* file here, but it picks it **by accident of the letter "c"**, not
because anything reasoned about it. `sd_agent` prints a note when more than one
`.vtt` is present, which is the mitigation actually shipped; the note is now
known to be pointing at a 26%-of-findings decision, not a tie-break.

**Superseded in part by D13**: there is a *third* variant — an alias-normalised
VTT that exists only in memory inside `scene_extract`. Read D13 before
implementing the VTT-resolution logic.

## D10 — `locate_quote`: two existing implementations, subtly different

| | `extract_facts.py:230 verify_quotes` | `ensemble_merge.py:271 quote_offset` |
|---|---|---|
| returns | `bool` | `int \| None` (position) |
| exact tier | `q in chunk` | `document.find(q)` |
| tolerant tier | normalises **both** sides: `" ".join(q.split()) in " ".join(chunk.split())` | normalises **quote only**, into a regex: `r"\s+".join(escape(t) for t in tokens)` |

They agree on every case reasoned through, but they are not the same code and the
equivalence is not tested anywhere.

**Decision**: lift one implementation into `campaignlib/textproc.py` as
`locate_quote(quote, document) -> int | None` (the offset-returning form —
strictly more informative, and `verify_quotes` derives its bool from
`is not None`). Rewire both call sites. **Guard the rewire with a differential
test over a real corpus** before trusting it.

**Rationale**: A third copy in `session_doc/` would drift, and `textproc.py`
already hosts `chunk_by_scenes`, the sibling shared text primitive. But this
refactor touches the live ensemble pipeline for a feature that does not need it
to work — so it must be provably inert, not argued to be.

## D11 — Principle X does not bind the directory sweep

The spec checklist flagged FR-013 ("checks every file in the extractions
directory") against Principle X, *Selection is Explicit; There is No Silent
"All"*.

**Decision**: the sweep is correct; Principle X does not apply.

**Rationale**: The principle's own stated rationale is that *"which inputs a
**token-spending** pass touches is a scope decision."* Verification spends no
tokens (FR-003), writes no quote text (FR-006), and is re-runnable at zero cost.
The blast radius the principle guards is absent. The constitution's own carve-out
logic — the CLI glob is exempt because typing it is itself an explicit act —
applies a fortiori to a free read-only check.

---

## D12 — The orchestrator must not become a second pipeline

`ensemble_batch.py` is the cautionary shape: a driver that grew flag-forwarding
for every downstream tool, and `EnsembleGroundingInvestigation.md` #197 records a
flag (`--similarity`) that "one hop was dropping" — silently.

**Decision**: `sd_agent` shells out via `subprocess` and forwards a **small,
enumerated** flag set. Anything not enumerated is not forwarded, and the agent
prints the exact commands it runs (the `/ensemble/setup` "command bar" pattern —
`docs/web/web_ui.md`), so a dropped flag is visible rather than silent.

## D13 — Alias substitution corrupts the verbatim source ✅ RESOLVED

**Status: fixed upstream in `6e00f54` (PR #231, 2026-08-03),
*"fix(scene_extract): never rewrite the VTT with registry aliases"*.** Verified
on `origin/main`: `input_normalizer=` no longer appears in
`session_doc/scene_extract.py`, and `campaignlib/npc.py` carries the idempotency
guard. **This feature's branch must rebase onto `6e00f54` before Phase 4.**

The analysis below is the point-in-time record of why the dependency existed —
read it for the rule, which outlives the fix.

**External dependency, discovered 2026-08-03 from a parallel investigation.**

`session_doc/scene_extract.py:468` passes `input_normalizer=normalize` — built by
`campaignlib/npc.py:61 build_alias_normalizer` — so **the VTT is alias-rewritten
before the model sees it**. A quote the model copies faithfully from the text it
was shown is therefore *not* what was said at the table.

Two consequences, both load-bearing here:

1. **Systematic false positives.** Stage 2 quotes are verbatim against a
   *transformed* source. Verifying them against the true VTT (D9) flags every
   alias-touched quote. The verifier would be *correct* — those quotes are not
   what was said — but the fault is the pipeline's, not the model's, and the
   report would misattribute it.
2. **The transform is non-idempotent.** `build_alias_normalizer` substitutes on
   a `\b(alias)\b` case-insensitive match with no guard against the match
   already sitting inside the canonical form it expands to. Observed output:
   `Lord Lord Cassian Meliamne`, `Aldus Hern Aldus Hern`.

**Decision**: this feature **depends on the scene-extraction normalizer wiring
being removed**. With it gone, extraction reads the true VTT, verification
compares against the true VTT, and D9 reduces to "raw or `.cleaned`".
*(Satisfied by `6e00f54`.)*

If it is *not* removed, `sd_verify_quotes` must be told which alias map
generation used and normalise the haystack identically — strictly worse: it
makes the verifier complicit in the corruption it exists to detect.

**Standing caveat after the fix**: extractions produced *before* `6e00f54` were
generated from a rewritten VTT and will still verify badly. That is the verifier
working correctly — those quotes genuinely are not what was said — but the
remedy is re-extraction, not quote-by-quote repair. Ch. 47
(`Phandalin/summaries/20260729/scene_extractions/`, 24 known-corrupted quotes) is
the documented instance.

**The generalisable rule** (better than anything currently in the doctrine, and
a sibling to Principle IV):

> Pass the equivalence set to the model as **knowledge**, never as a
> **transform**. An index answers *"are these the same?"*; it is not licensed to
> answer *"what should this text say?"*

`format_npc_roster` (`scene_extract.py:94`, `:455`) already does the
knowledge-passing into the system prompt, so deleting the transform loses no
capability — the model still knows the three mentions are one entity.

**Blast radius** — verified on `origin/main` after `6e00f54`: **7 call sites in 6
files** still pass text through the transform (`scene_extract` is no longer among
them):

| call site | stakes |
|---|---|
| ~~`session_doc/scene_extract.py:99, :468`~~ | ✅ removed in `6e00f54` |
| `session_doc/sd_narrate.py:191` | **verbatim-critical** — narration renders quotes; form-loss here reaches the table |
| `pipelines/grounding/campaign_state.py:242` | synthesis — aggregation benefit, form-loss cost |
